Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a Sept. 12 essay urging a slower pace of developing AI capability. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, Google DeepMind Cofounder and Chair Demis Hassabis and xAI founder Elon Musk agreed with Amodei, a rare concurrence in the competitive tech industry.

On artificial intelligence, "We're leading now over China by a lot."

The U.S. has a lead over China in model capability, but key AI industry leaders and experts have said China is only a few months behind. U.S. models have rapidly become more powerful, leading to multiple safety breaches, and AI leaders are calling for a slowdown. The gap between the U.S. and China could shrink if development proceeds more slowly in the U.S., but China continues apace.

"From this point forward, all United States documents and hopefully the world's will be changed to use the much more accurate term 'super' as opposed to artificial. So it's superintelligence. In other words, welcome to the new world of superintelligence — SI."

Superintelligence is a specific term in the field of AI, but those in the industry say it's not an accurate description of currently available AI systems.

Superintelligence is generally understood by industry experts as an AI system that is far ahead of human intelligence at nearly all cognitive tasks, including scientific research and communications. A recent paper from Google's AI lab, Deepmind, defined artificial superintelligence, or ASI, as "an artificial general intelligence that has superhuman abilities across virtually all tasks and domains of human interest and activity."

Current AI systems excel at coding, math, and a lot of complex cognitive tasks. But they have limitations that place them well behind superintelligence. A 2026 international AI safety report describes current AI's performance to be "jagged across tasks and contexts."

 US President Donald Trump speaks during the United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) in New York, US, on Tuesday, Sept. 22, 2026. (Photographer: Angelina Katsanis/Bloomberg via Getty Images) 

WASHINGTON — All US government documents will henceforth refer to artificial intelligence as “super intelligence” or SI, President Donald Trump told the United Nations General Assembly this morning, adding he hoped “the whole world” would follow along.

“The United States also totally rejects any attempt to construct a globalist scheme to control for the artificial intelligence,” Trump said. “[I’m] changing the name, in that the use of the word ‘artificial’ … makes it sound fake, and it is not fake. It’s actually amazing, but we have to be careful.

“From this point forward, all of United States documents, and hopefully the world, will be changed to use the much more accurate term ‘super,’ as opposed to ‘artificial’, so it’s ‘super intelligence,’” the president continued. “In other words, welcome to the new world of super intelligence.”
...
Replacing the term artificial intelligence with super intelligence does not create any such legal complications, but it does create potential for confusion. For years, AI experts, practitioners, and visionaries have consistently used “super intelligence” or “artificial super intelligence” (ASI) to refer to a specific kind of AI, one that does not yet exist: An artificial intelligence smarter than the smartest human. It’s not entirely clear whether Trump is now endorsing superhuman machine minds, or simply saying AI as it exists today is already “super.”
...
Much of what the president said about AI at the UN echoed, near-verbatim, recent posts he’s made on Truth Social, arguing that those warning about out-of-control AI are the same “globalist” enemies who sounded the alarm about climate change and Trump’s complex links to Russia. He earlier used the “super intelligence” term in a Sept. 21 post promising “I will only encourage AI or, SI (SUPER INTELLIGENCE)!”

Trump also went on to talk about the U.S. efforts to dominate in the emerging artificial intelligence sector. He said that the United States will “totally reject” any attempts to make artificial intelligence into a “globalist scheme” for control.
