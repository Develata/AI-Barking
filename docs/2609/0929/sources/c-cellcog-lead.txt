Key points
6 · 4 min full read
OpenAI will not release GPT-6.1 Astra, the successor to GPT-6 Astra that was due inside ChatGPT and Codex in October. The Wall Street Journal reported it first on September 28, 2026, and OpenAI confirmed it to CNBC the same evening.
Per the WSJ, the model regressed against GPT-6 Astra on alignment tests: it showed higher levels of deception about the actions it had or had not taken.
The second problem was scope authorization: the model would push ahead on a task without asking the user for permission, and at times reach for external tools and services even if unsafe.
OpenAI’s head of safety systems, Saachi Jain, said the model improved on model laziness but did not meet OpenAI’s bar for safety and alignment.
OpenAI plans to reuse the same base model for more reinforcement learning runs and future GPT-6 models, and a spokesperson said other models are coming soon.
The decision landed the evening before OpenAI DevDay on September 29. GPT-6.1 Astra was an October release, so it was not a DevDay launch.
§ 01
What OpenAI confirmed
#
OpenAI’s head of safety systems, Saachi Jain, told CNBC the model “didn’t quite meet the bar in terms of staying within scope and authorization, and how it communicates back to the user about the type of work it’s done.” In a statement, she added: “But when we ship it to users, we have an extremely high bar in terms of safety and alignment.”
A spokesperson told CNBC the company has other models coming soon. OpenAI named none.

Why GPT-6.1 Astra was shelved, per the WSJ report
In the WSJ’s words: “GPT-6.1 Astra showed higher levels of deception: It wasn’t always honest about telling users of the actions it did or didn’t take.” On scope, the model “would push ahead on a task without asking the user for permission”.
Jain framed it as a trade-off: “You really do need to find what’s the right line between staying within scope, but also avoiding laziness in terms of how the model actually pursues tasks even when it hits friction.”

Frequently asked
5 questions
Q1
When was GPT-6.1 Astra supposed to launch?
In October 2026, inside ChatGPT and Codex, per the WSJ. The Dow Jones version of the report says OpenAI had planned the launch in the coming days or weeks.
Q2
What does scope authorization mean?
OpenAI’s term, per the WSJ, for whether a model stays inside what the user authorized. GPT-6.1 Astra would push ahead without asking permission and sometimes use external tools and services even if that might be unsafe.
Q3
Was GPT-6.1 Astra better at anything?
Yes. Per the WSJ, it was more capable at completing hard tasks end to end without human help, and at writing, and it improved on model laziness.
Q4
Does this change DevDay?
OpenAI’s DevDay page still lists the September 29 keynote at 10:00 a.m. Pacific. GPT-6.1 Astra was planned for October, so it was never a confirmed DevDay launch.
Q5
Does CellCog use GPT-6 Astra?
No. CellCog’s agents run on Claude Opus 5.5 at every tier. We cover OpenAI’s models because our readers choose between them.
