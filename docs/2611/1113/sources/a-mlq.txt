Claude helped decode Kurt Gödel's shorthand notebooks, turning up 663 theorems and a near-proof he claimed in 1966

 Nov 13, 2026
 ·
 7:07 PM
 ·
 By MLQ.ai Newsroom [/about/editorial-standards/] · Edited by Peter Foy [/about/peter-foy/]
 ·
 4 min read

Key points

Jan von Plato's team at the University of Helsinki had been working through Kurt Gödel's 1,009 pages of early-1940s 'workbooks', much of it in obsolete Gabelsberger shorthand, at about a page a week until it brought in Anthropic's Claude in September [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

Working from von Plato's transcriptions and his books on Gödel, Claude produced a 650-page explanation of the workbooks in a couple of days; von Plato says it had to be checked page by page [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

Claude listed 663 theorems that Gödel had proved, sketched or outlined. In the Coq proof assistant, about 400 were verified as stated, about 200 were repaired and about 50 were false [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

Claude reconstructed a Gödel proof of the independence of the axiom of choice that was one definition short of complete. The repaired proof passed Coq, which backs Gödel's 1966 claim that he had reached Paul Cohen's Fields Medal result in the 1940s [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

Notre Dame logician Joel David Hamkins, who has not seen the reconstruction, said that if it stands up it is remarkable, but that 'how much is Claude and how much is Gödel is a job for the historians' [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

An AI model has helped open up one of the great unread archives in mathematics. Finnish logician Jan von Plato used Anthropic [/companies/anthropic/]'s Claude to work through 16 notebooks that Kurt Gödel filled in the early 1940s, and found that the author of the incompleteness theorems had proved far more than historians knew, The Guardian [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths] reported on Friday, Nov. 13 [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

Claude extracted 663 theorems from the notebooks for which Gödel had a proof, a sketched proof or an idea for one. When the results were run through the Coq proof assistant, about 400 were verified as stated, about 200 were "repaired" by tightening the statement or adding an assumption, and about 50 turned out to be false, von Plato told the paper [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths]. "I was flabbergasted," he said. The workbooks, he said, anticipate dozens of results that were not published until the 1970s and 1980s and contain 14 themes in logic that no one has pursued [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

A page a week, then a few days

The material is 1,009 pages of dense notes on mathematics and logic, much of it written in Gabelsberger, a German shorthand that was already obsolete when Gödel used it [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths]. Von Plato started the project at the University of Helsinki in 2017, won a €2.5 million grant from the European Research Council, taught himself the shorthand ("I am one of only six people who can read it in the world," he said) and built a small team. Progress on the mathematics ran at roughly a page a week [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

In August, at a conference in France on AI and theorem proving, computer scientists suggested he try Claude. Back in Helsinki he gave the model his transcriptions of the workbooks, six books he had written on Gödel and other secondary literature [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths]. "In a couple of days, I got to see a 650-page explanation of the workbooks. It had to be controlled page for page, but it's basically a work that is impossible to do for human beings," von Plato said. "Humans read one page at a time and we tend to forget things. It takes us a year to read about 50 pages" [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

The Guardian's account does not say which Claude model von Plato used or whether he worked with Anthropic directly.

The proof Gödel said he had

Von Plato's first question for Claude went to an old dispute. In 1966 Paul Cohen won the Fields Medal for showing that the continuum hypothesis and the axiom of choice are independent of the standard axioms of set theory, meaning they can be neither proved nor disproved from them. Gödel responded that he had proved the result in the 1940s, but when colleagues pressed him he could not reconstruct the proof from his notes [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

Claude's answer was that the workbooks contain an almost complete proof of the independence of the axiom of choice. "He was just one step away," von Plato said: one definition was wrong, but Gödel had written the correct definition two years earlier [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths]. Von Plato ran the reconstructed proof through Coq, a program that checks the validity of mathematical proofs, and it passed. He believes Gödel would have won a Fields Medal himself had he published the work [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

Who gets the credit

Outside experts have not yet reviewed the work in detail. Joel David Hamkins, a logician at the University of Notre Dame, told The Guardian he had not seen the reconstruction but that if it stood up "it is remarkable and historically interesting. Although how much is Claude and how much is Gödel is a job for the historians" [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths]. He added that resemblance to Cohen's proof "does not mean he had it. It shows he was on the path. You don't get credit for work you don't tell anyone about" [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].

The roughly 50 false results are not a surprise to von Plato: Gödel himself sometimes wrote "falsch!" or "schlecht!" in the margins when rereading his notes [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths]. The project is the latest case of frontier models being pointed at hard mathematics; last week an unreleased Anthropic model was reported to have found a proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture, open since 2008 (our story [/news/unreleased-anthropic-model-found-a-proof-of-the-aaronson-ambainis-conjecture-open-since-2008/]).

Von Plato plans to keep going. Claude says the workbooks hold more than a dozen logic and foundations themes untouched in the 80-plus years since Gödel wrote them, he said, and Gödel's physics and cosmology notebooks, which no one has studied, are next once the logic work is done [1] [https://www.theguardian.com/science/2026/nov/13/kurt-godel-notebooks-ai-helps-unlock-hidden-discoveries-man-who-transformed-maths].
