Abstract. The Gödel papers contain several thousand pages of
notes on results in logic and foundations. The main part of these,
some 1700 pages, were written in 1940-42, but their contents have
been hidden behind the veil of an archaic, forgotten German
shorthand. After a description of the shorthand, its deciphering, and
the nature of the sources, three examples of results will be discussed.
Two of them are cleared by methods somewhat different from those
of Gödel. The third one is a claim shown to be false by a
counterexample, the correction of which leads to a surprising new
result on the relation between classical and intuitionistic logic.

Gerhard Gentzen’s Shorthand Notes on Logic and Foundations of Mathematics


Gerhard Gentzen is best known for his development of the proof systems of natural
deduction and sequent calculus, central in many areas of logic and computer science
today. Another noteworthy achievement is his resolution of the embarrassing situation
created by Gödel's incompleteness results, especially the second one about the
unprovability of consistency of elementary arithmetic. After these successes, Gentzen
dedicated the rest of his short life to the main problem of Hilbert's proof theory, the
question of the consistency of analysis. He was arrested in the summer of 1945 with other
professors of the German University of Prague and died soon afterward of starvation

in a prison cell. Attempts at locating his lost manuscripts failed at the time, but several
decades later, two slim folders of shorthand notes were found. In this volume, Jan von

archival and systematic studies, and essential for placing the translations of shorthand

the way they show the birth and development of Gentzen's central ideas and results,
sometimes in a well-developed form, and other times as flashes into the anatomy of
the workings of a unique mind.

Theorem 5. Gödel’s truth table theorem. If A is a tautology
and ±P1 & ⋅ ⋅ ⋅ ± Pn any conjunction of the atoms and negations
of atoms in A, then ⊢I ±P1 & ⋅ ⋅ ⋅ ± Pn ⊃ A.
The easiest way to prove this result is to show that if A is a
tautology in the atoms P1 , . . . , Pn , its classical derivation can be
converted into an intuitionistic derivation of
  (P1 ∨ ¬P1 ) & . . . & (Pn ∨ ¬Pn ) ⊃ A
The antecedent is equivalent to the disjunction of all the
primitive conjunctions (Gödel’s term) ±P1 & ⋅ ⋅ ⋅ ± Pn .

Theorem 7. Intuitionistic logic adds no formulas inside the
classical Boolean lattice. This holds for any number of atomic
formulas.
Proof idea. For P & Q, assume C to be a non-contradictory
formula such that ⊢I C ⊃ P & Q. By Theorem 6,
⊢I DNF (C) ⊃ P & Q. Prove by induction on the length of C that
⊢I C ≡ P & Q.
The proof generalizes easily to any number of atomic formulas.
