 Japanese firm helps discover vulnerabilities in Nginx software

By RYO SANADA/ Staff Writer

November 19, 2026 at 17:30 JST

 A post of the National Cybersecurity Office (NCO) on X calls on users to apply security patches as soon as possible. (Captured from X)

 [/ajw/articles/photo/87569514]

The developer of Nginx, a web server software used by about 60 percent of websites in Japan, warned of multiple vulnerabilities, including critical ones, and urged users to quickly apply security patches.

A Japanese cybersecurity company was among those credited with discovering the flaws.

The Japanese government’s National Cybersecurity Office (NCO) also posted on X at around 1 a.m. on Nov. 19 that the Nginx vulnerabilities could allow for the remote, unauthenticated execution of arbitrary code.

The office called on administrators of affected systems to immediately apply updates.

Nginx is used by about 30 percent of websites worldwide, according to W3Techs, which tracks the use of web technologies.

“No exploitation in the wild has been reported yet,” CERT-EU, a cybersecurity organization that supports institutions of the European Union, said Nov. 18.

JAPANESE COMPANY HELPS OUT

Ikotas Labs Inc., a Tokyo-based cybersecurity firm, said it worked with OpenAI to help uncover the vulnerabilities and report them to the software developer.

Satoki Tsuji, president of Ikotas Labs, said the company has regularly collaborated with OpenAI on vulnerability discovery and security research.

Ikotas Labs Inc.’s post on X announces the discovery of vulnerabilities. (Captured from X)

Tsuji said GPT-6 Astra, an AI model developed by OpenAI, was used in the discovery process.

Greg Brockman, co-founder and president of OpenAI, on Nov. 19 reposted Ikotas Labs’ post about the discovery.

“Further close collaboration to help secure the internet, from discovery to responsible disclosure,” Brockman wrote.

Regarding the vulnerabilities, Tsuji said, “Exploiting them would require advanced hacking skills.”

He warned that malicious hackers could potentially use AI tools to detect and exploit the same vulnerabilities at an early stage.

Tsuji urged affected organizations to take action promptly.
