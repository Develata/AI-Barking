Google's latest AI model is faster, cheaper and smarter than its flagship predecessor from just weeks ago

 [https://the-decoder.com/author/maximilian-schreiner/]

 Maximilian Schreiner [https://the-decoder.com/author/maximilian-schreiner/]

 Nov 19, 2026 

 Google Deepmind

Key Points

Google Deepmind released Gemini 4 Neon, which ranks third behind OpenAI and Anthropic in the Artificial Analysis benchmark.

At roughly 110 tokens per second, Neon runs almost twice as fast as Gemini 4 Carbon and costs less despite higher token usage, thanks to lower base prices.

On coding tasks, Neon beats Anthropic's competing model at a fraction of the cost, and it shows strong results in image analysis and safety tests.

Google DeepMind has released Gemini 4 Neon, its fourth model in seven weeks. Neon is nearly twice as fast as its sibling Carbon, beats it on most benchmarks and costs significantly less. Google continues to ship at a remarkable pace.

Google DeepMind has released Gemini 4 Neon [https://the-decoder.de/google-veroeffentlicht-gemini-4-neon-als-neues-frontier-modell-fuer-komplexe-aufgaben/], already its fourth AI model in seven weeks. Neon offers a one-million-token context window for text, images, audio and video, and Google positions it between Carbon [https://the-decoder.com/gemini-4-carbon-arrives-just-in-time-to-feel-like-yesterdays-news-in-the-fastest-model-cycle-yet/] and Hydrogen [https://the-decoder.com/googles-gemini-4-hydrogen-outscores-the-bigger-argon-just-four-weeks-after-its-launch/].

 [https://the-decoder.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/AAII-Gemini-4-Neon-scaled.png]
Google's Gemini 4 Neon ranks third on the Artificial Analysis Index.

At its highest reasoning level, "xhigh," Neon scores 61 points on the Intelligence Index [https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/gemini-4-neon], edging past Gemini 4 Carbon at 60 points and landing in third place behind OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra and Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2, both of which Google omits from its own comparison table.

The Cost Advantage Comes from List Price, Not Token Efficiency

Through Google AI Studio [https://aistudio.google.com/prompts/new_chat?model=gemini-4-neon] and Gemini for Business [https://cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise], one million input tokens costs three dollars (or $0.15 when cached) and one million output tokens costs fifteen dollars. That is 25 percent below Carbon's regular price of four and twenty dollars, but 50 percent above Carbon's promotional price, which remains in effect through the end of November.

Ad

According to Artificial Analysis, per-task costs for Neon at xhigh come in at $6.44, roughly 17 percent below Carbon's undiscounted figure of $7.76. Neon actually uses about 15 percent more output tokens per task than Carbon, so the savings come entirely from the lower per-token list price. At the second-highest level, "high," Neon matches Carbon's intelligence score while cutting per-task costs in half. Processing time for the benchmark also drops from about 36 minutes to roughly 21 minutes because Neon generates around 110 tokens per second, nearly double Carbon's rate of 57 tokens per second.

Half the Cost for Coding Agents and Strong Visual Analysis

The model is especially competitive on the Coding Agent Index [https://artificialanalysis.ai/agents/coding-agents#coding-agents-index]. Inside Google Antigravity, Neon at xhigh scores 59 points, beating Carbon (55 points) as well as Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Opus 5.5 (58 points each) at a fraction of the price. An average coding task costs $6.86 with Neon, compared to $12.61 for Opus and $15.47 for Fable. In the SWE-sweep test, the Antigravity environment triples Neon's hit rate to 39.0 percent, according to Google.

Neon slots in between Hydrogen and Carbon, though it beats Carbon on nearly every benchmark. | Image: Google

Artificial Analysis measured large gains in visual processing. On the document test GDP.pdf, Neon at "high" hits the top score of 33.4 percent, five points ahead of Carbon, though it oddly falls back to 28.0 percent at "xhigh." On the AA-Briefcase test, Neon takes the lead for best presentation quality with 1771 Elo points, ahead of Opus 5.5.

In Google's own evaluations, Neon also leads individual categories on the Vals Index [https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/vals_index], Zapier's AutomationBench [https://zapier.com/benchmarks] (56.3 percent) and science tests such as RiemannBench [https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06802] (84.0 percent). For security professionals, Google is launching a cyber variant through the Fairwind Program [https://deepmind.google/fairwind-program/] that delivers top scores on SEC-bench Pro (90.5 percent) and WebExploitBench v1.2 (51.26 percent) at half the price of competing models. According to Neon's (very brief) Model Card [https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Model-Cards/Gemini-4-Neon-Model-Card.pdf], the model blocks 100 percent of indirect prompt-injection attacks on the Gray-Swan benchmark.
