Gemini 4 Neon takes on Google’s flagship model

 Google’s new AI model Gemini 4 Neon matches flagship Gemini 4 Carbon on benchmarks, but runs faster and will soon cost less.

 [/news/Gemini-4-Neon-macht-Googles-Spitzenmodell-Konkurrenz-11530483.html]

 4 min. read

 By

Tomislav Bezmalinović [/autor/Tomislav-Bezmalinovic-4880854]

 Advertisement

Google DeepMind has introduced Gemini 4 Neon, the fourth model in the Gemini 4 family in seven weeks. Although designed for efficiency, Neon can keep up with Google’s flagship model, Carbon, and even narrowly surpasses it on some benchmarks. Starting in December, Neon will also cost less per token than Carbon, once Carbon’s introductory prices expire.

 Continue after ad

Google positions Neon for complex programming tasks, analytical reasoning and productive enterprise use. Carbon [/news/Gemini-Argon-war-gestern-jetzt-kommt-Carbon-11503849.html?from-en=1] remains intended for especially demanding, long-term projects, while Hydrogen [/news/Gemini-4-Hydrogen-Googles-effiziente-Alternative-zu-Argon-und-Carbon-11523818.html?from-en=1] is for everyday routine tasks, Google says [https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/]. Neon is meant to fill the gap between them. In Google’s AI coding environment Antigravity, it can plan tasks and assign Hydrogen agents code changes and tests.

According to the model card [https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Model-Cards/Gemini-4-Neon-Model-Card.pdf], Neon was derived from Carbon. It processes text, images, audio and video and generates text. It offers a context window of 1 million tokens and four reasoning levels. Its training data goes through March 2026.

Argon [/news/Gemini-4-Argon-Google-oeffnet-Spitzenmodell-fuer-zahlende-Nutzer-11487463.html?from-en=1], previously positioned for programming and knowledge work, is absent from Google’s new three-tier lineup. In the Gemini app, Neon replaces the model. Argon remains available through the API and in Antigravity.

Neon matches Carbon’s performance

In Artificial Analysis’ benchmarks [https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/gemini-4-neon-intelligence-gain] (AA), Neon scores 61 points on the Intelligence Index with its highest reasoning level, Xhigh, placing it third behind GPT-6.2 Astra [/news/GPT-6-2-Astra-OpenAI-warnt-vor-neuen-Risiken-durch-KI-Selbstverbesserung-11528955.html?from-en=1] and Claude Fable 5.2 [/news/Anthropic-Claude-Fable-5-2-ist-guenstiger-aber-Tempo-kostet-extra-11518102.html?from-en=1]. That puts it just ahead of Google’s own flagship model, Carbon, which scores 60 points. However, Carbon currently supports only the High reasoning level, at which the two models are tied.

 [/imgs/18/5/2/0/4/3/4/6/AA-Intelligence-Index-Gemini-4-Neon-fe416abcfa73f467.jpg]

Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index combines results from nine benchmarks into a weighted overall score.

 (Image: Artificial Analysis)

According to AA, Neon shows slight improvements over Carbon in agentic knowledge work, image understanding and coding. In the Coding Agent Index, Neon scores 59 points with Xhigh, compared with 55 for Carbon with High. That puts it on par with Claude’s flagship models, Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2, which each score 58 points. However, the Claude models were tested with Anthropic’s coding agent, Claude Code, while Neon was tested with Google’s Antigravity CLI. In the Vals Index [https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/vals_index], Neon ranks second behind Claude Fable 5.2 and just ahead of Carbon. The index measures AI models’ performance on practical tasks in finance, programming, law and taxes.

Price advantage starts in December

Through the Gemini API, Neon costs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. According to the pricing page [https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing], Carbon costs $2 and $10, respectively, through Nov. 30 under its introductory pricing. On Dec. 1, the prices rise to $4 and $20, respectively. Neon is therefore currently 50% more expensive per token, but will be 25% cheaper than Carbon after that.

 Continue after ad

AA found that Neon, at its maximum reasoning level, costs $6.44 and takes about 21 minutes per Intelligence Index task, compared with $7.76 and 36 minutes for Carbon. Neon therefore takes about 40% less time and costs about 17% less. At the same High reasoning level, Neon even achieves Carbon’s index score for less than half the cost. Both cost comparisons use Carbon’s regular prices, starting Dec. 1.

 [/imgs/18/5/2/0/4/3/4/6/AA-Cost-Per-II-Task-Gemini-4-Neon-b54f58edb75ffdd0.jpg]

Artificial Analysis' cost comparison shows the average cost of completing Intelligence Index tasks.

 (Image: Artificial Analysis)

Neon is available through the Gemini API, Google AI Studio and Gemini for Business. There is no free API access. Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers also get access through the Antigravity AI development environment and the Gemini app. In the latter, Carbon remains exclusive to the more expensive Ultra plan.
