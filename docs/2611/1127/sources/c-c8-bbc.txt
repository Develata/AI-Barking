OpenAI counter-sues Apple over alleged theft of trade secrets

4 hours ago
ShareSave

Add as preferred on Google [https://www.google.com/preferences/source?q=bbc.com]

Amy Walker

Reuters

ChatGPT-maker OpenAI has counter-sued Apple, alleging that the iPhone maker stole trade secrets through a former employee.

In a federal lawsuit filed in July, Apple sued the artificial intelligence (AI) company and two of its employees who previously worked for Apple, claiming it had engaged in a "pattern of theft" of proprietary information. 

A countersuit, filed in California on Friday, alleged that Apple gained confidential information through the hiring of a former OpenAI employee and accuses it of discouraging hardware suppliers from working on future OpenAI devices.

Apple denied the counterclaims, describing them as "a transparent attempt to distract from the substance" of its own case.

OpenAI is developing AI-enabled personal devices, which it claims in the countersuit could threaten to "outcompete Apple on what Apple views as its own turf".

The filing alleged Apple had gained confidential information by rehiring a former employee who had worked at OpenAI for six months. 

OpenAI accused the employee, who it said gained access to information on a new device and the company's roadmap for future physical products, of sending "dozens of OpenAI documents" to his personal phone numbers. It also said he presented OpenAI's plans to senior Apple staff "at Apple's request".

OpenAI said: "Given the timing, it now appears that stealing those documents may have been his reason for joining OpenAI in the first place."

The counterclaim also alleged Apple encourages employees to mix work files with their personal iCloud (Apple's cloud storage service) accounts so it could later accuse those who leave to work for competitors of theft.

A spokesperson for Apple said: "This case is about OpenAI employees deliberately stealing, accessing, and using Apple's secret and confidential information regarding our unreleased technologies, processes, and products - not about inadvertent retention of Apple information."

In its own lawsuit, the tech giant alleged that at least two long-time Apple workers who left the company to join OpenAI took part in a "pattern of theft" by emailing themselves internal Apple information.

Apple also accused the AI firm of undertaking a "strategy to extract Apple's confidential information". 

A spokesperson for OpenAI previously denied the claims, telling the BBC: "We have no interest in other companies' trade secrets."

Along with OpenAI, and two former Apple employees, Apple is also suing io Products, the design startup founded by Sir Jony Ive, a long-time Apple executive who designed the iMac, iPod, iPhone and iPad. 

OpenAI acquired the company last year [https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c5y66yemjdmo] in a $6.5bn (£4.7bn) deal.
