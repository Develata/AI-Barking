Landlord’s AI Makes Rent Deal With Woman, Actual Landlord Breaks Deal and Evicts Her

"It's ridiculous."

It turns out that double-dealing is fine if an AI chatbot is doing it on your behalf.

A North Carolina woman says her landlord evicted her despite receiving an email from the leasing office saying she’d have an extension to pay her $4,000 in overdue rent. That official communication, it turned out, was written by the landlord’s AI assistant, who claims that the AI did so without their knowledge, according to WRAL News [https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/raleigh-eviction-after-ai-bots-promise-push-for-protection-nov-2026/].

Now the tenant, Laura Mack, has gone to court to try reverse the eviction. She says she feels deceived because there was no obvious indication at the time that she was communicating with an AI. The sympathetic emails sounded, to her, human. Unfortunately, it was the humans here who would ultimately prove heartless. 

“I’m baffled,” Mack said. “I need a place to stay.”

The situation raises a hairy question in an era when many industries are rushing to automate communications [https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/customers-fed-up-ai-service-agents]: if the landlord is choosing to let AI represent them, can they go back on [https://futurism.com/the-byte/airline-chatbot-bereavement-funeral] their, or their AI’s, word? 

The emails from Colony Townhome Apartments, viewed by WRAL, showed the AI asking what date would work best for her to pay before approving her request: “Paying on November 30, the last day of the month, works,” it said.

Then, days later, she received an abrupt eviction notice. When Mack confronted the leasing office, the leasing agent allegedly said that it was the AI agent, Olivia, that had been emailing her and mistakenly granted her an extension. 

According to WRAL, the complex informed residents back in December 2025 that the new AI agent could handle their rent and maintenance questions. Normally, Olivia’s messages are supposed to be marked as coming from an AI, but the ones that Mack received showed no disclosure. In fact, the AI sounded awfully compassionate and knowledgeable about her situation.

“I also know you’re dealing with the hole in the downstairs bathroom ceiling,” the AI wrote at one point, per WRAL. “I’m sorry you’re dealing with that.”

The emails were signed, “Best regards, Colony Townhome Apartments.” 

“I thought it was someone from the office because all my information was in there and it mentioned my bathroom,” Mack told the news station.

The incident has infuriated local lawmakers.

“It’s ridiculous,” state senator Jim Burgin said in an interview [https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/raleigh-eviction-after-ai-bots-promise-push-for-protection-nov-2026/] with WRAL Friday. “I think notification is one thing, and I think liability is another.” 

“Before someone evicted that lady, they should have reviewed their documents,” he added.

Burgin has proposed a “Bot Bill” that would require users be informed whenever they’re communicating with an AI, but in the past three years that he’s tried introducing it, it hasn’t advanced in the legislature, per WRAL.

Mack is now living out of a hotel until she finds a permanent place to stay — or wins her battle in court.

“If you told me till the 30th, I’ll get out by the 30th,” Mack said. “But give me that. You at least honor that. You have to have somewhere where you’re taking responsibility.”
