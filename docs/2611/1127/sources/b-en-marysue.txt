North Carolina woman says she was allowed to delay payment for overdue rent, until an eviction notice revealed who she’d been speaking with

Laura Mack, a woman living in Raleigh, North Carolina, said she believed her apartment complex had given her until the end of November to pay her overdue rent after an email exchange with the leasing office, only to discover she had been receiving AI-generated messages when she was handed an eviction notice. The apartment complex is now defending itself after being subjected to backlash.

“We recognize how difficult it is to fall behind on rent and face eviction, and we take Ms. Mack’s situation seriously,” Colony Townhome Apartments’ spokesperson Alfred Leach told WRAL [https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/apartment-response-ai-bot-raleigh-eviction-november-2026/] on Tuesday, Nov. 24. However, the spokesperson argued that Mack had already been notified of her pending eviction, and that “a payment arrangement would not change the court-ordered lockout.”

Mack told the outlet [https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/eviction-raleigh-apartment-ai-leasing-agent-november-2026/] she had received an email signed by Colony Townhome Apartments on Nov. 8, asking what date she wanted to pay her $3,934.17 rent. When Mack said she wanted to pay at the end of the month, she claimed to have received an email from the leasing office that said, “Paying on November 30, the last day of the month, works.”

Mack said she did not know she was talking to an AI

Mack said she felt relieved when she was allowed the extension, and there was no indication that she was speaking to an AI assistant [https://www.themarysue.com/woman-believed-she-was-having-a-casual-conversation-with-her-friend-on-tiktok-then-finds-out-she-was-actually-speaking-to-an-ai-the-entire-time/]. “I thought it was someone from the office because all my information was in there and it mentioned my bathroom,” Mack said, adding that she previously contacted the leasing agents about a leak in her bathroom.

Between Nov. 8 and 16, Mack said she did not receive any other messages from the leasing office until she received the eviction notice. When Mack argued that she had until the end of November to pay her rent, a leasing agent acknowledged that she had communicated with an AI assistant named “Olivia” twice on Nov. 8, but that the permission to extend the rent deadline [https://www.themarysue.com/man-alleges-chatgpt-assured-him-he-could-withhold-rent-then-he-was-evicted-and-slept-behind-a-car-with-two-dogs-you-will-not-end-up-on-the-street/] was a mistake, according to Mack.

“I’m baffled. I need a place to stay,” Mack said. “I’m sad. Like, I didn’t even have time… I’m still ordering things to pack.”

Olivia was reportedly first introduced in December 2025, with Colony Townhome Apartments notifying its tenants of the AI assistant that could help with their rent and maintenance inquiries at all hours of the day. Mack said tenants were initially told when they were communicating with Olivia, but the emails about her rent extension did not identify the messages as AI-generated.

“If you told me till the 30th, I’ll get out by the 30th,” Mack said. “But give me that. You at least honor that. You have to have somewhere where you’re taking responsibility.”

North Carolina court gave the apartment permission to evict Mack

Leach said a Wake County court granted the apartment complex possession of Mack’s unit on Oct. 23, weeks before she received the eviction notice, and that the outstanding rent balance was “owed regardless of possession.” The outlet confirmed the court documents and found that the Wake County Sheriff’s Office had also issued Mack a lockout notice. Mack’s motion to appeal the eviction was reportedly denied as well, per court records.

Mack acknowledged that she had previously paid rent late and faced similar eviction proceedings. She said she believed the leasing office had withdrawn those cases after she made the payments.

“They’ve overridden them when I paid and let me stay in the apartment,” Mack said. “They’re still refusing to take responsibility for what their AI bot told me.”

Leach, on the other hand, maintained the apartment complex’s stance on the issue. However, he reportedly did not address the concern regarding the AI assistant.

“Our leasing office told Ms. Mack directly on Nov. 18 that a payment arrangement would not change the court-ordered lockout,” Leach said. “We spoke with Ms. Mack on Nov. 23 and remain in contact with her. We wish her well.”
