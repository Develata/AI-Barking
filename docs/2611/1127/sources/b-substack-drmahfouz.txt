A North Carolina woman lost her home after an email appearing to come from her apartment complex approved a later deadline for paying nearly $4,000 in overdue rent. She says the message was sent by an AI assistant without her knowledge.

Laura Mack, a tenant at Colony Townhome Apartments in Raleigh, was evicted on 19 November despite receiving an email indicating she could settle her outstanding $3,934.17 balance by 30 November.

The case raises questions about whether businesses should be held accountable when automated systems provide financial assurances that customers believe come directly from company representatives.

Mack says a leasing-office employee subsequently blamed the conflicting messages on Olivia, the apartment complex’s AI assistant. The property has not publicly confirmed that explanation.

AI Assistant Allegedly Approved Rent Extension

The confusion began on 8 November when Mack received an email signed ‘Colony Townhome Apartments’ asking when she could settle her outstanding balance.

She replied that she intended to pay at the end of the month. The response appeared to approve the arrangement.

‘Paying on November 30, the last day of the month, works,’ the email stated.

However, Mack received an eviction notice on 16 November, eight days after the exchange. She was evicted three days later, 11 days before the payment date mentioned in the message.

The emails, first examined by local broadcaster WRAL, reportedly contained no indication that an AI assistant was responsible for the correspondence.

Mack said she believed she was communicating with the leasing office because the messages included personal information and referred to a maintenance problem involving her bathroom ceiling.

Residents had previously been informed that Olivia was available around the clock to answer questions about rent and maintenance.

Mack acknowledged responsibility for her overdue rent but argued that the apartment complex should also answer for the apparent approval of a later payment date.

Tenant Left Staying in Hotel After Eviction

Mack attempted to challenge the eviction notice at the Wake County courthouse on 19 November, but the effort did not prevent her removal from the property.

By 21 November, she was staying in a hotel while considering her next steps.

Colony spokesperson Alfred Leach said on 20 November that the property owners were gathering information and expected to provide an update the following week.

The company has not publicly clarified whether Olivia was authorised to approve payment extensions or whether employees reviewed the exchange before proceeding with the eviction.

It also remains unclear whether the emails created an enforceable agreement or whether eviction proceedings had already begun when Mack received the apparent approval.

North Carolina Lawmaker Calls for AI Disclosure

North Carolina state Senator Jim Burgin has questioned the lack of transparency surrounding Mack’s communications with the apartment complex.

Speaking to WRAL, Burgin said the emails should have disclosed that Mack was interacting with an AI assistant.

‘I think notification is one thing, and I think liability is another,’ he said.

Burgin previously introduced a proposal known as the ‘Bot Bill’, intended to require disclosure when people interact with AI rather than a human representative.

The measure has not advanced through the state legislature, although Burgin intends to pursue it again during the next legislative session.

The dispute comes amid wider questions about companies’ responsibility for automated advice. In a separate 2024 case, British Columbia’s Civil Resolution Tribunal found Air Canada liable after its website chatbot gave a customer incorrect information about bereavement fares.

The Canadian ruling does not determine the legal position in North Carolina, but it illustrates why businesses face scrutiny when customers rely on information provided through their automated systems.
