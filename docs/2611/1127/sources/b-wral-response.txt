 By Tolly Taylor, WRAL Investigative Reporter

A Raleigh apartment complex is defending itself after coming under scrutiny for evicting a tenant who says AI-generated messages misled her into thinking she could pay her overdue rent at the end of November.

Colony Townhome Apartments told WRAL Investigates on Tuesday that the tenant — Laura Mack — had already been notified of a pending eviction and that “a payment arrangement would not change the court-ordered lockout.” 

Other WRAL Top Stories

"We recognize how difficult it is to fall behind on rent and face eviction and we take Ms. Mack's situation seriously,” Alfred Leach, a spokesman for the apartment complex near the corner of Millbrook and Six Forks roads. 

WRAL first reported [https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/eviction-raleigh-apartment-ai-leasing-agent-november-2026/] on Mack’s eviction last week. Her story has garnered national attention [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XXc_A-EuElI] and a renewed push [https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/raleigh-eviction-after-ai-bots-promise-push-for-protection-nov-2026/] by state lawmakers for AI-focused consumer protections. 

Mack received an email message from Colony Townhome Apartments' leasing office on Nov. 8, saying she could have until the end of the month to pay her overdue rent balance of almost $4,000. But on Nov. 16, she received an eviction notice. Mack says she was told by a leasing agent that the company's AI agent — called Olivia — had mistakenly given her permission via email to make her rent payment on Nov. 30. 

In his statement Tuesday, Leach said Wake County court sided with the apartment complex on Oct. 23, which resulted in the eviction notice Mack received weeks later. Leach said the Nov. 8 email concerned her outstanding rent balance, “which is owed regardless of possession.” He noted that the Nov. 8 email to Mack didn’t address the court's order but didn’t change what the court had ordered. 

WRAL reviewed court documents showing that a Wake County court issued an Oct. 23 order allowing the landlord to evict Mack. Mack also received a lockout notice from the Wake County Sheriff's Office. A motion to appeal the eviction was denied on Nov. 19, court records show. 

“Our leasing office told Ms. Mack directly on Nov. 18 that a payment arrangement would not change the court-ordered lockout,” Leach said. “We spoke with Ms. Mack on Nov. 23 and remain in contact with her. We wish her well."

Mack was locked out on Thursday. In an interview last week, she took accountability for being late with the rent. This week she acknowledged that there have been similar eviction proceedings against her in the past but that the landlord agreed to withdraw them after she made good on payments. 

“They've overridden them when I paid and let me stay in the apartment,” she told WRAL Investigates on Tuesday. “They're still refusing to take responsibility for what their AI bot told me."

WRAL Investigates reviewed documents provided by Mack. Some of the purported emails — including the one that gave her a payment extension — didn’t disclose that they were generated by artificial intelligence. One of the email messages included sympathetic language about issues with the apartment, leaving Mack with the impression that she was dealing with a human. 

WRAL Investigates asked Colony Townhome Apartments to clarify its policy on tenants receiving emails from Olivia — and how it would be handled in the future. Leach's statement contained no mention of artificial intelligence or Olivia, the AI agent.
