A North Carolina state lawmaker says a Raleigh woman's fight against her eviction hinges on two key points.

 By Tolly Taylor, WRAL investigative reporter

A Raleigh woman's fight against her eviction — one that she says was caused in part by a sympathetic artificial intelligence bot — is prompting a state lawmaker to revive a proposal to better protect consumers from AI. 

North Carolina state Sen. Jim Burgin said in an interview Friday that the eviction underscores what can go wrong when companies use AI bots for business decisions. Burgin, R-Harnett, says he plans to continue a push for AI-focused consumer protections in the next legislative session. 

Other WRAL Top Stories

WRAL Investigates first reported on the eviction Thursday [https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/eviction-raleigh-apartment-ai-leasing-agent-november-2026/&ved=2ahUKEwjT18DG45eYAxWBFmIAHeKlC6sQvOMEKAB6BAgiEAE&usg=AOvVaw0XnrGPTnCpwxhZWiik8Ps7]. 

Laura Mack says she received an email from Colony Townhome Apartments' leasing office on Nov. 8, saying she could have until the end of the month to pay her overdue rent balance of almost $4,000. But on Monday, she received an eviction notice. Her landlord blamed the confusion on an AI-powered property management service, she says. Mack says she was told that the company's AI agent — called Olivia — had mistakenly given her permission via email to make her rent payment on Nov. 30. WRAL Investigates reviewed the documents, which were provided by Mack. The purported emails didn’t disclose that they were generated by artificial intelligence. One of the email messages included sympathetic language about issues with the apartment, leaving Mack with the impression that she was dealing with a human.

“I also know you're dealing with the hole in the downstairs bathroom ceiling,” one section of an email said. “I'm sorry you're dealing with that.”

Mack was evicted Thursday. She is staying at a hotel through the weekend while she figures out her next steps. 

"It's ridiculous," Burgin said. "I think notification is one thing, and I think liability is another." 

He added: "I think they've got a real big problem."

A spokesperson for the apartment complex told WRAL on Friday that its owners are looking into the eviction. "Colony Townhome Apartments is gathering all information related to this matter and will be providing an update to WRAL next week," the spokesman, Alfred Leach, said. Burgin said the Nov. 8 email to Mack from the leasing office should have notified Mack that it was written by Olivia. And he said the landlord will have to defend the fact that the bot told her in writing that she could have the extension. 

"AI is like Pinocchio," Burgin said. "It has no conscience, it has no morals, it has no ethics. And we humans have to make sure that there's a human component."

"Before someone evicted that lady, they should have reviewed their documents," he added. 

Burgin says companies need to be better about disclosing their use of artificial intelligence. Burgin’s “Bot Bill,” would require a user to be alerted if they are speaking with or interacting with AI. He has introduced legislation in each of the past three years, but it hasn’t advanced in the legislature. He plans to try again in the next legislative session.

Colony Townhome Apartments disclosed to residents that it was using an AI agent in a December 2025 email, with section headings like “Who is Olivia?” and “Why we’re excited.”

In the following months, emails from Olivia to residents were clearly marked.

But the emails to Mack on Nov. 8 were signed, "Best regards, Colony Townhome Apartments." 

"I thought, honestly, that it was someone from the office," Mack said in an interview Thursday. "I had plans to have until the 30th to either get it paid or go, because that's honestly that's what my email said."

Mack knows her overdue rent payment is her problem, not the leasing office's. 

"It's my fault," Mack said. "I'm taking full responsibility for that. But someone in that office has to take responsibility” for the mixed messages.
