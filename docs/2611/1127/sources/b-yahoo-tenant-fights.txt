North Carolina tenant fights eviction after landlord blames AI for extension on $4,000 rent debt

Morgan Taylor

Fri, November 27, 2026 at 10:59 PM UTC

3 min read

Add Yahoo as a preferred source to see more of our stories on Google.

 [https://www.google.com/preferences/source?q=yahoo.com]

Photo Credit: iStock

An apartment complex's artificial intelligence assistant approved extra time for a North Carolina tenant to pay $4,000 in overdue rent.

The leasing office's refusal to honor that agreement has led the tenant to fight her eviction.

Here's what to know

Emails from Colony Townhome Apartments appeared to set Nov. 30 as tenant Laura Mack's deadline for paying her overdue balance, according to Futurism [https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/landlord-ai-deal-evicts-woman], citing reporting by WRAL News. The messages asked when she could pay and accepted the date she proposed.

"Paying on November 30, the last day of the month, works," the assistant said in emails viewed by WRAL.

Mack challenged an eviction notice she received just days after the emails. A leasing agent allegedly attributed the mistaken approval to Olivia, the complex's AI assistant.

According to WRAL, Mack's emails lacked the disclosure that normally identified Olivia's messages as AI-generated. They were signed "Best regards, Colony Townhome Apartments," and Mack said she believed she had been corresponding with an employee.

More background

Along with discussing overdue rent, the assistant acknowledged a maintenance problem in Mack's home.

Advertisement

Advertisement

"I also know you're dealing with the hole in the downstairs bathroom ceiling," it said. "I'm sorry you're dealing with that."

"I thought it was someone from the office because all my information was in there and it mentioned my bathroom," Mack told WRAL.

In December 2025, the complex told residents they could ask Olivia about rent and maintenance.

What's being done?

Mack has asked a court to overturn the eviction and is staying in a hotel while seeking permanent housing or a favorable court outcome, according to Futurism.

North Carolina state Sen. Jim Burgin has proposed a "Bot Bill" that would require people to be told when they are communicating with AI. His efforts to introduce the measure over three years have not advanced in the legislature, according to WRAL.

Advertisement

Burgin also questioned the apartment complex's handling of Mack's eviction.

"It's ridiculous," he told WRAL. "I think notification is one thing, and I think liability is another."

He added, "Before someone evicted that lady, they should have reviewed their documents."

"If you told me till the 30th, I'll get out by the 30th," Mack told WRAL. "But give me that. You at least honor that. You have to have somewhere where you're taking responsibility."

Where can I learn more?

Mack's fight over an AI-approved payment deadline raises questions about who takes responsibility when landlords and other institutions rely on these tools.
