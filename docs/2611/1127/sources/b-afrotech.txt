NC Woman Fights To Overturn Eviction After AI Chatbot 'Mistakenly' Grants Extension On Late Rent

A Raleigh, NC, woman was evicted despite receiving an email from her apartment leasing office saying she had been granted an extension to pay her nearly $4,000 in overdue rent.

According to [https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/raleigh-eviction-after-ai-bots-promise-push-for-protection-nov-2026/] WRAL News, the extension Laura Mack believed came from her landlord on Nov. 8, 2026, was actually generated by the apartment complex’s AI chatbot, [https://afrotech.com/ai-chatbot-advice-racial-bias] identified as Olivia. Mack says she was baffled to receive an eviction notice on Nov. 12, 2026.

She notes that emails from Colony Townhome Apartments show that during the exchange on Nov. 8, she was asked what date would work best for her payment before her request was seemingly approved by the sender.

“Paying on November 30, the last day of the month, works…,” Colony Townhome Apartments responded, per a copy of Mack’s email shared with WRAL.

The emails reportedly included the apartment complex’s official signature and no mention of Olivia.

How An AI Chatbot Conversation Led To An Eviction

However, Mack says her landlord notified her that the chatbot Olivia had granted the extension without the company’s knowledge. Mack is now living in a hotel while searching for permanent housing and fighting the eviction in court.

Though she takes full responsibility for owing the back rent, she says some blame remains with the landlord. 

“If you told me till the 30th, I’ll get out by the 30th,” Mack said, expressing her frustration with the situation to WRAL in an earlier report. [https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/eviction-raleigh-apartment-ai-leasing-agent-november-2026/] “But give me that. You at least honor that. You have to have somewhere where you’re taking responsibility.”

Colony Townhome had shared in December 2025 that Olivia could assist with residents’ rent and maintenance questions. While the chatbot’s messages should’ve included an AI disclosure, the messages Mack received contained no indication, WRAL reports.

Mack says she feels deceived, noting there was no obvious indication that she was communicating with AI [https://afrotech.com/three-major-school-districts-are-considering-ditching-google-over-ai] at the time. The chatbot’s sympathetic responses appeared human to her, even acknowledging issues she had experienced with the apartment and offering apologies.

Who Is Responsible When AI Gets It Wrong?

Alfred Leach, a spokesperson for Colony Townhome, told WRAL on Friday, Nov. 20, that property owners were “gathering all information related to this matter” and would provide an update the following week.

The situation has drawn criticism from local lawmakers, including North Carolina State Sen. Jim Burgin. Burgin has proposed a “Bot Bill” that would require people to be notified when they are interacting with AI.

The senator has introduced the legislation in each of the past three years, but it has yet to advance in the legislature, per WRAL. He says he plans to continue pushing for the measure in the next legislative session.

“[AI] has no conscience, it has no morals, it has no ethics. And we humans have to make sure that there’s a human component,” Burgin said, per WRAL. “Before someone evicted that lady, they should have reviewed their documents.”

As AI becomes increasingly integrated into everyday business operations, from pitching stories to newsrooms [https://afrotech.com/ai-agents-hustling-pitching-literary-magazines-money] to handling financial inquiries, [https://afrotech.com/banks-block-ai-agents-customer-accounts-financial-risks] Mack’s situation raises a broader question about accountability. When a company allows AI to communicate on its behalf, should it be held responsible for the commitments its AI assistant makes?
