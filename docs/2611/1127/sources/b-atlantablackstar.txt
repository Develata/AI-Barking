North Carolina Woman Gets Kicked Out After Trusting Landlord’s Email About Rent Extension, Only to Discover She’s Been Dealing with an AI Chatbot

A North Carolina woman says her apartment complex evicted her after its AI chatbot appeared to approve a payment extension, raising questions about accountability.

What to Know

Laura Mack says her apartment complex's AI assistant agreed to let her pay nearly $4,000 in overdue rent by Nov. 30.

Mack says she believed she had a deal with the leasing office until an eviction notice revealed she had been communicating with a chatbot.

A North Carolina lawmaker wants businesses to disclose AI interactions as questions grow over who is responsible for automated promises.

A North Carolina woman says her apartment complex evicted her after its artificial intelligence assistant allegedly assured her she could wait until the end of the month to pay nearly $4,000 in overdue rent.

Laura Mack, a resident of Colony Townhome Apartments in Raleigh, believed she had reached an agreement with the leasing office to pay her outstanding balance by Nov. 30. Emails appeared to confirm the arrangement. However, Mack later learned she had been communicating with the property’s AI assistant rather than a human employee.

After receiving an eviction notice, Mack went to the Wake County courthouse in an attempt to challenge the action after relying on messages she believed represented an agreement with her landlord.

“I’m baffled,” Mack told WRAL [https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/eviction-raleigh-apartment-ai-leasing-agent-november-2026/]. “I need a place to stay.”

The exchange began on Nov. 8, when Mack received an email asking when she could pay the $3,934.17 she owed. She responded that she wanted to settle the outstanding balance on the last day of the month.

“I responded, ‘I’d like to pay on the last day of the month,’” Mack recalled. She told the outlet that she understood she had until Nov. 30 to pay what she owed or find somewhere else to live.

The response appeared to confirm her understanding. “Paying on November 30, the last day of the month, works,” the email stated.

Documents Mack provided to WRAL showed that some of the emails, including one granting her an extension to make a payment, did not disclose that artificial intelligence had generated them. 

One message even expressed sympathy for her apartment-related problems, leading Mack to believe she was communicating with a real person.

That understanding changed when Mack received an eviction notice on Monday, Nov. 16. She contacted the leasing office to question what happened and said a leasing agent informed her that Olivia, the property’s artificial intelligence assistant, had sent the emails on Nov. 8.

Mack acknowledged that she was behind on rent but said the apartment complex should take responsibility for the payment deadline that its AI assistant appeared to approve. She also admitted that there have been similar eviction proceedings against her in the past but that landlords agreed to withdraw them after she made good payments. 

“They’ve overridden them when I paid and let me stay in the apartment,” she said. “They’re still refusing to take responsibility for what their AI bot told me.”

Mack attempted [https://www.wral.com/video/ai-eviction-state-senator-raleigh-woman-november-2026/] to contest the eviction at the Wake County courthouse on Nov. 19, but her efforts did not stop her from losing the apartment.

She is currently staying in a hotel as she weighs her next steps.

Colony Townhome Apartments introduced Olivia to residents in December 2025, promoting the AI-powered assistant as a round-the-clock resource for handling rental and maintenance inquiries, including requests made outside regular office hours.

A spokesperson for the apartment complex, Alfred Leach, is defending the apartment complex’s actions, saying the complex had notified Mack of a pending eviction and that “a payment arrangement would not change the court-ordered lockout.”

“We recognize how difficult it is to fall behind on rent and face eviction, and we take Ms. Mack’s situation seriously,” Alfred Leach told WRAL [https://www.wral.com/news/investigates/apartment-response-ai-bot-raleigh-eviction-november-2026/]. 

Leach said Wake County court sided with the complex on Oct. 23, which is why Mack received her eviction notice weeks later. 

He added that the Nov. 8 email addressed Mack’s outstanding rent balance, which he said remained due regardless of possession. Although the message did not address the court’s order, he said it did not alter the terms of the ruling.

Leach’s statement did not mention what the apartment complex’s policy is on tenants receiving emails from Olivia or how it would be handled in the future. 

The case raises broader questions about accountability when property managers rely on artificial intelligence to communicate with tenants. It also highlights the potential consequences when automated messages concern overdue rent, payment deadlines, and other matters that could determine whether someone keeps their home.

North Carolina state Sen. Jim Burgin questioned the lack of disclosure surrounding Mack’s exchanges with the apartment complex’s AI assistant. Speaking to WRAL, Burgin said apartment complexes should inform tenants when they are communicating with an automated system rather than a human representative.

“I think notification is one thing, and I think liability is another,” Burgin said.

Burgin previously introduced legislation known as the “Bot Bill,” which would require businesses to disclose when customers are interacting with artificial intelligence. The proposal has not moved forward in the state legislature, but Burgin plans to revisit the measure during the next legislative session.

Mack said she expected the apartment complex to honor the date its messages gave her, regardless of who or what generated them.

“If you told me till the 30th, I’ll get out by the 30th,” she said. “But give me that. You at least honor that. You have to have somewhere where you’re taking responsibility.”
