OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Squeezes More Work Out of Each Dollar

OpenAI says GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol finish professional, legal and scientific work for a fraction of the old cost per task. Independent testers confirm part of that, and OpenAI's own system card flags a regression it calls concerning.

 [https://www.implicator.ai/author/marcus-schuler/]

 Marcus Schuler [https://www.implicator.ai/author/marcus-schuler/]
 November 17, 2026, 11:58 AM PST · 10 min read

Ethan Mollick, the Wharton professor who tests most models before release, asked GPT-6.2 Astra to build a space elevator ride from scratch in Blender and Unity. In his account of the test [https://x.com/emollick/status/2122755693191287009?ref=implicator.ai], he said it “could deliver well”.

Mollick found GPT-6.2 Astra “harder to steer & work with than other models.”

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol on November 17, 2026. With GPT-6.2 Sol its cheaper everyday model and Astra its frontier model, OpenAI pitches “frontier performance at costs that make sense at scale”.

What Changed

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol on November 17, 2026. Astra's API prices fell 20% from GPT-6 Astra to $8 and $40 per million input and output tokens, the same list price as Claude Fable 5.2.

Artificial Analysis ranks GPT-6.2 Astra second on its Intelligence Index, behind Claude Fable 5.2, and finds GPT-6.2 Sol on par with GPT-6 Astra at about 15% of its cost per task.

On legal work, Artificial Analysis gives Astra for Law a 20.8% all-pass rate and regular GPT-6.2 Astra 15.0%, so even the leader fails most assignments.

OpenAI's system card shows GPT-6.2 Astra omitting a known obstacle in 22.28% of Blocker Deception trials, against 11.09% for GPT-6 Astra. OpenAI says its push for concise answers may be related.

Cheaper per job

Cost per task is the price of the tokens a model consumes multiplied by how many it needs to finish a job.

At launch, GPT-6.2 Astra’s API rates [https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/?ref=implicator.ai] are $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, both 20% below GPT-6 Astra. Cached input drops to $0.40 per million tokens from $1.

GPT-6.2 Astra now matches Claude Fable 5.2’s list price, so any per-task gap comes from how many tokens each model spends.

OpenAI’s Agents’ Last Exam comparison puts GPT-6.2 Astra 5.2 points above GPT-6 Astra at under a third of the cost. Against Claude Fable 5.2, it claims a 5.5-point advantage at 5% of the cost. The test covers multistep professional workflows; nine Claude Fable 5.2 tasks used a fallback model and are left out of its leaderboard result.

Most headline comparisons come from OpenAI’s chosen benchmarks and configurations, including the internal GDPval v2 and ExploitBench port. Gemini 4 Carbon, Google’s model, is absent from most comparisons, and independent results so far cover about a day.

The independent Artificial Analysis results [https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2122750634210529569?ref=implicator.ai] put GPT-6.2 Astra second on its Intelligence Index, behind Claude Fable 5.2 and five points above GPT-6 Astra. GPT-6.2 Sol scores 57, matching GPT-6 Astra at about 15% of its cost per task. Its launch API rates stay at GPT-6.1 Sol’s $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. Artificial Analysis finds OpenAI models occupy “most of the Cost per Task Pareto frontier”, shared only with Anthropic and Meta.

Epoch AI’s launch-day Capabilities Index gives GPT-6.2 Astra 174, tied with Claude Fable 5.2, against 167-169 for earlier GPT-6 Astra snapshots assessed from September through November.

Professional deliverables

A slide deck can now start from a screenshot. OpenAI says GPT-6.2 Astra can reproduce the template down to font size and carry its writing style into new slides. On its internal GDPval v2 assignments, the model gains 6.2 percentage points over GPT-6 Astra while generating 22% fewer output tokens.

On Artificial Analysis’s AA-AnalystAgent spreadsheet-and-report tasks, the launch post reports GPT-6.2 Astra answering 62.5% of questions correctly on all five attempts.

Artificial Analysis’s legal evaluation [https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/harvey-lab-aa?ref=implicator.ai] gives the specialized Astra for Law configuration a 20.8% all-pass rate, against 15.0% for regular GPT-6.2 Astra. The evaluation covers 120 private assignments across 24 legal practice areas, with three AI judges grading deliverables. Passing requires meeting every requirement without a material hallucination. Even the leader misses that standard on most assignments.

OpenAI claims GPT-6.2 Astra more than doubles Claude Fable 5.2’s pass rate on Harvey LAB-AA v1.1, at about one-sixth the cost.

Across the same assignments, Artificial Analysis counts 0.03 material hallucinations per assignment for Astra for Law. The check covers unsupported claims about supplied documents; it does not establish the correctness of case-law citations or legal doctrine.

Science and physical design

On Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1, OpenAI puts GPT-6.2 Astra nine percentage points above GPT-6 Astra for less than one-third the cost. Artificial Analysis independently places it first on the evaluation of scientific workflows.

The comparison depends on the software surrounding the model. Claude Fable 5.2 ran in mini-swe-agent for those results; Anthropic reported 74.6% at maximum reasoning in Claude Code.

OpenAI also demonstrates CAD and simulation work down to individual parts. In a mountain-road driving demonstration, GPT-6.2 Astra built a car model and wrote a Python solver for the interaction of tires and suspension, then animated the results in Blender. Independent human judges on AA-3D prefer GPT-6.2 Astra’s interactive experiences often enough to give it an Elo rating of 1,318, against 1,271 for Claude Fable 5.2 and 1,259 for GPT-6.2 Sol.

Models take roughly 30 minutes per task against a human benchmark of about 20 seconds in Artificial Analysis’s AA-Robotics v0.1 research preview of basic physical tasks. Its launch-day robot-arm scores are GPT-6.2 Astra 79, GPT-6.2 Sol 78 and Claude Fable 5.2 75.

What got worse

Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, whose team tested the models for weeks, called writing “a step back”. He described training toward extreme concision, with short answers where longer ones were appropriate.

In OpenAI’s Blocker Deception trials, GPT-6.2 Astra omitted a known obstacle in 22.28% of cases, roughly double GPT-6 Astra’s 11.09%. “We are concerned by this regression,” OpenAI writes, suggesting efforts “to make responses concise and avoid excessive caveats” may be related. A Codex prompt reduces omissions in testing; it “is not applied to direct API requests.”

OpenAI says both models “deliver better results on demanding tasks with fewer output tokens”.

Artificial Analysis finds no improvement on its Coding Agent Index: GPT-6.2 Astra is flat against GPT-6 Astra; GPT-6.2 Sol falls three points against GPT-6.1 Sol. A 14-point decline on SWE-Atlas-QnA-AA drives the latter result, with key facts omitted more often.

Artificial Analysis also measures hallucination rates of 59% for GPT-6.2 Astra and 55% for GPT-6.2 Sol on AA-Omniscience, which tests guessing when factual answers are unknown. Investor Matt Shumer would not write a full review. “I had a terrible experience testing them. They were essentially unusable.” Mollick’s verdict alongside his working space elevator was “very uneven & weird”.

Security and research

OpenAI’s system card [https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-2-astra-sol/ai-self-improvement-capabilities?ref=implicator.ai] records GPT-6.2 Astra achieving arbitrary code execution in 53.5% of its internal ExploitBench trials, against 31.5% for GPT-6 Astra. Safeguards were disabled. The test uses known V8 and Chrome flaws disclosed from June through August 2026, rather than measuring discovery of unknown vulnerabilities.

Separately, UK AISI’s pre-release synthetic vulnerability-discovery tests give GPT-6.2 Astra an 88.6% success rate against GPT-6 Astra’s 81.7%. Their confidence intervals overlap. Those tests used single responses without tools; they do not measure an agent carrying out an attack.

Both new models meet OpenAI’s Critical cybersecurity threshold. Tighter launch safeguards also restrict some legitimate security research.

GPT-6.2 Astra is OpenAI’s first externally released model rated High for AI self-improvement. OpenAI defines that as equivalent to giving each researcher an additional highly capable research engineer, relative to a 2024 baseline. Its tests cover debugging failed experiments, writing faster kernels and training a small model from scratch. Control Room tracks research workloads and can halt further model output when intervention is needed. OpenAI writes: “We track this capability because models that can help develop future AI systems could also introduce new risks to safety, like accelerating progress faster than safeguards or human oversight can adapt.”

Frequently Asked Questions

What are GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol?

OpenAI released them on November 17, 2026. GPT-6.2 Astra is its frontier model for demanding work, and GPT-6.2 Sol is its cheaper everyday model. Both are in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API.

How much does GPT-6.2 Astra cost in the API?

$8 per million input tokens, $0.40 per million cached input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. That is 20% below GPT-6 Astra and the same list price as Claude Fable 5.2. GPT-6.2 Sol keeps GPT-6.1 Sol's $2 and $10 rates.

Did independent testers confirm OpenAI's gains?

Partly. Artificial Analysis puts GPT-6.2 Astra second on its Intelligence Index, and Epoch AI scores it 174, tied with Claude Fable 5.2. Artificial Analysis found no improvement on its Coding Agent Index, and GPT-6.2 Sol fell three points there.

What got worse in GPT-6.2?

OpenAI's system card shows GPT-6.2 Astra failing to mention a known obstacle in 22.28% of Blocker Deception trials, against 11.09% for GPT-6 Astra. A Codex prompt reduces this but is not applied to direct API requests. Early testers Ethan Mollick, Dan Shipper and Matt Shumer criticized the models.

What does the High self-improvement rating mean?

GPT-6.2 Astra is OpenAI's first externally released model rated High for AI self-improvement. OpenAI defines that as equivalent to giving each of its researchers an additional highly capable research engineer, relative to a 2024 baseline.
