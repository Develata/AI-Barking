1.3.2 Latest date of data (acquisition/collection for model training):
The data used to train the model is composed of different datasets with varying publication and cutoff
dates, with datasets collected as late as March 2026 for pre-training and synthetic data generated
through November 2026 during post-training. The model will not be continuously trained on new or
dynamic data while in production, but subsequent versions may undergo additional fine-tuning and be
released as later versions.

1.3.4 Date the training dataset was first used to train the model: July 2026
1.3.5 Description of the linguistic characteristics of the overall training data:
Text datasets used to train the model are primarily in English. Training data also includes German,
Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, Chinese (Simplified), Russian, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Arabic,
and Hungarian. Additional limited coverage also includes Swedish, Romanian, Polish, Dutch, and Czech,
among others. Post-training synthetic data is predominantly in English. Coverage and quality vary by
language.
1.3.6 Other relevant characteristics of the overall training data:
Training data combines publicly available, commercially acquired, and crawled web data used to pre-
train and mid-train the MAI 12B base model with post-training data focused on software engineering. Pre-
training and mid-training data includes a high proportion of code (public repository files, pull requests,
commits, notebooks, and shell scripts) and vision-language data. Post-training uses synthetic data
generated across approximately 537,000 task prompts (about 5.1 million completed policy rollouts).
Post-training prompts cover 12 task families and include text-only tasks and tasks with accompanying
images or visual artifacts. A portion of post-training tasks is derived from development-assistant
telemetry (see Section 2.4).
Microsoft-approved safety classifiers are used to filter and remove sensitive data from data used for
training and provide additional safety guardrails to the frontend user experience where the model is
deployed in products. User interaction data from the model itself is not used. Synthetic data is used in
post-training and for quality classification, captioning, and metadata generation in pre-training data
curation.
1.3.7 Rationale or purpose of data selection:
Training data for MAI Code 2 Flash was selected to transfer software engineering capabilities from a
larger teacher model into an efficient 12B model suitable for high-volume developer use. Post-training
data was selected to maximize coverage of agentic software engineering tasks (repository-level issue
resolution, code editing and testing, CLI/terminal operation, MCP and tool use, UI/web interaction, and
office/document productivity), developer-facing instruction-following, and STEM reasoning, while
retaining broad reasoning and language understanding from pre-training. Telemetry-derived tasks were
included to align training with real-world developer workflows, and safety/responsible AI tasks were
included to support policy-aware behavior.

2.3.3 Purposes of the crawler(s): Index web content for both search and model training
2.3.4 General description of crawler behavior:
For training purposes, we filter publicly available crawled data sources to exclude content behind
paywalls, content that violates Microsoft’s Responsible AI (RAI) policies, or sites that have opted out of
training using published web controls (for example, we respect robots.txt files and meta tags on third
party websites). This includes respect of captchas, password-protected websites and paywalls,
robots.txt, and other protocols while crawling. Training data does not include domains listed in the Office
of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) Notorious Markets for Counterfeiting and Piracy list.
2.3.5 Period of data collection: July 2010 to March 2026
2.3.6 Comprehensive description of the type of content and online sources crawled:
Crawled training data includes a wide variety of filtered, publicly available web content spanning
everyday topics, technical and STEM domains, news, blogs, forums, community Q&A websites,
educational sites, and reference sources. Both English and multilingual content is included. Code-
specific data covers public code repository files, pull requests, commit histories, notebooks, and shell
scripts. Publicly available crawled data sources are safety-filtered, quality-filtered, and deduplicated
(exact and fuzzy).

2.3.8 Summary of the most relevant domain names crawled:
The top 10% domains by volume crawled and used to train MAI Code 2 Flash span a broad mix of content
types. These include publicly available reference and knowledge sites, blogging and personal-publishing
platforms, social and professional networks, educational and study platforms, document-sharing
services, technical Q&A and developer communities, scientific and academic publishers and archives,
news outlets and news archives, creative-writing and design communities, e-commerce marketplaces,
and large search portals. General web hosting, blogging, social, and e-commerce platforms make up a
large share of the total. The content is multilingual. Alongside English, it includes significant volumes of
Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French, German, and Russian, as well as a large East-Asian presence in
Chinese, Japanese, and Korean. A substantial share of the top domains are China- or Japan-based.
Prominent country-code TLDs include .jp, .cn, .co.uk, .es, .fr, .ca, and .nz, alongside the .eu institutional
domain. The top domains include government and public-sector sources, academic (.edu) sources, and
recognized authoritative references and scholarly publishers.

2.5.4 Information about other AI models, including provider’s own AI model(s) not available on the

Internal Microsoft AI models not available on the market were used to generate, verify, and grade post-

answering, instruction-following, and safety tasks.
2.5.5 Provide a description of the need or desired purpose for using synthetic data for a model or
system’s intended purpose:
Synthetic data was used to scale verifiable training signals for software engineering tasks where high-
quality human-annotated data is scarce. It supports repository-level issue resolution, code editing and
testing, CLI and tool use, agentic task completion, instruction-following, and reasoning, and is also used
to support automated quality and topic classification during pre-training data curation. Synthetic data
was selected because it provides scalable, targeted training signals that would not be feasible to obtain
from human annotation alone at the required volume.

3.2 Removal of illegal content
3.2.1 General description of measures taken to avoid or remove illegal content: Microsoft follows
applicable laws and best practices to avoid or remove content that is illegal under EU law from training
data. For example, before training, data is screened by hash matching, including Microsoft PhotoDNA,
against databases of known illegal content: child sexual exploitation and abuse imagery (CSEAI, also
called CSAM), nonconsensual intimate imagery (NCII), and terrorist and violent extremist content
(TVEC). Matches are removed from training data. Domains associated with piracy and counterfeiting are
excluded, as described in Section 2.3.4, as are web pages subject to valid DMCA takedown notices or
court blocking orders and adult domains on the UT1 blocklist.
