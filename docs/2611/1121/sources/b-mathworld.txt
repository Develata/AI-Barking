3SUM Problem

The 3SUM problem asks whether a given collection of numbers [/Number.html] contains three distinct
 elements whose sum is zero. Its integer [/Integer.html] and real variants
 are fundamental problems in the study of algorithms [/Algorithm.html].
 The 3SUM hypothesis asserts that no algorithm [/Algorithm.html] solves
 the problem in time 
 for any fixed .

Alman and Vassilevska Williams (2026) reported a deterministic algorithm [/Algorithm.html] with running time 
 for integers [/Integer.html] of magnitude bounded by a polynomial [/Polynomial.html]
 in , on a random access
 machine [/RandomAccessMachine.html] with -bit
 words. Their method also gives a truly subcubic all-pairs
 shortest path [/All-PairsShortestPath.html] algorithm [/Algorithm.html]. They state that known
 reductions extend the refutation of the 3SUM hypothesis to the real-input version.

Alman and Vassilevska Williams (2026) state that Anthropic's Claude discovered the core algorithm [/Algorithm.html], which they checked, simplified, and
 extended. They also report a Lean 4 formalization and take responsibility for the
 results.
