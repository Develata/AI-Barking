Title:The Hyperclique Hypothesis is False

Authors:Nick Fischer [https://arxiv.org/search/cs?searchtype=author&query=Fischer,+N]

View a PDF of the paper titled The Hyperclique Hypothesis is False, by Nick Fischer

 View PDF [/pdf/2611.03880]
 HTML (experimental) [https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03880v1]

 Abstract:The Hyperclique Hypothesis postulates that, for all constants $k > h \geq 3$, detecting a $k$-clique in an $h$-uniform hypergraph with $n$ vertices requires time $n^{k-o(1)}$. This hypothesis is false: there is an algebraic algorithm that detects (and even counts) $k$-cliques in $h$-uniform hypergraphs in time $O(n^{(1-\epsilon_h)k})$. Via known reductions, this also refutes the recently proposed $k$-AP Hypothesis. As a consequence, many problems with conditional lower bounds based on these two hypotheses may admit faster algorithms after all. This result is almost entirely due to AI.
