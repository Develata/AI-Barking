                                  FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

       I.      The New York Times and Its Mission

               A.      175 Years of High-Quality, Original, Independent Journalism

       21.     The New York Times is a trusted source of quality, independent journalism

whose mission is to seek the truth and help people understand the world. Begun as a small, local

newspaper, The Times has evolved into a diversified multi-media company with millions of

readers, listeners, and viewers around the globe. As of June 30, 2026, more than 13 million

subscribers pay for Times journalism, which includes everything from news to opinion, culture

to business, cooking to games, and shopping recommendations to sports.

       22.     Founded in 1851, The New York Times has a long history of providing the public

with independent journalism of the highest quality. When Adolph Ochs bought the newspaper

out of bankruptcy in 1896, he vowed that The Times would be fiercely independent, dedicated to

journalism of the highest integrity, and devoted to the public welfare. He articulated his objective

         23.   Producing original independent journalism is at the heart of this mission. Times

journalists cover the most important stories across the globe; in a typical year, The Times sends

journalists to report on the ground from more than 150 countries, including more than 30

international bureaus. Together, along with editors, photographers, audio producers,

videographers, graphic designers, data analysts, and more, The Times’s newsroom produces

groundbreaking journalism across every major storytelling format.

         24.   The quality of The Times’s coverage has been widely recognized with many

impactful. Academics, teachers, and scientists have used it to educate and innovate. Lawmakers

have cited it to introduce legislation. Judges have referenced it in rulings. And tens of millions of

people rely on it every day.

         25.   Times journalists are experts in their subject matter and among the most

                D.     Brave’s Products Threaten High-Quality Journalism

          42.   Delivering great journalism is harder than ever. Over the past two decades, the

traditional business models that supported quality journalism have collapsed, forcing the

shuttering of newspapers all over the country. It has become more difficult for the public to sort

fact from fiction in today’s information ecosystem, as misinformation floods the internet,

television, and other media. If The Times and other news organizations cannot produce and

protect their original journalism–especially news reporting–there will be a vacuum that no

computer or AI can fill.

          43.   The protection of The Times’s intellectual property is critical to its continued

ability to fund world-class journalism in the public interest. When The Times and its peers

cannot control the use of their content, their ability to monetize that content is harmed. With less

revenue, news organizations will have fewer journalists able to dedicate time and resources to

important, in-depth stories—creating the real risk that those stories will go untold. Less

journalism will be produced, and the cost to society will be enormous.

                B.      Brave’s Products

        55.     Brave’s products either include features powered by Large Language Models

(“LLMs”) or serve as components for developing and operating LLMs. LLMs are a type of AI

system that generates expressive language resulting in text or other data, rather than just

analyzing existing data. LLMs work by predicting words that are likely to follow a given string

of text based on the content used to train them. They use algorithms to weigh the relevance of

different parts of the input data (e.g., a user’s inquiry) when generating text. LLM operators

“train” their models on vast datasets of written material, allowing them to encode patterns and

relationships between words and sentences.

        56.     Once trained, LLMs can generate text by taking a seed input (again, for example,

a user’s inquiry) and iteratively predicting the most likely next word based on the patterns they

have learned. Through this process, LLMs can generate answers to questions about content that

is included in their training data.

        57.     LLMs are also capable of copying content from a search index like the Brave

Index or other sources as input, then outputting, excerpting, summarizing, or answering

questions about that content. For example, LLMs may also be deployed in conjunction with a

technique called “retrieval augmented generation” (“RAG”), also known as “grounding,”

“retrieving,” or “contextualizing.” Through RAG, trained LLMs may be provided with

information specific to a use case or subject matter in order to “ground” their outputs. For

example, an LLM may be asked to generate a text output based on specific external data, such as
                                                 20
           Case 1:26-cv-09549             Document 1          Filed 11/02/26         Page 21 of 72




a document, provided as context. Using this method, a GenAI product based on an LLM will: (1)

receive an input, such as a prompt; (2) retrieve relevant content related to the input prior to

generating a response; (3) combine the original input with the retrieved documents; and (4)

provide the combined content to an LLM, which generates a response.

        58.      Brave’s RAG process involves copying, distributing, and displaying high-quality,

expressive, engaging, fact-checked content like that of The Times. Brave’s AI products depend

on material, including journalism, that human beings report, research, write, edit, and create. As

explained further below, Brave copies and distributes that content without permission or

compensation on a massive scale.
