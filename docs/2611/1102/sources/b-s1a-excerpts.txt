Quarterly Trends
Revenue
Revenue increased sequentially in each of the quarters presented, from $376 million for the three months 
ended March 31, 2025 to $17.0 billion for the three months ended September 30, 2026. The increases in each 
quarter were driven by successive model and product releases that drove higher usage, expansion, and broader 
adoption across our offerings, with both consumption-based and subscription-based revenue contributing. The 
number of customers with $100,000 or more of revenue on a TTM basis grew from approximately 400 as of 
March 31, 2025 to more than 11,900 as of September 30, 2026.
For the three months ended September 30, 2025 to the three months ended December 31, 2025, the increase in 
revenue due to higher usage was partially offset by a decrease of approximately $70 million from price 
reductions introduced as part of new model versions and contractual discounts. For the three months ended 
June 30, 2026 to the three months ended September 30, 2026, the increase in revenue due to higher usage was 
partially offset by a decrease of approximately $600 million from price reductions introduced as part of new 
model versions and contractual discounts. Price changes did not have a material impact on the other quarterly 
revenue trends presented.

Operating Expenses
Compute and Infrastructure
Compute and infrastructure expenses increased sequentially in each of the quarters presented, from $944 
million for the three months ended March 31, 2025 to $9.9 billion for the three months ended September 30, 
2026, driven by the delivery of committed compute under our multi-year arrangements. Because we typically 
procure compute 12 to 18 months in advance of availability, these expenses are generally fixed in the near term 
and increase as committed compute becomes available to us. Compute and infrastructure expenses as a 
percentage of revenue decreased from 251% for the three months ended March 31, 2025 to 48% for the three 
months ended June 30, 2026, as revenue growth outpaced the growth of our committed compute cost base, and 
increased to 58% for the three months ended September 30, 2026, reflecting the delivery of additional 
compute. This includes $5.0 billion of compute under short-term commitments, which currently costs 
significantly more than comparable compute procured under our multi-year commitments.
Personnel
Personnel expenses increased sequentially in each of the quarters presented through the three months ended 
June 30, 2026, driven by headcount growth and the associated increases in compensation, including stock-
based compensation. Personnel expenses decreased in the three months ended September 30, 2026, primarily 
driven by lower stock-based compensation expense due to certain option awards becoming fully vested, 
partially offset by higher cash compensation resulting from headcount growth.
Bartz settlement
In the three months ended September 30, 2025, we recorded a $1.5 billion charge to settle the Bartz litigation. 
See Note 13 to our consolidated financial statements included elsewhere in this prospectus.
Sales, Marketing, and Partnerships
Sales, marketing, and partnerships expenses increased sequentially in each of the quarters presented, with the 
most significant increases observed in the three months ended March 31, 2026 and June 30, 2026. The 
increases were primarily driven by fees paid to channel partners, reflecting the growth of our business, and 
increased marketing and advertising expenses, driven by higher media spend to support product launches.
Sales, marketing, and partnerships expenses as a percentage of revenue decreased from 22% for the three 
months ended March 31, 2026 to 14% for the three months ended September 30, 2026, primarily driven by a 
decrease in revenue generated from sales through channel partners as a percentage of total revenue and 
changes in the terms of our channel partner distribution arrangements.
Other Operating and Administrative
Other operating and administrative expenses increased over the quarters presented, other than in the three 
months ended March 31, 2026, which reflected lower donation matching program charges than in the three 
months ended December 31, 2025. The overall increase reflected vendor costs related to data quality and 
performance evaluation services, information technology and cloud software costs, payment processing and 
platform fees, and professional services costs as our business scaled. The increases in the three months ended 
December 31, 2025 and September 30, 2026 were primarily driven by non-cash charges related to our donation 
matching program.
