Europe's Largest Software Maker SAP Considers Restructuring Affecting 10,000 Jobs

 Park Chan-beom

 Seoul

 Published
 2026.11.27 08:17

 View
 View Count

 Enlarge image

 ▲ SAP sign 

 Europe's largest software company, SAP, is considering restructuring affecting at least 10,000 jobs as it prepares for the artificial intelligence (AI) era, Bloomberg reported, citing anonymous sources. 

 Headquartered in Walldorf, Germany, SAP employs more than 110,000 people worldwide, meaning the restructuring is expected to impact about 9% of its total workforce. 

 All departments have reportedly been asked to identify potential headcount reductions, with product, engineering, and sales departments known to be major targets under review. 

 The final scale of the layoffs has not yet been finalized and is subject to change during negotiations with labor unions. 

 In Germany, where SAP has its largest workforce, the works council reached an agreement this year guaranteeing employment through 2028. 

 The restructuring plan emerged as Chief Executive Officer Christian Klein drives an "AI-first" strategy. 

 Already, two of the six executive board members are set to leave the company next March. 

 Chief Product Officer Muhammad Alam sees his contract expire in March, and Sales Chief Thomas Saueressig announced last week that he would leave SAP to become the CEO of the Ionos Group. 

 CEO Klein is currently pushing to integrate AI tools across the company's applications. 

 In a statement, SAP said, "The company continuously reviews its organization to align with strategy and customer requirements," but declined to comment on whether there are specific layoff plans. 

 SAP has previously announced large-scale restructuring alongside its annual earnings announcements at the end of January. 

 It also cut about 10,000 jobs in 2024, though it maintained its total headcount by simultaneously hiring for new roles.
