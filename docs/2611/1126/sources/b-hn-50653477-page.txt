Proton can’t change the fundamental nature of IMAP or SMTP (and still be an “email” service. Their MTA must be able to read certain fields in the plain text email header; this doesn’t even change with PGP encrypted mail. Most MTAs use TLS for transport security. That prevents eavesdropping in transit. If you want maximal privacy from Proton, then you have the option of using PGP which introduces very high bars to working with others over email- you have to exchange keys out of band in a secure fashion, and then the other party has to either use a PGP-enabled mailer or has to know hotel to encrypt and decrypt.
Proton claims that their MTAs store the messages encrypted with a key for which they don’t possess the decryption key. This doesn’t seem like an unreasonable claim; this is a very well established pattern.
There is a window of time where for some messages they possess the plaintext of the message body that arrived at the MTA, and they make claims (I haven’t checked recently but was an early adopter and read them thoroughly when I first subscribed) that they don’t use that plaintext for business purposes and that they eliminate it from memory as fast as possible. Since I am not trying to defend myself against nation states, I’m willing to accept terms of service and implied contract as risk mitigation there.
If I capture the attention of Law Enforcement, then they can theoretically obtain orders to hand over logs, stored data, encryption keys, or (depending on jurisdiction), install or reconfigure systems to intercept plaintext prior to encryption. For this, I chose Proton because I live in the USA and my law enforcement agencies can’t skirt our fourth amendment with national security letters etc- they MUST obtain a warrant and they MUST work cooperatively with the law enforcement authorities in another country. I don’t think that in the reality we live in, that I as an individual would it’s only the resources of an individual can still use email in daily life and get any higher level of assurance. Anything else is fantasy.
I’m worried about protons business pricing- they are already outrageously expensive and getting more so and they are building AI dreck that I don’t care about; I just want secure email.

nmjenkins [user?id=nmjenkins] 1 day ago [item?id=50654185] | root [#50654101] | parent [#50654132] | next [#50654416] [–] [javascript:void(0)]

> Did you just scan for tokens called "paperless*"
Yes, exactly that. As for why…
Paperless was absolutely hammering our server. The internal email thread about it began: "Every 10 minutes we see a massive outgoing network spike that's almost 3 times our normal bandwidth. What is going on there?"
Turned out paperless was downloading the entire contents of every single message in the account it's connected to (often GBs of data!) every 10 minutes. We run ludicrously powerful hardware and give ourselves capacity headroom so we could cope, but this was clearly broken behaviour and had the potential to impact other users. We added better per-user throttling to deal with it on our end, but also made sure that Paperless fixed this, and then wanted to ask people who looked like they might be using it to update. It will save you a ton of bandwidth and compute too.
