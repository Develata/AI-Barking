Examiner Says Four iPhone 16 Features Were Not Delivered as Advertised, Adding to U.S.-Korea Digital Trade Tensions

Apple’s iPhone 16 [Yonhap]

The Korea Fair Trade Commission (KFTC) is seeking to impose a fine of up to 53.6 billion won, or approximately $39.93 million, on Apple over its artificial intelligence (AI) advertising, raising the possibility of renewed digital trade tensions between South Korea and the United States. With the U.S. government having publicly voiced concerns over South Korean regulators’ actions involving American technology companies, attention is turning to whether the KFTC’s latest enforcement move could escalate into a bilateral trade issue.

The KFTC’s secretariat said on Nov. 26 that it had submitted an examiner’s report to the commission on Nov. 24 concerning suspected violations of the Act on Fair Labeling and Advertising by Apple Inc. and Apple Korea, and had also sent the report to the companies. The examiner determined that four of the nine AI features Apple promoted in connection with the iPhone 16, launched in September 2024, were not provided as advertised. The features in question are Genmoji, a tool for creating personalized emojis; Image Playground, an AI image-generation feature; Siri’s ability to understand personal context; and Siri’s ability to provide step-by-step guidance.

The KFTC said Apple changed the information on its website over time without adequately informing consumers about when the features would become available. At the time of the iPhone 16 launch, Apple said the features would initially be released in U.S. English by the end of 2024. A month later, it changed the wording to say the service was already available in U.S. English. In February 2025, the company said Korean-language support would arrive in early April that year. However, the Siri feature that understands personal context was not made available in Korean until October 2026. Its U.S. English version was launched in September 2026.

The examiner determined that the conduct constituted a serious violation of the Act on Fair Labeling and Advertising in the form of deceptive advertising. The examiner recommended corrective measures, including an order prohibiting similar conduct in the future and a public notice, as well as a fine. The revenue related to the alleged violation was estimated at approximately 3.35 trillion won. Applying the surcharge rate of 0.8% to 1.6% for serious violations, the basic fine would range from approximately 26.8 billion won to 53.6 billion won. This is the examiner’s estimate, however, and the final amount will depend on the revenue recognized by the commission, the severity of the violation and any aggravating or mitigating factors. The fine could exceed 53.6 billion won if the commission classifies the conduct as a very serious violation.

This is not the first time South Korean regulators have taken action against Apple. In 2021, the KFTC approved a 100 billion won support package from Apple in connection with allegations that the company had shifted advertising costs to mobile carriers. Apple was also subject to a 300 million won administrative fine for obstructing an investigation. In October 2026, the Korea Communications Commission imposed a 20.81 billion won fine on Apple over practices affecting user interests, including requiring the use of specific payment methods and delaying app reviews. The series of regulatory actions involving American companies has raised questions about whether such measures could become a broader issue in bilateral trade relations.

The U.S. government has voiced concerns over South Korean regulators’ actions involving American companies. A U.S. State Department official said investigations by the KFTC into American companies could threaten the potential development of the U.S.-South Korea alliance, adding that Washington was reviewing policy options in response to what it sees as differential treatment. Some U.S. lawmakers and industry representatives have also argued that South Korea’s digital regulations could put American companies at a disadvantage. If the United States launches a Section 301 investigation into South Korea’s digital regulations or takes further action, the case could expand beyond an individual company’s advertising practices into a broader bilateral trade issue.

Apple said it disagreed with the conclusions of the examiner’s report. In a statement, the company said it had cooperated fully with the investigation and would present its position during the commission’s deliberations. Apple also said that, following the introduction of Korean-language support for Apple Intelligence, users can access dozens of features across its platforms, and that Korean-language support for the new personalized Siri has also begun.

Apple has six weeks from the receipt of the examiner’s report to submit a written response and may request access to and copies of relevant evidence. The examiner’s findings in the report are not binding on the commission’s final decision. The KFTC plans to proceed with deliberations after giving Apple an opportunity to submit its response and exercise its right to defense. The commission will then determine whether the company violated the law and decide on any final sanctions.
