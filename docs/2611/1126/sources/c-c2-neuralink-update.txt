Neuralink Receives FDA Approval to Launch LINK Pivotal Study

Neuralink has received approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to launch the LINK Pivotal Study, the first pivotal trial for an implantable brain-computer interface (BCI) in the United States. The goal of the study is to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of the Neuralink BCI system in individuals with severe paralysis due to amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS).

The study builds on findings from Neuralink’s ongoing early feasibility studies in the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom, where over 30 implanted participants have collectively contributed thousands of hours of real-world BCI use. Participants have used their Neuralink implants to pursue higher education [https://neuralink.com/updates/two-years-of-telepathy/], express their creativity [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R03-DWvWmAY], play video games [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=05EgtB4L8x4], communicate with their loved ones [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SqrANrm-QMY], and more. The approval of the LINK Pivotal Study represents an important step toward bringing implantable BCI technology to market to help restore autonomy and expand access to communication and control for individuals living with severe neurological conditions and unmet medical needs.

The LINK Pivotal Study will evaluate the ability of participants to use Neuralink technology to control digital devices through thought, with a focus on functional outcomes that improve independence in daily life. The study will also continue to assess the long-term safety and reliability of the system across multiple clinical sites.

We are grateful to the participants, families, clinicians, regulators, and research teams who helped make this next step possible. Enrollment for the LINK Pivotal Study is now open and we look forward to continuing to expand access to the study over time.

To learn more about the LINK Pivotal Study and join our Patient Registry, visit neuralink.com/trials/device-control/ [https://neuralink.com/trials/device-control/]
