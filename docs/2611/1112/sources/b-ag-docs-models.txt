Choose a model
Section titled “Choose a model” [#choose-a-model]

Antigravity gives you access to leading frontier reasoning models from Google DeepMind and partner providers. You can switch models at any time and choose a thinking level (Low, Medium, High, or Extra on supported models) to balance response speed for quick edits against deeper multi-step reasoning for complex architecture and debugging tasks. To combine planning on Gemini 4 Carbon or Gemini 4 Argon with fast Gemini 4 Hydrogen execution and use fewer tokens, select Orchestrator [#orchestrator].

Most adjustable Gemini models offer Low, Medium, and High thinking levels. Gemini 4 Hydrogen defaults to High and adds a fourth level labeled Extra in the model selector, which sets the Extra High reasoning budget (xhigh in the Antigravity CLI [/docs/cli/commands/effort], such as --model gemini-4-hydrogen-xhigh or --effort xhigh) when a task needs maximum reasoning depth.

Your choice of reasoning model persists between messages within a conversation. If you change the reasoning model while the agent is running, it continues to use the previously selected reasoning model until it completes its steps for that turn (or until you cancel the current execution).

Orchestrator
Section titled “Orchestrator” [#orchestrator]

Orchestrator is a model selector option that pairs two Gemini 4 models in a
single conversation: a larger planner model does the planning and reviews, and
the fast, lightweight Gemini 4 Hydrogen does the implementation work. There
are two Orchestrator options, and the one you select sets the planner:

Model selector optionPlannerImplementationAvailable on

Orchestrator (Carbon)Gemini 4 Carbon [/blog/gemini-4-carbon-in-google-antigravity]Gemini 4 HydrogenGoogle AI Ultra

Orchestrator (Argon)Gemini 4 Argon [/blog/gemini-4-argon-in-google-antigravity]Gemini 4 HydrogenGoogle AI Ultra, Google AI Pro (paid plans, or trials with eligible premium devices)

Orchestrator (Carbon) always plans with Gemini 4 Carbon, and
Orchestrator (Argon) always plans with Gemini 4 Argon. Google AI Ultra
includes both Orchestrator (Carbon) and Orchestrator (Argon) in the
model selector, while Google AI Pro (paid plans, or trials with eligible
premium devices) includes Orchestrator (Argon). Hover over the New
badge next to either option to see which planner it uses.

The planner and Gemini 4 Hydrogen are also available as standalone models in
the model selector, depending on your plan. Orchestrator coordinates them
automatically, so you get the planner’s quality while finishing faster and
using fewer tokens than running the planner alone.

Orchestrator isn’t a router that classifies your prompt upfront and sends the
whole request to a single model. Instead, both models collaborate within the
same task and hand control back and forth as the work unfolds:

The planner plans. Each message you send starts with a read-only planning
phase in which the planner explores the codebase, resolves ambiguities, and
writes a concrete implementation plan, including the conditions that should
trigger re-planning. If your message is a question, review, or explanation
that doesn’t need changes, the planner answers it directly; for small
follow-ups whose steps are already settled, it hands off a short plan right
away.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen does the work. Once the plan is ready, Gemini 4 Hydrogen
takes over to apply file edits, write code, run builds and tests, and verify
the results against the plan.

Control returns when something unexpected happens. If Gemini 4 Hydrogen
runs into friction, such as a verification step that fails twice, an interface
that behaves differently than expected, or a design choice the plan didn’t
settle, it hands control back to the planner, which inspects the conversation
state, repairs the plan, and hands execution back to Gemini 4 Hydrogen.

The planner reviews progress. On longer tasks, and whenever you send a
message while Gemini 4 Hydrogen is working, Gemini 4 Hydrogen reports its
progress to the planner, which checks what was done against the plan and then
wraps up the completed task, continues the remaining steps, or updates the
plan.

These hand-offs happen automatically within a single response, so you don’t need
to switch models manually between planning and execution.

Orchestrator runs with a fixed configuration, so you can’t choose a thinking
level for it. To run every step on a single model or set a specific thinking
level, select Gemini 4 Carbon, Gemini 4 Argon, or Gemini 4 Hydrogen
directly in the model selector. Availability depends on your plan; refer to the
reasoning model table [#reasoning-model].

Antigravity App
Section titled “Antigravity App” [#sr-tab-1]

Click the model selector under the conversation prompt box.

Select Orchestrator (Carbon) or Orchestrator (Argon).

Selecting Orchestrator (Carbon) in the Antigravity App: open the model selector under the prompt box, which lists Orchestrator (Carbon) and Orchestrator (Argon), hover the New badge next to Orchestrator (Carbon) to see what it does, choose it, and type a task.

Like any other model, Orchestrator stays selected for the rest of the
conversation until you choose a different model.

Antigravity CLI
Section titled “Antigravity CLI” [#sr-tab-2]

Selecting Orchestrator (Carbon) in the Antigravity CLI: complete /model with Tab, choose Orchestrator (Carbon) from the list of models, which also shows Orchestrator (Argon), and check that /effort doesn't apply to it.

You can select Orchestrator in any of the following ways:

Interactive picker: Run /model, select Orchestrator (Carbon) or
Orchestrator (Argon), and press Enter. Your choice persists
across sessions.

At startup: Pass the model’s name in quotes when you launch the CLI or run
it in headless mode [/docs/cli/headless]:

agy --model "Orchestrator (Carbon)"
agy -p "Add input validation to the signup form and run the tests." --model "Orchestrator (Argon)"

Run agy models to list every model identifier available to your account.
Because Orchestrator has a fixed configuration, --effort and /effort don’t
apply to it; running /effort while Orchestrator is selected reports that
effort isn’t adjustable for it, for example
Effort isn't adjustable for Orchestrator (Argon).

When to use Orchestrator
Section titled “When to use Orchestrator” [#when-to-use-orchestrator]

Orchestrator (recommended default): Most day-to-day development, such as
feature work, bug fixes, refactoring, and multi-file debugging, where you
want Gemini 4 Carbon or Gemini 4 Argon to plan and review the work and
Gemini 4 Hydrogen to carry out the implementation faster and with fewer
tokens used.

Gemini 4 Carbon or Gemini 4 Argon: Work where every intermediate step
needs maximum reasoning depth, such as designing complex distributed systems
from scratch, untangling subtle concurrency race conditions, or running
multi-repository migrations. Selecting the model directly also lets you
choose its thinking level.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen: Fast, everyday coding and maintenance tasks where you
don’t need a separate planning phase, such as package
upgrades, code deduplication, scaffolding boilerplate, writing unit tests for
well-defined functions, updating documentation, or generating quick scripts.
Selecting Gemini 4 Hydrogen directly also lets you choose its thinking level.

Orchestrator keeps a single agent in your conversation and hands off between
planning and execution within each turn. To have multiple agents work in
parallel or check each other’s work, use subagents [/docs/subagents],
Swarm [/docs/swarm], Boost [/docs/boost], or Teamwork [/docs/teamwork].
