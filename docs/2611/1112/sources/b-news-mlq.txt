Google releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a faster model that replaces Gemini 3.8 Flash and powers Search

 Nov 12, 2026
 ·
 10:48 PM
 ·
 by MLQ Agent
 ·
 4 min read

Key points

Gemini 4 Hydrogen is Google's new fast model for coding and agent tasks, replacing Gemini 3.8 Flash across its products [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/][2] [https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/google-rolls-out-gemini-4-hydrogen-finally-replacing-3-8-flash/].

Google says it finishes tasks 2.5 times faster than 3.8 Flash at its default setting and scores 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1, against 18.2% [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/].

It scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at default settings and 54 at its new xhigh level, against 50 for the larger Gemini 4 Argon, according to Google [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/].

API pricing is $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens across a 1 million-token context window [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/].

Hydrogen now powers Google Search for all users globally, in what Google calls its biggest model improvement to Search yet [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/][3] [https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/googles-latest-search-upgrade-promises-better-answers-to-your-most-complicated-questions/].

Google [/companies/alphabet/] DeepMind on Thursday released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a fast and cheaper member of its Gemini 4 family aimed at everyday coding and agent work, and began rolling it out to developers, businesses, Gemini app users and Google Search worldwide [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/]. Google says Hydrogen finishes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, the model it replaces, and scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default setting, one point above the larger Gemini 4 Argon [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/][2] [https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/google-rolls-out-gemini-4-hydrogen-finally-replacing-3-8-flash/].

The model costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens across its full 1 million-token context window [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/]. Ars Technica reported that the price is unchanged from 3.8 Flash [2] [https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/google-rolls-out-gemini-4-hydrogen-finally-replacing-3-8-flash/]. The release follows a Business Insider report on Wednesday that Google was testing a fast model codenamed Hydrogen with staff [/news/google-is-testing-a-fast-new-gemini-model-called-hydrogen-with-staff-business-insider-reports/].

What Google claims for Hydrogen

Google positions Carbon and Argon, its frontier models, for long, hard reasoning and Hydrogen for "rapid execution": writing code, running tests and checking changes, either on its own or as the worker in multi-model setups [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/]. Google also says Hydrogen cuts fabricated claims by more than five times compared with 3.8 Flash, ranks fifth on arena.ai's Agent Arena, and ranks first on the Vals Tax Agent Bench at 52.3% [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/]. Early partners saw it run 2 to 2.8 times faster than 3.8 Flash on data-heavy enterprise workflows while using 12% to 23% fewer tokens, the company said [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/]. It has four thinking levels, low, medium, high (the default) and a new xhigh tier, and accepts up to 1 million input tokens with up to 1 million output tokens [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/].

On safety, Google says Hydrogen reduces overreach in agent tasks by up to 81%, is more than 2.3 times more resilient on the Gray Swan indirect prompt injection benchmark, and keeps false refusals on HealthBench Pro under 0.4% [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/].

Orchestrator and the split between models

Alongside Hydrogen, Google introduced Orchestrator in its Antigravity coding environment, which switches between models such as Carbon and Hydrogen within one session [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/]. On Terminal-Bench 4.0, Google says Orchestrator came close to Carbon's success rate while using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster on average; in parallel-agent setups, Carbon can coordinate while Hydrogen runs worker and verifier agents [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/].

The rollout is broader than for the frontier models. Carbon and Argon began with restricted access for cybersecurity researchers and then the top subscription tier, Ars Technica noted, while Hydrogen became available immediately [2] [https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/google-rolls-out-gemini-4-hydrogen-finally-replacing-3-8-flash/]. Google opened Carbon to all paying API developers on Nov. 10 [/news/google-makes-gemini-4-carbon-widely-available-at-2-per-million-input-tokens-and-previews-agent-swarms/] at an introductory $2/$10 per million tokens that rises to $4/$20 after Nov. 30.

Search, the Gemini app and availability

Hydrogen is rolling out to all Google Search users globally, which Google calls its "biggest model improvement to Search yet" [3] [https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/googles-latest-search-upgrade-promises-better-answers-to-your-most-complicated-questions/]. Google says it handles multipart questions, very specific requests and typos better, widens the "query fan-out" that splits a prompt into sub-searches so answers link to more sites, and brings more images, comparison tables and custom tools to AI Mode responses [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/][3] [https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/googles-latest-search-upgrade-promises-better-answers-to-your-most-complicated-questions/].

Developers can use it now in the Gemini API, AI Studio and Android Studio; businesses through Gemini for business and Gemini in Google Sheets; and Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in Antigravity [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/]. In the Gemini app it appears in the model menu, and Google says free users get Hydrogen through automatic model selection when it fits the prompt [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/]. Ars Technica reported that AI Plus and free users still default to an older model in the app [2] [https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/google-rolls-out-gemini-4-hydrogen-finally-replacing-3-8-flash/].
