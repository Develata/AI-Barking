Google releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen as a faster, cheaper model for coding and agentic work

 By t2ONLINE Web Desk

Published: 13 Nov 2026, 12:42PM|2 min read

Picture: Google

Advertisement

Google has released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a faster and lower-cost model in its Gemini 4 family built for everyday coding and agentic tasks. It is rolling out across the Gemini app, Google Search and the company’s developer platforms.

Hydrogen sits alongside Google’s frontier models, Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon, which are designed for deep, long-horizon reasoning on complex problems. It is priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, a flat rate across its full one-million-token context window.

The model is available on all tiers of Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, Gemini for business, the Gemini app and Search. Paid Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers also get it in Google Antigravity.

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, Hydrogen scores 51 at its default “high” thinking level, ahead of Argon’s 50, and reaches 54 at a new “xhigh” setting. Four thinking levels (low, medium, high and xhigh) let users trade reasoning depth against speed and cost.

Advertisement

Compared with its predecessor, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Google said Hydrogen finishes tasks 2.5 times faster at its default setting. Its Terminal-Bench 4.1 score rises from 18.2 per cent to 57 per cent, and it makes more than five times fewer fabricated claims. Partners testing graph, SQL and data-heavy enterprise workloads saw speeds two to 2.8 times faster and token usage 12 to 23 per cent lower.

Hydrogen scored 58.8 per cent on HLE-Diamond and 53.4 per cent on the new Multimodal Knowledge Agent benchmark. It took first place on the Vals Tax Agent Bench with 52.3 per cent and tied for first on the Vals Legal Research Bench with 55.8 per cent. It scored 71.6 per cent on Agents’ Last Exam v1.1 and ranks fifth on arena.ai’s live Agent Arena benchmark.

The model takes up to one million input tokens and can produce up to one million output tokens. It can work across PDFs, spreadsheets, charts, audio and recorded video.

Google has also introduced Orchestrator in Antigravity, which switches between models such as Carbon and Hydrogen within a single session. On Terminal-Bench 4.0, Orchestrator came close to Carbon’s task success rates while using 24 per cent less quota on average and finishing 19 per cent faster. In parallel-agent setups, Carbon can act as the coordinator while Hydrogen runs the worker and verifier agents in isolated workspaces.

Advertisement

In the Gemini app, Hydrogen is designed to follow instructions more closely over multi-turn conversations, stick to the tone and format a user sets, and call the right tools more often. It can pull context from uploads, Gmail, Google Drive and connected apps such as Airtable and Dropbox.

In Search, which is getting the model for all users globally, Hydrogen is meant to handle multi-part queries, typos and highly specific requests and to give more visual responses. An upgraded query fan-out technique will link users to a wider range of sites.

On safety, Google said that compared with 3.8 Flash, Hydrogen cuts overreach in agentic tasks by up to 81 per cent and under-service by up to 79 per cent. It also reduces misaligned actions and task shortcutting in tool-use and coding evaluations by a factor of 12. When given impossible or blocked tasks, it is up to half as likely to bypass permission boundaries, and it achieved a 96.7 per cent honesty rate on an internal agent evaluation.

The company reported 99.9 per cent chain-of-thought legibility with no deceptive reasoning edits across more than 200,000 audited turns. Hydrogen is also more than 2.3 times as resilient on the Gray Swan indirect prompt injection benchmark, and its false refusals on HealthBench Pro stay under 0.4 per cent.
