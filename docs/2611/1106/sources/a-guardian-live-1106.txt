Here's a recap of where things stand so far

Stephen Miller, one of Donald Trump’s fiercest allies and architect of the administration’s hardline deportation agenda, will now be the White House chief of staff. The role is a promotion for Miller, who currently serves as an adviser. Trump said that Miller will start the job on Monday 9 November, less than a week after Republicans’ crushing defeat in the midterm elections.

Wiles will now take a role in Trump’s political operation, which includes his Super Pac Maga Inc. On social media, she said that serving the president has been “the honor of a lifetime”. Wiles has been in the president’s orbit for over a decade, and prior to her role at the White House, she managed, alongside Chris LaCivita, Trump’s winning 2024 presidential campaign.

Trump later told reporters that he decided to promote Miller because he “wanted to make a change”. He praised Wiles, and said that Miller had been by his side “from the beginning”. “Sometimes in life you have to make moves,” the president said.

Meanwhile, Democrats blasted Trump’s decision, after Republicans faced a drubbing in the midterm elections. Chuck Schumer, the Senate minority leader, said that the president “learned nothing from his historic defeat”, and Congressman Robert Garcia – poised to be the chair of the House oversight committee – advised Miller to “lawyer up”. Garcia said that in the majority, Democrats on his panel will “continue to investigate [Stephen] Miller for his misconduct, and we will fight against his hateful policies in Congress and the courts”.

Closing summary

This concludes our live coverage of the second and final Trump administration [https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/trump-administration] at the end of a momentous week. We leave you with the news:

The elevation of Stephen Miller, the architect of Donald Trump’s brutal and unpopular mass deportation agenda, to White House chief of staff suggests that the president has no intention of rolling back the crackdown, even after voters delivered a sweeping rebuke by electing Democrats to every House seat along the US border with Mexico.

While Trump’s hardline supporters were thrilled, there was bipartisan dismay at the news. “Stephen Miller belongs nowhere near the Oval Office,”the NAACP president said. “He belongs before a judge. He’s a thug who will be remembered as the architect of Trump’s vile and authoritarian regime.”

Republican congressman Marlin Stutzman, who said on Wednesday, “I think Stephen Miller should be fired this morning”, told Semafor appointing Miller was a “horrible decision.”

Senator Chris Murphy was released from a hospital on Friday following treatment for a stroke and plans to spend several weeks at a rehabilitation center as he works to recover, the Connecticut Democrat said in a statement.

Multiple reporters said that the phrase “wartime consigliere” had been used by the White House to describe Miller, which is a reference to what a close adviser to mafia boss is called in The Godfather [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sql3pQoSito]. The phrase has been used repeatedly in recent years by people close to Trump to describe what he is looking for in a right-hand man, who will use slash-and-burn tactics to defend him at all costs.

“Mr President, there’s people describing Stephen Miller as your ‘wartime consigliere’ – do you agree with that characterization?” the reporter asked [https://bsky.app/profile/atrupar.com/post/3mzonmptufc26].

That final remark was perhaps an indication of just how cloistered the president is, given that there has been widespread anger at dismay at his choice of Miller from Democrats and some Republicans, but the White House issued a press release [https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/11/president-trump-names-stephen-miller-as-33rd-white-house-chief-of-staff/] on the appointment which claimed “The news was met with widespread praise”, followed by quotes lauding Miller from five dozen current and former administration officials, Republican lawmakers and pundits.

In 2023, as Trump faced multiple criminal investigations, Steve Bannon described [https://www.nytimes.com/2023/03/14/us/politics/boris-epshteyn-trump-lawyer.html] his legal adviser Boris Epshteyn as “a wartime consigliere.”

The role Trump seems to be looking to fill is one that was first played in his life by Roy Cohn, his former personal lawyer and fixer, who had been a top aide to senator Joseph McCarthy during investigations into supposed communist infiltration of the US government in the 1950s.

It was Cohn who had first advised Trump in 1973 to respond to the federal government’s allegation [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bkkTQZW5Uc] that Trump and his father had violated laws against racial discrimination in housing by refusing to rent to Black tenants, by going on the offensive and countersuing the government for $100bn.

In 2017, when the support for Trump’s campaign from the Russian government was under investigation, Trump was enraged that his first attorney general, Jeff Sessions, decided to recuse himself instead of undermining the probe. “Where’s my Roy Cohn?” the president reportedly asked aides [https://www.nytimes.com/2018/01/04/us/politics/trump-sessions-russia-mcgahn.html].
