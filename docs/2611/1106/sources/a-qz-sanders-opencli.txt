Bernie Sanders is demanding a national data center moratorium after Democrats' election wins

Sanders cites local ballot results, while a Color of Change leader says a blanket freeze is contested even among Democrats

By Colleen Cabili
·
2 min read
·
Updated November 5, 2026
Add QZ to Google

Credit: Bloomberg / Getty Images

Sen. Bernie Sanders called for a national data center moratorium in a post on X $TWTR 0.00% after Democrats won control of Congress in Tuesday's midterm elections.

Sanders, an independent from Vermont who caucuses with Democrats, said "Americans don't want Big Tech oligarchs wrecking the environment, gutting jobs and threatening humanity's future." He said he and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez introduced an AI data center moratorium, and that communities voted for local versions of it on Tuesday.

The bill from Sanders and Ocasio-Cortez would freeze the construction of any new data centers across the country until lawmakers set safeguards covering power prices, environmental damage and local consent, according to TechCrunch. Democratic control of Congress may strengthen its prospects, but a presidential veto could still block it.

Rep. Yassamin Ansari, D-Ariz., wrote on X that the top of the Democratic agenda on day 1 should be a pause on advanced AI development, regulation of the industry and a ban on artificial superintelligence. Rep. Greg Casar, D-Texas, who chairs the Congressional Progressive $PGR -2.32% Caucus, responded "Yes!" to her post, according to CNBC.

Democrats are not united on the issue. Nadine Smith, president and CEO of Color of Change, said some Democrats fear that a ban could hamper the advance of AI and undermine national security, according to TechCrunch. "Even among Democrats, a blanket freeze is contested," she said.

Data centers featured in several winning campaigns. Gina Hinojosa, who defeated Republican Gov. Greg Abbott $ABT -3.23% in Texas, said in a late-October video that she would halt approval of every data center project until lawmakers enact safeguards for Texans, according to CNBC. Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro put his name to an executive order in August meant to curb the growth of data center projects.

According to CNBC, 56% of voters in its Voter Poll called AI and data centers a crucial or major factor in how they voted. Of those voters, 65% cast ballots for a Democratic candidate for Congress.

Industry is already responding. Meta $META +0.84% Platforms President Dina Powell McCormick said on Wednesday that launching new U.S. data center projects will grow more difficult, and OpenAI pledged on Thursday to cover all electricity and infrastructure costs at future campuses it owns and operates. Voters in towns and cities across the country backed bans, moratoriums and other curbs on data centers in Tuesday's ballot measures.

Chip and data center stocks fell for a second day on Thursday as investors worried that Democratic gains could slow data center construction. The iShares Semiconductor ETF was down as much as 11%.
