House Democratic Leader Jeffries calls on lawmakers to 'act urgently' on AI

"Decisive action" is needed "to protect the American people," Jeffries said.

ByIsabel Danzis [https://abcnews.com/author/isabel_danzis]

September 13, 2026, 12:26 PM

9:01

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries speaks during a press conference in the U.S. Capitol, September 3, 2026 in Washington.

Mariam Zuhaib/AP Photo

House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries said Sunday that lawmakers need to take urgent action on the growing threat of artificial intelligence (AI). 

“We certainly need to begin to act urgently to address the challenges that exist," Jeffries said on ABC’s “This Week with George Stephanopoulos."

"We should take decisive action now so that we can slow down, as the CEOs have recently acknowledged, slow down the pace of development in order to protect the American people and ensure that AI is proceeding safely,” Jeffries added. 

Jeffries’ comments came after former Anthropic Researcher Jacob Coxon issued stark warnings on the future of AI development. Coxon said on an X thread [https://nam04.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fx.com%2Fhilbertspaess%2Fstatus%2F2097476196791709843&data=05%7C02%7CChristopher.W.Watson%40abc.com%7C85b788f8ad754810e87508df11a20dcd%7C56b731a8a2ac4c32bf6b616810e913c6%7C1%7C0%7C639249059839693985%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C0%7C%7C%7C&sdata=AkDrf78%2BWyOfD3C5F%2BITQE8E4P7ycJQh7969OjR7k9A%3D&reserved=0], “The people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade.” 

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries speaks during a press conference in the U.S. Capitol, September 3, 2026 in Washington.

Mariam Zuhaib/AP Photo

Coxon told "This Week" [https://abcnews.com/Politics/former-anthropic-openai-employee-sounds-alarm-ai-development/story?id=136401554] Sunday that within tech companies, conversations sharing his dire sentiments have been increasing in "recent months" and he urged people to stay engaged with tech companies and AI leaders. 

"It's pretty important to keep your eyes open about the direction things are heading and not necessarily leave it all up to the people that are building it because, you know, they want engagement. The people building the technology want to have conversations with people," Coxon said. "One of the main sticking points over the last few years has been the inability to wake people up about the danger that they're creating."

According to Jeffries, the Democratic caucus plans to meet on Tuesday and a “high priority will be action on artificial intelligence.”

Also on Sunday, Jeffries dismissed President Trump’s repeated promises to pay Americans $5,000 if Republicans win in November’s high-stakes midterm elections.

“It wasn't a serious proposal that was presented by Donald Trump. He's broken every promise that he's presented. Donald Trump promised to actually lower costs on day one. Costs haven't gone down. They've gone up,” Jeffries said Sunday.
