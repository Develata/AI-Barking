Key Takeaways by nexos.ai, reviewed by Cybernews staff.

A ban on AI superintelligence and a pause on advanced AI may be underway following the Democrats' midterm win.

Congresswoman Yassamin Ansari called the AI industry “out of control” and reiterated that AI must be put on pause to give the government ample time to safeguard the public.

The bill promises to pause AI advancement until appropriate regulations are put in place to prevent AI systems from outpacing human understanding and mutating into intelligence that can no longer be controlled.

Not only will this affect existing models, but the bill would also penalize “rogue actors” who attempt to create or possess AI superintelligence, an advanced form of AI that matches or exceeds human intelligence.

Anyone, including rogue actors and policymakers, who violates these laws should expect to be fined and face a maximum of 20 years in prison.

Researchers or developers who attempt to create superintelligence will be banned from working in the field for up to 10 years.

AI and data center concerns were partly responsible for the Democrats' wins, as 65% of people said that this was a crucial or major factor in who they voted for to lead Congress, according to NBC exit polls.

What is classed as advanced AI

If the bill passes, advanced artificial intelligence systems will be regulated based on the model's size, training data, and the amount of compute used.

While no AI company openly publishes how much compute was used to train its models, the AI research institute Epoch AI estimates that roughly 30 publicly announced models fall into or exceed the advanced AI category.

These may include top-end models from OpenAI, Meta, DeepSeek, SpaceXAI, and Anthropic, according to Epoch’s estimations.

This comes after various AI chiefs, including Anthropic’s Dario Amodei, OpenAI’s Sam Altman, SpaceXAI’s Elon Musk, and other major figures in AI, such as the “godfather of AI,” Geoffrey Hinton, warned that AI is advancing at such a rate that we can’t keep up.

While AI pros all over the world have warned of a potential apocalypse, experts like Meta’s former AI chief, Yann LeCun, have zero concerns about AI wiping out humanity.

LeCun went as far as to call the doomsday warnings from AI chiefs “the worst marketing campaign” yet.

Cybernews asked over 100 AI experts whether these claims are true. Almost 70% of researchers said that human misuse and the concentration of power by AI companies are the major causes for concern, not the AI itself.
