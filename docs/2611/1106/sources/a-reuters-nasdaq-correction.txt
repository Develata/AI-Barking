Nasdaq set to confirm correction as AI spending worries mount

By Utkarsh Hathi [/authors/utkarsh-hathi/] and Johann M Cherian [/authors/johann-m-cherian/]
November 5, 20263:19 PM UTCUpdated November 5, 2026

REUTERS/Shannon Stapleton/File Photo Purchase Licensing Rights, opens new tab [https://www.reutersconnect.com/item/screen-displays-trading-information-outside-the-nasdaq-market-site-in-new-york-city/dGFnOnJldXRlcnMuY29tLDIwMjY6bmV3c21sX1JDMlEzT0FXN0RLSA%3D%3D/?utm_medium=rcom-article-media&utm_campaign=rcom-rcp-lead]

Nov 5 (Reuters) - The Nasdaq slid more than 10% from its ​record close on Thursday, on track to confirm a correction as ‌worries over massive AI spending and heightened scrutiny of data-center expansion rattled tech stocks.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite Index (.IXIC), opens new tab [https://www.reuters.com/markets/quote/.IXIC] fell nearly 4% to 24,712.60, more than 10% below its record closing ​high of 27,599.89 reached on October 6.

Thursday's slide erased the previous session's ​gains after Democrats swept both houses of Congress, as investors ⁠weighed the prospect of greater scrutiny of further AI infrastructure buildout.

"When you go ​from full unified control under one party to full unified control under another ​party that can also represent quite a bit of uncertainty, discontinuity and abrupt change to policy," said Sameer Samana, senior global market strategist at Wells Fargo Investment Institute.

"When it looked like ​the Democrats were going to take significant majorities in both the House ​and the Senate, we thought that at the very least, it would lead to markets taking ‌a ⁠little bit of a pause."

A decline of 10% from the most recent record closing high is widely considered a correction. It would mark the Nasdaq's second correction this year.

Sentiment toward AI-linked stocks has deteriorated in recent weeks, with the ​Nasdaq shedding more than $2.5 ​trillion in market ⁠value in October. Several big tech companies are tapping debt markets to help finance billions of dollars in planned investments [https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/tech-companies-tap-debt-markets-fund-ai-cloud-expansion-2026-11-02/] in ​data centers, chips and other infrastructure required to support AI ​development.

Investors have ⁠increasingly questioned how quickly those investments will translate into profits, particularly as persistent geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and fears of prolonged inflation have kept borrowing ⁠costs elevated.

Earnings ​from hyperscalers Alphabet (GOOGL.O), opens new tab [https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/GOOGL.O] and Meta (META.O), opens new tab [https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/META.O] last month failed ​to reassure investors about the returns on AI spending, contributing to a broader selloff in AI-linked ​stocks.

Reporting by Utkarsh Hathi and Johann M Cherian in Bengaluru; Editing by Anil D'Silva
