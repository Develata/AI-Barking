Ansari Wants a Day-1 AI Pause; Her Bill Sets a 10^25 Line

AI Business Brief [https://fourweekmba.com/category/ai-business-brief/] / By 

 Gennaro Cuofano 
 [https://fourweekmba.com/author/thefourweekmbateam/]

 / November 5, 2026 

Rep. Yassamin Ansari, the Arizona Democrat whose re-election the Associated Press called on 3 November, wrote in a post on X [https://x.com/RepYassAnsari/status/2118054336077127797] at 18:54 UTC on 4 November: “Top of the Democratic agenda on day 1 needs [https://fourweekmba.com/maslows-hierarchy-of-needs/] to be a pause on advanced AI development, regulation of this out of control [https://fourweekmba.com/locus-of-control/] industry [https://fourweekmba.com/industry-types/], and a ban on artificial superintelligence.”

Her post does not name a bill. She is one of 10 House members named on H.R. 10538 [https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-119hr10538ih], the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act of 2026, as introduced on 24 September, by our count of the bill text. That bill defines the systems it would pause as those trained on 10^25 or more integer or floating point operations.

The bill she is named on would, by its text, bar training, modifying or fine-tuning systems trained on 10^25 or more operations, and bar deploying unreleased ones, until a new department is staffed and has issued rules; by our reading it has no provision on data centers.

What the Pause in Her Bill Covers

The bill was introduced by Rep. Greg Casar, and Ansari is listed among the members introducing it with him. It would create a Department of Artificial Intelligence. Section 8 says that, for a set period, each advanced artificial intelligence system “shall be subject to a mandatory pause” and that “no unreleased advanced artificial intelligence system may be deployed during this period”.

Under its definitions, a system under a mandatory pause “may not be trained, modified, or fine-tuned, including through recursive self-improvement”. The 10^25 threshold is adjusted each year by the Secretary to reflect gains in training efficiency.

The pause has no fixed end date. It runs from enactment until the Secretary determines that the Department is fully staffed and has issued rules covering pre-development plans, monitoring, auditing and “Final pre-deployment approval before any advanced artificial intelligence system is released to the public”. Casar’s release [https://casar.house.gov/media/press-releases/news-casar-sanders-introduce-legislation-create-new-federal-agency-ban] of 23 September describes it as “Pausing Advanced AI development until a new, federal AI regulatory body is up and running”.

Section 13 sets penalties. A policymaking individual at a chartered company, or a rogue actor, who recklessly violates the pause, the prohibitions or the charter rules faces a fine and up to 20 years in prison. By our reading of the introduced text, it contains no provision on data centers; the pause is on training and deploying models above the compute threshold.

The Structural Read

As we read the two texts side by side, her post and the bill use the same term, advanced AI development, and the bill defines it by a compute line rather than by naming companies.

As we read the 4 November coverage, two different pauses are now in the debate: local ballot limits on data centers, and a federal bill that pauses model [https://fourweekmba.com/what-is-a-business-model/] training above a compute threshold.

Three Implications

FOR FRONTIER AI LABS The bill applies to systems trained on 10^25 or more operations, a line the Secretary adjusts each year, and its rules must include final pre-deployment approval before any advanced system is released to the public.

FOR PEOPLE AT AI COMPANIES The bill sets fines and up to 20 years in prison for policymaking individuals or rogue actors who recklessly violate it, and a 10-year bar from the AI industry [https://fourweekmba.com/industry-types/] for other individuals.

FOR DATA CENTER BUILDERS By our reading of the introduced text, the bill has no provision on data centers; the local limits that lead in 40 of 41 ballot measures by our recount are a separate track.
