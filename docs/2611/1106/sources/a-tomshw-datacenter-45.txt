Several counties in the U.S. have put data center concerns on the midterm ballot, and early results show that Americans are overwhelmingly voting against them. According to Data Centers on the Ballot [https://datacentersontheballot.com/], 33 measures restricting data center development have already won, with six more questions leading. Only one measure in Harwood, North Dakota, which adds an extra review process for industrial projects, including data centers, has failed, while a further five have no posted results. This is a major setback for the Trump administration, which has been pushing for AI development in the U.S. [https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/president-trump-has-announced-plans-for-new-ai-force-and-ai-czar-amid-growing-ai-safety-concerns-new-unit-will-cherish-ai-and-not-stifle-it-trump-clarifies-while-dismissing-safety-warnings-as-hoaxes], but not a surprising result, as one database has shown that “America hates data centers [https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/america-hates-data-centers-new-database-reveals-more-than-90-percent-of-10-000-public-comments-are-negative-only-21-percent-mention-ai-73-percent-of-meetings-had-no-data-center-supporters-water-is-the-number-one-concern],” with polls showing that 70% of Americans oppose AI data center build-outs [https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/over-70-percent-of-americans-oppose-ai-data-centers-us-protests-intensify-as-more-arrests-are-being-made-almost-40-arrested-this-year-in-backlash-to-ai-factory-buildout].

👨&zwj;💻 THREAD on how data center-related ballot measures are faring tonight
data centers are on the ballot in ~40 localities across 13 states, including proposed bans, votes on specific projects, and recalls of pro-data center politiciansNovember 4, 2026 [https://twitter.com/cantworkitout/status/2117786709173645427]

Note that the issues put to a vote are varied, ranging from outright permanent data center bans to moratoriums, time-limited actions, limitations based on size or consumption, and more. The mid-term elections also resulted in the recall of the mayor and two council members in Piqua, Ohio, and the mayor of Yukon, Oklahoma, for previously supporting data center bans. A second tracker of some 46 data center measures across 10 states reveals that of 33 measures to limit data centers, every single one is set to be passed. Of a further 13 positive measures to permit data centers and their construction, 11 look set to be defeated.

Despite the overwhelming opposition to AI data centers, it’s unclear if they will affect projects that are already underway. In fact, out of the 300-plus local data center bans [https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/300-plus-local-data-center-bans-have-only-delayed-just-three-us-projects-bans-much-more-bark-than-bite-only-2-3-gigawatts-of-data-centers-are-held-up-versus-38-gigawatts-expected-online-in-2027] enacted across the U.S., only three developments totaling 2.3 gigawatts have been delayed, with 38 gigawatts expected to come online by 2027, and that investment into the infrastructure is still on track to hit $32 trillion by 2050 [https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/local-opposition-blocked-usd68-billion-worth-of-data-center-projects-in-the-second-quarter-of-2026-data-center-investments-reportedly-still-on-track-to-hit-usd32-trillion-by-2050]. Nevertheless, these votes could affect future developments, especially as the U.S. Senate accuses Big Tech [https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/senate-investigation-says-that-some-ai-data-center-claims-are-misleading-senators-question-number-of-permanent-jobs-projects-bring-to-communities-but-companies-refuse-to-divulge-data] of misleading the public on the advantages of AI data centers. It’s also the reason why OpenAI and Anthropic are scrambling for small data center projects [https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/openai-and-anthropic-are-reportedly-seeking-out-smaller-data-center-deals-to-meet-current-demand-20-30-mw-facilities-to-provide-capacity-as-mega-structures-undergo-construction] that range in capacity from 20 to 30 megawatts, especially as their hyperscale gigawatt sites face delays.

The major problem with building power hungry data centers in space is that you're purely limited by the amount of solar panels you can put on it. Plus you're also limited by the amount of heat you can dissipate. Since there's no medium to transfer heat away from a device other than thermal radiation, which is a slow process even though it's like -455F in space. There's no air or water or anything to absorb heat and take it away from the satellite. So it would just sit there baking in its own heat unless you build massive surface radiators with liquid pumps to move the heat out of the core and into the radiators spread all over the surface of the ship. 

In the end, Musk's idea to put data centers in space is just completely impractical unless you use a radio isotope generator (the "nuclear battery" Voyager 1 & 2 are powered by) to produce the baseline power you need along with solar panels. So that adds even more heat into the equation to dissipate, not to mention now you have plutonium floating around in space that could come crashing back down and spread nuclear material over a small area. Still, I wouldn't want one of these massive places in my back yard either.
