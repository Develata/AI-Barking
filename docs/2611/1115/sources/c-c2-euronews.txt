 Eurozone funds have bought heavily into the technology, leaving European savers exposed if the boom turns to bust in the US.

The European Central Bank (ECB) has warned that investors could suffer heavy losses if confidence in artificial intelligence falters.

 ADVERTISEMENT

 ADVERTISEMENT

Speaking in Frankfurt, Germany, on Monday, ECB vice-president Boris Vujčić said the “rapid expansion of AI is creating new challenges for financial stability” in the 21-country eurozone.

"A reassessment of the AI narrative may trigger an abrupt repricing in markets and significant valuation losses," he said.

Vujčić noted that the amount raised through new stock market listings, measured against the size of the US economy, is on track to exceed the peak of the dot-com boom of the late 1990s. "Historically, such surges have often coincided with market peaks, pointing to the risk of excessive investor optimism," he said.

His warning comes days before the AI company Anthropic makes its stock market debut [https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/16/five-things-you-need-to-know-before-anthropic-goes-public]. Shares in the Claude maker are expected to start trading on Nasdaq on Thursday. The listing could be the second-largest in history, raising about $61bn (€55bn) and valuing the company at about $1.2 trillion (€1.1 trillion).

 Related

Five things you need to know before Anthropic goes public [/2026/11/16/five-things-you-need-to-know-before-anthropic-goes-public]

Markets have already been unsettled by the resignation of Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang after a scandal, and by the US midterm elections. Vujčić said the market falls after the elections, in which Democrats won both chambers of Congress, were a reminder to be cautious about AI valuations.

Major US tech companies are projected to spend a combined $5.5 trillion (€5 trillion) on capital investments by 2030. Vujčić said a sharp fall in AI valuations would hit the eurozone hard because the region’s investment funds have become more exposed to the sector.

Government debt

The ECB also pointed to high public debt [https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/key/date/2026/html/ecb.sp261116~db374b56ab.en.html] and weak public finances in some eurozone countries. With long-term borrowing costs already rising, investors could suddenly demand much more to lend to those governments. Hedge funds help keep government bonds trading smoothly but can quickly unwind their positions when markets are under stress.

Non-bank lenders

The third risk comes from the fast growth of private lenders and investment funds. Some can borrow heavily, be hard to monitor and have close ties to the rest of the financial system, all of which can amplify shocks.

The ECB wants funds’ rules on withdrawals to better reflect how quickly they can sell their assets. It also wants regulators to consider limits on how much hedge funds can borrow.

Vujčić warned that all three risks could hit at the same time. Eurozone banks, by contrast, have strong capital and liquidity positions, he said.
