Anthropic’s 7 founders cashed out nearly $2B in record-breaking IPO

 Julie Bort [https://techcrunch.com/author/julie-bort/]

4:43 PM PST · November 19, 2026

 [https://www.facebook.com/sharer.php?u=https%3A%2F%2Ftechcrunch.com%2F2026%2F11%2F19%2Fanthropics-7-founders-cashed-out-nearly-2b-in-record-breaking-ipo%2F]

 [https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropics-7-founders-cashed-out-nearly-2b-in-record-breaking-ipo/]

Anthropic became a public company on Thursday, raising a total of $81.3 billion, including $1.95 billion for its seven founders. As we previously reported, each of them ponied up about 3.4 million shares apiece [https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/all-7-anthropic-founders-will-each-cash-out-about-250m-in-ipo/] to sell in the IPO.

At the upgraded IPO price of $83 per share, that means they each pocketed about $280 million before underwriting fees and taxes. It looked like they had calculated the number of shares they planned to sell to give them each around $250 million if shares hit $74. That was the midpoint price from Anthropic’s original price range. So that’s a cool $30-ish million more for each of them than perhaps they were gunning for.

Anthropic implied in its IPO filings that the founders aren’t entirely in this for the money though. The company said that each of the founders has pledged to dedicate 80% of their personal equity in the company to philanthropy. [https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropics-ipo-could-deliver-156-billion-to-charity/]

However, if any of them struggle to live on their quarter of a billion, they can scale back their charitable commitments. The company points out that these commitments are “voluntary, revocable expressions of each co-founder’s philanthropic intent and do not constitute legally binding obligations.”

With the IPO in the record books, there were a few other interesting numbers worth highlighting.

$104.8 billion. That’s really the amount Anthropic raised for its coffers (with a possibility of $117 billion, if the bankers buy all of their allotment of shares). In addition to selling its Class A common stock, it also sold a previously announced $26 billion worth of a special non-voting Class N stock to some of the biggest tech names in the industry.

Nvidia bought $10 billion of these Class N shares, Google bought $10 billion, and AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, and TSMC divvied up the rest.

Just for comparison: When Facebook had its then record-breaking IPO in 2012, it raised $16 billion on IPO day. [https://archive.nytimes.com/dealbook.nytimes.com/2012/05/17/facebook-raises-16-billion-in-i-p-o/]

28% stake to Amazon and Google combined. All told, at the higher IPO share price, Google wound up owning 13.4% of the company. Amazon also now has a big stake in Anthropic because when it previously invested in the AI lab as a startup, [https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/amazon-and-google-will-together-own-nearly-30-of-anthropic-after-its-ipo/] it was granted promissory notes that could be converted to equity. It converted a small percentage of them as part of the IPO, leaving it with a 14.6% stake. Had it converted all of them, Amazon could have owned nearly 24%.

The two large cloud providers now own 28% of Anthropic, the AI lab says.

More than $1 trillion. As mind-blowing as it is to raise nearly $105 billion, giving Anthropic nearly $230 billion of working capital available (along with capital it already had on hand), the company’s need for future capital is still far greater.

Anthropic needs about $70.7 billion just to cover its compute and infrastructure spending commitments in the next 12 months as of the quarter ended September 30, 2026. It needs $451.6 billion to cover these commitments before the end of 2030. And it has racked up hundreds of billions of other commitments that are either conditional (and may not happen) or haven’t been priced yet. 

All told, the company has disclosed a ballpark of $1.05 trillion to $1.08 trillion in infrastructure commitments in its foreseeable future. This was a huge IPO. But it is a pricey business to run.
