TL;DR

Signal is introducing protections to prevent Gemini and Siri AI from scanning private conversations on Android and iOS 27.

The protections also block users from taking screenshots inside Signal, but they can be disabled.

Signal is calling on Google, Apple, and Microsoft to give app developers better tools to restrict AI access without compromising app functionality.

AI assistants are increasingly doing things on our phones that would previously require us to open an app ourselves. Gemini can already order food and book rides using third-party Android apps [https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-galaxy-s26-pixel-10-control-other-apps-3643939/], and just yesterday, Google started rolling out similar computer-use capabilities on Macs [https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-mac-computer-use-3737396/]. Meanwhile, Apple’s Siri AI can understand what’s on your iPhone’s screen. But what happens when the app an AI assistant tries to read contains information that was never meant to leave it?

That’s a question Signal is tackling head-on. The encrypted messaging service announced [https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/] today that it is introducing protections against AI assistants capturing its content on Android and iOS 27.

On Android, Signal will automatically enable its Screen security setting when it detects an AI assistant attempting to read your screen. On iOS 27, Signal has introduced a Block Siri AI setting. Both measures also prevent users from taking screenshots inside the app, although you can turn off these protections in Signal’s settings.

Signal faced a similar problem last year when it blocked Microsoft’s Recall feature [https://signal.org/blog/signal-doesnt-recall/] from taking screenshots of conversations on Windows 11. Even disappearing messages could potentially outlive their intended lifespan in Recall’s searchable database.

The concern is that although Signal’s end-to-end encryption protects messages in transit, it cannot protect their contents once an AI assistant captures them directly from your screen. Remember, Gemini can already automatically analyze your screen [https://www.androidauthority.com/gemini-screen-context-3625824/] without requiring you to manually share a screenshot when asking about on-screen content.

However, Signal acknowledges that its solution is incomplete. It cannot reliably implement similar protections on macOS or cover every Android device. Blocking screenshots also does nothing to prevent AI agents from potentially accessing sensitive information through other means, such as local files. This explains why Signal wants Google, Apple, and Microsoft to give developers better documentation and tools to block AI access without sacrificing existing app functionality.

Even if you don’t use Signal, consider what this could mean for your banking, healthcare, or workplace apps. As AI assistants become more useful by gaining access to more of our digital lives, developers need equally capable tools to determine where that access ends.
