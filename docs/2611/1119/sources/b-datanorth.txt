Signal now blocks screenshots to keep Siri AI and Gemini out of your chats, except on the Mac

20-11-2026
Home [https://datanorth.ai]/News [https://datanorth.ai/news]/Signal now blocks screenshots to keep Siri AI and Gemini out of your chats, except on the Mac

Signal now blocks screenshots on iPhone and Android so AI assistants like Siri AI can't capture your chats. It's on by default, but not on the Mac.

Written by:

 [https://datanorth.ai/blog/author/jorick]

Jorick van Weelie

Marketing Lead at DataNorth | AI Enthusiast & Tech Storyteller

Sign up for our Newsletter

 Table of Contents

Signal is restricting screenshots in its iPhone and Android apps. It wants to stop AI assistants built into the operating system, such as Apple’s Siri AI and Google’s Gemini Intelligence, from capturing and storing your chats. Signal announced the change on 20 November and switches the protection on by default. It cannot do the same on the Mac, and it calls its own fix incomplete.

On iOS 27 screenshots stop, on Android the block waits for an AI assistant

On iOS 27, Signal prevents screenshots inside the app and adds a setting called Block Siri AI. It targets Siri AI’s onscreen awareness, which Apple describes as answering questions or taking actions based on what is on your screen.

On Android, Signal takes a lighter route. It switches on its existing Screen security setting as soon as it detects an AI assistant trying to read the screen. Signal says it chose detection so that screenshots would not simply stop working and confuse people. It took a similar step on Windows 11 last year, where it blocks Microsoft’s Recall by default.

The reason is how these agents collect information. “In order to gain access to information about you, these OS AI agents ‘scan’ the information that’s on your screen,” writes Signal president Meredith Whittaker. “All of this is outside of Signal’s control once the data has been captured.” Signal also names third-party agents such as Meta’s Muse, which reach other apps through the accessibility permissions people grant during setup. Reuters reported earlier this week that Muse could reach Signal messages on a Mac and on an Android phone. Meta told Reuters that Muse only accesses other apps through permissions users explicitly grant.

Can you still take screenshots in Signal?

Yes, if you switch the protection off in Signal’s settings: Block Siri AI on iOS, or Screen security under Privacy on Android. Signal warns that doing so “will potentially expose your (and your contacts’) Signal content to these AI agents.” The choice on one phone therefore affects everyone in the conversation. On the iPhone, teams that save chat screenshots as a record of decisions will notice the change first.

The Mac stays open, and screenshots are not the only way in

Signal says it cannot reliably block screenshots on macOS, because Apple offers no clearly documented way for apps to do so. Its advice for macOS 27 users is to add Signal to Excluded Apps under System Settings > Siri. Detection on Android has gaps too: on many manufacturers’ versions of Android, Signal cannot tell when a screenshot was attempted. And blocking screenshots does nothing against an agent that reads messages already stored on the device through the file system.

For readers in Europe, the iPhone switch matters less for now. Apple says Siri AI is not available in the EU on iOS, iPadOS and watchOS. The Mac is not on that list, and it is exactly where Signal has no protection to offer.

Signal wants Apple, Google and Microsoft to give every app an off switch for AI

Whittaker calls the situation “an existential threat to Signal and our ability to responsibly uphold our mission.” Signal asks the three operating system makers for two things. Apps should get an easy, documented way to opt out of AI data access, covering screen reading, file access, notifications and other system routes. And the makers should publish how their agents access and process data.

“Signal is the canary in the coal mine, but this affects the entire ecosystem,” Whittaker writes. Banking, health and legal apps hold the same kind of data. According to Signal, no major operating system gives developers an easy way to restrict AI access today.

Screen-reading agents need an app list before they reach work laptops

For organisations, Signal’s move is a warning in two directions. Apps that hold confidential data will start refusing system-level agents, so agent workflows that depend on reading the screen can run into a blank window. And an agent that reads the screen sees whatever the employee sees: client chats, HR files, a password manager left open.

Our advice is to settle access before rollout, not after. Before Siri AI, Gemini on the Mac, Recall or Muse lands on company devices, decide which apps an agent may see. Exclude chat, HR and finance apps from the start. On Apple devices, the Excluded Apps list in the Siri settings does this today. Where an agent needs business data, give it a connector or API with set permissions instead of letting it read the screen. The opt-out Signal asks for depends on Apple, Google and Microsoft, so plan without it.

For more information, visit the official announcement of Signal’s screenshot protections on the Signal blog [https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/].
