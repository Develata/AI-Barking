Signal Restricts Screenshots to protect User Privacy from AI assistants

 [https://cybersecuritynews.com/author/guru/]
By Guru Baran [https://cybersecuritynews.com/author/guru/]

November 20, 2026

Signal is restricting screenshots on iOS 27 and some Android devices to stop built-in AI assistants from reading private chats through screen capture.

The encrypted messaging app is targeting Apple’s Siri AI and assistants such as Google Gemini. On Android, the block turns on when Signal detects an assistant trying to read the screen.

The change addresses what happens after a message arrives. End-to-end encryption protects messages as they travel between devices, but Signal must show readable text when someone opens a chat.

An assistant that can read the screen does not have to break that encryption to see the message. It can capture the same text the recipient sees.

On iOS 27, Signal’s “Block Siri AI” setting prevents Siri from scanning Signal content and blocks screenshots inside the app and in the App Switcher.

On Android, Signal automatically turns on “Screen security” when it detects an assistant seeking screen access. Signal says detection varies across Android versions, so the protection does not work the same way on every phone.

The move follows Signal’s 2025 decision to block screenshots by default in its Windows 11 desktop app to keep chats out of Microsoft Recall’s searchable history. Other developers took a different route. As Cyber Security News reported, Brave blocked Microsoft Recall while preserving regular screenshots [https://cybersecuritynews.com/brave-browser-blocks-microsoft-recall/].

Signal Restricts Screenshots

Apple says Siri’s onscreen awareness lets it answer questions or act on what users are viewing. Google’s Gemini help page says some screen actions upload visible content to give the assistant context.

That does not mean either assistant routinely records every Signal chat. The risk is that once an assistant captures a message, Signal cannot control how that copy is handled.

Users can turn off the protection in Signal’s privacy settings to take screenshots again, but doing so can also expose messages from other people in the chat. The Electronic Frontier Foundation points to a separate option on iOS 27.2: open Settings → Siri → Excluded Apps and add Signal to block Siri’s access, including on-screen awareness.

Screenshot blocking does not cover every route to private data. Signal says it cannot offer the same protection on macOS and warns that an assistant may still reach content already saved on a device.

In related coverage, Meta’s Muse read Signal message text through Android notifications and Mac permissions [https://cybersecuritynews.com/muse-reads-encrypted-messages/] without breaking Signal’s encryption.

Signal is asking Apple, Google and Microsoft to give developers a clear way to opt out of AI access to screens, files and notifications without disabling ordinary features such as screenshots. It also wants better documentation about what assistants can read and where that data goes.

The distinction matters for anyone sharing sensitive information. A screenshot block can close one route into a private chat, but it cannot decide what a phone shows in a notification or what permissions another app receives. Those controls sit with the operating system, which is why Signal is pressing platform makers for stronger limits.
