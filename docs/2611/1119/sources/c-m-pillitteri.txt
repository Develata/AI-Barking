Gestalt is an artificial intelligence model with no declared maker, ranked first on the Frontier Design Benchmark with 1,931 Elo points. On November 18, Contra Labs and BenchFlow put it ahead of the human reference designers (1,739) and Claude Fable 5.2 (1,713), without saying who built it.

Contra is a creative work marketplace, where clients post briefs and designers carry them out and get paid by the platform. Contra Labs is the arm that evaluates AI models alongside the professionals in its network (more than two million creatives, according to the benchmark blog [https://contralabs.com/frontierdesign/blog]). BenchFlow [https://benchflow.ai], the partner company, runs the agents in isolated environments and records the output, cost and trajectory of every attempt.

Elo ranking of the Frontier Design Benchmark, values from the official Contra Labs page (November 2026)

What the Frontier Design Benchmark is

The Frontier Design Benchmark is a ranking that measures how well AI models build websites and apps from real client briefs, judged blind by professional designers. At launch it counted 33 projects, 35 judges and 4,311 votes, collected over five rounds.

Of the 33 briefs, 27 come from real jobs posted on Contra and rewritten for made-up clients, the other six from partner startups that supplied their logo, images and old site. They range from a festival portal with ticket sales to a museum audio guide, from a crowdfunding campaign checkout to a six-page site for a cybersecurity company. The team chose to start there because the median of the 4,600 web jobs it reviewed is a 138-word brief with a $1,050 budget, and three out of four impose at least two constraints, across brand, audience and business goals. The usual "build a landing page for a coffee shop" measures very little.

The models ran through OpenRouter in BenchFlow's sandboxes and, where possible, inside the harness (the agent's execution environment) of their own lab, a detail that matters because switching harness moves the score by 24 points [/en/news/30849/harness-leaderboard-coding-agent-harness-ranking] in tests on coding agents. To set the human bar, Contra's designers prepared three moodboards, built the site and polished the work after the team's comments. Those versions, the "golden" ones, enter the ranking like any other contestant.

Judges see two sites for the same brief, without knowing who made them, pick the better one and can explain why. From the votes Contra derives an Elo score with the Bradley-Terry statistical model. Splitting the designers into two random halves and recomputing the ranking 200 times, the two rankings agree with a Spearman correlation of 0.95. Alongside the votes run two other measures, a rubric with more than thirty design criteria (type hierarchy, spacing, contrast) and a detector of "slop", that is, traits of a page made by AI.

Among the models with a name attached, the top spot stays with Claude Fable 5.2 [/en/news/31474/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-launch-price], the same one that earlier this month beat Opus 5.5 in six tests out of seven [/en/news/32638/fable-5-2-beats-opus-5-5-roberts-test]. None of them, though, outdoes the flesh-and-blood designers. The human sites win 74% of matchups (425 votes out of 574) despite execution flaws the rubric punishes, like a button label that is too dark or a logotype cut off by the section above.

What Contra says about Gestalt

Contra describes Gestalt as a high-quality design model "currently in stealth", that is, presented without the name of the lab behind it, and calls it the first to land above the human references. The ranking was launched by Ben, from Contra, in a post on X [https://x.com/contraben/status/2123144312494178479] that opens by saying the AI industry has a benchmarking problem.

The blog's numbers, written at launch, give Gestalt 1,928 points and 89% of votes (378 out of 423). The live ranking has already climbed to 1,931 and 90%, so the two Contra pages differ by three points because of the update. When judges put a Gestalt site next to a human golden, they preferred Gestalt 20 times out of 28.

The slop detector says something else. On a scale from 0 to 100 (the higher it is, the more the page resembles those made by AI) the human goldens score 0.9, Gestalt 1.2, the three Claudes between 31 and 38, the two GPTs between 41 and 53, the two Geminis between 54 and 57. Contra explains that Gestalt sits at the bottom of the scale because its pages are finished and do not fall into the typical tics, like the small-caps microcopy label above every headline (93% of the AI pages in the training sample, against 11% of the human ones) or the numbered sections 01, 02, 03. The benchmark also records the signatures of individual labs. Horizontal arrows (→) on links are a habit of the Claude models, diagonal ones (↗) of the GPTs.

Limitations to keep in mind

The party doing the measuring also sells services on the topic. Contra invites labs to contact its team for real briefs, goldens and designer votes, so a first place no third party has replicated should be read as a result declared by the ranking's operator.

Gestalt's sites cannot be viewed. The public brief gallery shows the outputs of named models, and Gestalt's are not there, so without seeing a single one of its projects readers have no way to tell what the 35 judges actually rewarded.

The comparison with humans rests on few votes. The 20 wins out of 28 are a small sample, picked by a closed panel of judges tied to the Contra network, and a result like that can swing quite a bit with a few dozen more votes.

The cost is missing. In the cost-quality chart Fable 5.2 appears at $127.22 per site produced (a lower-bound estimate) and Gestalt has no point, so there is no telling whether its edge comes cheap or very expensive.

Who is behind Gestalt?

Contra is not saying. The ranking's operator names no maker, release date, price or way to try it, and only mentions ongoing work with the model's team to assess its quality and gather feedback.

Guesses are flying around social media. As Die KI Woche [https://kiwoche.com/1500/stealth-modell-gestalt-frontier-design-benchmark-contra-labs/] sums it up, some think of an unreleased Anthropic model, perhaps held back for the IPO of these days [/en/news/34957/anthropic-ipo-priced-83-dollars-raises-81-billion], others of Meta, others of a Chinese lab. None of the three leads has a source, and a score is not enough to pin down the maker.

In August the stealth model Ox Alpha was unmasked by its tokenizer, which pointed to GLM-5.3 [/en/news/12473/ox-alpha-mistaken-for-gemini-glm-5-3] and not to Gemini as first claimed. That kind of analysis, however, requires direct access to the model, while for Gestalt there is no public access and only the designers' votes remain. Its identity stays an open question and, in the end, only a lab choosing to claim it can settle it.

What changes for people who build sites and design

Today, next to nothing. Gestalt cannot be used, and anyone picking a model to build sites faces the same names as yesterday, with Fable 5.2 [/en/news/31474/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2-launch-price] on top among the known models and a bill that, in the benchmark, tops a hundred dollars for every site produced.

The more useful finding lies elsewhere. Designers picked the human site in 74% of cases even when the rubric, which adds up technical errors, ranked it only sixth out of sixteen (73.6 points). Taste does not reduce to a checklist, and Gestalt's edge sits precisely where the checklist does not reach. Its pages, Contra says, look like they were made by a person.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

1. How does the benchmark's Elo score work?

Short answer. The Frontier Design Benchmark's Elo score comes from designers' votes on pairwise matchups, turned into a rating with the Bradley-Terry statistical model. Winning often, especially against strong opponents, pushes the score up; for Gestalt a 90% win rate is worth 1,931 points, for Fable 5.2 73% is worth 1,713.

2. Why do Gestalt's numbers differ between the blog and the ranking?

Short answer. Because the ranking updates live and the blog captures the moment of launch. The launch post reports 1,928 points and an 89% win rate, the leaderboard page 1,931 and 90%, and with each new round of votes the gap may change again.

3. What is the Slop Detector and what does it measure?

Short answer. It is a classifier trained on pages made by people and pages made by AI, which assigns each site a value from 0 to 100. The number indicates the share of AI-generated pages it most resembles and comes with the traits that pushed it up, like the microcopy labels above headlines or a card for every block of text.

4. Where can the sites produced by the models be seen?

Short answer. In the benchmark gallery [https://contralabs.com/frontierdesign], which for each brief sets the human golden beside the outputs of 15 models. There are also blind matchups to vote on, but Gestalt's projects do not appear to be published.

What to watch next

Three signals will tell whether Gestalt is a breakthrough or a showcase. The first, the publication of its sites in the gallery next to everyone else's. The second, the arrival of a name, with a lab claiming it and the terms of use. The third, a cost per site that allows a comparison with Fable 5.2's $127. Until then it remains a record score with a faceless maker, and the full ranking can be read on the leaderboard page [https://contralabs.com/frontierdesign/leaderboard].
