Architect Labs Frostbite: A CPU Designed by AI Agents Claims 4.59x Better Performance per Watt

Architect Labs introduced Frostbite, a general-purpose CPU built on interaction nets that it says AI agents discovered, designed and verified end to end. In pre-silicon tests on 25 workloads it reached 4.59 times the performance per watt of a comparable conventional core.

i10x Perspective [#i10x-perspective]

A startup says its AI agents designed a new kind of CPU largely on their own. Architect Labs introduced Frostbite on 18 November 2026, a general-purpose processor with a new instruction set that it says was “discovered, designed, and verified end-to-end by AI.” In the company’s pre-silicon tests, one Frostbite cluster reached 4.59 times the performance per watt of a comparable conventional CPU core across 25 workloads.

i10X Quick Take
Quick Take

What it is: a general-purpose CPU built around interaction nets, a model of computation where every step is local and every value has exactly one owner.

Built by agents: Architect Labs says its AI systems iterated autonomously over the compiler, instruction set, architecture, microarchitecture, RTL, verification and final layout (GDS).

Results: 4.59x performance per watt on average across 25 workloads: 6.02x on the 10 it was tuned on and 3.83x on 15 held-out workloads. Performance per area averaged 8.77x.

Process: synthesized on a 7 nm process, 800 MHz at synthesis and 500 MHz in the final layout.

Caveat: these are pre-silicon numbers against a dual-issue, in-order reference core; no chip has been manufactured yet.

In plain English

Normal CPUs spend a lot of energy figuring out which instructions depend on each other and keeping big shared caches in sync. Frostbite runs programs written so those dependencies are explicit from the start, which lets many small tiles work in parallel with their own local memory. The startup let swarms of AI agents explore and build the whole design, then checked their work with formal proofs.

What happened

Architect Labs, the trade name of Architect Silicon Inc., set out to answer whether “our AI systems & tools lead to a novel hardware discovery that radically outperforms human-made designs.” It previously showed an AI-built inference accelerator called Redwood.

According to the company, agents produced a 141-page architecture proposal, built a software model of the chip, then moved to a formal specification language it calls ASL that translates to the Lean theorem prover. By the end, the Frostbite specification lowered to more than 800,000 lines of Lean. Across four search rounds and a final set of ablations, agent swarms tried 58 ideas and kept 37.

One feature, the company says, emerged without human direction: tiles run their own work first, steal work from a busy neighbor if idle, and otherwise go to sleep to save power. “This behavior was never specified by any human,” Architect Labs wrote.

How it works

Programming: programs are written in Vine, a Rust-like language, and compiled to an interaction net intermediate representation called Ivy.

Hardware: a grid of tiles, each with local memory, a hardware work queue and a frame manager that wakes the next step when parallel tasks finish.

Comparison: one Frostbite quad with four L3 nodes measures 0.792 mm², against 0.895 mm² for the reference core on the same process.

Energy: excluding clock distribution, the reference core spends 57% of its energy on memory and 27% on fetch, decode and issue; Frostbite cuts those to 38% and 6%, and spends 26% on its on-chip network and scheduler.

Why it matters

For chip design, it is a concrete claim that AI agents can explore architectures humans would not try, not only speed up existing design flows. OpenAI made a related claim this week when it said its models found up to 14% gains on AMD’s flagship GPU design [https://i10x.ai/news/openai-chip-design-amd-gpu-14-percent-ppa-jalapeno].

For AI infrastructure, if agent-designed chips hold up in silicon, the time and cost to build specialized processors could fall sharply.

For developers, the trade-off is software: Frostbite’s gains depend on programs written for interaction nets, not existing C or Python code.

What’s still open

Real silicon results; all reported numbers are pre-silicon.

How Frostbite compares with larger out-of-order and multi-core CPUs. Architect Labs says SPEC and PARSEC benchmarks against such designs are coming in the next months.

How much human guidance went into setting goals, checking results and choosing workloads.

i10x Perspective

The benchmark gains are impressive, but the process is the real story: a formal specification as the single source of truth so agent swarms cannot quietly cut corners. That pattern of agents plus machine-checkable contracts could matter well beyond chips. More in our Infrastructure section [https://i10x.ai/news/category/infrastructure].
