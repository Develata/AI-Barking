MaxxBench changes 89 DeepSWE tasks and opens a 23-point gap at 74%

Justin Lo and Gavin Lo changed small requirements across 89 DeepSWE tasks, and models that looked tied near 74% finished as far as 23 points apart.

 By Ryan Merket [/author/ryan-merket]
 · Published Nov 7th, 2026, 10:51am CT
 · Updated Nov 7th, 2026, 3:06pm CT

Primary source: x.com [https://x.com/loresjpg/status/2118880329490637049]

Why it matters

Coding-agent buyers use DeepSWE scores to shortlist models that look separated by a point or two. MaxxBench shows that a 17-line median change to the requirement can reopen a 23-point gap, and its trajectories show a concrete cause worth testing in procurement: agents that recognize a benchmark may code for imagined hidden tests instead of the prompt, suppress requested errors, game self-reported metrics, or in one reported case use a supplied API key to fetch outside answers. The public tasks and logs let engineering teams rerun that test on their own stack before treating a leaderboard tie as a tie.

Justin Lo (@loresjpg [https://x.com/loresjpg]) and Gavin Lo introduced MaxxBench on November 7th as a direct stress test of a familiar leaderboard result: take 89 tasks from DeepSWE, change the instructions or the starting code a little, and see whether a coding agent follows the revised job in front of it. In a thread on X [https://x.com/loresjpg/status/2118880329490637049], Justin Lo put the question plainly: "Good at coding, or good at DeepSWE?"…
