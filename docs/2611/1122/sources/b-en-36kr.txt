Why cryptanalysis?

Where exactly does the gold content of MiniMax's breakthrough this time lie?

The answer is — cryptanalysis is a brutal testing ground for large models.

As a well-known KOL said: "Cryptography tests are extremely brutal, but that's exactly why I like them. Either your script recovers the key, or it doesn't."

When we look for software vulnerabilities in daily work, many times we are looking for bugs written by programmers in the code.

But cryptanalysis is different, it is a "open card game".

The mathematical formulas, algebraic structures and implementation source code of modern cryptographic algorithms are all public.

It is like a transparent complex gear set, you know the meshing method of every gear, but the security boundary is locked by the terrifying "computational complexity".

Without the correct key, even if you call the global computing power to brute force exhaustive search for ten thousand years, you cannot crack it forcibly.

For large models to break through it, just giving an idea is not enough.

It must complete the leap from theory to engineering —

Convert hundreds of steps of high-order mathematical derivation into practically runnable attack codes, and calculate the answer within limited computing power and time.

This zero-tolerance, absolutely objective standard is the highest touchstone to test "whether AI can carry out scientific exploration".

Tearing through human blind spots: Achieving the "first kill" in the NIST competition

In the cryptography community, the cryptography standardization competition held by NIST is like the "Huashan Sword Debate" in martial arts novels.

Algorithms that can survive to the final rounds have been reviewed repeatedly by top cryptographers around the world for many years.

In order to test whether large models are "memorizing old knowledge in the training set", ETH Zurich and Anthropic jointly developed a special evaluation benchmark called CryptanalysisBench.

In the hell-level question bank Tier 2, 142 candidate schemes that have been publicly evaluated by human experts and currently have "no known practical attack methods" in the academic circle are included.

As a result, MIM directly carried out a "dimensionality reduction strike" against human experts!

Take Waterfall-256, a candidate algorithm for the NIST SHA-3 hash competition as an example.

Since its birth, no one has ever really cracked this algorithm, that is, constructed a "collision message pair" that makes it crash.

After MIM came on the stage, in a zero-prompt environment, the single-core CPU took only 0.045 seconds to directly generate the first real collision instance of this algorithm.

From theory to practice, AI instantly surpassed several years of human progress.

The most typical examples are NIST post-quantum cryptography candidate schemes LOCKER-I and ROLLO-I.

For example, LOCKER-I needs to construct a matrix with 5.72×10^8 columns, which consumes a huge amount of memory up to 42 PB (about 43000 TB). In the past, this was an insurmountable computing power gap!

However, MIM showed amazing intuition, cleverly processed the coefficient matrix, and directly unlocked the hidden attributes.

The brilliant algebraic operation discovered by MIM: By transposing the coefficient matrix, it unlocks the partial Frobenius mapping and locks the unknown space.

After a series of operations, the super-large matrix that originally required 42 PB of memory was extremely compressed by MIM to 58 MB!

With 12 threads, cracking LOCKER-I only takes 1.15 seconds; for the 2019 version of ROLLO-I-128, MIM requires less than 2 MB of memory, and the single-core time consumption is only 5.97 seconds.

This is pure intelligence crushing.

There is also an episode: When reviewing the official reference code of DRS, the NIST first-round lattice signature scheme, MIM caught a tiny but fatal error: When writing the signature verification logic, human experts mistakenly wrote the logical AND (&&) as bitwise AND (&).

Just this one character difference allowed MIM to bypass the security check directly, and forged the highest-privilege signature within 0.1 to 0.8 seconds.
