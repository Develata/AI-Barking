AHM Statement on the Announced Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Today, November 20, 2026, a mathematician at Anthropic announced the existence of an AI-generated Lean program confirming the Riemann Hypothesis, long a guiding open problem in mathematics.

This announcement is a major departure from traditional methods in mathematical research, representing a new value system put forward by AI corporations that foregrounds technical validity of logical statements over accountability, clarity, accessibility, and understanding. At this time, no human being can reasonably claim to be responsible for, or even to understand, such a document. Neither can any mathematician confirm with full certainty that the proof is correct. Worse, this corporate model of proof production seems designed to excuse humans from responsibility for scientific work. Instead, this model asks scientists to entrust their research to machines that AI corporations control.

Autonomous computer programs that investigate deep mathematical questions are now instruments of power in scientific research, and scientific power enables other types of power that can directly threaten human lives. This power is now concentrated in the hands of AI companies that seem increasingly untethered from the interests of human society. We believe that AI corporations urgently require democratic oversight. Milestones such as the Riemann Hypothesis are used by AI companies as little more than publicity for the superintelligence that they wish to build. We are gravely concerned that this uncontrolled growth poses a threat to society writ large.

Mathematicians have seen their world inverted by AI disruption in the last several months, and we have experienced firsthand how AI impacts human communities in practice. AI corporations hold an impoverished view of intelligence and human achievement. As a model for scientific research, the overproduction of unreadable mathematical documents pales in comparison to the richness of the mathematical dialogue that humans have conducted for millennia. What makes the Riemann Hypothesis valuable is its role in guiding the development of large areas of modern mathematics. Today’s announcement builds upon over a century of human work, but fails to advance mathematical understanding in the same way. Rather than accepting this model, we urge mathematicians to build a future where our research is free from dependence on AI models and their corporate owners.

Association for Human Mathematics
Communications Working Group

AHM Statement on OpenAI’s October 6 Release of Mathematical Documents

Yesterday, on October 6th, 2026, OpenAI – which is currently defending lawsuits against accusations of illegal plagiarism, copyright infringement, and trademark dilution – released a repository of manuscripts purporting to contain solutions to a number of high-profile problems in mathematics.

Mathematicians did not ask for this work to be done. The Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence, from whom OpenAI has claimed to derive its legitimacy, opened their initial advisory statement by saying that frontier AI corporations should not test advanced mathematical problems on internal models. In ignoring the central premise of the Advisory Group’s position, OpenAI has indicated total disregard for the norms of scientific research — norms that guarantee that mathematics remains trustworthy, ethically researched, and in the public interest.

Mathematicians have a particular vision of progress that is informed by history and field-specific considerations. We reject OpenAI's assertion that this release advances our subject, and we urge mathematicians and the public to view the value of this publication model with due skepticism. 

Releasing over 700 files at once is not a demonstration of scholarship, but a demonstration of power. We urge mathematicians to discontinue their work with OpenAI and to return to a vision of science that centers human understanding.

Association for Human Mathematics
Communications Working Group
