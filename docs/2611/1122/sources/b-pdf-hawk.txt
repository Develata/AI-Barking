       Improved Practical Key Recovery on HAWK

                                              Abstract
         HAWK is a lattice-based digital signature scheme introduced at ASIACRYPT 2022 that
     bases its security on the module lattice isomorphism problem over power-of-two cyclotomic
     rings. Its secret key is a unimodular two-by-two basis over the ring of integers of degree n,
     while the public key is the associated Hermitian Gram matrix. For the challenge parameter
     set HAWK-256 at ring degree n = 256, the specification claims 64 bits of security against key
     recovery. Recently, Straznickas and Weis (ePrint 2026/1593) reduced HAWK-n key recovery
     to shortest vector computation in a public rank-n cocycle lattice associated with the half-
     period Galois involution, followed by an intertwiner sublattice separation and a tower descent
     over nested subfields. Their released HAWK-256 pipeline requires progressive lattice sieving
     up to dimension 118 and consumes approximately 1.28 × 106 CPU-core-seconds across 96
     cores (3 h 42 min wall-clock).
         In this paper, we present an improved end-to-end key recovery attack against HAWK-256
     driven solely from a genuine 450-byte reference public key on a single CPU core in 1,851.8
     CPU-core-seconds (30.9 minutes). First, rather than applying modulo-two parity only as
     a post-hoc filter, we close the parity congruence into a public sublattice Λ′ ⊂ Λτ of index
     2n−1 . While Λ′ preserves both the ambient rank n and the provable two-block SVP oracle
     dimension n/2 + 1, its covolume sparsification isolates the secret cocycle element as the
     unique shortest vector up to sign with gap λ2 /λ1 = 2, eliminates the intertwiner stage, and
     lowers the expected BKZ block size by ∆β(n) = ln22 n/ ln n + O(n/ ln2 n) (from 92 → 58
     at HAWK-256, 205 → 156 at HAWK-512, and 432 → 351 at HAWK-1024). Second, we
     show that the root Hermite factor required to reach λ1 (Λ′ ) at n = 256 is strictly less than
     unity, and we overcome severe condition-number squaring in Gram-mode basis reduction
     via an exact high-precision Cholesky embedding coupled with fast integer lattice reduction.
     This basis preconditioning lowers the required maximum sieve dimension from 118 to 80,
     yielding a 690× end-to-end speedup verified by the reference implementation and reducing
     the heuristic gate security of HAWK-256, HAWK-512, and HAWK-1024 by 9.28, 11.15, and
     23.74 bits, respectively.


1    Introduction
Lattice-based cryptography provides versatile foundations for post-quantum digital signatures,
most notably through the hash-and-sign paradigm pioneered by GPV [14] and realized over
NTRU lattices in FALCON [19]. While FALCON achieves compact signatures and public keys,
its signing algorithm relies on floating-point fast Fourier orthogonalization and variable-time
Gaussian sampling over cyclotomic fields, which complicates constant-time implementation and
side-channel protection.
    To address these implementation challenges, Ducas, Postlethwaite, Pulles, and van Woer-
den introduced HAWK at ASIACRYPT 2022 [9], basing its security on the Module Lattice
Isomorphism Problem (Module-LIP) [11, 9]. Instead of hiding a secret basis of a specific NTRU
module, HAWK fixes the canonical module lattice Z2n —viewed as the rank-two free module
Rn2 over the power-of-two cyclotomic ring Rn = Z[ζ2ℓ ] of degree n = 2ℓ−1 —and selects a short
unimodular basis B ∈ SL2 (Rn ) as the secret key. The public key is the Hermitian Gram matrix
Q = B ∗ B ∈ M2 (Rn ), where B ∗ denotes the conjugate transpose under the complex conjugation

                                                   1
involution of Rn . Because the secret geometry is isomorphic to the trivial orthogonal lattice
Z2n , Gaussian sampling during signing reduces to simple integer sampling in the secret frame
followed by multiplication by B −1 , avoiding floating-point arithmetic altogether.
    The HAWK NIST specification [10] defines three parameter sets: HAWK-256 (n = 256,
ℓ = 9), which serves as a cryptanalytic challenge instance claiming 64 bits of classical security,
and the NIST Round 3 standardization candidates HAWK-512 (n = 512, ℓ = 10, NIST Level I)
and HAWK-1024 (n = 1024, ℓ = 11, NIST Level V). Following the τ -cocycle cryptanalysis of
Straznickas and Weis [23], the HAWK specification was withdrawn from the NIST post-quantum
standardization process on July 29, 2026 [10].

1.1   Prior Cryptanalysis
Direct lattice reduction attacks against HAWK embed the public Gram matrix Q into a rank-
2n Euclidean lattice and search for any unit-norm vector of the quadratic form defined by Q,
corresponding to a secret basis column. Under standard lattice reduction heuristics, solving
this search problem in dimension 2n requires prohibitively large block sizes even at n = 256.
Subsequent algebraic analyses investigated symplectic automorphisms for the one-more-SVP
problem [16], subfield-guessing attacks and their super-polynomial complexity [17, 21], and a
conditional reduction from rank 2n to rank n given a nontrivial automorphism [13].
     A major breakthrough was achieved by Straznickas and Weis [23], who made the rank-n
descent unconditional by exploiting the Galois tower of power-of-two cyclotomic fields. Let
τ : ζ2ℓ 7→ −ζ2ℓ denote the Galois involution fixing the subfield of degree n/2. Rather than
recovering B directly, they targeted the multiplicative τ -cocycle element Vτ = B −1 τ (B) ∈
SL2 (Rn ). Because Q = B ∗ B is public, Vτ satisfies two public Q-linear matrix constraints (an
adjoint symmetry condition and a Gram intertwining relation, defined formally in Section 3).
The integer matrices satisfying both constraints form a public rank-n lattice Λτ , termed the
τ -cocycle lattice.

1.2   Our Contributions and Technical Overview
In this work, we demonstrate that end-to-end key recovery against HAWK-256 at its full ring
degree n = 256 can be executed on a single CPU core in 1,851.8 CPU-core-seconds (30.9 minutes
CPU, 31.0 minutes wall-clock)—7.2× faster in wall-clock time on 1/96 of the cores and a factor
of 690× lower in allocated core-seconds (601× in active thread-seconds) than the previously
published pipeline [23]. Our attack takes only the 450-byte reference public key as input and
outputs a 592-byte secret key accepted by the authors’ unmodified reference signer and verifier.

5.2   Saturated Integer Kernel Extraction
Given the 450-byte public key, we decode Q ∈ M2 (R256 ) and assemble the homogeneous linear

After forming Λ′ = 2Λτ +Zw0 and its 256×256 integer Gram matrix Gp = Gram(Λ′ ) (1.9 CPU-

LLL pass to finish, but it consumes 299–333 s and leaves the shortest basis vector at 2.828 × 105
times the target squared norm 512.

7.2      Sieve Dimension Reduction from d = 118 to d = 80
The 690× speedup over [23] reduces to a single structural quantity: the maximum sieving dimen-
sion dmax required for the reduction to expose ±Vτ . In [23], the progressive BGJ-style sieve [24]
is driven to a final sieving dimension of 118 along the slide schedule (60, 70, 80, 95, 108, 118). In
our pipeline, the progressive sieve reaches the minimum of Λ′ at dmax = 80.
    Standard heuristic and empirical scaling laws explain why reducing dmax from 118 to 80
yields a factor of ≈ 500–690 in runtime. A lattice sieve in dimension d maintains a database of
size Ndb (d) ≈ (4/3)d/2 ≈ 20.2075 d [4, 2]. Practical CPU sieving runtime scales approximately as
Tsieve (d) ∝ Ndb (d)1.13 ≈ 20.2345 d (in standalone single-pump probes on Λ′ , g6k builds databases
of 65,394 vectors in 10.6 s CPU at d = 70 and 275,569 vectors in 124.0 s CPU at d = 80, while
full progressive tours take 331.8 s and 691.6 s CPU at d = 70 and d = 80). Table 3 compares
the predicted scaling ratios between d = 118 and d = 80 against our end-to-end measurement.
    As Table 3 demonstrates, the empirical throughput law predicts a 481× reduction in sieve
work, closely matching our measured 690× end-to-end speedup (with the remaining gap reflect-
ing elimination of the 16-minute intertwiner stage, 96-core parallelization overhead, and g6k’s
early-exit progressive tour versus svp tool). Two mechanisms account for the drop 118 → 80:

1. Unique-SVP isolation and block-size reduction in Λ′ (Section 4): Doubling the n/2
   spuriouspminimal pairs of Λτ increases the covolume vol(Λ′ ) = 2n−1 vol(Λτ ) while keeping
   ∥Vτ ∥ = n/4 unchanged, which lowers the required BKZ block size from β = 92 to β = 58
   (9.28 bits in the AGPS20 gate model) and eliminates the intertwiner stage.

8     Scope and Limitations
To keep our claims rigorous and reproducible, we explicitly state the scope and boundaries of
this work:

1. Algebraic Attribution vs. End-to-End Engineering: The τ -cocycle lattice Λτ and the
   τ -tower descent algorithm are due to Straznickas and Weis [23], whose tower descent proce-
   dure we use unmodified. What is new in this work is closing the modulo-two congruence into
   the unique-SVP sublattice Λ′ ahead of reduction, deriving the second-order asymptotic block-
   size shift ∆β(n) = ln22 n/ ln n and concrete β reductions across HAWK-256/512/1024, iden-
   tifying the structural root Hermite factor barrier δ0,req = 0.996624 < 1, eliminating Gram-
   mode condition-number squaring via pre-LLL exact Cholesky preconditioning, flatter, and
   exact unimodular Gram pullback G1 = U Gp U T (v = uU ), and demonstrating that the com-
   plete chain executes on a single core at n = 256 in 1,851.8 CPU-core-seconds (690× faster
   than previously published).

2. Single Reference Key Instance: Our experimental measurements at n = 256 are con-
   ducted on a genuine reference public key (alongside controlled ablation runs for HNF/LLL,
   Gram-mode MPFR, and BKZ warm-up), comparing against the published Λτ baseline. Be-
   cause Theorem 4 guarantees that the isometry class of Λ′ is strictly independent of the secret
   key, the target norm, determinant, and gap λ2 /λ1 = 2 are identical for every HAWK-256
   key, though minor run-to-run variance in sieve tour timing is expected across seeds.

3. Reproducibility Suite: Our verification suite supports both fast verification from the
   saved reduced basis (validating the sublattice certificates, GSA/AGPS20 security estimates,
   coordinate recovery, and C reference signing in seconds) and full end-to-end re-execution
   from the 450-byte public key alone.

claims and refined the presentation of the manuscript.
