About

 Hawk is a cryptographic signature scheme that was intended to be secure against both classical and quantum computers.

 It is the only lattice-based scheme in the third round [https://csrc.nist.gov/Projects/pqc-dig-sig/round-3-additional-signatures] of the Additional Digital Signatures [https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/pqc-dig-sig] for the NIST PQC Standardization Process.
 Hawk has been withdrawn [#withdrawal] from the process.

 There are two isochronous implementations available: a "Reference implementation" and an "AVX2 Optimized implementation", which are in the submission package.
 For the source code, see the GitHub repo.

Download the submission package (zip) [submission.zip]

See the implementations () [https://github.com/hawk-sign/dev/]

Advantages

Speed

The procedures for generating and verifying signatures are fast on all devices, including low end devices.

Hawk-512 signature generation and verification take <0.1ms on an average desktop PC.

Compactness

The keys and signature sizes are all rather small.

A Hawk-512 public key is 1024 bytes, a signature 555 bytes.

Well-suited for various hardware

Hawk is free of floating-point arithmetic, i.e. CPUs do not need to support double.

Moreover, Hawk-512 only needs 14 kiB of RAM to work.

Timeline

HAWK is withdrawn

29 July 2026

 Hawk is withdrawn [https://groups.google.com/a/list.nist.gov/g/pqc-forum/c/2r2u6SbHun4/m/0_I2KOZ_CQAJ] from NIST's ongoing additional signature scheme standardisation process,
 because Anthropic discovered [https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses] a new attack that approximately halves the block size required in lattice reduction to recover an (equivalent) private key.
 Naive approaches to circumvent this, such as doubling parameters or moving to higher rank modules, make Hawk uncompetitive compared to other (standardized) lattice candidates.

Round 3

14 May 2026

 NIST announced [https://csrc.nist.gov/News/2026/nist-advances-9-candidates-to-the-3rd-round-of-pqc] that Hawk is one of the 9 schemes that advances to round 3!

Version 1.1

5 February 2024

Updated the omSVP game to include a efficiently computable symplectic automorphism as a 'trivial win', and added new literature.

Round 2

24 October 2024

 NIST announced [https://csrc.nist.gov/news/2024/pqc-digital-signature-second-round-announcement] that Hawk advances to round 2!
 Among the 14 schemes that advanced to round 2, Hawk is the only lattice-based scheme.

Version 1.0.2

26 September 2024

Minor additions added to the specification document.

Round 1

17 July 2023

NIST announced 40 candidates [https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/pqc-dig-sig/round-1-additional-signatures] in the first round of the “onramp” call for additional signature schemes.
 There are 6 other lattice-based signature schemes.

Website released

29 June 2023

The initial website is released.

Hawk submitted

1 June 2023

 Hawk was submitted to the Call for additional Digital Signature Schemes [https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/pqc-dig-sig] organised by NIST.
