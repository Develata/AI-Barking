About

The Lean FRO🔗 [fro/about/#the-lean-fro]

The Lean Focused Research Organization works to make formal verification more accessible across mathematics research, software and hardware verification, and AI-assisted theorem proving. Since its formation in July 2023 as a non-profit organization under Convergent Research, the FRO pursues a focused mission to improve Lean's critical systems through enhanced scalability, usability, documentation, and proof automation while guiding Lean toward long-term self-sustainability.

The Lean FRO is led by Chief Architect and co-founder Leonardo de Moura [https://leodemoura.github.io/blog/], currently a senior principal applied scientist in the Automated Reasoning Group at Amazon Web Services. De Moura created Lean in 2013 and previously worked on automated reasoning and theorem proving at Microsoft Research and SRI International.

Working alongside de Moura is Head of Engineering and co-founder Sebastian Ullrich and a team of skilled researchers and engineers [/fro/team] with specialized expertise across diverse aspects of Lean development. The Lean FRO team is globally diverse and many team members are recruited directly from the very active Lean community. The team actively engages with the community to promote Lean adoption across academic and industry contexts.

The Lean FRO gratefully acknowledges support from its funders and supporters, including Alex Gerko, Alfred P. Sloan Foundation, Richard Merkin Foundation, Simons Foundation International, and Convergent Research.

A Brief History of Lean🔗 [fro/about/#a-brief-history-of-lean]

November 2026 - Levent Alpöge releases [https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf] a Lean-verified proof [https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/] of the Riemann Hypothesis; Max Tegmark announces [https://x.com/tegmark/status/2120570162428441021] 80% completion of Signal Shot, Beneficial AI Foundation's program to fully verify the Signal messaging platform [https://www.beneficialaifoundation.org/blog/signal-shot-milestone], following BAIF efforts and complementary work by Google DeepMind [https://google-deepmind.github.io/code-verification/]; the fall 2026 edition of Software Verification in Lean [https://beneficial-ai-foundation.github.io/SVIL2026_Fall/] takes place in Palo Alto

October 2026 - OpenAI announces [https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/] a Lean-verified refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis and results on the Kakeya and Stein restriction conjectures, covered by New Scientist [https://www.newscientist.com/article/2596045-ai-refutes-30-year-old-mathematical-assumption-in-computer-science/]; Axiom releases [https://axiommath.ai/research/the-enormous-theorem-formalized/] the first complete Lean formalization [https://cfsg.axiommath.ai/] of the Classification of Finite Simple Groups; &Truth [https://andtruth.com/press/andtruth-emerges-from-stealth/] launches out of stealth with $70M in seed funding for Lean-based software verification, covered by The New York Times [https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/29/science/ai-eradicate-computer-bugs.html]; Google DeepMind's Gemini 4 Argon [https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/] and Anthropic's Claude [https://www.anthropic.com/research/designing-efficient-quantum-circuits] design efficient quantum circuits, with key components verified in Lean; Google DeepMind publishes [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22603] a Gemini-based agentic pipeline for the Busy Beaver problem, integrating formal theorem proving in Lean

September 2026 - Anthropic announces [https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem] Lean formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem, covered by Nature [https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02822-9]; OpenAI and Alpöge & Buckmaster announce Lean-verified results on the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem, covered by The New York Times [https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/08/science/openai-proof-millennium-problem.html], Quanta Magazine [https://www.quantamagazine.org/ai-has-solved-one-of-maths-1-million-millennium-prize-problems-20260908/] and New Scientist [https://www.newscientist.com/article/2588115-major-breakthrough-made-on-famous-millennium-maths-problem/]; OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra [https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/] helped establish a stronger prime gap bound of 186, with Lean formalization [https://github.com/openai/PrimeGaps186]; Lean FRO releases con-leche [https://github.com/leanprover/con-leche], an external Lean checker proven in Lean to be consistent; XTX Markets [https://www.xtxmarkets.com/news/2026-update-on-xtx-markets-ai-philanthropy/] announces additional support for Lean FRO

August 2026 - OpenAI provides Lean certificates [https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs] and announces progress [https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/] on 10 research-level mathematical problems; Anthropic announces new results [https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta] related to the Riemann zeta function and provides Lean formalizations [https://github.com/anthropics/zeta-23-lean] that pass Comparator validation; Lean FRO publishes Year 4 Part 1 Roadmap [https://lean-lang.org/fro/roadmap/y4-1/]; International Business Times [https://www.ibtimes.com/chris-hsu-how-one-programming-language-rewrote-mathematics-why-software-next-3806974] interviews Chris Hsu: 'How One Programming Language Rewrote Mathematics and Why Software Is Next'; Kim Morrison launches Palomar [https://palomar-registry.org/], a Registry of Lean-Verified Mathematics

July 2026 - Amazon Automated reasoning announces the single largest donation [https://www.amazon.science/news/amazon-is-investing-in-the-lean-focused-research-organization] to Lean FRO in the FRO's history; Microsoft Research [https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/verifying-rust-cryptography-in-symcrypt-from-standards-to-code/] reports on how Lean, via the Aeneas toolchain, formally verifies production cryptographic algorithms in SymCrypt; New Scientist [https://www.newscientist.com/article/2533518-mathematicians-put-ai-to-work-on-fermats-last-theorem/] reports on AI use supporting the Lean formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem; the Hex project [https://kim-em.github.io/hex-dev/], a library for computational algebra, is released; a 1.2M line human-in-the-loop proof [https://lean-lang.org/eval/problems/erdos_unit_distance_conjecture_false/] of the falsity of the Erdos unit-distance problem is submitted to Lean Eval; Tau Ceti [https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/] launched, AI-authored Lean mathematics downstream of Mathlib, with human-owned roadmaps and AI adversarial review against open rubrics; Bob DyLean [https://github.com/BobDyLean/dylean], a framework for the symbolic analysis of cryptographic protocols, is open-sourced; Google DeepMind publishes on AI conjecture machines [https://deepmind.google/public-policy/conjecture-machines-ai-agents-and-the-new-validation-bottleneck-in-science/] and Lean as the validation layer for AI-generated science; White House OSTP report Science: A New Golden Age [https://www.whitehouse.gov/science/] highlights AI-assisted formal verification of mathematics

May 2026 - Google DeepMind announces AlphaProof Nexus [https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763v1] and autonomous proofs of 9 Erdős problems [https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphaproof-nexus-results]; SAIR convenes the Science x AI Summit [https://sair.foundation/events/science-ai-summit-2026] in Palo Alto, foregrounding Lean and AI-assisted proof discovery; NASA holds its Formal Methods Symposium [https://nfm2026.github.io/] with keynotes [https://nfm2026.github.io/keynote_speakers/] on Lean [https://lean-lang.org], CSLib [https://www.cslib.io/] and Aristotle [https://aristotle.harmonic.fun/]; TorchLean [https://lean-dojo.github.io/TorchLean/] is released, bridging PyTorch and Lean
