 MiniMax publishes AI-discovered cryptographic attacks with papers and code

MiniMax has published research showing how an internal AI model helped discover attacks against experimental cryptographic schemes and solve previously unanswered public challenges. The company announced the work on Sunday, following the release of technical papers and supporting code on November 20.

The results span encryption, digital signatures and hash functions. In the TII McEliece challenge, MiniMax reports recovering 94 private keys, including 55 instances without previously published solutions, with speedups of up to 43,400 times over existing implementations. Other findings include practical attacks on selected signature schemes and reduced-round versions of the ARADI block cipher.

MiniMax says the findings primarily originated from its internal model and were organized and verified by its research team. The published work concerns specific research designs, historical competition candidates and challenge parameters; it does not establish attacks on mainstream standards such as AES, RSA or SHA-256. The repository includes papers and code for examining the individual results. [MiniMax [https://github.com/MiniMax-AI/Research]]
