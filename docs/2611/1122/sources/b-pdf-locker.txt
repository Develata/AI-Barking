    Algebraic Cryptanalysis of LOCKER and ROLLO

                                                Abstract
          Ideal Low-Rank Parity-Check (LRPC) key encapsulation mechanisms replace Hamming
      weight with rank weight over a binary polynomial quotient ring. They were submitted to
      the first round of the NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography standardization process as Low-
      Rank Parity-Check Codes Encryption (LOCKER), Low-Rank Parity-Check Key Exchange
      (LAKE), and Rank-Ouroboros, and merged in the second round as Rank-Ouroboros, LAKE,
      and LOCKER (ROLLO). Because an irreducible binary modulus of degree n over F2m
      eliminates proper ideals when m and n are coprime, prior algebraic attacks unfold ring
      multiplication into a generic linear code of length 2n over F2m , requiring hundreds of millions
      of minor variables and petabytes of memory on first-round parameters.
          In this paper, we show that the irreducibility of the ring modulus enables a direct al-
      gebraic attack whenever the field extension degree m exceeds 2r for error rank r or 2d for
      secret key rank d. Because the binary quotient ring modulo the irreducible polynomial is it-
      self the extension field F2n , transposing the n × m binary coefficient matrix maps the scheme
      ring isomorphically onto a polynomial quotient ring over F2n . Under this transposition, any
      subspace of the original base field F2m consists of polynomials with binary coefficients and
      is fixed pointwise by squaring coefficients in F2n . Iterating this partial Frobenius auto-
      morphism generates an orbit of conjugate equations over the identical unknown support
      from a single passive ciphertext or public key with zero decapsulation queries. We derive
      polynomial-time ciphertext support recovery via local Schur elimination when m > 2r , and
      direct public-key secret recovery via principal minors and a closed-form Moore-matrix gauge
      recursion when m ≥ 2d . Across all 27 published parameter sets (24 official NIST submission
      sets from 2017–2020 and 3 additional new2ROLLO-I sets), our attacks improve upon the
      best published complexities on 14 sets—recovering the shared secret of LOCKER-I in 1.15 s
      using 58 MB of memory on a 12-thread workstation and recovering the 2019 ROLLO-I-128
      secret key in 5.97 s on a single core—while proving via exact Frobenius syzygy counts that
      the 2020 revised ROLLO and new2ROLLO-I parameter sets with m ≤ 2r and m < 2d
      remain immune.


1     Introduction
1.1    Background and Design Rationale
Code-based public-key cryptosystems in the Hamming metric, such as Classic McEliece, require
public keys of several hundred kilobytes because information-set decoding scales only linearly
with the error weight in the exponent. Rank-metric cryptography replaces Hamming weight
over F2 with the F2 -rank of vectors over an extension field K ∼
                                                               = F2m . Because generic Rank
Syndrome Decoding (RSD) algorithms scale quadratically with m and the rank weight r in
the exponent, rank-metric constructions achieve 128-bit security with public keys under one
kilobyte.
    Introduced by Gaborit, Murat, Ruatta, and Zémor [15], Ideal Low-Rank Parity-Check
(LRPC) cryptosystems compress keys to a single ring element by operating in the polynomial
quotient ring R = K[X]/(P (X)) of degree n. A secret key consists of a d-dimensional F2 -
subspace F ⊂ K and two polynomials x, y ∈ F n whose coefficients lie in F , while the public


                                                     1
key is the quotient h = x−1 y mod P (X) (747 bytes in LOCKER-I). Encapsulation samples an
r-dimensional F2 -subspace E ⊂ K and error polynomials e1 , e2 ∈ E n , outputting the syndrome
u = e1 + e2 h mod P (X). Decapsulation computes s = xu ≡ xe1 + ye2 (mod P (X)), whose
coefficients lie in the product subspace F · E of dimension at most rd < m, and intersects
fi−1 Supp(s) across a basis of F to recover E.
    Across the NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography standardization process and contemporane-
ous cryptanalytic literature, 27 Ideal-LRPC parameter sets (24 official submission sets and
3 literature sets) were specified across four parameter families. In November 2017 (NIST
Round 1), Aragon et al. [3] submitted three sister schemes: LAKE (an ephemeral IND-CPA
KEM), LOCKER (an IND-CCA2 KEM with nine parameter sets LOCKER-I..IX at r ∈ {5, 6}
and d ∈ {7, 8, 9} designed for decryption failure rates below 2−64 ), and Rank-Ouroboros. In
March 2019 (NIST Round 2 [4]), the three submissions were merged into ROLLO-I (LAKE),
ROLLO-II (LOCKER), and ROLLO-III (Rank-Ouroboros), comprising nine parameter sets with
r ∈ {5, 6, 7}, d ∈ {6, 7, 8} on ROLLO-I/II and secret-key rank d = w ∈ {5, 6, 7}, error rank
r = wr ∈ {6, 8, 8} on ROLLO-III. Following the algebraic attacks of Bardet et al. [8, 9]—
where [9] also proposed three illustrative large-m parameter sets new2ROLLO-I-128/192/256
(m ∈ {73, 89, 103}, r ∈ {7, 8, 9}, later re-evaluated in [10])—the April 2020 Round 2 revision [5]
dropped ROLLO-III and raised (r, d) to 7–9 across six sets (ROLLO-I/II-128/192/256). In July
2020, NIST declined to advance ROLLO to Round 3 [20], citing rapid progress in algebraic
cryptanalysis of rank-metric codes.

Principal Minor Assignment Problem [18, 16]. Given a matrix M ∈ Ld×d and a subset
Z ⊆ {0, . . . , d−1}, the principal minor ∆Z (M ) := det M [Z, Z] is the determinant of the |Z|×|Z|
submatrix on row and column indices Z (with ∆∅ (M ) = 1). Because det(DM̃ D−1 )[Z, Z] =

position. Conversely, classical results on the Principal Minor Assignment Problem (PMAP)

to diagonal conjugation D and transposition M̃ ↔ M̃ ⊤ . In general, the (d − 1)-dimensional
diagonal gauge D cannot be recovered from the principal minors of M alone; in Section 6.1, we
show that when M = BA−1 links two Moore matrices A and B, the Frobenius shift structure
of A and B uniquely determines D in closed form.
