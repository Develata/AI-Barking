Key takeaways
In The Rebundling of Freelance Work in the AI Age, OpenAI and Upwork compare
tasks in a sample of personal and work-related ChatGPT conversations with the
projects clients post on Upwork. The authors also examine which tasks clients
combine and how the scope of AI-related projects differs from other freelance
work.

AI use is widespread at work, but its effects remain difficult to detect in aggregate
economic statistics. Data from freelance platforms may provide early evidence that work
is changing by documenting trends in the types of jobs clients post and the activities those
jobs involve. The faster pace of freelance work can make these trends visible sooner:
contracts are typically short, hiring is streamlined, and clients can quickly adapt postings
as their needs evolve.

AI use and freelance demand
The authors compare the work people do with ChatGPT with freelance job postings on Upwork.
Some of the results follow an expected pattern: Content writing or customer tech support appear
frequently in ChatGPT use and account for small shares of Upwork postings (Figure 1).

Content writing’s share of Upwork postings fell by roughly 31% from the first six months of 2025 to
the same period in 2026. Firms may be using AI directly for this task instead of hiring remote
workers.
At the same time, many tasks that people could perform with AI still appear frequently in freelance
postings. Although AI can assist with video production work, it is less common in ChatGPT use and
remains common in Upwork postings.
These differences point to a broader gap between what AI systems can do and how people
currently use them. OpenAI calls this gap the capability overhang.
Many tasks don’t fit into either pattern. Graphic and presentation design, for example, are relatively
common in both ChatGPT and Upwork data. Even if AI can perform many aspects of such a task,
people still serve a valuable role. Graphic design is complex, and while people use AI commonly for
image generation, freelance demand for graphic design remains substantial.

Project budgets and hours
AI-related jobs request AI skills, require AI tools, or involve improving AI-generated output. Upwork
clients can post a fixed budget for a project or an hourly rate. Within the same work subcategory
and posting month, posted fixed-price budgets were about 52% higher for AI-related projects
(Figure 2).

The corresponding difference in posted hourly rates was 1.5%. Among hired hourly contracts with
positive recorded hours, AI-related work involved about 41% more hours in the first 90 days. These
patterns suggest that clients may commission larger projects when the work involves AI.

The mix of tasks in AI-related work
The mix of tasks in AI-related postings broadened between early 2023 and late 2025. Task diversity
rose by about 45% (Figure 3). Video production, social-media campaigns, marketing automation,
and AI integration all gained share within AI-related work.

Clients also combined tasks in different ways within individual postings. Among postings with at
least two tasks, historically uncommon pairs made up 8.4% of task pairs in the average AI-related
posting and 2.4% in the average non-AI posting. These pairs had appeared together in fewer than
200 earlier postings (Exhibit 3.2, Panel A of the full report).


What this could mean for freelance work
Content writing’s declining share of postings suggests that some clients may be using AI for work
they previously commissioned from freelancers. That shift could put pressure on freelancers who
specialize in writing.
AI may be changing the composition and scope of work without reducing overall labor demand,
which could help explain why unemployment has not increased substantially. AI can reduce
demand for some tasks while expanding what clients ask workers to deliver in others. When AI
makes individual tasks easier, clients may ask for more: another version of a design, a more
ambitious video, or an app with more features. Some of AI’s effects on work may therefore show up
first in the scope of projects clients post, the wages clients pay for those projects, and the tasks
they ask freelancers to perform.
