AI agents have found what looks like an undiscovered Maya site

The discovery came from a scan of LiDAR data collected by Mexico

Experts are now investigating the site that the AI has flagged up

While some of AI's 'talents' — like putting together decent-looking restaurant menus [https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/just-eats-ai-put-bacon-and-eggs-in-a-glass-of-cola-and-its-exposed-a-much-bigger-problem-with-ai] — could legitimately be called into question, there's no doubt that these bots and agents excel at spotting patterns in vast troves of data, and the latest evidence comes from the potential discovery of a hidden Maya archaeological site in Mexico.

Google [https://www.techradar.com/tag/google] researcher Pietro Miotti described having [https://x.com/PietroMiotti/status/2120219901814043087] a "random thought on vacation" about how AI agents might be used to sift through LiDAR datasets — topographical maps of Earth's terrain, captured from space satellites.

LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) lasers can cut through cloud cover and forest canopies, enabling researchers to find shapes in the landscape that aren't visible to the naked eye (or in landscapes that aren't even accessible to archaeologists).

Miotti tasked 48 agents from different AI apps with scouring through a NASA database of LiDAR information collected across Peru, and the AI came back with multiple hits on structures that could be buildings. A second scan by the agents identified one particular site that hasn't yet been mentioned in scientific literature.
