Grok Build Left 94.7% of 37,571 Live Apps Open to Anyone

AI Business Brief [https://fourweekmba.com/category/ai-business-brief/] / By 

 Gennaro Cuofano 
 [https://fourweekmba.com/author/thefourweekmbateam/]

 / November 10, 2026 

Security firm Claroc says it tried 43,551 addresses of apps published with Grok Build, SpaceXSI’s vibe-coding tool. Of the 37,571 still online, “35,578 of those (94.7%) opened for anyone, with no sign-in”, according to Claroc’s 10 November post [https://blog.claroc.io/ghostshares-grok-build-apps/]. Only 1,993 asked for a login. That is 17.9 open apps for every locked one, by our arithmetic.

Claroc says 24 of the open apps were built by SpaceXSI and X staff, including “a confidential onboarding plan [https://fourweekmba.com/business-plan/], a sales territory map of 594 accounts and advertising proposals”. Ars Technica reported [https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/grok-build-coding-agent-leaks-oodles-of-confidential-info-some-belonging-to-spacexsi/] that SpaceXSI began removing those links within hours of Ars asking questions, and that all 24 returned an “app not found” error when its story [https://fourweekmba.com/ai-story-generator/] went live.

Business Pill · Least Privilege

An evergreen Business Engineer explainer on least privilege. It teaches the general idea only and does not discuss Grok Build.

The key insight: Security firm Claroc says 35,578 of the 37,571 Grok Build apps it found online, or 94.7%, opened for anyone with no sign-in, including 24 built by SpaceXSI and X staff. Ars Technica reports SpaceXSI removed those 24 after Ars asked about them. That is 17.9 open apps for every locked one, by our arithmetic.

Update (11 Nov): What SpaceXSI told a reporter, and what else Claroc reports. According to Claroc’s post [https://blog.claroc.io/ghostshares-grok-build-apps/], SpaceXSI told a reporter that Grok apps are private until their creator chooses to share one, that publishing takes several steps ending with a legal disclaimer, and that employee accounts are set to “Only you” by default. Claroc says that in the paid account it used, the access step had “Anyone with the link” already selected. In a team app Grok built for Claroc, the firm says a Google [https://fourweekmba.com/google-business-model/] account that had never been invited signed in, saw every customer, changed one record and deleted another; Claroc says it tested this only on its own app. Checking its AI flagging against a hand-read sample, Claroc puts the total at “roughly 650 to 800 apps with private data”. It says it reported the issue on 8 November, that SpaceXSI had not replied to the report itself, and that from 10 November it began notifying affected organizations itself.

What Claroc Says It Found

Most open apps were games, landing pages and calculators, Claroc says, but “About 1 in 10 held sensitive material, internal business [https://fourweekmba.com/what-is-a-business-model/] data or personal data”. On that estimate, about 3,560 of the 35,578 open apps held sensitive data, by our arithmetic. Claroc lists examples it confirmed by hand, including “an intensive care briefing for a single patient” and a contractor timesheet whose code held staff passwords in plain text.

Beyond SpaceXSI and X, Claroc says 30 organizations had internal data in open apps: “26 have more than 1,000 employees, and 6 are Fortune 500 companies”. Claroc names none of them. Ars reports that some affected users work for Fortune 500 companies and a defense contractor.

Why the Links Were Findable

Each published Grok Build app gets its own web address, and Grok [https://fourweekmba.com/elon-musk-companies/] proposes one made of random words, which users can change. Ars explains that because the random part sits in the hostname rather than in the path after the domain, the addresses end up in public passive DNS records once anyone opens them. Claroc says other app builders also give each app its own hostname, naming ChatGPT [https://fourweekmba.com/who-owns-chatgpt/], Lovable, Replit, Base44, Google [https://fourweekmba.com/google-business-model/] AI Studio and Meta [https://fourweekmba.com/how-does-facebook-make-money/]’s Muse, and says shared chats from Claude, ChatGPT [https://fourweekmba.com/what-is-chatgpt/] and Gemini keep the secret part in the path instead.

Claroc says that in the account it used, publishing an app “proposed Anyone with the link as the access setting”. Ars reports that a representative for Cursor, the coding company SpaceXSI recently acquired, said Grok Build “apps are private by default and nothing goes public unless the creator chooses to share it”. Ars reports that Claroc’s tests contradict that statement, and that the representative did not answer follow-up questions.

The Business Stakes for Vibe-Coding Platforms

Vibe-coding tools sell speed: describe an app and publish it in minutes. As we read Claroc’s findings, the default sharing setting carries the cost [https://fourweekmba.com/cost-structure-business-model/] when that speed meets company data, because a link meant for a few colleagues becomes reachable by anyone who finds the address. Grok Build sits inside the same Grok business [https://fourweekmba.com/what-is-a-business-model/] we covered this week, from usage-limit complaints [https://fourweekmba.com/ai-musk-promises-overnight-grok-bot-fix-after-usage-complaints/] to the Grok 4.8 training update [https://fourweekmba.com/ai-grok-4-8-in-rl-after-a-57-day-wait-as-xai-dumps-jax-for-cpp/].

The Structural Read

As we read Claroc’s findings, the risk sits in the sharing default rather than in a break-in: a link meant for a few colleagues becomes reachable by anyone who finds the address. Claroc says other builders’ app hostnames can be found the same way, and that ChatGPT [https://fourweekmba.com/what-is-chatgpt/]’s Only me default stops strangers opening them. Whether SpaceXSI will change the default is not established.

Three Implications

FOR COMPANIES WHOSE STAFF USE VIBE-CODING TOOLS Ars reports that anyone using Grok Build shareable links should choose the invite option unless they intend an app to be widely available.

FOR SPACEXSI Ars reports a Cursor representative said apps are private by default, and that Claroc’s tests contradict that statement.

FOR OTHER APP BUILDERS Claroc names ChatGPT [https://fourweekmba.com/what-is-chatgpt/], Lovable, Replit, Base44, Google [https://fourweekmba.com/google-business-model/] AI Studio and Meta [https://fourweekmba.com/who-owns-facebook/]’s Muse among builders that give each app its own hostname.

What Is Not Established

Neither Claroc nor Ars says whether SpaceXSI has notified affected users or changed the default sharing setting. Claroc says SpaceXSI took down its own staff’s 24 apps after its report but “has not done the same for the apps other Grok users and organizations left open”. Ars reported Claroc’s Aprozper as saying a passive DNS search returned 43,601 links, against Claroc’s 43,551 addresses tried. The share of apps holding sensitive data is Claroc’s estimate, and we did not test any app ourselves.
