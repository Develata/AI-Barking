                                 Let Students Search

Google’s mission is to organize the world’s information and make it universally accessible and
useful. Nowhere is that commitment more apparent than in our commitment to Education and in
the way people use Google Search everyday to find information to educate themselves and get
things done.


However, the recent announcement that Google will turn Search off for school districts that
disable AI features jeopardizes our ability to carry out our mission. The way the decision has been
reported in the press is poor, and makes Google, and our Education and Search products as a
result, look like we’re punishing schools for not using AI.


Many students, educators and parents have concerns about AI in the classroom, as is shown by
many school districts who have placed moratoriums or bans on such technology. It is our job as
Googlers to respect the user, and listen to them when they have concerns about our products.


Any opportunity to drive positive change with AI in education will rely on educators having the
freedom to choose how to implement it in their curriculum. Cutting off access to Search for
schools that don’t wish to use AI will not help persuade our users, and will more likely result in them
shutting out Google products altogether.


This will also harm the conditions under which we work. Both because it will harm the business of
Google’s Education and Search products and because the reputation of our work will suffer
among students, parents and educators.


We urge Chris Phillips, Sundar and Elizabeth Hamon Reid to reconsider this decision, and allow
schools to turn off AI features in Search without disrupting their ability to use search as a whole.
