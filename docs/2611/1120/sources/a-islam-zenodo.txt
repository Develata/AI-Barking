The Bit in the Definition: Two Derivations of the Riemann Hypothesis in One Day, Their Tautology, and the Public Record They Land In

Description

On 20 November 2026 two derivations of the Riemann Hypothesis were made public. L. Alpöge posted a Lean 4 archive deriving Mathlib's RiemannHypothesis: 175,187 files, 17.4 million lines, the three standard axioms, no human-readable proof; an independent run has since built it with no error and two kernels have accepted it. A. M. Masullo's 940-page draft, The Music of the Primes, written by hand, claims GRH for Hecke L-functions through a trace formula. We read both against the one-bit normal form of the Riemann Hypothesis, public in our git repository since October 2026 in Lean and since August 2026 in prose, and filed on Zenodo on 16 October 2026. Term by term, each route's conclusion is our bit by a proved equivalence, its exit is our band reduction, and each is one instance of our gate theorem, whose quantifier runs over every proposition. Alpöge decides the bit on the prime side of Weil's explicit formula, Masullo on the zero side; our record printed the bit as the identity between the two sides, and its one premise. Each premise is a definition, which a law of logic proved in our file classifies as strictly stronger than the value: both derivations are tautologies of their premises, and the standard axioms carry nothing. Four seals build silently in core Lean 4.19.0 and are printed whole. We close with a provenance ledger and a critique of both artifacts.
