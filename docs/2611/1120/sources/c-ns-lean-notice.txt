This package proves the three theorems of Challenge.lean and the two theorems of FractionalChallenge.lean. Suppose that the self-similar form of the incompressible viscous flow equations has a nonzero smooth solution that is periodic in time up to a fixed linear isometry (possibly orientation-reversing) and has the stated decay. Then breakdown_on_space states that for every viscosity there is smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free initial data on three-dimensional space for which the incompressible viscous flow equations have no solution that is smooth for all time and has bounded energy, and breakdown_on_torus states that for every viscosity there is smooth, divergence-free initial data of period one in each coordinate for which they have no solution that is smooth for all time and whose velocity keeps this periodicity. The theorem breakdown_on_space_of_weak reaches the conclusion of breakdown_on_space from a nonzero weak solution of the same self-similar equations that is periodic in time up to a fixed linear isometry (possibly orientation-reversing) and has the stated integrability.

In FractionalChallenge.lean the viscous term is ν(−Δ)^α with 1 < α < 5/4, where (−Δ)^α is the normalized singular integral of the second-order remainder defined there. Suppose that, for some positive viscosity, the unforced fractional equations have a nonzero smooth divergence-free self-similar profile with a smooth pressure in a frame that rotates at a constant rate about a fixed axis, with (−Δ)^α of the profile smooth, and with the following decay: for every j ≥ 0 there is a constant C such that at every point y the norms of the j-th derivative of the profile, of the (j + 1)-th derivative of its pressure and of the j-th derivative of (−Δ)^α of the profile are at most C(1 + |y|)^−(2α − 1 + j), C(1 + |y|)^−(4α − 1 + j) and C(1 + |y|)^−(4α − 1 + j) respectively. Then fractional_breakdown_on_space states that for every viscosity there is smooth, compactly supported, divergence-free initial data on three-dimensional space for which the fractional equations have no solution that is smooth for all time and has bounded energy, and fractional_breakdown_on_torus states that for every viscosity there is smooth, divergence-free initial data of period one in each coordinate for which they have no solution that is smooth for all time and whose velocity keeps this periodicity.

It also proves the theorems left open in eight further statement files; the last three, FractionalCellSystemChallenge.lean, CellLayerChallenge.lean and CellLayerTheorems.lean, are described below. Three of the theorems of the other five derive a conclusion from the existence of a certificate: finitely many functions and numbers, with a set of symmetries, satisfying the conditions stated in its file. The others give sufficient conditions for some of those conditions, or are auxiliary facts. No certificate is given in this package. In CertificateChallenge.lean a certificate includes a box of parameters; for each parameter a period and a divergence-free candidate profile with two continuous derivatives (jointly in the parameter, time and space), periodic in rescaled time, with a continuously differentiable pressure and the stated decay; finitely many auxiliary fields, each with a multiplier depending on the parameter; weights; target values, pinning fields, slope matrices and a twist; and constants. Its conditions include that the equations linearized about each profile satisfy the stated coercive estimate; that the amount by which each profile fails to solve the self-similar equations, with the auxiliary fields times the profile's multipliers added as a force, satisfies the stated bounds; that the quadratic form frozen at each profile is bounded below as stated; that profiles, auxiliary fields and weights are invariant under a stated set of symmetries; and that the stated smallness, slope, inclusion and nondegeneracy conditions hold. The theorem certificate_orbit states that if a certificate exists, then the self-similar equations have a nonzero weak solution of the kind that breakdown_on_space_of_weak assumes.

CoerciveChallenge.lean gives sufficient conditions for the coercive estimate of a certificate, in terms of weights attached to the modes of a perturbation in time and in angle about an axis (modes are indexed by pairs of a time frequency and an azimuthal order). It proves in place that the multiplier inequality defined there, with constants satisfying the stated inequalities, gives the coercive estimate. The theorem multiplier_density states that admissible weights and valid bounds for a given profile satisfying the stated regularity, periodicity, divergence and decay conditions, with a weight function bounded above and auxiliary fields satisfying the stated conditions, give, for each perturbation solving the equations linearized about that profile with a given right-hand side satisfying the stated conditions, a multiplier field of controlled size for which an integrated positive density is bounded by an integrated negative density up to a stated gap. The theorem mode_azimuthal_gain states that for a perturbation and each of its modes whose azimuthal order n satisfies |n| ≥ 2, at almost every point of time and space, the squared mode of the perturbation times (|n| − 1)²/r² is at most the squared corresponding mode of its gradient, and the squared mode of its gradient times (|n| − 2)²/r² is at most the squared corresponding mode of its second derivative, where r is the distance to the axis. The theorem stretch_mean_bound states that, for a divergence-free profile with two continuous derivatives, valid bounds for it, a positive length function and any perturbation, at almost every point of space, the integral of the stretching term over one period and over all rotations about the axis is at least −2π times a sum of nonnegative terms, one for each mode. The theorem multiplierInequality_of_density states that if the radii of the box and the constants multiplying the pinning and ball terms are nonnegative, the profiles and auxiliary fields satisfy the stated regularity, measurability, periodicity, divergence, decay and integrability conditions, the weight function is bounded above as stated, and, at every parameter of the box, the weights are admissible with common constants, the bounds on the profile are valid, and for every perturbation, right-hand side and multiplier field as stated the integrated negative density is finite and the integrated positive density minus it is at least the stated quadratic expression in the multipliers, the multiplier field, the right-hand side and the pairings of the gradient of the perturbation with fixed fields, then the multiplier inequality holds with the stated constants. The theorems piece_multiplier_density and multiplierInequality_of_pieceDensity are the corresponding statements for weights defined piece by piece.

ExtendedCertificateChallenge.lean defines an extended certificate, which differs from a certificate in several ways. The candidate profile need only be continuously differentiable, with a weak second derivative, and its pressure is replaced by a field orthogonal, in time and space, to smooth compactly supported divergence-free fields. The parameters range over a box in some coordinates and a ball in rescaled coordinates in the others. The coercive estimate is made about a model profile near the candidate profile; its term with the norm of the right-hand side on a ball is replaced by the square root of a weighted sum of squared pairings of the right-hand side with finitely many orthonormal fields; and the multipliers carry a weight. The slope and inclusion conditions are replaced by other conditions, involving finitely many adjoint fields and a frame matrix. The theorem extended_certificate_orbit states that an extended certificate gives the same conclusion as certificate_orbit. ExtendedCoerciveChallenge.lean gives sufficient conditions for the coercive estimate of an extended certificate when the second derivative of the model profile is known only in the weak sense: it proves in place that the row form of the multiplier inequality, with constants satisfying the stated inequalities, gives that estimate, and the theorem extendedCoerciveClause_of_pieceData states that if the radii and the auxiliary fields satisfy the stated conditions, the weight function is bounded above as stated, the model profile has at each parameter a weak second derivative of the kind defined there, and the constants satisfy the stated inequalities, then weights defined piece by piece, with common constants, that satisfy the stated admissibility and remainder conditions give it. These two files also leave open five auxiliary facts, which Solution.lean proves: isExtendedCertificate_withUnitWeight and isExtendedCertificate_atProfile state that the two normalizations of an extended certificate defined there, to multiplier weight one and to a model equal to the candidate profile, are again extended certificates; IsWeakSecondDerivState.secondDeriv_symm states that a weak second derivative of the kind defined there is, at each time, symmetric at almost every point; vortGradMap_secondDerivField states that for a field with two continuous derivatives jointly in time and space, the derivative of the vorticity is the stated linear function of the second derivative; and pieceMultiplierDensityWeak is the density statement for weights defined piece by piece, with a weak second derivative.

FractionalCertificateChallenge.lean defines, for the fractional equations, a certificate made of time-independent data, and the theorem fracCertificate_profile states that for 1 < α < 5/4 such a certificate gives the rotating profile that the theorems of FractionalChallenge.lean assume.

The statement files also contain theorems with proofs in place that link these statements. Seven theorems with proofs in place rely on theorems that the files leave open, so the statement files alone do not prove them: multiplierInequality_of_density, multiplierInequality_of_pieceDensity, extendedCoerciveClause_of_pieceData, isAdmissibleWeak_of_isAdmissible, pieceBlockWith_secondDerivField, pieceEpsBlockWith_secondDerivField and vortGradSupWith_secondDerivField. Solution.lean proves the statements left open; it also proves outright three theorems that the statement files derive in place from theorems they leave open (multiplierInequality_of_density from multiplier_density, multiplierInequality_of_pieceDensity from piece_multiplier_density, and extendedCoerciveClause_of_pieceData from pieceMultiplierDensityWeak), and adds three consequences: certificate_breakdown_on_space and extended_certificate_breakdown_on_space state that a certificate, or an extended certificate, gives the conclusion of breakdown_on_space, and fractional_certificate_breakdown states that for 1 < α < 5/4 a fractional certificate gives the conclusions of fractional_breakdown_on_space and fractional_breakdown_on_torus. The file comparator-certificate.json lists certificate_orbit, extended_certificate_orbit and the seven theorems about the coercive estimate named above (multiplier_density, mode_azimuthal_gain, stretch_mean_bound, multiplierInequality_of_density, piece_multiplier_density, multiplierInequality_of_pieceDensity and extendedCoerciveClause_of_pieceData), and comparator-fractional-certificate.json lists fracCertificate_profile; the five auxiliary facts and the three consequences are listed in no comparator file.

FractionalCellSystemChallenge.lean defines, for the fractional equations, data of seven kinds (cell data, profile data, auxiliary data, a cell mesh, model data, row data and cell system data) and a condition CellSystemCondition on them, made of twelve conditions defined in that file: IsGivenData (on the given data), IsSystemInstance (on how the cell system data match the other data), IsBaseSystem (on the fractional certificate data that the cell data extend), IsCellNumbers, IsReducedLosses, IsExteriorSystem, IsSpectralSystem and IsSpectralBounds (on spectral data), IsSplitSystem, IsMeshData, IsTreeSystem, and IsCellSystemFacts for every cell. These conditions include bounds on explicitly given functions over continuous ranges of their arguments, and upper bounds that the condition assumes, finitely many for each polynomial degree (in IsSpectralBounds, and AxisGradientBound in IsSpectralSystem), on quadratic forms on polynomials of bounded degree relative to a weighted square integral, that is, on the largest eigenvalues of these forms. The data also include finitely many real matrices, among them a matrix for each cell, finitely many further matrices and, for a finite rooted tree, a matrix at each node; the conditions state that each further matrix is the product of a column vector with its own transpose, that each cell's matrix and each further matrix is attached to exactly one node, that the matrix at each node is bounded above, as a quadratic form, by the sum of the matrices attached to it and of the matrices at its children, that the matrix at the root plus a global matrix is positive on a subspace of the stated dimension, and, for each cell, inequalities between the quadratic forms of that cell's matrices and explicitly defined forms. The theorem fractional_breakdown_of_cell_system states that if data of these kinds satisfy CellSystemCondition, then FractionalBreakdownOnSpace and FractionalBreakdownOnTorus hold for α = 501/500, that is, the conclusions of fractional_breakdown_on_space and fractional_breakdown_on_torus hold for that exponent. No such data are given in this package. The file comparator-fractional-cell-system.json lists this theorem.

CellLayerChallenge.lean concerns the equations of Challenge.lean. It defines, among other things, cell layer data, which include the data of an extended certificate (ExtendedCertificateData in ExtendedCertificateChallenge.lean, not assumed to satisfy IsExtendedCertificate), instance data, a cell calculus entry, which depends on the cell layer data, a tree entry and cell layer entries, which depend on the cell layer data and on the entries before them, coercive numbers and reduction entries, and, in the namespace CellLayerSystem, a condition CellSystemCondition on data of these seven kinds, made of fifteen conditions. Among them are the conditions CertificateClauses on the extended certificate data and conditions relating the cell layer data to the other data. The theorems breakdownOnSpace_of_cell_system_condition, breakdownOnTorus_of_cell_system_condition and breakdownOnSpaceAndTorus_of_cell_system_condition state that if data of these kinds satisfy CellSystemCondition, then BreakdownOnSpace holds, BreakdownOnTorus holds, and both hold, respectively; these are the conclusions of breakdown_on_space and breakdown_on_torus. No such data are given in this package. CellLayerChallenge.lean imports ExtendedCoerciveChallenge.lean and repeats in one file, for reading, the further definitions that these three theorems use, together with the three theorems. CellLayerTheorems.lean states the same three theorems on top of the library module Breakdown/CertCell/StatementDefs.lean and the modules it imports, which contain these definitions; each declaration of CellLayerChallenge.lean other than the three theorems has the same type as the declaration of the same name there and, up to proofs, the same definition. The file comparator-cell-layer.json lists the three theorems as stated in CellLayerTheorems.lean.

It depends on Mathlib.
