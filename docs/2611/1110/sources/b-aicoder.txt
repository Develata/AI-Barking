Starting 2026-11-07, many developers on X complained that Claude Opus 5.5 felt "nerfed" in Claude Code: ignoring instructions, repeating mistakes and spawning sub-agents for small jobs. User Shane Melaugh (@shanemelaugh) posted that he had Gemini 4 Argon analyze 1,400 of his Claude Code sessions and found that around November 3 the model name stayed the same but its reasoning style took a sharp turn; at capture the post had about 966 likes, 134 replies and 112k impressions. T3 CEO Theo (@theo) later said that after digging in, Anthropic appears to be rolling out a change to the reasoning summarizer per account, and that he saw no sign of degraded capability. Anthropic had not commented publicly at the time of writing. This is a community debate, not a confirmed downgrade or an official announcement.

In-Depth Technical Analysis

Core Background & Industry Pain Points

"Did they quietly nerf the model?" comes up for almost every flagship model. From November 7 a new wave of complaints on X targeted Claude Opus 5.5: users said that in Claude Code it ignored instructions, repeated the same mistakes and spawned sub-agents even for a typo fix, and X's trending summaries picked it up. For teams that lean on coding agents, the hard part is telling whether the model changed, the tool changed, or their own prompts and projects changed.

Architecture Highlights & Underlying Mechanisms

The focal post came from Shane Melaugh, who said he had Gemini 4 Argon analyze 1,400 of his Claude Code sessions and found that around November 3 the model name stayed the same but its reasoning style took a sharp turn. Theo dug in and offered another explanation: Anthropic appears to be rolling out a change to the reasoning summarizer per account. Per Claude's official docs, extended thinking returns summarized thinking blocks, so a different-looking "thinking" style does not by itself mean a weaker model. Separately, Claude Code 2.1.318 shipped on November 3 (UTC) and changed how sub-agents pick effort levels; Anthropic has not said whether that is related.

Benchmarks & Measured Results

Anthropic has published no regression results, and no independent benchmark confirms or rules out a downgrade. At capture Shane's post had about 966 likes, 134 replies and 113k impressions; Theo's follow-up had about 367 likes. Shane's analysis covers one user's sessions, judged by another model, and the raw data is not public.

Developer Hands-on Guide

Rather than going by feel, pin a few regression tasks in your own repo, record pass rate, time and tokens, and compare across Claude Code versions and effort settings. When a reasoning summary looks odd, trust the test results. This article summarizes an X debate and is not an Anthropic statement.

Einsia and Tsinghua Release WorkflowArena, a Multi-Agent Orchestration Benchmark: 145 Long-Horizon Tasks, 25 Claude Code and Codex Configurations, and Even Top-Ranked Opus 5.5 Fully Resolves Only 36.95%

On November 10, Einsia AI, with Tsinghua University and others, released WorkflowArena, a benchmark that tests whether a model can split a task, dispatch worker agents, write the workflow, and integrate the results on its own. It has 145 long-horizon tasks across 8 professional domains, scored by hidden verifiers, with every delegation recorded. The paper tests 25 configurations of 9 models in Claude Code and Codex: the best, Claude Opus 5.5 at max effort, fully resolves only 36.95% of tasks on average, the best SWE rate is 29.41%, and law never exceeds 9.09%. Claude models delegate rarely but in large groups, GPT models often and in small units, and workflows written by Opus raise weaker executors by 6.41 points on average. Code and paper are public.

Anthropic Publishes a Claude Model Selection Guide: Start Most Coding on Opus 5.5 at Medium, Tune Effort Before Switching Models, Put Subagents on Haiku 5.5

On November 9, Anthropic's developer blog claude.dev published a selection guide by Addy Osmani, "Which Claude model should you build with?"; the ClaudeDevs post drew about 9,200 likes and 12,000 bookmarks. The core advice: start most coding and knowledge work on Opus 5.5 at medium effort (the Claude Code default); drop to Sonnet 5.5 for well-scoped bug fixes and spec-driven work; use Haiku 5.5 for classification, routing, extraction, and log-reading subagents; and move up to Fable 5.2 for major refactors, migrations, and deep research. Before switching models, tune effort and give the model a way to verify its own work. The guide also lists prices, default effort levels, and compatibility differences that can trigger 400 errors when migrating.

X Discussion: Ethan Mollick had Claude build a 3D "Portals" site on its own; about 25,000 agent runs made 471 worlds, and the agents grew a 204-section, 87,500-word rule book

Wharton professor Ethan Mollick shared an experiment on X on November 9: from a one-paragraph description, Claude built a site that flies endlessly through 3D worlds linked by portals. Twenty hours in there were 204 worlds; the open-source repo now holds 471 worlds and about 1.4 million lines of JavaScript. On his blog he says the agent teams later built their own review bureaucracy, with a rule book that grew to 204 sections, slowing output from hundreds of worlds a day to about a dozen. The post drew 1,262 likes and 158 replies in about 10 hours.
