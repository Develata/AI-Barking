Anthropic's AI model Claude Opus 5.5 may have seen a drop in reasoning performance around the 3rd of this month. [Photo: Anthropic]

[DigitalToday reporter Jae-won Choi] Anthropic's AI model Claude Opus 5.5 has become embroiled in suspicions that its reasoning ability has recently deteriorated. Some developers said the model's response style and problem-solving ability changed, but it was also raised as a possibility that the method of summarising its thought process changed rather than any actual performance drop.

On the 10th (local time), Japanese media outlet Gigazine reported that developer Shane Melaugh (셰인 메로) said an analysis of about 1,400 Claude Code usage logs showed clear changes in sentences displaying the AI's thought process and its work methods from the 3rd.

Melaugh analysed logs from the 9th of last month to the 7th of this month using Google's Gemini 4 Argon. As a result, the share of thought-summary sentences starting with "I'm" surged to 61.3 percent from 20 to 30 percent. By contrast, expressions such as "I should" and "I need to" fell to 10.9 percent from about 50 percent.

He also claimed there was a difference in the amount of reasoning. Comparing about 29,400 interactions around the 3rd of this month, Melaugh found the median number of thought tokens shown before the first task fell about 36 percent to 54 from 84. Error indicators related to code execution and fixes also rose 47 percent.

There were differences in an actual coding test as well. When asked to fix the same audio playback error, a model set to reasoning intensity "high" failed to solve the problem even after 160 interactions. Under the "xhigh" setting, it succeeded in fixing it in 5 interactions.

Still, those results alone make it difficult to conclude that the model itself has become less capable. According to Anthropic's official documentation, the thought content shown to users is not the AI's actual internal reasoning but a summary generated by a separate model.

Developer Theo Browne (테오 브라운) offered a different interpretation after analysing about 458,000 responses. He confirmed a change in the writing style of reasoning summaries, but said the timing of its application differed by account, and that the average number of thought tokens per response increased to 413 from 369.

Browne said he did not find signs of performance degradation in his environment and raised the possibility that Anthropic is changing the reasoning-summary method on an account-by-account basis.

Melaugh also acknowledged that the investigation was not a rigorous performance evaluation. So far, it has not been confirmed whether Anthropic actually reduced the model's performance, and further verification is needed.
