Google Promised in 2023 That Humans Wouldn't Read Your Gmail Through Its AI. Now Its Contractors Can

Google's Gemini help pages say human reviewers, including contractors, can see excerpts of Gmail and Drive content from connected apps, sometimes the whole email. A 2023 promise now carries an October 16 caveat.

Louis Corneloup [https://www.linkedin.com/in/louis-corneloup-0036b5138/]Founder, Dupple · Updated November 10, 2026 · 3 min read

In September 2023, when Google first let its chatbot read your inbox, it made a clear promise: content from Gmail, Docs and Drive "is not seen by human reviewers" and is not used to train the model. That sentence is still on Google's blog [https://blog.google/products/bard/google-bard-new-features-update-sept-2023/]. Right after it now sits a note dated October 16, 2026, saying that for newer features the same data "can be used to train generative AI models" and "can also be subject to human review in limited circumstances."

The change went viral this week after people dug into what those limited circumstances look like in practice. Google's own help page spells it out.

What reviewers can see

The rules live on Gemini's Connected Apps help page [https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16836988?hl=en], which covers Personal Intelligence, the feature that lets Gemini pull from Gmail, Drive, Docs, Calendar, Photos, Wallet and more on a personal Google account. It says "a subset of Connected Apps data processed by Gemini is reviewed by human reviewers (including trained service providers)," meaning outside contractors, who grade Gemini's answers and write better ones.

What they review are the summaries, excerpts and inferences Gemini draws from your emails and files. Google says it does not train "directly" on your inbox or Drive, but it does train on those excerpts. And there is a catch in the fine print: when an email or file is short or especially relevant to the question, "the summary may just be the email or file." In other words, the full text.

Google says it strips the account link and reduces personal details before contractors see anything, unless it is dealing with abuse or harm. Send feedback on an answer, though, and reviewers "may also review some of your Gmail inbox or Photos library media." The page ends with a blunt warning: don't connect apps that hold confidential information you wouldn't want a reviewer to see.

Three years, and delete doesn't help

Once a conversation has been reviewed, it stays. Gemini's privacy hub [https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en] says reviewed data is kept for up to three years, disconnected from your account, and the help page says deleting your Gemini activity won't remove anything that trained reviewers have already seen.

None of this is brand new. The same human review language has been on the help page since Personal Intelligence launched in January, and the October 16 note mostly brings the old blog post in line with it. It also lands on top of an earlier rewrite, when Google changed its privacy policy [https://gotechpresso.com/blog/google-gmail-photos-ads-privacy-policy-personal-intelligence-loophole] so that Gmail, Drive and Photos content can shape ads for people who connect those apps.

Work and school accounts play by different rules. Google's Workspace privacy hub [https://support.google.com/a/answer/15706919?hl=en] says business and education content "is not human reviewed or otherwise used for Generative AI model training outside your domain without permission." The contractor clause is for everyone else: people using Gemini on the personal Google accounts where their private email actually lives.
