MAGI-2 vs Seedance 2.5 vs Kling 4.0: One 30-Second Prompt, Measured

MAGI-2 vs Seedance 2.5 vs Kling 4.0: One 30-Second Prompt, Measured

Table of Contents

MAGI-2, Seedance 2.5 and Kling 4.0 all generate 30 seconds of video with sound in one request. On November 25, 2026, the day after Sand.ai released MAGI-2, we sent the same four-shot script to all three, once each, and measured what came back: where the cuts landed, whether the two spoken lines were said, which details of the characters survived, how long each render took and what it cost.

Which of the three followed a 30-second shot list most closely? Kling 4.0, in this run. Its three cuts landed within 0.08 seconds of the times in the script and it kept every clothing detail. MAGI-2 was within 0.44 seconds, kept the yellow raincoat and Mei's navy but left the hood up, and was the only one to shoot the last shot from the low angle the script asked for. Seedance 2.5 was within 0.50 seconds and changed two colors. All three spoke both lines word for word, in the right shots. MAGI-2 was the cheapest at $2.40 for 1080p and by far the slowest to arrive, at 85 minutes.

Shot 3 of the same script, left to right: MAGI-2, Seedance 2.5, Kling 4.0. The script asks for a yellow raincoat with the hood down.

The 30-second script all three models received

The script was written to test what a 30-second limit is for: several shots in one generation, with timing, two characters, speech and a sound bed. Every detail in it can be checked in the result.

PromptCopy

30-second scene in four shots with hard cuts. A rainy night at a small ramen stall under a red awning on a quiet side street: warm lantern light, rising steam, wet asphalt reflecting neon signs. Characters: Mei, a cook in her fifties with short grey hair, a navy apron and rolled-up sleeves. Tom, a bike courier in his twenties wearing a yellow raincoat with the hood down, wet dark hair.

Shot 1 (0-7s): wide shot from across the street, the camera slowly pushes in toward the stall. Tom parks his bicycle and ducks under the awning.

Shot 2 (7-15s): medium close-up of Mei behind the counter, ladling broth into a bowl. She looks up and says: "Last bowl of the night. You made it just in time."

Shot 3 (15-23s): over-the-shoulder shot from behind Mei, looking at Tom on his stool. He wipes the rain off his face, smiles and says: "Smells like it was worth the ride."

Shot 4 (23-30s): cut on the action as Mei sets the bowl down on the counter. Low-angle close-up of the bowl; Tom lifts noodles with chopsticks, steam drifts past his face, and the camera tilts up to his eyes closing.

Sound: steady rain on the awning, broth simmering, the bowl touching the counter, distant traffic. No music. Realistic, cinematic, shallow depth of field.

The prompt went in unchanged, with no prompt rewriting and no retries. MAGI-2 ran through a third-party API that resells it at Sand.ai's list price, because Sand.ai's own platform was not accepting top-ups yet. The three resolutions differ, so this test does not score sharpness; it scores whether each model did what the script said.

Cut times were read from the files with ffmpeg's scene detection. The dialogue was transcribed with Whisper, with a timestamp for each word. Clothing, framing and camera moves were checked on frames pulled from each shot.

MAGI-2: right colors, low-angle last shot, hood left up

MAGI-2, 30 seconds, generated at 1080p and shown here at 720p without sound.

MAGI-2 cut slightly early each time: 0.12, 0.20 and 0.44 seconds ahead of the script. Mei's line starts at 11.5 seconds and Tom's at 20.1, each inside its own shot, and both are word for word.

It read the setting more literally than the other two. The stall is a shopfront under a red awning on a narrow street, with neon reflected on the road, and Tom rides in on a bicycle before the first cut. It was also the only model to place the camera low for the last shot: the bowl enters the frame in Mei's hand at counter height, then the camera tilts up through the steam to Tom's closed eyes.

The miss is the hood. The script says "with the hood down" and MAGI-2 keeps it up from the first frame to the last. It is at least consistent about it: the raincoat is the same in shots 1, 3 and 4, and Tom's face and wet curly hair match between shots 3 and 4.

Seedance 2.5: all the action beats, two wrong colors

Seedance 2.5, 30 seconds at 480p, shown without sound.

Seedance 2.5 held the first two shots a little long (cuts at 7.38 and 15.21 seconds) and left the third half a second early. Both lines are word for word; Mei starts at 12.0 seconds and Tom finishes his at about 21.3.

It staged the action most completely. Tom arrives by bicycle, enters with his hood up and has it down by the time he speaks, wipes his face, and the fourth shot opens on the bowl coming down in two hands before he lifts the noodles and closes his eyes. Mei's short grey hair matches the script.

Two colors do not. Tom's raincoat is black instead of yellow, with an invented logo on the chest, and Mei's apron is cream instead of navy. In a 30-second script with a dozen details, color words attached to clothing were the ones that slipped. The bowl in the last shot is framed from above rather than from a low angle.

Kling 4.0: cuts on the frame, every clothing detail kept

Kling 4.0, 30 seconds at 720p, shown without sound.

Kling 4.0's cuts are at 7.00, 15.04 and 23.08 seconds. At 24 frames per second, 0.04 seconds is one frame, so the first cut is on the exact frame and the other two are one and two frames late. Mei's line starts at 11.1 seconds and Tom's at 19.9, both word for word.

Every clothing instruction holds: yellow raincoat, hood down, navy apron, rolled sleeves. The one character detail it loosened is Mei's hair, which is grey but a chin-length bob rather than short.

Its reading of the place is the loosest of the three. The stall is a free-standing cart under a red canopy on an open street, with "ramen" written across it in Japanese, rather than a stall on a side street, and Tom is already beside the cart with his bicycle when the clip starts instead of arriving. The last shot shows the bowl from above and tilts up to Tom looking down at his noodles; his eyes lower but do not clearly close.

MAGI-2's 85 minutes is a launch-week number, not a property of the model: the request sat in a reseller's queue one day after release. It is still worth knowing before you plan a deadline around it this week.

The price gap is the more durable finding. MAGI-2 has one tier, 1080p at $0.08 per second, so its 30-second clip cost about half of Kling 4.0's at 720p. Kling 4.0 at 1080p lists at $0.235 per second, or $7.05 for 30 seconds. The other side of a single tier is that MAGI-2 has no cheap draft: a test take costs the same $2.40 as a final one, where Seedance 2.5 lets you draft at 480p and re-render the keeper.

Which 30-second model fits which job

A scene cut to music or to a voice-over, where a cut has to land on a beat: Kling 4.0. One or two frames of error is inside what an editor would accept without trimming.

A 1080p take on a small budget, or a script where camera height and angle matter: MAGI-2. Check every wardrobe detail in the result; one instruction was ignored here.

A scene built from references, or one you want to draft cheaply and then finish at 1080p or 4K: Seedance 2.5. This test was text only, so it did not use what Seedance 2.5 is strongest at: up to 50 reference files per request. When colors must hold, give it a reference image instead of a color word.

Dialogue: no difference in this run. All three delivered both lines, in order, in the right shot, with the speaker's mouth moving on the line.

What one run per model cannot tell you

One generation per model is a sample. A second run of the same prompt could put Seedance 2.5's raincoat in yellow or MAGI-2's hood down; this test does not measure how often each model gets a detail right, only that each got these ones right or wrong once.

The three clips are at different resolutions, so picture quality is not compared. Sound was checked for the spoken lines only; the mix, the effects and the "no music" instruction were not scored. And a text-only prompt leaves out references, first and last frames and video editing, where the three models differ more than they do here.

FAQ

Is MAGI-2 better than Seedance 2.5?

Not across the board. In this run MAGI-2 kept more of the scripted details than Seedance 2.5 (clothing colors and the low camera angle) and cost less at 1080p, while Seedance 2.5 staged the action more completely and finished in 15 minutes instead of 85. Seedance 2.5 also takes more references and has 480p, 720p and 4K tiers, which MAGI-2 does not.

Is MAGI-2 better than Kling 4.0?

Kling 4.0 followed the timing and wardrobe of this script more exactly, and rendered in 9 minutes. MAGI-2 cost about half as much for a higher resolution and followed the setting and the camera angle more literally.

Which model has the most accurate cut timing?

Kling 4.0 in this test: its three cuts were 0.00, 0.04 and 0.08 seconds from the times in the prompt. MAGI-2's largest miss was 0.44 seconds and Seedance 2.5's was 0.50.

Can all three models generate dialogue?

Yes. All three produced both spoken lines word for word, in the shot the script assigned, with picture and sound from the same generation.

How much does a 30-second clip cost on each?

MAGI-2 is $2.40 at 1080p at Sand.ai's list price. Kling 4.0 is $4.71 at 720p and $7.05 at 1080p through the API. Seedance 2.5 is billed by tokens; on SeedVideo AI a 30-second clip is 92 credits at 480p and 183 at 720p at the base rate.

How long does a 30-second generation take?

In this test, 9 minutes for Kling 4.0 at 720p and 15 minutes for Seedance 2.5 at 480p. MAGI-2 took 85 minutes one day after its release, most of it waiting in a queue.
