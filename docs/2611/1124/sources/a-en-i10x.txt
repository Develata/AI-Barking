MAGI-2 Video Model: 30-Second 1080p Clips at a Seventh of Seedance’s Price

Sand.ai's MAGI-2 is a 480B video model that makes 30-second 1080p clips with sound in one pass and lists at $0.08 per second. Our comparison puts a 30-second clip at $2.40 against $17.07 on Seedance 2.5, and we flag four details to check before planning around it.

i10x Perspective [#i10x-perspective]

Sand.ai has launched MAGI-2, a 480-billion-parameter video model that generates up to 30 seconds of 1080p video with synchronized sound in a single pass, according to Sand.ai’s launch post of November 24 [https://sand.ai/blog/magi-2/]. The company’s own API lists it at $0.08 per second of output. Set against the published prices of its closest rivals, that makes MAGI-2 one of the cheapest ways to buy long 1080p clips from a model near the top of the blind-test rankings. A few details in the launch materials still do not line up, though, and one of them decides whether you can use the API at all today.

What Sand.ai scaled up

MAGI-2 follows a research preview from August that had about 114 billion parameters, of which roughly 6 billion were active per step. The full model is a mixture-of-experts design with 480 billion parameters and about 28 billion active, trained on roughly three times as much data (4 trillion tokens). Sand.ai says this lifted its average Elo across four tasks on the Artificial Analysis Video Arena from 998.5 to 1150.3.

In practice the model takes one text prompt plus up to 20 references in a request: up to 10 images, 5 video clips and 5 audio clips. It supports six aspect ratios, cuts at set points on a timeline, edits existing footage and writes dialogue with lip sync, sound effects and music in the same pass. The API accepts prompts of up to 15,000 characters. Sand.ai is open about weak spots: very fast motion can still break continuity, 2D animation in medium and wide shots can look too smooth, and complex soundscapes lack depth.

At list prices, a 30-second 1080p clip costs $2.40 on MAGI-2 and $17.07 on Seedance 2.5, so MAGI-2 comes in at about 14% of ByteDance’s price, or roughly one seventh. Google’s Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash is close on price per second at about $0.104 for upsampled 1080p, but it stops at 15 seconds, so a 30-second scene means two generations and a cut. For a team that produces 100 thirty-second ad variants a month, the difference between MAGI-2 and Seedance 2.5 is about $240 against $1,707 before rerolls. Since most real projects need several attempts per shot, the gap grows with every retry.

Where it ranks in blind tests

On the Higgsfield Video Arena, where directors, animators and designers vote on unlabeled pairs, MAGI-2 entered in second place with a score of 1146 and a 60.5% overall win rate (snapshot of November 24). Only Seedance 2.5 (1219) ranks higher and won 61% of their direct matchups. MAGI-2 beat Seedance 2.0 in 71% of pairs, Wan 3.0 in 79% and Kling 4.0 and MiniMax H3 in 83% each. Our look at the Seedance 2.5 API launch [https://i10x.ai/news/seedance-2-5-4k-api-volcano-engine-byteplus-draft-mode] shows what the leader offers for its higher price, including 4K and a cheap 480p draft mode, and our coverage of Kling 3.0’s benchmark run [https://i10x.ai/news/kling-3-0-tops-1080p-pro-ai-video-benchmark] shows how fast this ranking has been changing.

Four things to check before you plan around it

1. Paying for the API. Sand.ai’s pricing page currently says account top-ups are not supported yet. The $0.08 figure is a list price; check whether you can actually load credit before you budget for it.

2. Where it runs. Higgsfield announced MAGI-2 as available “exclusively” on its platform at 2.5 credits per second, while Sand.ai documents its own generation endpoint. Expect both routes, with different effective prices depending on your Higgsfield plan.

3. Model size. Sand.ai says 480 billion parameters; Higgsfield’s announcement says 400 billion. The technical report Sand.ai has promised should settle it.

4. Open weights. Sand.ai says it will open-source MAGI-2, but gives no date and no license. Even when the weights arrive, a model with 28 billion active parameters out of 480 billion is a multi-GPU server job, not something for a single workstation.

i10x Perspective

MAGI-2 is the first model in the top two of a professional blind test that sells 1080p video at a price close to what mid-tier models cost. That puts pressure on ByteDance, whose Seedance 2.5 is still better but charges about seven times as much per second. We expect a price response from the leaders within weeks, either through cheaper 1080p tiers or longer free draft modes. For now, the sensible split is to test MAGI-2 for long single takes and reference-heavy scenes, where its 20-reference input and 30-second length save editing work, and keep the most expensive model for the few hero shots where the last bit of quality matters.
