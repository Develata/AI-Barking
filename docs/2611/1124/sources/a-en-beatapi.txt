MAGI-2 is a native audio-video generation model released by Sand.ai on November 24, 2026 (PT), built on a 480B-parameter (~28B active) single-stream Transformer with Multi-Head Latent MoE. In Sand.ai's lineup, MAGI-1 was the 2025 open-source autoregressive video model, MAGI-2 Preview was the August 2026 open-weights research model (114B total, ~6B active), and MAGI-2 is the full-scale flagship version built by scaling Preview more than 4x.

MAGI-2 generates 4 to 30 seconds of synchronized 1080p, 25 fps audio and video in a single request. You can feed it up to 20 reference images, videos, and audio clips, direct shot cuts on a timeline, or edit parts of an existing video.

Both Sand.ai's official API and Higgsfield's API price MAGI-2 at $0.08 per second at 1080p (0.5 CNY/s in China). An 8-second 1080p clip costs $0.64, which sits well below other multi-reference flagship models. Even so, you cannot wire MAGI-2 into high-concurrency production traffic yet. Sand.ai's API platform does not offer self-serve top-ups, Higgsfield caps every account at 1 concurrent job across all MAGI-2 modes, and the 480B open weights are not out. Solo creators can test 30-second shots and multi-reference workflows today on VidMuse, Higgsfield, or LibTV. Engineering teams that need metered, high-concurrency video generation today still need live APIs such as Seedance 2.5 [/seedance-2.5-api], Wan 3.0 [/wan-3-0-api], Kling 4.0 [/kling-v4-api], or MiniMax H3 [/minimax-h3-api].

Quick answer

Sand.ai scaled MAGI-2 from 114B total parameters (~6B active) in MAGI-2 Preview to 480B total (~28B active, 5.8% sparsity) and tripled pre-training data to 4T tokens.

MAGI-2 pairs Multi-Head Latent MoE with Head Parallel so cross-GPU communication stays fixed regardless of top-k routing. At 480B scale and equal compute, pre-training runs about 22% faster than single-head LatentMoE. During 1080p upscaling, the upscaler reads the original reference images again to rebuild small faces and on-screen text.

Sand.ai reports a 4-task average Elo of 1150.3 on Artificial Analysis Video Arena v2.1 (+151.8 over Preview), and Higgsfield's 6-model blind arena ranks MAGI-2 second at 1146. Public Artificial Analysis video leaderboards do not list the 480B model yet, and extreme high-speed motion and layered soundscapes remain weak spots.

Hugging Face under sand-ai still lists only Apache 2.0 MAGI-1 and MAGI-2-preview. Sand.ai has not published the 480B weights, a release date, or a license.

What MAGI-2 outputs and four real limits

MAGI-2 generates video and audio together inside one single-stream Transformer sequence. Motion, lip-sync, ambient sound, foley, and music come out of the same backbone. For shots where sound has to land on an exact visual beat, like a box lid snapping shut, a character speaking mid-turn, or rain carrying over a cut, you skip separate audio post-sync.

Single requests cover 4 to 30 seconds at 1080p and 25 fps. A 30-second window lets you block a setup, action beat, and reaction close-up inside one generation instead of stitching 5-second clips where wardrobe, lighting, and room tone drift apart.

Four hard limits apply before you write any code:

1080p only, no draft tier: Both API endpoints output 1080p across six aspect ratios (21:9, 16:9, 4:3, 1:1, 3:4, 9:16). You cannot drop to 480p or 720p for cheaper prompt iteration.

Per-request reference caps: Native requests accept 1 prompt plus up to 20 media items: up to 10 images (under 30 MB each, 16 to 10,000 px per side, 16 MP max total pixels), 5 videos (MP4 or MOV, H.264/H.265, up to 200 MB each, 2 to 30 seconds per clip and 30 seconds total), and 5 audio files (WAV or MP3, up to 15 MB each, 2 to 30 seconds per clip and 30 seconds total).

Start/end frames exclude general references: The Ark-compatible endpoint supports first_frame and last_frame (making text optional), but forbids mixing them with general image, video, or audio references. The native v2 endpoint does not support start or end frames at all.

15,000-codepoint prompt ceiling: Sand.ai's Create Generation spec [https://platform.sand.ai/docs/create-generation/] caps prompts at 15,000 Unicode codepoints. Some launch summaries cite 20,000 characters; validate client inputs against 15,000.

How Sand.ai trained 480B parameters: architecture, data, and upscaling

Scaling a video model to 480B usually breaks on cluster communication and load imbalance. Standard Expert Parallel runs data-dependent top-k routing first, then sends full tokens across GPUs via All-to-All traffic. Because video sequences mix image patches, audio frames, and reference clips, dynamic routing crowds some GPUs while starving others, and cross-GPU traffic scales linearly with top-k.

Starting with MAGI-2 Preview [https://sand.ai/blog/magi-2-preview/], Sand.ai left MAGI-1's chunked autoregressive design behind and moved to a single-stream Transformer with Multi-Head Latent MoE. Each token splits into independent heads, and each head has its own router and expert pool. Multi-GPU training uses Head Parallel: GPUs exchange fixed-shape latent slices by head assignment first, then run data-dependent top-k routing locally on each GPU. Cross-GPU traffic stays fixed at N · H_latent, decoupled from top-k, which eliminates dynamic load skew.

Sand.ai rewrote its training stack around that layout. MagiFSDP decouples static slice exchanges from sharded expert parameters, gradients, and optimizer states. MagiMoE speeds up local execution across thousands of fine-grained experts per layer and overlaps shared-expert compute, activation recomputation, and gradient communication. MagiMuon extends the Muon optimizer to sharded expert matrices. At 480B scale with matched total FLOPs and optimal configs for each model, Multi-Head Latent MoE trains about 22% faster than single-head LatentMoE. In matched-parameter ablations, an 8-head setup reaches the same validation loss with about 23% less compute than single-head LatentMoE and stays ahead of Standard MoE throughout.

Standard Expert Parallel routes dynamically across GPUs; MAGI-2 exchanges fixed latent slices first and runs top-k routing locally.

Two data and upscaling choices do just as much work on visual quality:

Unfiltered realism in the 4T pre-training corpus: Sand.ai labeled roughly 1B samples in three weeks without stripping out multi-person interaction, fast cuts, on-screen text, or noisy real-world audio in pursuit of a uniform cinematic look. Once 480B parameters absorb that distribution, structural mistakes like extra fingers and limb clipping drop during pre-training, leaving post-training RL free to focus on lighting depth, surface texture, and hair detail.

Re-injecting original reference images during 1080p upscaling: The 480B base model generates low-resolution latent audio-video and anchors face and text trajectories. A dedicated spatio-temporal upscaler then brings the clip to 1080p at 25 fps while reading the user's original reference images a second time, rebuilding small faces and screen text from the actual source pixels instead of guessing.

MAGI-2's two-stage pipeline re-injects original reference images directly into the 1080p video upscaler.

On the hardware side, MAGI-2 already runs on domestic Chinese accelerators including Hygon and Moore Threads (vLLM-Omni also previously ran MAGI-2 Preview on 8 Moore Threads MTT S5000 GPUs). Once Sand.ai opens the 480B weights, teams outside NVIDIA clusters will have a viable deployment path.

Why pre-training skills got weaker in the final release

When Sand.ai tested the 480B pre-trained checkpoint, the team turned external LLM prompt rewriting (Prompt Enhancement, or PE) off to verify what the video model itself had learned. With PE off, three unprompted behaviors showed up. First, basic visual reasoning: on shape-marking, symmetry, maze, and 9x9 Sudoku tests, the base model kept given numbers intact and filled empty cells by the rules, though errors still occurred. Second, autonomous directing: given only character/scene images and high-level story beats, it chose framing, shot-reverse-shot angles, and close-ups on its own. Third, direct cross-lingual dialogue: it translated speech and synced lips in another language without an LLM rewriting the script first.

After fine-tuning and RL on a few thousand curated cinematic clips, visual polish jumped sharply, while those reasoning, autonomous directing, and cross-lingual behaviors became much harder to trigger. Sand.ai compares this to the "alignment tax" seen in LLM post-training: a small, aesthetics-heavy dataset narrows the output distribution for cleaner visuals at the cost of broader pre-trained generalization.

In practice, do not expect the shipped MAGI-2 release to handle logic puzzles or full shot planning from a short raw prompt. When you care about camera angles, blocking, and dialogue order, turn enhance_prompt on or use a text model upstream to break your brief into timed beats, explicit reference tags, and sound cues. That matches MAGI-2-preview's split architecture (which uses Qwen3.5-27B as its text encoder): let a text model write the script and let MAGI-2 render it.

If you already have a Sand.ai API key for api.sand.ai, watch out for five documented constraints before writing your client:

Pick the right endpoint for start/end frames: Native v2 (POST /v2/generations) passes 1 required text part plus media references in parts[], rejects role, and does not support keyframes. If you need first_frame, last_frame, or drop-in compatibility with the Volcengine Ark Python SDK (volcenginesdkarkruntime), call the Ark-compatible endpoint [https://platform.sand.ai/docs/ark-generations/] (POST /compatible/ark/api/v3/contents/generations/tasks) with base_url set to https://api.sand.ai/compatible/ark/api/v3.

Omit watermark completely: Both endpoints reject the watermark field with HTTP 400, even when you explicitly pass "watermark": false. The Ark endpoint also rejects non-null values for unsupported fields such as frames, callback_url, draft, and return_last_frame.

Do not auto-retry timeouts blindly: Creation endpoints are not idempotent. Submissions return 202 Accepted or a task ID, and retrying after a network timeout or 5xx can bill twice. Inspect the response for details.generation_id before polling or retrying.

Stagger polling and download finished videos quickly: Default Sand.ai quotas allow 20 creates and 20 reads per 60 seconds with 5 concurrent jobs. Poll every 10 seconds for a single job, or every 20 seconds when running 5 concurrent jobs. Finished content.video_url links expire in 1 hour (re-fetching the task refreshes the signed URL).

Parse asset IDs as 64-bit integers in JavaScript: Uploading media through POST /v1/assets [https://platform.sand.ai/docs/upload-asset/] returns an id as an int64 JSON number. Standard JS JSON.parse can lose precision; parse int64 IDs losslessly into exact decimal strings before passing assetId or asset://<ID>.

FAQ

Is MAGI-2 400B or 480B parameters?

480B. Sand.ai specifies 480B total parameters and ~28B active parameters, more than 4x larger than the 114B / ~6B MAGI-2 Preview. Higgsfield's "400B" figure is a rounded shorthand, not a smaller model.

Is MAGI-2 open source yet? Can I download the 480B weights?

Not yet. Hugging Face under sand-ai currently hosts only MAGI-1 and MAGI-2-preview (both Apache 2.0). Sand.ai plans to open-source MAGI-2 and publish a full technical report later, with no release date or 480B license announced.

Is MAGI-2 only available on Higgsfield?

No. Higgsfield's "exclusively on Higgsfield" phrasing covers its early third-party distribution partnership. Creators can also use MAGI-2 on Sand.ai's own VidMuse web app and LibTV in China, and developers can apply directly through Sand.ai's API Platform.

Why is MAGI-2 locked to 1080p, and how do I keep 30-second test costs down?

Both the native v2 and Ark-compatible endpoints fix output at 1080p and 25 fps with no 480p or 720p draft tier. Since a full 30-second clip costs $2.40 at $0.08/s, test character likeness, pacing, and sound cues with 4- to 8-second runs ($0.32–$0.64) first, then raise duration to 15–30 seconds once your reference set works.

Should I use Sand.ai's native v2 endpoint or the Ark-compatible endpoint?

Use native v2 (POST /v2/generations) when your job centers on multimodal references (up to 10 images, 5 videos, and 5 audio clips). Switch to the Ark-compatible endpoint (POST /compatible/ark/api/v3/contents/generations/tasks) whenever you need first_frame or last_frame control, or already use the Volcengine Ark Python SDK. Remember that keyframe mode cannot be combined with general references, and both endpoints forbid the watermark field.

How should I choose between MAGI-2, Seedance 2.5, Wan 3.0, and Kling 4.0?

Test MAGI-2 when you want 15- to 30-second single takes with heavy multimodal references near $0.08/s at 1080p (best suited to solo work or small test batches right now). Pick Seedance 2.5 [/seedance-2.5-api] for top blind-test quality when budget allows, Wan 3.0 [/wan-3-0-api] for text-to-video and 480p/720p/1080p cost tiers, Kling 4.0 [/kling-v4-api] for high-concurrency 30-second production jobs with reference video today, and MiniMax H3 [/minimax-h3-api] or Veo 3.1 [/veo-3.1-api] Fast for high-volume 1080p batches at the lowest per-clip cost.

Next step: If you are making clips by hand, run one 15- to 30-second test on VidMuse or Higgsfield with a character sheet, a location photo, and one line of dialogue tied to a clear physical sound, then inspect faces past the 20-second mark and any fast-motion frames. If you are wiring an app backend, check the five API constraints above against your concurrency and keyframe needs; if you need metered production traffic today, start on Seedance 2.5 [/seedance-2.5-api], Wan 3.0 [/wan-3-0-api], or MiniMax H3 [/minimax-h3-api] through BeatAPI and keep the model ID in an environment variable so you can benchmark MAGI-2 on the same script once public metered billing opens.
