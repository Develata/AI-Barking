CoC
The Chain of Causation

In a Chain of Causation, the physical outcome of one action sets the initial conditions for the next. Consider a robot grasping an apple. As the arm rises, the apple rises with it. The gripper carries the apple over a bowl. When it opens, the apple falls inside.

Training a model to sustain these chains begins with the data. We built PhysTracer to identify and annotate clips that preserve the links between actions and their physical outcomes. Each clip pairs a global scene description with timestamped captions that trace individual actions to their observable effects. During training, Event-Aligned Conditioning (EventAlign) binds each caption to its interval in the video latents. For Halo, the audio latents follow the same timeline. The teacher can attend across events to learn how one outcome sets up the next.

Physics-guided recursive self-improvement (RSI) targets the links the model repeatedly misses. If a character wades through water and the surface stays still, the agent looks for clips that show both the foot entering the water and the ripples that follow. We mix those clips into the training data. After retraining, the agent checks which responses improved and which regressed, then uses those results to guide the next round.

How the world responds

To steer a generated world, users need to see the result of each action in time to decide what to do next.

Physical interaction

On our held-out benchmark of 319 video cases, scaling the training set from 150K to 1.2M examples raised the Physical Score from 32.43 to 68.07 for ABot-World 0.5 Iris and from 41.50 to 84.32 for ABot-World 0.5 Halo. The score measures physical and causal consistency.

Action and Prompt Control

Keyboard controls let users move, turn, crouch, and sprint throughout a rollout. Language prompts can introduce events while the user keeps steering. During training, EventAlign lets each video latent attend directly to the local prompt for its own time interval. In this demo, “Incendio!” cues a burst of fire ahead of the rider, who continues past the scorched ground as the flames subside.

Streaming audiovisual generation

Halo generates video and audio together in a single 30B diffusion transformer. Each chunk pairs three video latent frames with audio latents covering the same interval. On four NVIDIA B200 GPUs, Halo streams 720p video at 24 FPS with synchronized audio.

FantasyDecoder reconstructs video frames and audio waveform samples as each chunk arrives. It reuses features from the previous chunk and refines the video on a compact grid before upsampling to full resolution.

On 22-frame, 480p clips, FantasyDecoder delivers 8.26× the video decoding throughput of the Wan2.2 causal decoder on an NVIDIA RTX A6000. It also improves PSNR by 0.79 dB and reduces temporal flicker by 13.9%.
