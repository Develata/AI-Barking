 The Lean 4 proof of the Riemann Hypothesis
 Checked by machine.
 Not yet read through by a mathematician.

 leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis [https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis]
 is the community repository for a Lean 4 formalization of the 2026 proofs of the Riemann
 Hypothesis and of GRH for Dirichlet L-functions. The README of
 lsernau/grh-lean [https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean],
 the edition upstream main is based on, says where it stands in these words:
 “The proof has passed machine checks only … No mathematician has read it through, and no
 paper comes with it.”
 Its “readable edition” is Lean code laid out for reading, about 300,000 lines with a guide,
 not a written paper. Levent Alpöge’s
 announcement [https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf]
 says he chose to present only Lean for now and that a proper write-up is still to come.

 So the work that is open to contributors is work on the code: making it faster to build,
 easier to read, and better connected to Mathlib and to the literature. This site explains
 how to read the proof and how to send a pull request to it, and it keeps a dated record of
 what the repository has done.

 The critical line, laid on its side
 height t from 0 to 52

 This is the line Re(s) = 1/2, turned so that height runs left to right, the way
 the timeline on this site runs. The ten dots are the first ten nontrivial zeros of ζ, at
 their published heights:
 14.134725, 21.022040, 25.010858, 30.424876, 32.935062, 37.586178, 40.918719, 43.327073, 48.005151, 49.773832.
 The curve is Hardy’s function Z(t), which is real and is zero
 exactly where ζ(1/2 + it) is. Your browser computed it when the page loaded, from
 the Euler-Maclaurin formula, and it crosses the line at each dot.
 The hypothesis says every other zero, without end, sits on this same line. A picture can
 show ten. The Lean proof is about all of them.

About this site

 This is an independent site. It is not written or endorsed by the maintainers of the upstream
 repository. A number on it names the commit it was measured at and who measured it, whether
 that is upstream’s own README, a published verification record, or this site’s scripts. Where
 a figure is this site’s own measurement and cannot be reproduced from a public source, the
 page says so.

 Upstream’s README asks contributors for three things: a proof that is accessible (builds
 without large amounts of compute), readable (code quality moving toward Mathlib’s), and
 integrated with the community (references to prior work, reuse of existing formalization
 projects). It also names what it does not want: reports of your own machine’s builds or
 comparator runs, and files the proof does not need.
 Read the README [https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/main/README.md]
 before you start; it is short and it is the authority.
