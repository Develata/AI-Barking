OpenAI says heavy ChatGPT users may hit limits sooner as it merges Chat and Work

12:03 PM PST · November 25, 2026

OpenAI has started rolling out a unified Chat and Work experience in ChatGPT to a few of its users, amid criticism from some subscribers that the merger could restrict their usage.

The ChatGPT maker began introducing the update to a small group of Pro subscribers, product lead Thibault “Tibo” Sottiaux said [https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2125284868414394550] in a post on X on Tuesday. The rollout follows OpenAI’s earlier announcement [https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/openai-is-finally-merging-chatgpts-chat-and-work-tabs/] of the merger in an email to users. An OpenAI spokesperson told TechCrunch that while most users are not expected to face more frequent usage resets, a small fraction of its heaviest users may hit their limits sooner.

That’s among the concerns raised [https://x.com/Mr_Salio/status/2125288213975605724] by some ChatGPT subscribers [https://x.com/StefanoGPT/status/2125287717642617266] on social media and OpenAI’s community forum [https://community.openai.com/t/feedback-please-reconsider-merging-the-chat-and-work-tabs-or-add-a-toggle/1417767] recently. The users fear that merging the usage limits for Chat and Work could leave them with less access [https://www.reddit.com/r/codex/comments/1xs110r/openai_is_merging_chat_and_work_but_are_they/] to advanced reasoning models than they currently have, even as they continue paying the same subscription fees [https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/58742].

Under the new system, Chat and Work will share a single usage meter across ChatGPT, the OpenAI spokesperson told TechCrunch. Conversations using the Instant model will, however, remain unlimited and will not count toward the shared allowance. Users can also continue using Instant after exhausting their usage limits. The spokesperson added that OpenAI would continue working to improve efficiency so subscribers could get more out of their plans.

While the rollout has started for a small group of users, OpenAI plans to bring shared usage limits more broadly in December, per its support page.

Similar to OpenAI, rival Anthropic also moved [https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/anthropic-merges-claude-chat-and-cowork-in-one-interface/] to bring its conversational chat service Claude and work-focused Cowork under one interface in September.

The changes come as AI companies face mounting infrastructure costs and growing demand for increasingly capable models, putting pressure on their computing resources. Bringing conversational and work-related tools into a single interface could also make AI chatbots simpler to use, allowing users to move between chats and more complex tasks without switching tabs.
