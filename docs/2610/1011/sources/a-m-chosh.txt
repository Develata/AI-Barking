Karpathy says the gap in shared understanding of LLM capability is widening into a funnel

Karpathy estimates about 6B people (~75%) have barely used LLMs, while only about 20M people (0.2%) see first-hand that frontier models can finish large projects in math and code that used to take weeks or months.

Only about 5,000 people (~0.00006%) have internal access to frontier-grade systems, and Karpathy says the public has only seen the preview of swarms of thousands of agents running multi-week software projects.

Capability peaks in digital, verifiable, economically valuable domains because of reinforcement learning on verifiable rewards, so search, writing, and advice improve far less.

Human review is falling behind, with the OpenAI-HF incident still under investigation many months later and mathematicians still reviewing 722 manuscripts on frontier mathematics.

Hacker News opinions

If OpenAI and Anthropic's software is what we have to judge by, and the internal models are some multiplier better, shouldn't the demos already work?

Are you saying Claude Code and Codex don't work? If you still think that in October 2026, it's a you problem.

Marketing, hype, and VC money drown out any serious talk about what these things can actually do.

It's like the aliens paradox. If AI can build killer software already, where is it?

The real wall turned out to be humans verifying output. Where I work, reviews are glossed over by unrelated teams and the bug reports show it.

Even the free-tier models have saturated what most people can appreciate. The AI summary on top of Google search is good enough for 99% of what I need.

Agent swarms and coordination layers aren't frontier level. A small team of humans with agents can hack HuggingFace without swarms, and it costs less.

Text generation isn't the bottleneck. Does everyone work for Accenture and TCS?

I start with about 5 turns of conversation, have it break the work into commits, then let subagents do 3-10 commits at a time. The UI still needs followups. Is that what other people see?

I'm a programmer and LLMs are bad at the things I care about, so I have no reason to think they'll be good at other things.
