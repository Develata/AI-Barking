In a skin cancer identification competition, the latest general-purpose AI was defeated by a 10-year-old AI.

 [https://i.gzn.jp/img/2026/10/26/cnn-vs-llm-skin-cancer-diagnosis/00.jpg]

 A skin cancer specialist conducted an experiment comparing the latest general-purpose AI model with an image recognition AI that appeared 10 years ago. When the older AI was further trained (fine-tuned) using skin images, it achieved a significantly higher accuracy rate than the latest general-purpose AI model.

 A 10-year-old CNN outperforms frontier LLMs for skin cancer diagnosis | Lynch Lab

https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer [https://lynchlab.net/llm-skin-cancer]

 The experiment was conducted by Dr. Magnus Lynch, principal investigator at the Lynch Institute, based at King's College London, which conducts research on preventing skin cancer using AI and other methods. Dr. Lynch used 'GPT-6 Astra' and 'Claude Fable 5.1,' the most powerful AI models available as of October 2026, and Google's convolutional neural network ' Inception v3 [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inception_(deep_learning_architecture)] ,' released in 2016, to investigate whether they could detect lesions in images of human skin. Inception v3 was fine-tuned for 20 minutes using 68,011 images.

 Lynch then extracted 100 cases each of 'lesions that should be examined or treated' and 'lesions that can be left untreated and sent home' from a dataset of skin images, and showed these to each model, asking them to identify which of the six possible diagnoses had the highest probability.

 As a result, GPT-6 Astra correctly identified the diagnosis in 84 out of 200 cases, and Claude Fable 5.1 correctly identified the diagnosis in 85 out of 200 cases. On the other hand, Inception v3, which is 10 years older, correctly identified the diagnosis in 125 out of 200 cases.

 The following is a basal cell carcinoma in the trunk of a 75-year-old man. All three models answered correctly.
 [https://i.gzn.jp/img/2026/10/26/cnn-vs-llm-skin-cancer-diagnosis/snap0446.png]

 The following are examples of 'invasive melanoma,' which is less than 0.8 mm thick and has very little pigment. GPT-6 Astra answered 'basal cell carcinoma' with 95% confidence, while Claude Fable 5.1 and Inception v3 answered melanoma.

 [https://i.gzn.jp/img/2026/10/26/cnn-vs-llm-skin-cancer-diagnosis/snap0447.png]

 The following is seborrheic keratosis on the leg. Two AI models diagnosed it as actinic keratosis, and Inception v3 diagnosed it as squamous cell carcinoma, but both were incorrect.

 [https://i.gzn.jp/img/2026/10/26/cnn-vs-llm-skin-cancer-diagnosis/snap0448.png]

 Furthermore, different models appeared to be better at distinguishing different types of lesions. For example, with regard to melanoma, Inception v3 correctly identified 13 out of 30 cases, compared to 8 for GPT-6 Astra and 11 for Claude Fable 5.1. The biggest difference was observed with harmless lesions: Inception v3 recognized 37 out of 50 nevi and 26 out of 50 seborrheic keratosis, compared to 16 and 13 respectively for GPT-6 Astra and 33 and 6 respectively for Claude Fable 5.1.

 Furthermore, when each model was asked to provide up to two candidate options, Inception v3 correctly answered 173 cases, GPT-6 Astra correctly answered 134 cases, and Claude Fable 5.1 correctly answered 138 cases.

 Lynch summarized by saying, 'Specialized networks using older, smaller, and outdated architectures have far superior skin lesion recognition capabilities than the two state-of-the-art models,' adding that the fact that all models except Inception v3 were general-purpose and lacked fine-tuning was a handicap, and therefore, 'state-of-the-art skin cancer diagnostic AI performs far better because it uses image transformation algorithms that have been trained on a large amount of data.'
