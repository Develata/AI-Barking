About the Lab

The Lynch Lab is based in St John's Institute of Dermatology at King's College London, on the Guy's Campus. We are motivated by two of the key challenges in dermatological surgery: preventing skin cancer and minimising scarring. To understand how fibroblasts and keratinocytes interact, we combine single-cell genomics, spatial transcriptomics, non-invasive skin imaging, AI and 3-dimensional organotypic cell culture models.

Our Research

Our approach is translational: clinical problem → mechanistic insight → therapeutic strategy → IP → clinical trial or industry partnership.

Pillar 1

Skin cancer prevention and early detection

Skin cancer is the most common cancer in humans, and many patients face a lifelong cycle of new tumours. We ask:

Why do some mutant keratinocyte clones become dangerous?

Which fibroblast subtypes promote tumour progression?

Can we reprogramme the tumour microenvironment to reduce cancer risk?

How can we diagnose skin cancer early without cutting the skin?

Targets for preventing keratinocyte carcinomas3D culture models with single-cell and spatial datasets to find druggable pathways that reduce basal and squamous cell carcinoma.

Laser approaches to preventionCan fractional and vascular lasers reduce the incidence of basal cell and squamous cell carcinoma?

Pillar 2

Regenerative dermatology and scarring

Scars follow trauma, surgery and inflammation such as acne. We want to understand how skin heals, and how to make it heal better:

What makes some wounds scar more severely than others?

How do fibroblast subpopulations contribute to fibrosis?

Why do some scars become hypertrophic or keloid?

Can cell-based or molecular therapies reduce scarring?

Fibroblast-based therapies (Fibrodyne)Building on our discovery of human fibroblast subpopulations to develop cell-based therapies for scars.

Pillar 3

AI and computational models

We use AI to diagnose skin cancer and inflammatory skin conditions from clinical images and from non-invasive microscopy, such as dermoscopy and optical coherence tomography. We ask:

How accurate are AI models on the lesions that really worry clinicians, not just on curated test sets?

Where do they fail, and can they tell when they are unsure?

How do general-purpose large language models compare with models built for dermatology?

AI and optical coherence tomographyMachine learning on OCT images to tell BCC and cSCC from benign lesions without a biopsy.

Commercial Partnerships & Translation

We translate our research from bench to bedside through commercial collaborations and spin-out companies.

Fibrodyne

Dr Lynch is a co-inventor on two patents covering the sorting and therapeutic use of human dermal fibroblast subpopulations. They form the core IP of Fibrodyne, a King's College London spin-out co-founded with Professor Fiona Watt, which secured £2M in seed investment to develop cell-based therapies for scars.

Epistat Therapeutics

Epistat Therapeutics is a King's College London spinout, founded on the lab's skin cancer prevention research, developing the first preventive medicines for squamous and basal cell skin cancer for high-risk patients.
