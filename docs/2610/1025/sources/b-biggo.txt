Kimi K3.1 Pro Rate-Limited Two Days After Launch; Moonshot AI Compensates Members With One Week's Quota

Kimi, the AI assistant developed by China's Moonshot AI, apologized early on October 26 for rate limiting and abnormal quota consumption following the launch of K3.1 Pro, and announced it would reset the monthly quota of all active paid subscribers by 25% of their monthly total—roughly one week's usage—with accounts whose remaining usage falls below that level reset to zero. The company said it has adjusted configurations to cut unnecessary sub-agent calls and output length. K3.1 Pro, released on October 24, is the first model in Moonshot's K3.1 series, with 2.8 trillion total parameters and a 1-million-token context window, and the company plans to release the model weights by November 4.

Kimi, the AI assistant developed by China's Moonshot AI, issued a public apology early on October 26 for service problems following the launch of its new K3.1 Pro model and announced quota compensation for all paid members—just two days after the model's official release.

According to Kimi's statement, some members encountered rate limiting while using K3.1 Pro, along with unnecessary consumption of their quotas. The company attributed the cause to its own configuration problems, saying "the problem was ours, and we should have fixed it sooner."

The compensation covers all active subscription members regardless of tier. Specifically, accounts will have 25% of their monthly total quota reset, which the company said is equivalent to one week's usage; members whose current remaining usage is already below one quarter of their monthly total will be reset to zero. The adjusted usage progress bars will be displayed before the end of October 25 US Pacific Time (around the afternoon of October 26 Beijing time).

Beyond compensation, Kimi said it has adjusted system configurations and is reducing unnecessary sub-agent calls and output length, with the goal of enabling the same quota to complete more actual tasks. The statement points to the technical root of recent experience problems: the extra computing overhead from multi-layer agent orchestration may be the source of the abnormal quota consumption.

K3.1 Pro was released on October 24 as the first model in Moonshot AI's K3.1 series and the company's most capable flagship product to date, with parameter count in the trillions and support for a 1-million-token context window. The rollout was aggressive—on launch day it simultaneously arrived on the web, iOS and Android apps, the Kimi Work desktop client, the Kimi Code programming tool and the API, with free trials offered.

The free quota is not unlimited. According to the pricing page, the free plan is labeled "limited use" on the web and in Kimi Work without disclosing a specific cap; K3.1 Pro Highspeed, which is two to three times faster, requires a Pro tier or above.

On technical specifications, K3.1 Pro is a post-trained version built on Kimi K3, released in July, with significantly more compute invested in the post-training phase. The model uses a mixture-of-experts architecture with 2.8 trillion total parameters and 104 billion parameters activated per request. The company's self-reported Terminal-Bench 4.0 score is 65.7%, slightly below Claude Opus 5.5's 66.4% and above GPT-6 Astra's 58.2%. On the independent Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, the model scored 55, versus 58 for Opus 5.5; the index covers 215 models, with K3.1 Pro ranking fourth behind Opus 5.5 (58), Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Muse Wonder Full (56 each), while K3 scored 44. On the BenchCAD evaluation for 3D modeling (with tools), K3.1 Pro scored 92.7%, close to Opus 5.5's 96.2% and GPT-6 Astra's 95.9%; on OfficeQA Pro v2 for financial document analysis it scored 64.4. API pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens at $0.20 per million—about one-third lower than Kimi K3's $3 and $15.

Notably, the controversy comes against the backdrop of plans to open-source K3.1 Pro. The company said full model weights will be released by November 4, when outsiders will be able to deploy it themselves, and built-in safety guardrails may also be removed. To that end, Moonshot AI deliberately stripped cybersecurity-related data from its training corpus and reinforcement learning environments during post-training to suppress the model's vulnerability-discovery capabilities. Internal tests showed that a checkpoint without these measures could solve 90.5% of vulnerability-exploitation tasks, while the final K3.1 Pro dropped to 81%; by comparison, the original K3 scored only 33.3% on the same test. The company said its goal is "not to release open weights that would expand the absolute frontier of cyber capabilities," but to gradually raise the capability level of open-source models. Third-party evaluators noted that once the weights are public, K3.1 Pro will become the strongest open-source model on their intelligence index.

For users, the short-term impact centers on two points: whether the compensation truly covers earlier quota losses, and whether rate limiting eases with the configuration adjustments. For Moonshot AI, capacity and billing disputes arising within 48 hours of its flagship model's launch exposed the real-world constraints of high-parameter models in inference costs and concurrency capacity. At a time when Chinese large models are iterating intensively and price wars continue, service stability and quota transparency are becoming key variables for subscription products seeking to retain paying users.
