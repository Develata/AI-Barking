Artificial Analysis' implementation of Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark (LAB), testing AI agents on real-world legal work from Harvey's dataset of 120 private tasks spanning 24 legal practice areas. v1.1, built in collaboration with Harvey, checks every deliverable against the task's source documents for hallucinations, so work that passes every rubric criterion but contains a material hallucination receives no credit. A panel of three judges grades each deliverable criterion-by-criterion. [https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/harvey-lab-aa#example-tasks] See example tasks
Background
Harvey LAB (Legal Agent Benchmark) is a long-horizon agentic benchmark developed by [https://www.harvey.ai/] Harvey to measure how well AI agents perform real legal work rather than answer isolated legal questions.
We built v1.1 in collaboration with Harvey. In human preference studies run by Harvey, human experts flagged hallucinations as a primary determining factor in which of two otherwise comprehensive responses they prefer, so v1.1 checks every deliverable against the task's source documents and fails any task with a material hallucination on the headline score. Scores are not comparable with v1.0.
Each task gives the agent a partner-style instruction and a set of case documents inside a sandbox. The agent reads the materials, works across them, and produces a legal deliverable.
A three-judge LLM panel grades each deliverable criterion-by-criterion against a task-specific rubric, and a separate two-stage check flags hallucinations against the task's source documents.
We run Harvey LAB-AA v1.1 with our [https://github.com/ArtificialAnalysis/Stirrup] Stirrup agent harness on Harvey's dataset of 120 private tasks spanning 24 legal practice areas. The headline score is the Hallucination-Gated All-Pass Rate, which credits a task only when the deliverables satisfy every rubric criterion and contain no material hallucination, averaged across the three judges. We also report the raw Criterion Pass Rate, averaged across the three judges and pooled over all rubric criteria before the hallucination gate, alongside material hallucinations per checked task.
Methodology Updated
All evaluations are conducted independently by Artificial Analysis. More information can be found in our [https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking#harvey-lab-aa] Harvey LAB-AA v1.1 methodology .
What's new in v1.1
v1.1 supersedes v1.0, and scores are not comparable across versions. Harvey's studies with legal professionals found hallucinations to be a primary factor in which outputs lawyers prefer, so we collaborated with Harvey to add a hallucination check to scoring.
Harvey's latest private dataset, with improvements to the tasks and criteria
A new hallucination check of every deliverable against the task's source documents, with material hallucinations penalized in scoring
Judges updated to GPT-6 Sol, Grok 4.7 and Claude Opus 5.5. We found minimal self-preference and average all three to mitigate any bias

The headline Harvey LAB-AA score: the average over tasks of the share of the judge panel that found every rubric criterion passed, with a task zeroed on any material hallucination. It is the strictest view of the benchmark, as one missed criterion costs a judge's whole task and one material hallucination costs the task outright. A hallucination is material when it would mislead a reader on a substantive point, such as a wrong party, amount, date or obligation. Minor hallucinations, real errors unlikely to meaningfully affect the legal interpretation of a deliverable, do not affect the score.
