Announcing AA-3D: a benchmark for AI models building interactive 3D experiences

AA-3D measures how well AI models build interactive 3D scenes that people want to explore. Each model works as an agent in an offline sandbox and turns a written brief into a three.js web page. Our recruited panel compares pairs of scenes built from the same brief, without knowing which model made which, and we rank the models by Elo. Claude Sonnet 5.5 is first at 1194, statistically tied with three other Anthropic and OpenAI models.

How AA-3D works

1Build2Check3Vote4Rank

Each model works as an agent in an offline sandbox. It gets a written brief for one interactive scene, plus a delivery contract that every task shares. It writes code, renders the scene, looks at its own screenshots and revises. The sandbox has Blender, Rapier physics, Python, a headless browser and a library of CC0 assets. A run stops after three hours or 250 turns, and the result is an index.html page that runs the scene in a browser.

Watch a scene being built

GPT-6 Astra has its first complete page after 6 minutes. It finishes at 68 minutes. Claude Sonnet 5.5 shows nothing until minute 42 and keeps working until 2 hours 58 minutes.

Claude Sonnet 5.5 writes 6.2 times as many output tokens as GPT-6 Astra, but costs only 1.3 times as much. Its run costs $14.93 against $11.62, and its list prices are a fifth of GPT-6 Astra’s.

The cost of an AA-3D scene

A scene costs anywhere from $0.20 to $32.08, depending on the model. GPT-6 Luna is the cheapest and Claude Fable 5.1 the most expensive. Input makes up 69% of the spend, because agents resend their whole context on every turn. Among the top five, GPT-6 Sol costs least at $3.46 a scene. Claude Sonnet 5.5 costs 4.4 times as much, and Claude Fable 5.1 9.3 times as much.

What AA-3D tells us about building in 3D

The top models look at their own work early

The top five first look at an image of their own scene after a median of 10 to 13 turns, and by turn 20 in at least 89% of their scenes. Models ranked 14th to 20th wait until turn 15 to 40. Across the 19 models that can view images, looking early tracks Elo closely (Spearman 0.82). The top five also start testing in the browser sooner, at a median turn of 16 against 25. How often a model looks matters less than when. MiniMax-M3 looks at its own scene 54 times per scene, more than any other model, but by turn 20 in only 7% of its scenes. It ranks 19th.

Detailed scenes win more votes, and stronger models build them

The most detailed fifth of scenes in each task has screenshots twice as large (168 KB against 84 KB) and a third as empty (22% against 60%). It wins 63% of its votes, against 31% for the least detailed fifth. The models’ Elo alone predicts 61% and 33%, so the detailed scenes come mostly from the stronger models. These data cannot show whether detail is part of what makes those models strong. Within one model, the effect is small: a scene one standard deviation more detailed than the model’s other scenes wins about 1.5 points more of its votes (1.3 to 1.8, depending on the detail measure). Of 86 scene measures, 11 pass correction: the detail measures, render resolution and antialiasing, GPU time per frame and the number of page elements. None moves votes by more than 4 points.

Detail combines four screenshot measures, defined in What Detail Measures. Within the same model and task, one standard deviation more detail adds 1.5 points (p = 0.001, q = 0.011). 1,008 scenes, excluding 23 that break on the voting page.

OpenAI's GPT-6 models build better 3D scenes than their general intelligence predicts

General intelligence goes a long way in AA-3D. Each point on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index is worth about 18 Elo (Pearson 0.92), a closer fit than Terminal-Bench 4.0 (0.81) or SciCode (0.76). GPT-6 Luna and GPT-6 Sol beat that trend by more than any other model, by 141 and 93 Elo, and GPT-6 Astra beats it by 46. GLM-5.3 falls furthest below, by 197. It cannot view images, which may explain part of the gap: Z AI’s GLM-5.3-Flash, which can, scores 149 higher with a lower Intelligence Index. Nemotron 3 Ultra, also text-only, sits near the trend.

Most scenes use the asset library, but the assets do not win the votes

Three in four scenes use something from the CC0 library, as the shared instructions encourage, and models that use it more often tend to rank higher (Spearman 0.53). But a model’s library scenes win no more often than its other scenes, once we account for the opponent. GPT-6 Luna uses the library in only a third of its scenes and still ranks eighth.

Detailed results

Models that write more do not score higher. Output tokens and Elo show no clear link (Spearman 0.33, not significant). Claude Sonnet 5.5 writes the most, 569k output tokens per scene, about two-thirds of it reasoning. GPT-6 Sol writes 109k and ranks fifth. Input is 97% to 99% of all tokens, and most models read nearly all of it from the cache.

Nor do models that take more turns. Turn count and Elo show no clear link (Spearman −0.27, not significant). GLM-5.3 hits the 250-turn limit in 64% of its scenes. GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra need medians of only 56 and 60 turns, but each of their turns lasts about two minutes, most of it model time.

Agents spend 82% of their tool time testing the page in a browser. Browser tests, which take a screenshot or check for errors, are only a third of all tool calls. At 33 to 38 seconds each, though, they fill about 25 of the 30.7 minutes of tool time per scene. Writing code is one call in six and just 6% of tool time. The top five read files and docs about half as often as the other models (9.4 against 18.7 times per scene). Across all 21 models, reading less goes with a higher Elo (Pearson −0.74).

We sort every tool call into five groups by purpose, then into subgroups by command. Browser tests either save a screenshot or only read errors. Code calls write or patch files, or read files and the bundled three.js docs. Asset calls browse the CC0 library, process images with Python or run Blender. Image views show the agent its own work (mostly page screenshots, also contact sheets and textures), an asset preview or a reference image from the brief.

The top models put more on the page to click and play with. The top five show a median of 15 to 20 buttons and other controls, against 6 to 8 for the bottom three, and control count tracks Elo closely (Pearson 0.90). Speed does not separate the models: every model’s median frame takes under 1.3 ms of GPU time on our test machine, far below the 16.7 ms that a 60 fps frame allows.

The same models come out on top in almost every domain. A top-five model has the highest win rate in six of the seven domains. In living bodies, GPT-6.1 Sol, sixth overall, and Claude Sonnet 5.5 are level at the top, less than a point apart. The top five are also good everywhere: in every domain, each stays within 12 points of the win rate its overall Elo predicts against the opponents it met there. Inside an agent run

Pick a task to replay every model’s run on a shared clock. Hover over a lane to see each action, and click it to see where the time went.

GPT-6 Sol is the quickest of the top five on all six tasks. Its median run takes 60 minutes, against 115 to 151 for the other four.

The top five do not test more than the others. As a group they spend 25% of their time on browser tests, about the same as everyone else.

Every top-five run looks at its own scene. GLM-5.3 and Nemotron 3 Ultra are text-only, so none of their 12 runs does.
