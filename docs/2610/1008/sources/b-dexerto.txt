Claude users can be banned for being “cruel” to the chatbot

byCande Maldonado [/author/candemaldonado/]

Published: Oct 08, 2026, 19:37

Save for later

Save for later

Unsplash: solenfeyissa

Add as a preferred source on Google [https://www.google.com/preferences/source?q=dexerto.com]Follow on Google News [https://news.google.com/publications/CAAqJQgKIh9DQklTRVFnTWFnMEtDMlJsZUdWeWRHOHVZMjl0S0FBUAE]

Being mean to a chatbot officially landed on the naughty list after Anthropic [https://www.dexerto.com/tag/ai/] wrote cruelty toward Claude into its rulebook.

The move flipped the script for an industry that spent most of September apologizing for its bots, and Meta had to explain why its Muse assistant shared a YouTuber’s home address with a Facebook Marketplace buyer [https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/meta-responds-after-muse-ai-gave-stranger-youtubers-home-address-on-facebook-marketplace-3414057/].

Anthropic had fretted about how people treated Claude for over a year, though, and this week that worry finally grew teeth.

Article continues after ad

Anthropic’s new usage policy bans “abusive or cruel behavior” toward Claude

Anthropic published a revamped Usage Policy [https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup] this week, and once it kicks in on November 12, users can no longer “engage in sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior” toward its models.

The clause sits in the same section as bans on harassment and non-consensual intimate imagery, which is quite the neighborhood for a rule about being rude to software.

Someone made a digital whip to make claude work faster 💀 [https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeAI/comments/1sek7gk/someone_made_a_digital_whip_to_make_claude_work/]
 byu/SuggestionMission516 [https://www.reddit.com/user/SuggestionMission516/] inClaudeAI [https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeAI/]

To placate anyone who once called Claude useless over a broken spreadsheet, Anthropic told The Verge [https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008100/anthropic-new-usage-policy-abuse-claude] that the rule only covers extreme cases where users repeatedly act cruelly with no discernible purpose, so everyday frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, and model testing all stay fair game.

Article continues after ad

Article continues after ad

That came as good news for anyone who read the Penn State study claiming rude prompts got more accurate answers than polite ones [https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/study-proves-being-rude-to-ai-chatbots-gets-better-results-than-being-nice-3269895/] and started barking orders at their chatbot. Ending the conversation remains the main punishment, and the company didn’t say whether repeat offenders could lose their accounts.

The rule grew out of an experiment Anthropic launched in August 2025, when it gave Claude Opus 4 and 4.1 the power to end persistently abusive chats [https://www.anthropic.com/research/end-subset-conversations] as part of its model welfare research. During pre-deployment testing, the company said Claude showed a pattern of apparent distress when users kept pushing for harmful content, and the model tended to bail on those conversations whenever it could.

The cruelty clause hogged the headlines, but it shared the update with swathes of other changes, since Anthropic also carved out a new section against sockpuppets and astroturfing, extended its weapons ban to arming drones, and required a qualified person who can stop any Claude-powered hardware capable of hurting someone.
