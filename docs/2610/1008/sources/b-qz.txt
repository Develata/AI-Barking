Anthropic is cracking down on people being abusive to Claude

An updated policy targets extreme scenarios in which users are repeatedly cruel to Claude for no apparent reason, and leaves room for ordinary expressions of frustration

Anthropic published an updated usage policy [https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update] on Thursday that prohibits sustained abusive or cruel behavior toward its Claude AI models, alongside new or clarified restrictions on election interference, weapons development, and surveillance.

The company said the prohibition targets only extreme scenarios in which users are repeatedly cruel to Claude for no apparent reason, and leaves room for ordinary expressions of frustration, critical feedback, dark creative writing, and testing or research activities. Anthropic noted that Claude has had the ability to exit conversations with abusive users on Claude.ai and Claude Code since a prior update, and that conversation termination will continue to serve as the chief way the rule is enforced.

On elections, Anthropic consolidated several existing rules into a section titled "Do Not Undermine Democratic Processes," which bars using Claude to spread false information about candidates or voting, impersonate candidates or election officials, or attempt to suppress turnout. The company also removed a blanket ban on personalized vote and campaign targeting, saying the prohibition had been capturing legitimate civic uses — such as nonprofits producing voter information in other languages or election officials sending ballot cure notices.

Separately, Anthropic consolidated existing prohibitions on deceptive campaigns into a new section covering efforts to obscure the origin of a message, amplify content through fake accounts, or build infrastructure for influence operations. The company said it had observed state media outlets, government propaganda offices, and commercial firms using Claude to run networks of fake accounts and fabricated news sites.

On weapons, the revised policy specifies that the existing ban now extends to software and components integral to weapons operation, covering activities such as equipping drones and other autonomous vehicles with armaments. On surveillance, the revised policy clarifies that monitoring individuals without their knowledge or agreement is off-limits regardless of whether it is conducted live or derived from data gathered in the past, and that Claude may not play a role in determining or advising on who should be investigated, arrested, or prosecuted.

Anthropic's threat intelligence report, which documented misuse attempts between December 2025 and August 2026 [https://qz.com/anthropic-claude-biological-weapons-surveillance-misuse-091026], described state-linked actors using Claude for biological weapons research, cyberattacks against Ukrainian government targets, and surveillance of diaspora communities. The company said it banned accounts associated with all identified activity and shared intelligence with authorities. The updated policy draws on patterns observed in that report, Anthropic said.

The updated policy introduces a new provision covering situations in which Claude is integrated with hardware that can carry out physical actions on its own, requiring that a qualified operator have the means to monitor the equipment and intervene to halt it when necessary.

The updated usage policy takes effect on November 12.
