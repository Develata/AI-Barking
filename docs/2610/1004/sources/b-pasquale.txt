Google blocks Gemini Flash for free users and Pro for AI Plus on Oct 9
From October 9 free Gemini users keep only Flash-Lite and Google AI Plus loses the Pro model. Plan table, US prices and what changes for subscribers.

From October 9, 2026, anyone using the Gemini app without a subscription gets a single model, Flash-Lite, because Google pulls both Flash and Pro from the free tier. AI Plus subscribers keep Flash-Lite and Flash but lose Pro, on a date Mountain View will send by email to each subscriber individually. Pro and Ultra stay intact, and remain the only plans with Gemini Pro and Deep Think.

Who loses what on October 9

The official table on the Google support page, picked up by Android Headlines and XDA, leaves little room for doubt. The cut hits the two lowest rungs of the ladder and spares the pricier ones.

No subscription	Yes	No	No	No
AI Plus	Yes	Yes	No	No
AI Pro	Yes	Yes	Yes	Yes
AI Ultra	Yes	Yes	Yes	Yes

Flash-Lite is the smallest and cheapest model in the family, built for quick answers to simple questions. Flash sits one step above, Pro is the flagship for reasoning and code, and Deep Think (the mode that makes the model work through several hypotheses in parallel before answering) stays reserved for the Pro and Ultra plans.

Before the change, according to Clubic, even non-paying users could pick between Flash-Lite, Flash and Pro. Free Gemini therefore had nearly the whole catalog, and from Friday, October 9 it will have a third of it.

AI Plus loses Pro

The thorniest case is AI Plus subscribers on the entry-level plan, which costs $4.99 a month in the US (it was $7.99 until the June price cut) and includes 400 GB of cloud storage. Google has said subscribers will get an email with the exact day Pro disappears from their account, so the deadline is not the same for everyone, and per the outlets covering it, it falls later in October.

German press adds a detail worth knowing. As GoogleWatchBlog reports, in Germany plenty of Google One storage subscribers had AI Plus already included, and with it Pro access they never asked for. If you have a Google One plan, check in the Gemini app whether AI Plus is bundled in. There is no official confirmation for the US or the UK, but the same mechanism could apply there too.

ADVERTISEMENT
This space is for sale

XDA's Simon Batt wrote that Pro was one of the reasons he kept Plus and that he is now weighing rival services. It is a single opinion, not churn data. The plan remains, however, the cheapest way to get Flash plus extra cloud storage.

Why now

Google gives no reason, not even on the support page. The calendar speaks for itself, though. Big G launched Gemini 4 Argon on September 30 and nine days later pulls its most capable models from the free tier, right in the weeks when demand for the new models peaks.

Then there is the cost side. Since May 2026 Gemini usage limits are no longer counted in prompts but in compute, with a window that resets every 5 hours and a weekly cap. AI Plus gets double the standard limit, AI Pro four times, and AI Ultra 5 to 20 times the Pro limit depending on the tier. The more big models Google hands out, the more compute it burns, and a Plus user running Pro all day costs far more than the $4.99 a month it pays.

From experience, when a company tightens the free tier days after a major launch, overloaded servers usually explain it better than a sudden discovery of what paid plans are worth. That is my reading, and Google has not confirmed it.

What it costs to climb back

The only route back to Gemini Pro is AI Pro, which costs $19.99 a month in the US, against $99.99 for AI Ultra's starting price. The Plus-to-Pro jump is $15 more a month, roughly four times the entry fee. Android Headlines puts US AI Plus at about 5 dollars a month, and prices in other markets, the UK included, vary, so check the purchase page before deciding.

Students have an alternative. As covered in our guide to free Gemini for a year for university students, Google runs a dedicated offer. Verify it, though, because no source says whether it stays unchanged after October 9.

A pattern that keeps repeating

We saw the same scheme, the best model reserved for payers, when Claude Opus 5.5 reached Antigravity for Pro and Ultra only. The Gemini app is also being reshuffled fast, since on September 29 Skills arrived and Gems were scheduled to disappear in November.

Free Gemini stays usable but becomes a taster for people who haven't decided whether to pay. For quick questions it is more than enough.

Frequently asked questions
1. When does AI Plus lose Pro?

There is no single date. Google emails each subscriber the exact day of the switch. According to XDA the deadline falls in the second half of October. Until that email arrives, Pro keeps working as before.

2. Do subscriber usage limits change?

No, they stay as set in May. The multipliers are 2 times the standard for AI Plus, 4 times for AI Pro and 5 to 20 times the Pro limit for AI Ultra, calculated on compute consumed and not on message count. What changes is which models you can pick, not how much you can use them.

3. Do AI Pro or Ultra subscribers need to do anything?

Just update the app. Google recommends running the latest Gemini version on Android and iOS. For the two top plans the model list stays the same.

4. Will free Gemini still be useful?

For simple questions, yes. Flash-Lite answers fast and is enough for short summaries, translations or quick lookups. On long code, document analysis and multi-step reasoning the gap with Flash and Pro will show. That is exactly where Google aims to trigger the subscription.

Conclusions

The substance is simple. Free drops to Flash-Lite, AI Plus loses Pro, Pro and Ultra stay as they were. The delicate point is that Google made the cheapest and most popular plan pay the price, the one many ended up on almost by accident, inside a Google One subscription.

Anyone using Pro daily has two roads, step up to AI Pro or look around. Anyone who opened it now and then should test Flash before the switch to see whether it suffices. The rest Mountain View has settled, a cut delivered in app and by email, days of notice and no explanation.
