    Tech Giants Harvest Data from Users and Workers

    International technology companies collect vast amounts of personal data from
    European citizens. Public debate and policymaking often focus on data taken
    from consumers through e-commerce, social media, or streaming platforms. Yet
    many Europeans also use digital platforms to work.

    Online freelance platforms are usually discussed in terms of low pay and
    insecure “gigs”. Much less attention is given to another resource these
    platforms extract from workers: their personal data.

    Why Does This Matter in the Age of AI?

    This report examines the case of Outlier, a key data work platform for artificial
    intelligence. Outlier is operated by Scale AI, a company that recruits workers to
    perform tasks such as evaluating AI-generated responses, creating training data,
    and improving AI models. In 2025, Meta acquired 49% of the company for 14,5
    billion dollars.



Executive                        This report shows how digital platforms rely on data
                                 workers worldwide, including in the EU, to label,
                                 check, and organize training data. These data are
                                 harvested online. But Outlier also collects
                                 workers’ personal data, financial records,

    Results Overview

    Young and highly educated European workers in precarious conditions:
       Workers surveyed in 12 European countries
       Predominantly young (>50% under 30), highly educated (nearly all with
       bachelor's or master's degrees)
       An average wage of €1,214/month — 30% below the average cost of living in
       their countries
       Platform dependence: 70–100% of income depends on the platform
       Gain loss: Earnings decline sharply over time
       Income erosion: 1 in 4 workers earned nothing during the period of our study

    Wage theft and data expropriation are directly linked. Workers provide both
    labor and personal data with no corresponding compensation, as evidenced by
    the detection of 16 trackers on workers' computers:
        Social media behavioral trackers (Facebook, LinkedIn, Snapchat, TikTok,
        Twitter, Quora)
        Tools capturing clicks, keystrokes, mouse movements, and screen activity
        (FullStory, Amplitude, Google Analytics, PostHog, Sprig, Segment)
        High-liability identifiers (especially face recognition): Persona, Recruitics,
        Forethought, Sentry

                                                                                                                                                                         Humble Origins

                                                                                                                                                                         Founded in 2016 by Alexandr Wang and Lucy Guo, Scale AI is a specialist in data annotation:
                                                                                                                                                                         it helps companies find data workers to feed their AI models. Data annotation includes image
                                                                                                                                                                         tagging, text labeling, audio transcription, video frame analysis, and data categorization, all
                                                                                                                                                                         performed by a distributed network of remote workers. Scale AI creates its first work platform,
                                                                                                                                                                         Remotask, in 2017. At the time, it mainly recruits workers from Kenya, the Philippines, or
                                                                                                                                                                         Venezuela.

                                                                                                                                                                         Scale AI establishes itself as one of the major contributors for OpenAI’s ChatGPT development
                                                                                                                                                                         in 2022. Its workers carried out many of the RLHF (1) tasks that were necessary to turn their
                                                                                                                                                                         LLM into a publishable conversational model.

                                                                                                                                                                         Rebranding as Vendor of “Top AI Tutors”

                                                                                                                                                                         By 2023, the company undergoes a rebranding by distancing itself from Remotask, seen as a
                                                                                                                                                                         cheaper option populated mostly with people from the Global South. It begins marketing its
                                                                                                                                                                         workforce as the most talented in high-income countries. According to journalist Karen Hao,
                                                                                                                                                                         Scale AI's new strategy involves recruiting doctors, programmers, and physicists “primarily in
                                                                                                                                                                         the US (...) offering as much as forty dollars an hour.” (2)

                                              Evidence of Constant Surveillance.


  The HAR files reveal that analytics companies, data brokers, and social media companies partnering
  with Outlier are constantly collecting EU workers' personal data: full names, e-mail addresses,
  phone numbers, but also live screen recording and mouse movements, work hours, pay rates.
  Worryingly, Outlier also uses Persona an “ID verification” company, which collects official IDs and
  biometric data through facial recognition.


  Some of these trackers were clearly used to monitor worker activity or to further train AI solutions.
  Some personal data, however, remain unclear in their usage. This lack of transparence raises
  concerns about how data might be exploited for surveillance or AI training without the workers'
  knowledge or consent.


   (1) A HAR (Http ARchive format) file is a log of all the interactions between a browser (in this case, a data worker’s browser) and a website
   (in this case, the platform Outlier). It is easily accessible from a browser’s developer tools, and a recording of these interactions can be
   produced from the worker’s device without any specific technical competences. This method is quick, simple, and non-invasive.

THE SOCIAL
MEDIA
The use of social media trackers is prevalent online. Anyone who
browses websites or uses apps is closely watched by them. This
does not make them necessarily legal. For Outlier, it actually
makes matters worse. Social media data can be matched with
information collected while working. There is the potential for very
detailed profiling of the data workers, which could affect other
aspects of their lives.




Linkedin pixels are used to track the worker activity inside the
Outlier web application, possibly to correlate it to their activity
outside the platform.

Workers are tracked when they click the “Start task” button,
which can give Linkedin an idea of when they start working,
which task they are working on, and for how long.




A TikTok pixel is fetched for every data worker.

                                                                                                                                                                                   CONSUMERS’ PERSONAL DATA EXPOSED THROUGH AI (1)


                                                                                                                                           The AI Act and the GDPR are landmark pieces of legislation pioneered by the European Union. Other
                                                                                                                                           countries have since followed suit. But a concerted lobbying effort by the U.S. government (2),
                                                                                                                                           alongside lobbying by Big Tech representatives (3), is now pushing to roll back these safeguards.
                                                                                                                                           Enter the Digital Omnibus, a deregulation package that would weaken the legal protections
                                                                                                                                           established under the GDPR and the AI Act. The European Parliament is expected to vote on the
                                                                                                                                           package in early 2027.
                                                                                                                                           If these deregulation measures are adopted, Big Tech companies will be able to claim that
                                                                                                                                           collecting and transfering data from the EU to foreign countries for AI training is in their
                                                                                                                                           “legitimate interest”.
                                                                                                                                           This proposal would cement the loss of control over personal information while obscuring its use in
                                                                                                                                           AI training and the production of foreign military assets.
                                                                                                                                           EU citizens and workers would be left with few, if any, avenues to challenge this process of
                                                                                                                                           expropriation, whose costs are ultimately borne by them through increased prices and dependency,
                                                                                                                                           mass surveillance, and a loss of agency over their own lives.


                                                                                                                                              The Digital Omnibus raises cybersecurit y risks, as well as fundamental
                                                                                                                                              questions about digital sovereignty. It's essential to protect individual
                                                                                                                                             privacy not just to stop data ex traction, but also to reduce technological
                                                                                                                                                 dependency and regain control over Europe's digital infrastructure.
