TL;DR

All three results were Lean-formalized before announcement, but only the math proofs are public; Yang-Mills artifacts are withheld pending a Nobel-led shepherd panel's review.

The ETH disproof was generated October 16 and machine-checked October 17, but the conjecture's original proposers Impagliazzo and Paturi are leading independent verification before the community can act on it.

OpenAI's Yang-Mills result establishes existence of QCD in four dimensions but does not address the mass gap, so it does not satisfy the full Clay Millennium Prize conditions.

 Editor's note

OpenAI is routing three major scientific claims through named external shepherds rather than traditional journals, placing verification risk on the academic community for the next 90 days while announcing publicly. If the ETH disproof survives scrutiny, it collapses hundreds of conditional hardness results that have anchored algorithm complexity theory for three decades.

OpenAI says one of its internal models disproved the Exponential Time Hypothesis on October 16 and formalized that disproof in Lean the next day, according to The Next Web [https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-exponential-time-hypothesis-false-kakeya-yang-mills]. The company has published both the model's write-up and the formal proof on GitHub, alongside two more claims it is handing to the research community: a proof of Christopher Sogge's 1991 local smoothing conjecture in all dimensions, which would also settle the Kakeya conjecture and Stein's restriction conjecture, and a proof that pure Yang-Mills theory exists in four dimensions, part of a Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize problem.

The verification is where the real work still has to happen.

The computer-science claim has been handed to Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi at UC San Diego and Ryan Williams at MIT. In an open letter the three wrote that "Our involvement should not be taken as certification of the claim. A result of this significance requires careful, independent scrutiny," adding that "If the claim is correct, it will change an important part of theoretical computer science." Williams said the group "signed no non-disclosure agreements and will not be paid."

The physics piece is being handled differently. A group led by Nobel laureate David Gross, with Michael Douglas, Arthur Jaffe, Igor Klebanov and Nikita Nekrasov, "chose not to release the AI-produced material for now and will write up the work itself." Where OpenAI pushed the ETH disproof straight to GitHub as a Lean file plus a model write-up, the Yang-Mills claim is being filtered through its human authors before anything public lands.

OpenAI says it does not seek academic credit for the three results and "has also given $5m to the Simons Foundation for workshops and conferences on AI-produced results." Mathematicians raised adjacent concerns about AI attribution in the Leiden declaration in June. A handful of the researchers we track on our OpenAI page [https://aiweekly.co/ai-news-today/openai-news] started circulating the three announcements within hours of publication; whether they still matter three months from now depends on three names in San Diego and Cambridge.

 Editor's take

The ETH review has the fastest clock because the Lean artefact is already public; a verdict from Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams, positive or negative, is the first data point that will tell the field whether October 16-17 was a milestone or a press release.

Why did Gross's physics group decline to publish the AI's own write-up while the computer-science team pushed theirs straight to GitHub?

Did the Lean file verify every inference step in the ETH disproof, or only an outline the model filled in?
