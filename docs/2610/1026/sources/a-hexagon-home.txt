A continuum whose hyperspace of regular subcontinua is homeomorphic to the irrationals [/2610.00570v1]

Contributed by Lucas Almeida Almeida

For a metric continuum XXX, let D(X)D(X)D(X) be the hyperspace of subcontinua of XXX equal to the closure of their interior, with the Hausdorff metric. Camargo, Ordo\~nez and Ram\'irez asked whether D(X)D(X)D(X) can be homeomorphic to the rationals or to the irrationals. We answer the latter question affirmatively. We construct a continuum X⊆ℓ2X\subseteq\ell^2X⊆ℓ2 as the closure of a countable tree of scaled copies of an indecomposable plane continuum. Two types of piece alternate: blocks with dense interior, and spines with empty interior carrying densely attached blocks. We characterize all regular subcontinua by nonempty convex sets of pieces satisfying a density condition, and prove that this coding is a homeomorphism onto a GδG_\deltaGδ​ subset of a Cantor cube. We then show that no point of D(X)D(X)D(X) has a compact neighborhood. The Alexandrov--Urysohn characterization yields D(X)≅NND(X)\cong\N^\ND(X)≅NN, and hence D(X)D(X)D(X) is homeomorphic to the irrationals. We also explain why this construction does not extend to the rational case, which remains open.

math.GR — Group Theory

The Unbalanced Group Theorem [/2610.00575v1]

Contributed by Colin Defant, Ken Ono

The classification of finite simple groups (CFSG) identifies the basic building blocks of all finite groups. It grew from nineteenth-century work of Galois and Jordan into a collective effort involving many mathematicians, with Gorenstein playing a central organizing role. Its proof is a vast network of interdependent results such as the Feit--Thompson odd-order theorem, the signalizer functor theorem, and Aschbacher's classical involution theorem. The original papers make up the first-generation proof. The second-generation treatment, initiated by Gorenstein, Lyons, and Solomon, reorganizes and simplifies much of this work.

A key structural result in the first-generation program is the Unbalanced Group Theorem (UGT). Under the inductive hypotheses of the classification, it says that a nontrivial subgroup of odd order normal in the centralizer of an involution forces the ambient simple group to be a group of Lie type in odd characteristic or one of the alternating groups of certain degrees.

While formalizing the first-generation proof, we found counterexamples to a closure assertion used in a published route to UGT. This obstruction concerns that particular argument. UGT also has other proofs. Here we explain the obstruction and develop an alternative argument for the simple-group case using Solomon's 1976 theory of maximal 222-components, which provides a framework for comparing the simple pieces arising in different involution centralizers.

Contributed by Shengwei Qiu, Thomas Sinclair

The three-dimensional function system of the regular pentagon fails the lifting property, giving a counterexample to the operator-system formulation of the Smith--Ward problem with C∗^*∗-envelope C5\mathbb C^5C5.
We construct unital kkk-positive maps from this fixed system into matrix algebras where distance from every ucp map is bounded below independently of kkk. 

We also prove that every regular polygon system with at least five vertices detects nuclearity, while its dual characterizes the weak expectation property. These tensor-product characterizations follow from a graph dichotomy: a graph operator system and its dual are C∗^*∗-nuclear when the graph is 222-chordal; otherwise they detect nuclearity and WEP, respectively.

math.MG — Metric Geometry

Volume Computation and Comparison for Four-Dimensional Bodies of Constant Width [/2610.00561v1]

Contributed by Hangang Wang

We compute and compare the volumes of explicit four-dimensional bodies of constant width one. The original five-entry comparison consists of the simplex-symmetric projection body U4U_4U4​, the canonical raised Meissner pair, the canonical raise of U3U_3U3​, the body B5B_5B5​, and M4M_4M4​. We combine exact geometric formulas, numerical integration, interval enclosures and inscribed rational polytopes to obtain a certified strict ordering of their volumes. We also compare U4U_4U4​ with the direct four-dimensional AMO peabody. The two raised Meissner bodies are proved noncongruent but exactly equal in volume. An introductory account explains why different constructions benefit from different computational methods, and distinguishes approximate volume estimates from inequalities certified by exact or outward-rounded arithmetic.

math-ph — Mathematical Physics

Conjectures of Ashoke Sen: Scope, Exact Tests and Analytic Limits [/2610.00564v1]

Contributed by Deep Bhattacharjee

We examine thirty-six selected statements associated with Ashoke Sen's work on duality, tachyon condensation, black hole indices and string field theory. The purpose is to distinguish mathematical results within a specified framework, physical conjectures, and extensions beyond the assumptions used in a derivation. Exact product expansions reproduce known negative dyon indices in toroidal orbifolds; with the charge cone transcribed correctly, a finite scan contains thirty negative-index charge triples. These numbers concern the full index, whose sign need not agree with a horizon index after hair removal. For tachyon dust we give the characteristic flow, the corrected Hessian sign criterion, and precise hypotheses under which the first focusing time is attained. We also separate the D7 divisor on the F-theory base from its singular pullback to the orientifold cover and give a local weak-coupling family with an I0∗I_0^*I0∗​ divisor. These calculations delimit claims; they do not disprove the homogeneous rolling solution or Sen's positivity argument under its stated assumptions. The accompanying formal certificates check specified finite computations and algebraic identities, rather than the catalogue's physical interpretations. Established results and later corrections are credited to their sources.

math-ph — Mathematical Physics

Gibbs Approximation from Coarse-Graining of Finite Systems [/2610.00557v1]

Contributed by Zhenyu Sun, Chen Chen

Thermodynamic integrability in finite systems admits concave probability-response potentials beyond the canonical ensemble. We investigate how replacing an unresolved block of energy levels by its effective free energy constrains this class and determines the Gibbs distribution, both exactly and quantitatively. For a response family defined across all finite spectra, exact single-block replacement implies conditional independence within each block and selects the Gibbs family with its canonical free-energy normalization. On a bounded energy window, conditional log-odds discrepancies determine a common reference inverse-temperature scale and yield a dimension-independent total-variation bound. A discrete calibration protocol incorporates grid rounding, interpolation under stated regularity bounds, and finite-sample concentration guarantees. We bound the conditional log-odds discrepancies in terms of coarse-grained block-probability residuals through finite block suppression, a bounded outer-control displacement, and a local internal curvature bound. The estimate separates auxiliary system size, measurement precision, and probability conditioning. Smooth local perturbations show that finitely many observations do not uniquely determine the global response. Applications to finite-capacity reservoirs and fixed-coupling mean-force models distinguish work-form closure from temperature-energy scaling. For two subsystems sharing small, bounded, symmetric log-temperature fluctuations, the leading mutual information is half the squared log-temperature variance times the product of their reference canonical heat capacities.
