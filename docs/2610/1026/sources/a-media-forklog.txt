OpenAI says its AI refuted the Exponential Time Hypothesis

OpenAI says its AI refuted ETH and proved math and quantum field results.

 27.10.2026

 ForkLog [https://forklog.com/en/author/ado5jt4lotra/]

OpenAI’s internal AI models have refuted the Exponential Time Hypothesis [https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%93%D0%B8%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%B5%D0%B7%D0%B0_%D0%BE%D0%B1_%D1%8D%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%86%D0%B8%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BC_%D0%B2%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8] (ETH), proved several mathematical conjectures, and produced rigorous existence proofs for a number of quantum field theories, the company said [https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/?__cf_chl_f_tk=yvJtfN8BvLa3f64hhQ4yoSqJHBP0rRCiOnYTh35qPNc-1793084157-1.0.1.1-Y0.dzDB44c1nqGvltiNFurQxvLqLPo2R01JCcUAF9CM] on October 26.

To verify the results, the ChatGPT developer engaged independent groups of researchers, including the authors of ETH and Nobel laureate in physics David Gross.

Exponential Time Hypothesis

The most significant claim for computer science concerns the Exponential Time Hypothesis, formulated in 1999. It is part of computational complexity theory and posits that the the 3-SAT problem cannot be solved in subexponential time in the number of variables.

ETH is used as a basis for assessing the complexity of other computational problems—from graph and database processing to computational biology. Hundreds of papers rely on it to prove conditional computational hardness for various problems. According to OpenAI, its model found [https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/378.md] a way to refute the hypothesis.

The proposed approach does not guarantee fast solutions in practice. Although its asymptotic complexity is below exponential, runtime may still be extremely large.

Refuting ETH also does not resolve the P vs NP problem. That question concerns the existence of polynomial-time algorithms for problems whose solutions can be verified efficiently. OpenAI’s claimed result does not provide such an algorithm.

For independent review, a group has been formed under Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi at the University of California, San Diego, and Ryan Williams at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. The researchers emphasized that their participation should not be taken as an endorsement of OpenAI’s conclusion. The group intends to organize an open verification of the proof with the scientific community.

Mathematical conjectures and quantum physics

In mathematics, OpenAI’s models, according to the company, proved the local smoothing conjecture proposed by Christopher Sogge in 1991. It describes wave behavior and, in particular, suggests that the strongest concentration of wave energy cannot persist for a long time.

OpenAI claims its system proved the local smoothing conjecture in all spatial dimensions. According to the firm, this implies proofs of the Kakeya conjecture [https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B0_%D0%BE%D0%B1_%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BA%D0%B5] and Stein’s Fourier transform [https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9F%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%BE%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B5_%D0%A4%D1%83%D1%80%D1%8C%D0%B5] restriction conjecture, also in all dimensions.

The AI produced the proof on October 18, and on October 19 it was formalized in Lean. The company published [https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/079.md] the materials in an open repository for other mathematicians to review.

The third claim concerns theoretical physics. OpenAI said its models constructed a mathematically rigorous proof of the existence of certain interacting quantum field theories in four-dimensional spacetime.

These include quantum chromodynamics (QCD)—the theory of the strong interaction between quarks and gluons—as well as a unified theory that includes gauge fields and fermions of the Standard Model of particle physics. The work includes a proof of the existence of pure Yang–Mills quantum theory [https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A2%D0%B5%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%8F_%D0%AF%D0%BD%D0%B3%D0%B0_%E2%80%94_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BB%D0%BB%D1%81%D0%B0], which describes interactions of gauge fields without matter.

This result is related to one of the Clay Mathematics Institute’s Millennium Prize Problems [https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B8_%D1%82%D1%8B%D1%81%D1%8F%D1%87%D0%B5%D0%BB%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B8%D1%8F]. However, that problem requires not only proving the existence of Yang–Mills theory but also establishing the presence of a mass gap. OpenAI did not claim a full solution to both questions.

A group to study the physics proof was created under Nobel laureate David Gross. It includes Michael Douglas, Arthur Jaffe, Igor Klebanov, and Nikita Nekrasov. According to the company, the models obtained initial existence proofs for Yang–Mills theory on September 9 and for QCD on September 18. Formalization of the first result in Lean was completed on October 16.

OpenAI changes how it publishes research

The new initiative follows criticism of the company’s approach to presenting scientific results. In early October, researchers criticized [https://forklog.com/en/news/mathematicians-criticize-openais-approach-to-publishing-proofs] OpenAI for its approach to publishing mathematical proofs, pointing to insufficient pre-review, the difficulty of independent analysis, and questions about the allocation of scientific authorship.

OpenAI now proposes treating AI-generated materials as primary research data that must still be converted into accepted scientific knowledge. To that end, it has chosen three modes of engagement with researchers:

mathematics: publish the proof and its formalization openly without creating a dedicated reviewer group;

computer science: open publication of materials while simultaneously forming an independent group to check and explain the result;

physics: provide materials to experts first, who will determine how they are published thereafter.

The company said participation in the research groups will be voluntary and unpaid. Scientists will not be required to sign nondisclosure agreements and will be free to publish their own conclusions.

OpenAI also said it does not seek scientific credit for discoveries produced by its models. At the same time, the company acknowledged that the initial selection of invited specialists creates unequal access to information.

In September, OpenAI presented [https://forklog.com/en/news/openai-publishes-claimed-solution-to-navier-stokes-millennium-problem] a proposed solution to the existence and smoothness problem for the Navier–Stokes equations, also obtained with AI.
