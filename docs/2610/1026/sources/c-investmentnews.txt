 Microsoft's AI workforce reset targets 27% of global staff in new buyout

 Magnificent 7 firm continues to ‘right size’ workforce in AI-related shift also seen in financial services.

 OCT 27, 2026

 By 

 Steve Randall
 [/authors/steve-randall]

 [/authors/steve-randall]

Microsoft is accelerating its workforce restructuring under the pressure of artificial intelligence investment, announcing a new Voluntary Exit Offer (VEO) that an unnamed source cited by CNBC said could reach approximately 60,000 employees or around 27 percent of the company's global workforce.

The announcement was made by Amy Coleman, Executive Vice President and Chief People Officer at Microsoft, published on the company's official blog [https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/26/introducing-a-voluntary-exit-offer/] and communicated internally to staff the same day. The offer gives eligible employees who may be considering a future outside the company a new choice to make that transition with support, Coleman said.

The offer is available to eligible employees at level 70 and below in participating teams in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and India, and is described as time-limited and voluntary.

The scale of the October program appears significantly broader than the company's earlier workforce action. In April 2026, Microsoft offered a separate Voluntary Retirement Program to approximately 8,750 US employees - roughly 7 percent of its domestic workforce using a formula that required an employee's age and years of service to total at least 70.

Eligible employees in that April program could receive between eight and 39 weeks of base salary depending on their level and length of service, with the full cost expected to reach $900 million according to Microsoft Chief Financial Officer Amy Hood.

Coleman noted that the October VEO builds on what Microsoft learned from the earlier Voluntary Retirement Program and reflects an evolving approach to change that is intended to create greater flexibility and more ways for employees to shape their next chapter.

A Magnificent 7 stock under pressure

Microsoft's moves carry direct investment implications. As one of the Magnificent 7 its strategic direction is closely watched by portfolio managers and clients alike.

Microsoft made $37.5 billion in capital expenditures during the second quarter of fiscal 2026, much of which went toward data center buildout, as part of a broader AI infrastructure race reshaping both the company's cost structure and its headcount priorities. Fortune reported that Microsoft is expected to invest $145 billion in capital expenditure in the current fiscal year, part of a projected $700 billion wave of Big Tech AI infrastructure spending in 2026.

That level of capital commitment - funded in part by reducing legacy workforce costs - is the central tension the VEO represents. As InvestmentNews has reported on AI's growing impact on financial services jobs [https://www.investmentnews.com/transformation/ai-results-in-job-cuts-at-firms-but-are-advisors-getting-better-service/267002], the displacement playing out in corporate America is not confined to technology firms. Wealth management companies including UBS [/companies/ubs/263044] Group, Morgan Stanley [/companies/morgan-stanley/262940] and LPL Financial [/companies/lpl-financial/262995] Holdings Inc. have all implemented cuts with AI cited as a contributing factor.

Recently, American Express became one of the latest financial services heavyweights to announce job cuts [https://www.investmentnews.com/fintech/american-express-to-make-job-cuts-as-ai-revolution-ramps-up/268723] fueled by AI’s inexorable rise, while InvestmentNews learned that advisor layoffs have been conducted at Domain Money [https://www.investmentnews.com/ria-news/adam-dells-domain-money-lays-of-advisors-citing-ai-and-efficiency/268212], the RIA founded by venture capitalist Adam Dell, who is the brother of billionaire Dell Technologies founder Michael Dell. 

The broader AI displacement pattern

Microsoft is not acting in isolation. Fortune reported that approximately 92,000 employees had been laid off across the tech sector so far in 2026, as companies look to trim overhead while accelerating AI investment. Google has offered similar voluntary exit schemes to specific divisions, including search, ads and commerce.

Approximately 49,000 layoffs were attributed to AI integration across all sectors in just the first four months of 2026, according to data from Challenger, Gray & Christmas, a Chicago-based employment consulting firm.

The consensus from workforce researchers and financial services observers is that AI is restructuring the labor market in layers - eliminating or reducing operational roles while preserving, and in some cases expanding, demand for specialized client-facing professionals.

For advisors, the Microsoft VEO is a useful real-world case study to share with clients navigating AI anxiety in their own careers or portfolios: a Magnificent 7 company simultaneously cutting legacy roles and betting hundreds of billions of dollars on artificial intelligence is attempting, at least formally, to do so with a measure of structured choice for the people it affects.
