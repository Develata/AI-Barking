Investors temper Anthropic IPO valuation expectations over revenue growth slowdown, sources say
By Milana Vinn and Anirban Sen
October 15, 20265:00 PM EDTUpdated October 16, 2026

Anthropic reports third-quarter revenue of $16.8 billion, up 45% from previous quarter, sources say
Some investors disappointed as sequential growth slowed after more than doubling in second quarter, sources say
Some investors discussed valuation of about $1.5 trillion, sources say, below earlier projection
NEW YORK, Oct 15 (Reuters) - Some current Anthropic investors and prospective investors in its upcoming initial public offering are weighing a lower valuation ‌for the AI company after its latest financial disclosures, four people familiar with the matter said, amid broader concerns over the ballooning cost of financing AI.
Some investors who attended Anthropic's investor day on Wednesday in San Francisco discussed a valuation of around $1.5 trillion, three of the people said, a significant step down from the roughly $2 trillion valuation ​that investors had previously expected for the November share sale.

At Wednesday's meeting, Anthropic told prospective IPO investors that its third-quarter revenue jumped ​45% to $16.8 billion from the previous quarter, and that it was profitable on an adjusted basis during the quarter.
While ⁠those numbers assuaged some concerns about the massive costs of developing AI systems, some investors were disappointed by the slowing sequential growth rate in ​the latest quarter, as Anthropic's revenue more than doubled from the first quarter to $11.6 billion in the second quarter, the people added. They spoke on ​condition of anonymity because they are not authorized to speak publicly on the matter.

Anthropic did not immediately respond to requests for comment.
A cooler reception for Anthropic's IPO could weigh on the wider AI market, whose near-term prospects investors see as hinging largely on the share sale's success, followed by OpenAI's potential listing next year.

During Wednesday's meeting at Anthropic's headquarters in San Francisco, attendees, mostly the company's most prominent investors, met with CEO Dario Amodei and finance chief Krishna Rao.
The Information earlier reported that ​investors were considering a lower valuation.
Founded ​in 2021 by former OpenAI ⁠executives including Amodei and his sister Daniela Amodei, who is now Anthropic's president, Anthropic has emerged as one of the leading generative AI developers with its Claude family of models. Last year, Anthropic's revenue surged twelvefold to nearly $4.6 billion, even as the company lost more than $8 billion on an operating basis, excluding writedowns of various liabilities mostly tied to previous fundraising, Reuters reported last month. In its IPO prospectus seen exclusively by Reuters in September, Anthropic laid out a vision that AI will transform ⁠the global ​economy more profoundly than industrialization, electricity and the internet, even as it reported a loss ​of $42 billion in 2025 and projected more spending for the upcoming AI buildout.
The company has committed to spending at least $518 billion over a decade for cloud services, computing and infrastructure.
