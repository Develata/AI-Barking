FBI arrests Saudi OpenAI engineer over covert recording device, DOJ says

 By Thomson Reuters
 Oct 13, 2026 | 2:59 PM

By Jaspreet Singh

Oct 13 (Reuters) – US federal agents arrested a Saudi national who was an OpenAI engineer and charged him with installing a hidden audio recording device in an executive conference room at the AI company, the Justice Department said on Tuesday.

The case highlights growing security concerns around sensitive AI research ​as companies and governments compete fiercely for the talent and technology that could confer economic and strategic ‌advantages.

[当事人], 35, had worked as an engineer at OpenAI since July 2025, the Justice Department said in a statement. An email sent to an address associated with that name did not elicit a response, and it was not immediately clear who his lawyer was.

OpenAI discovered the recording device plugged into a USB port on the back of a television in an executive conference room at one of its San Francisco ‌buildings ​on September 30, according to the department.

The thumb-sized device, which had a microphone ⁠and could connect to a camera, was ⁠concealed in toilet paper, according to an FBI complaint. It was installed on or about September 28, while its stored data was encrypted and inaccessible. Investigators later found a similar customized device in [当事人]’s personal bag.

OpenAI said in a post on X that it used its internal AI models to identify the person responsible for planting ​the device and trace its origins.

The company said it had evidence that the employee intended to place additional recording devices in sensitive areas of its offices to secretly record meetings.

“At this time, we have no indications of this individual ⁠inappropriately accessing or exfiltrating model weights, source code, or customer data. ⁠The employee was terminated,” the company said.

OpenAI referred the matter to the FBI, which is ​continuing to investigate. The Saudi embassy in the United States did not immediately respond to a Reuters request for comment.

“[当事人] ​allegedly concealed a covert recording device inside his employer’s executive conference room to secretly capture sensitive ‌communications about the company’s super intelligence technology,” FBI Director Kash Patel said in a statement.

[当事人] also used an AI productivity agent to seek information about meeting-room calendars and capabilities for about a month through September 16, according to the FBI complaint. He has been a legal permanent resident of the US since September 2020.

If convicted, [当事人] faces up to five years ⁠in prison, a $250,000 fine and three years of supervised release.

A LinkedIn profile showed that [当事人] studied at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and the University of California, Berkeley. He previously worked at Meta and Microsoft, it said.

Reuters could not independently verify ⁠the details mentioned in his LinkedIn profile.

Separately, ‌the Information reported that OpenAI was in early talks to acquire Trail of Bits, ⁠a startup that identifies and fixes vulnerabilities in open-source software. The talks were ongoing ​and could fall ‌apart, the report said, citing people familiar with the matter.

OpenAI and Trail of ​Bits did not ⁠immediately respond to Reuters requests for comment.

OpenAI said on Friday it fired three researchers after an investigation found they had violated company policies on handling sensitive information.

The researchers, Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni, had published a letter detailing the circumstances of their dismissal and arguing that the abrupt firings could create uncertainty among employees and undermine a culture in which researchers could raise safety concerns.

(Reporting by Jaspreet Singh in Bengaluru; additional reporting by Deepa Seetharaman in San Francisco; Editing by Diti Pujara, ​Peter Henderson and Anil D’Silva)
