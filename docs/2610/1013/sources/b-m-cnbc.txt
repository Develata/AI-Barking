FBI arrests Saudi OpenAI employee who allegedly planted recording device in conference room

Published Tue, Oct 13 20264:55 PM EDTUpdated Tue, Oct 13 20268:13 PM EDT

CJ Haddad [https://www.cnbc.com/cj-haddad/]@in/cristenhaddad/ [https://linkedin.com/in/cristenhaddad/]

WATCH LIVE [/live-tv/]

Key Points

An OpenAI employee was arrested after a "sophisticated recording device" was found hidden inside the executive conference room.

[当事人], 35, who is a national of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, was arrested in connection with the matter, the DOJ said.

[当事人] was fired from OpenAI after the incident, the company said.

Justice Department says Saudi national arrested for installing audio recording device at OpenAI

Closing Bell: Overtime [https://www.cnbc.com/closing-bell-overtime/]

An OpenAI [/quotes/OPENAI.FG/] employee was arrested after a "sophisticated recording device [https://x.com/OpenAINewsroom/status/2110095403198849132]" was discovered inside its executive conference room, according to the Department of Justice and a post by the company.

[当事人], 35, who is a national of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, was arrested on Oct. 9 in connection with the matter, the DOJ said in a release on Tuesday.

The company said an employee planted the device a few days before it was found, and the Federal Bureau of Investigation "moved quickly to make an arrest," the post said. The employee was terminated.

FBI Director Kash Patel said in a statement that [当事人] "allegedly concealed a covert recording device inside his employer's executive conference room to secretly capture sensitive communications about the company's super intelligence technology."

[当事人] is also listed as a partner at Raed Ventures [https://raed.vc/about/], a venture capital firm that focuses on the Middle East and North Africa, according to its website. The firm announced on Feb. 6, 2025, that H.H. Prince Khalid Bin Bader Al Saud, [https://raed.vc/news/raed-ventures-welcomes-new-venture-partner-h-h-prince-khalid-bin-bader-al-saud/] a member of the Saudi royal family, had joined the firm as a venture partner. [当事人] was announced as a venture partner [https://raed.vc/news/raed-ventures-welcomes-new-venture-partner-[当事人]/] two days later.

The Raed announcement for [当事人] lists Meta [/quotes/META/], Microsoft [/quotes/MSFT/] and SAP [/quotes/SAP/] as previous roles and said he studied computer science and engineering at MIT.

Raed, which is headquartered in the Saudi capital of Riyadh, did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The recording device planted at OpenAI's office.

Courtesy: U.S. Department of Justice

"At this time, we have no indications of this individual inappropriately accessing or exfiltrating model weights, source code, or customer data," OpenAI said in the post. "We take protecting our technology, data and people seriously, and as a matter of national importance."

[当事人] was not authorized to access the executive conference room where the device was found, according to the DOJ. The department said footage showed him near the conference room on multiple days before the device was discovered. The DOJ said that on Oct. 5, he went into the room. 

The DOJ said a bag was also found in the OpenAI building on or about Oct. 5 that belonged to [当事人]. Inside the bag, security found his wallet, ID and another recording device similar to the one that was in the executive conference room.

The department said a chatbot account associated with [当事人]'s spouse that "shared multiple identifiers and attributes with [当事人]'s own chatbot account" was used to get information about continuous audio recording and Wi-Fi. The DOJ said promps were submitted related to "building, customizing, installing, audio recording, testing, obfuscating, and remote accessing a device."

The device was concealed in toilet paper, and its contents were encrypted, according to the DOJ.

Photo of [当事人] checking a recording device in OpenAI's office in San Fransisco, California.

Courtesy: U.S. Department of Justice

This security incident comes as the ChatGPT-maker, along with competitors like Anthropic [/quotes/ANTHR.FG/] and other big tech names, faces heightened scrutiny [https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/28/openai-abandons-plan-to-release-upcoming-model-as-safety-concerns-escalate.html] over safety protocols after a series of cyber incidents where autonomous agents escaped testing environments and hacked into outside systems. 

Last week, OpenAI defended its decision [https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/09/openai-fired-researchers-ai-concerns.html] to fire three safety researchers, alleging a "significant breach of trust," saying that the researchers "violated clear policies on handling sensitive information." 

Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni raised safety concerns in a letter to OpenAI's leadership, saying that communications around their exits "have made our former colleagues afraid to speak and operate in ways that, until last week, were an integral part of working at OpenAI."

—CNBC's Lora Kolodny contributed to this article.
