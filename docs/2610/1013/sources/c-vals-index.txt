Motivation
As AI capabilities rapidly advance, understanding their potential to transform economic sectors has become critical for organizations making deployment decisions. Unlike existing aggregated metrics that treat all capabilities equally, the Vals Index is designed to reflect the potential impact of AI models on the U.S. economy. We accomplish this by computing a weighted average of model performance across key sectors, where each sector’s weight is proportional to its share of U.S. GDP.
Vals AI has developed a comprehensive suite of benchmarks measuring AI models’ ability to perform real-world tasks across finance, coding, legal, and tax work — benchmarks that no other party has full access to. The Vals Index aggregates six private and two public benchmarks to provide high signal into the real-world tradeoffs between capability, latency, and cost of deploying AI systems.

Methodology
Benchmark Selection, Economic Weighting, and Formula
The Vals Index aggregates performance across four sectors — Finance, Coding, Legal, and Tax — each weighted in proportion to its share of U.S. GDP, using Bureau of Economic Analysis value-added-by-industry data (as published on FRED). While this represents a vast oversimplification of how AI might impact the economy, it provides a useful proxy for measuring the potential economic significance of model capabilities:
Finance: Finance & Insurance, ~8.0% of U.S. GDP
[https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/fabv2] Finance Agent v2 : Multi-step financial reasoning tasks
[https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/emb] Excel Modeling Benchmark : Building and editing financial models in spreadsheets
Coding: Information sector (the closest GDP proxy for software work), ~5.6% of U.S. GDP
[https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/terminal-bench-4] Terminal-Bench 4.0 : Command-line interface problem solving
[https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/vibe-code] Vibe Code Bench : End-to-end app-building tasks
[https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/code-migration] Code Migration : Porting projects to another language, including COBOL modernization
Legal: Legal services, ~1.2% of U.S. GDP
[https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/legal_research] Legal Research Bench : Case and statute research with citation-backed answers
[https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/hlab] HLAB : Harvey’s Legal Agent Benchmark, long-horizon legal work product creation
Tax: Accounting and tax preparation services (NAICS 5412), ~0.5% of U.S. GDP
[https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/tax_agent_bench] Tax Agent Bench : Research-grade US corporate tax questions answered with cited authority
Each sector is the average of its benchmarks, and the sectors are combined with the following formula: Finance = AVG (FinanceAgentV2, EMB) Coding = AVG (TBench4, VibeCodeBench, CodeMigration) Legal = AVG (LegalResearch, HLAB) Tax = TaxAgentBench Vals_Index = ( 8.0 * Finance + 5.6 * Coding + 1.2 * Legal + 0.5 * Tax) / 15.3
Component Scores
Each benchmark column on the index is that benchmark’s own published standalone score — the same number, under the same methodology. The exception is Code Migration: there, the index scores a fixed subset of the published run: 50 of the 120 CLI migration tasks, plus all 10 COBOL tasks, weighted 75% CLI and 25% COBOL to match the standalone benchmark’s balance. This subset was chosen by Monte Carlo search, scored against the full 120-task leaderboard. The chosen subset reproduces the full 120-task result to Spearman 0.99 and 0.8 points of mean absolute accuracy error, with no model moving more than three ranks; on models held out of the selection, mean absolute error is 1.1 points.
