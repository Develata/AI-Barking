 Google Secures 890 MW of Additional Nuclear Capacity From Constellation in PJM
 Google and Constellation announce a 3,590 MW agreement combining 890 MW of additional nuclear capacity and 2,700 MW from existing plants. The timeline, licensing and the PJM framework remain to be watched.

 Google and Constellation Energy Corporation announce a long-term electricity supply agreement in the area of grid operator PJM Interconnection. According to the joint press release published on October 6, 2026, it covers 890 MW of new nuclear capacity and 2,700 MW from the existing fleet. Aggregate contracted capacity thus reaches 3,590 MW, or 3.59 GW, a sum calculated from the two components rather than a measure of annual output. In another nuclear story, Poland is delaying the start of its first reactor to 2036.

Two distinct components, 20 years and 15 years

The 890 MW of additional nuclear capacity is covered by a 20-year power purchase agreement (PPA), according to the release. It is future capacity, stemming from the modernization of operating reactors. The complementary 2,700 MW component, with a 15-year term, supports existing assets in PJM. The additional nuclear share represents about 24.8% of the contracted total, according to a calculation based on the published figures.

The program covers 11 units across 6 sites in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey. The breakdown by unit has not been published. While the IDB and the IAEA sign a cooperation agreement on nuclear energy and NANO Nuclear, IP3 and Cybernetic Intelligence sign a nuclear AI agreement, the Google-Constellation deal illustrates the mobilization of the existing fleet. Amanda Peterson Corio, Google's global head of energy and power, and Joe Dominguez, chairman, president and chief executive of Constellation, are among the executives associated with the announcement.

Investments, timeline and licensing

Constellation announces more than $4.3 billion in investments in the program. That amount does not indicate the electricity price paid by Google, which has not been disclosed. The first power uprate is expected by 2028, according to the companies. The release does not provide the full program timeline.

A thermal power uprate requires a license amendment reviewed by the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, the U.S. nuclear safety authority. The commercial contract is therefore not a safety authorization. The filings specific to the facilities concerned remain to be reviewed.

PJM framework and precedents

PJM is seeking to combine the connection of large loads, new generation and demand reduction options. Its board outlined a plan to integrate large loads on January 16, 2026. The alternative Interim Resource Adequacy Service (IRAS) targets new large loads without their own new generation. According to PJM's official page, approval from the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) was still pending at the time of research, under docket ER26-3515-000.

The precedents of Microsoft with Constellation and of Google with NextEra Energy involve reactor restarts. They represent a different route from the uprate of operating plants, which is the approach used here for the 890 MW.
