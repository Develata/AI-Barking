AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE OFFICE OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE POLICY,
    THE DIVISION OF PROFESSIONAL LICENSING, MAGIC HEALTH, INC, AND
                                  MAGIC HEALTH SERVICES, LLC.

Section 1. Parties

A. The Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy created in Utah Code § 13-72-201, an office
within the Utah Department of Commerce (hereinafter “Office”).

B. The Division of Professional Licensing, a division within the Utah Department of Commerce
(hereinafter “Division”).

C. Magic Health, Inc. and its subsidiary, Magic Health Services, LLC, doing business as “Nolla
Health” (jointly hereinafter “Participant”).

Section 2. Commencement Date and Participation Period

A. This Agreement shall be effective for twelve (12) months beginning on the Commencement
Date.

B. The Commencement Date shall be the date on which Participant makes the AI technology
available to customers and delivers written notice to the Office that the AI technology is
available.

Section 3. Purpose and Authority

A. The Office is a state agency with the authority to create and administer the Artificial
Intelligence Learning Laboratory Program (Utah Code. § 13-72-301).

B. The Office may, in consultation with relevant agencies, negotiate and grant limited regulatory
mitigation agreements to further the purposes of the Artificial Intelligence Learning Laboratory
Program (Utah Code § 13-72-401).

C. The Division is a state agency that administers and enforces all licensing laws of Title 58,
Occupations and Professions (Utah Code § 58-1-103).

D. Participant has applied to the Office for this regulatory mitigation and the applicant’s final
proposal and safety protocol is incorporated in Schedule B of this Agreement (hereinafter
referred to as the “Proposal”).

                                                                                       1
E. This agreement is a grant of regulatory mitigation only and does not constitute an
endorsement or approval from the State of Utah or any of its political subdivisions of
Participant’s use of artificial intelligence technologies.

Section 4. Scope of Mitigation

A. The Office, in consultation with the Division, grants regulatory mitigation to Participant’s
provision of services using artificial intelligence technologies solely as outlined in the Proposal.
The term of this Agreement, along with the term of any subsequent agreement extensions, shall
constitute Participant’s demonstration period.

B. The demonstration period and accompanying mitigation specified in Schedule A shall become
effective on the Commencement Date, provided that, prior to or on such date, this Agreement has
been signed by all parties.

C. Participant is subject to all applicable Federal, State, and local laws not expressly waived or
modified by the terms of this Agreement, including any changes to existing law during the
demonstration period (Utah Code § 13-72-401(7)). If relevant laws change during the
demonstration period and affect the enforceability of any provision of this Agreement, the Office
may require the parties to amend this Agreement. If such amendment is required, the terms and
mitigation described in this Agreement will remain in full force and effect until any amendment
is executed. If no new agreement can be made, this Agreement will be terminated effective on
the day the relevant law went into effect or when the parties mutually agree no amendment can
be reached, whichever date is later.

D. Except as related to the relief granted in this Agreement, nothing in this Agreement prevents
any user of the Participant’s AI technology, any third party, the State of Utah, or any of its
subdivisions other than the Division, from seeking any legally available remedy from Participant
under laws not expressly waived or modified by the terms of this Agreement in the event such
user or third party is physically harmed as a result of using the Participant’s AI technology.

E. Participant protections set forth in this Agreement shall jointly apply to both Magic Health,
Inc. and Magic Health Services, LLC. Similarly, Magic Health, Inc. and Magic Health Services,
LLC shall jointly fulfill Participant obligations set forth in this Agreement.

Section 5. Scope of Activities

A. Participant shall perform all relevant activities in accordance with the methodologies and
safety protocols set forth in the Proposal. The Proposal may be amended by Participant or the

                                                                                       2
Office’s Director (in consultation with the Division) as long as the amendment approval is in
writing and signed by all Parties.

B. Participant may not use regulatory mitigation specified in this agreement to create or provide
goods, services, or labor not specified in the Proposal.

Section 6. General Participant Obligations

Unless the Office determines otherwise in writing, Participant agrees as follows:

A. Capacity

Participant shall possess the technical expertise, financial resources, and capability to responsibly
deploy and use the proposed artificial intelligence technology. Participant shall also have an
effective plan to monitor and minimize both identified and potential risks from the activities of
Participant’s AI technology.

B. Safeguards

         1. Participant shall abide by safeguards, including safety measures, testing protocols,
         quality assurance, and oversight methodologies outlined in the Proposal to protect users
         of Participant’s AI technology from potential harm that may result from such activities.
         Participant shall abide by any other safeguards prescribed by the Office set forth in this
         Agreement.

         2. Participant is fully responsible for the proper and efficient monitoring, operation, and
         maintenance of any activities stemming from the mitigation granted in this Agreement.

         3. Participant’s Terms of Service, Terms of Use, End User License Agreements, or any
         other agreements presented to or required of end users to engage in Participant’s artificial
         intelligence technology shall not disclaim, limit, restrict, or exclude Participant’s legal
         liability for physical, medical, or legal harm resulting from the outputs of Participant's
         artificial intelligence technology authorized under this Agreement. Participant shall
         submit all current and proposed user-facing Terms of Service, Terms of Use, and End
         User License Agreements to the Office for review prior to the Commencement Date.

C. Registration

Participant must be registered to do business in the State of Utah.

                                                                                       3
D. Permissions

         1. Participant may make public statements or engage in related publicity consistent with
         scope and purpose of this Agreement, except that Participant shall not include any
         statement in advertising, media, or promotional materials that implies the Office’s or
         State of Utah’s endorsement of Participant or Participant’s technology. Factual
         statements will not be considered as implying any endorsement.

         2. Except as related to the relief granted in this Agreement, Participant is responsible for
         obtaining and maintaining all necessary permits, licenses, and permissions required for
         the activities in Participant’s Proposal from other federal, state, or local government
         agencies having jurisdiction over activities in the Proposal. Participant shall furnish any
         such permits and approvals to the Office upon request.

E. Data Security

         1. Participant shall comply with all privacy and safe data usage requirements as set forth
         in the Proposal and as stipulated by the Office in this Agreement.

         2. Participant shall not advertise a specific product or service to a Utah user engaging
         with Participant’s artificial intelligence technology, unless Participant:

                  a. clearly and conspicuously identifies the advertisement as an advertisement; and

                  b. clearly and conspicuously discloses to the Utah user any sponsorship, business
                  affiliation, or agreement that the supplier has with a third party to promote,
                  advertise, or recommend the product or service.

         3. Participant shall not sell, lease, license, or otherwise transfer user data, personal health
         information, or derived metadata to any third-party.

         4. Notwithstanding Section 6(E)(3), Participant may disclose user data solely under the
         following defined circumstances:

                  a. To cloud infrastructure, hosting, or technical service providers acting strictly on
                  Participant’s behalf for services necessary to engage in the activities described in
                  Schedule B of this Agreement.

                                                                                       4
                  b. To licensed Utah pharmacists, pharmacies, or treating providers, solely as
                  necessary to issue, transmit, and fulfill prescription orders or deliver telehealth
                  care authorized by this Agreement.

                  c. To the Office, the Division, or OAIP-vetted third-party auditors to fulfill
                  mandatory reporting, statistical validation, and evaluation requirements under
                  Section 6(H) and Section 20(E), provided that individual-level data shared for
                  external validation is de-identified.

                  d. As required to comply with applicable federal or state law, judicial orders, or
                  administrative subpoenas.

                  e. To third parties specifically designated by the user/patient upon receiving the
                  user's explicit, affirmative consent or direct instruction in accordance with Section
                  20(D).

F. Cybersecurity

         1. Participant shall implement reasonable protections against data leaks to minimize the
         risk of breach of confidential information in accordance with the data security and
         privacy protection protocols described in Participant’s Proposal and with general
         cybersecurity best practices. These protections include, as applicable:

                  a. Keeping data system testing environments separate from production
                  environments;

                  b. Maintaining reasonable server and storage capacity to enable continuous
                  availability of data systems;

                  c. Formally managing technology assets through their lifecycle; and

                  d. Using integrity checking mechanisms to verify software, firmware, and
                  information integrity.

         2. Participant shall ensure that its cyber security framework is regularly updated to
         address emerging threats and vulnerabilities.

G. Disclosures

                                                                                       5
         1. Participant shall abide by the disclosure requirements specified in Schedule A of this
         Agreement.

H. Reporting

         1. Participant shall, for the duration of this Agreement, submit monthly, quarterly, and
         annual reports to the Office at ai@utah.gov. All reports shall include the information
         specified in Schedule A of this Agreement.

         2. Subject to the Office’s confidentiality obligation described in Section 10 of this
         Agreement, for the duration of this Agreement, the Office will make the quarterly reports,
         as submitted by Participant, available to the public.

         3. Participants will share with OAIP-vetted third-party auditors de-identified individual
         level data from the pilot for external validation at a monthly, quarterly, and annual basis.
         Participant shall comply with all reasonable data sharing requirements, including the
         minimum number of samples required for statistical testing articulated in Schedule A.

J. Failure to meet obligations.

If Participant fails to meet any of the obligations set forth in this Section or Schedule A, the
Office may request more comprehensive reports from Participant and a mutually agreeable plan
for resolving any issues related to such obligations. Participant shall have at least thirty (30)
business days to propose and implement a cure. The Office shall not unreasonably withhold or
delay acceptance of Participant's proposed plan or cure. If, after good-faith collaboration and the
expiration of the applicable cure period, Participant cannot put forward a plan to adequately
resolve the issue, as determined by the Office, the Office may elect to terminate this Agreement.
In the event the Office terminates this Agreement under this provision, the Participant shall be
given 30 days to cease operations of the AI Technology in Utah (“Unwinding Period”).

Section 7. Notice

All notices required to be given, by either Party to the other, shall be deemed fully given when
delivered by email addressed and sent, with any required copies, as provided on the signature
page of this Agreement. Any party may change its notice address by giving the other party notice
of the change in accordance with this Section.

Section 8. Indemnification

Section 9. Intellectual Property

Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed to transfer, assign, license, or otherwise convey
any intellectual property rights from one party to the other. Each party retains all right, title, and
interest in and to any intellectual property (including algorithms, models, software, data

processes, techniques, technology, tools, and any other proprietary rights) it owns, develops, or
otherwise controls, whether created before, during, or after the term of this Agreement, and
regardless of whether such development occurs in connection with, in performance of, or
independently of this Agreement. No rights are granted by implication, estoppel, or otherwise.

Section 10. Public Records

A. The Office is a governmental entity subject to the Government Records Access and
Management Act, Utah Code §§ 63G-2-103 to 63G-2-901 (“GRAMA”). Accordingly, records in
the Office’s possession, including this Agreement, may be subject to public disclosure. The
Office’s confidentiality obligations shall comply with GRAMA. The Office will classify
Participant’s application for regulatory mitigation and any reports required by Section 6(H) of
this Agreement as protected records, where permitted by applicable law, in accordance with Utah
Code §§ 63G-2-305 and 63G-2-309, except where explicitly stated in this Agreement or a Pilot
Addendum

B. Participant shall submit all confidentiality claims to the Office in accordance with Utah Code
§ 63G-2-309 for any confidential, proprietary, or trade secret information. To the extent
permitted by GRAMA, the Office shall treat Participant’s additional information (collectively,
with Participant’s application and any required reports, “Participant Information”) submitted in
connection with this Agreement or any Pilot Addendum as protected records in accordance with
Utah Code § 63G-2-305, except where explicitly stated in this Agreement or a Pilot Addendum.
The Office shall provide Participant with prompt written notice of any GRAMA request or other
legal process seeking disclosure of Participant Information and, where permitted, a reasonable
opportunity to seek protection prior to disclosure. To the extent disclosure is required, the Office
shall disclose only the portion required by law and use reasonable efforts to redact Participant
Information. The Office shall use Participant Information solely for purposes of administering
this Agreement and shall not use such information to develop or train artificial intelligence
systems without Participant’s prior written consent.

Section 11. Choice of Law, Venue, and Jurisdiction

This Agreement shall be governed by and construed pursuant to the laws of the State of Utah.

Section 12. Termination

The Office or Participant may terminate this Agreement at any time, and for any reason, prior to
the completion of the 12-month mitigation period or during any subsequent extension term.

shall be given an Unwinding Period. During this Unwinding Period, the Agreement continues in
full force and effect. Termination shall be effective upon expiration of the Unwinding Period.

Participant may not assign or transfer regulatory mitigation granted in this Agreement without
the prior written consent of the Office. Approval of an assignment does not establish any legal
relationship between the Office and any other third party. The Office shall not be liable for any
act or omission committed pursuant to such an assignment.

Section 14. Entire Agreement

A. This Agreement, including all referenced documents and attachments, constitutes the entire
agreement between the Parties regarding the subject matter hereof.

B. This Agreement, together with any amendments or addenda approved by the Office,
supersedes any other written or oral understanding of the Parties. This Agreement may not be
modified except by a written instrument executed by all Parties. This Agreement may be signed
in counterparts, and may be signed electronically.

Section 15. Agreement Extension

Participant may request no more than two (2) extensions of up to 12 months for regulatory
mitigation. Extension requests must be made no later than 30 days before the end of the ongoing
period. The Office, in agreement with the Division, shall grant or deny an extension request
before expiration of the initial demonstration period or any subsequent extension term in

the invalid or unenforceable portion will be severed from this Agreement but the rest of the
Agreement will remain in full force and effect.

Training Dataset and Scoring Framework:
Nolla has consolidated a dataset of over 100,000 clinically confirmed acne cases with images
labeled by trained experts using two established clinical scoring frameworks.

The FDA-recommended Investigator Global Assessment (IGA) provides a simplified 5-point
global assessment (0-4):

    •​ 0 = Clear: No inflammatory or non-inflammatory lesions
    •​ 1 = Almost Clear: Rare lesions, mostly non-inflammatory, no more than one papule
    •​ 2 = Mild: Some non-inflammatory lesions, few inflammatory lesions
    •​ 3 = Moderate: Many non-inflammatory lesions, some inflammatory lesions
    •​ 4 = Severe: Many inflammatory and non-inflammatory lesions, potential nodules

The IGA has demonstrated robust inter-rater reliability for over a decade, with weighted kappa
values ranging from 0.52 to 0.75 (Tan et al., 2013²⁵; FDA Guidance Document, 2018²⁶). The
Global Acne Grading System (GAGS) complements the IGA by dividing the face into six regions
(forehead, right cheek, left cheek, nose, chin, chest/back) and scoring based on:

    •​ Area: weighted by surface area
    •​ Oiliness: weighted by sebum production
    •​ Lesion type: No lesion=0, Comedone=1, Papule=2, Pustule=3, Nodule=4
    •​ Regional factors: Forehead=2, Right cheek=2, Left cheek=2, Nose=1, Chin=1,

         Chest/back=3

Total GAGS score ranges from 0 to 44, normalized to a 0-4 scale for harmonization with IGA.

Model Architecture and Training:
The Nolla acne severity scoring model is built on a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone,
fine-tuned end-to-end for continuous acne severity regression. The model processes
standardized facial images by dividing them into patches and analyzing spatial relationships
across the entire image simultaneously - an approach that captures long-range facial patterns
more effectively than traditional image recognition methods. Model inputs consist of a
standardized five-angle image sequence (front, up, down, left, right), ensuring full facial
coverage across all assessments.

The model was trained on over 100,000 (typical datasets contain <10,000 cases) labeled acne
cases spanning the full IGA severity range (0.0-4.0), using an aggressive augmentation strategy
to maximize robustness across real-world image conditions including variation in lighting, angle,
skin tone, and image quality. The result is a model that outputs a continuous severity score on
the IGA scale, rather than a coarse categorical label, enabling more precise treatment selection.

Treatment Plan Selection:
The AI-generated severity score is provided as a structured input to a large language model
(LLM) inference layer, which synthesizes the score alongside patient-specific contextual
information collected during intake - including prior treatments, reported symptoms, skin

Nolla Health – Utah AI Pilot Proposal | Magic Health Services, LLC | April 2026 | Page 18

                                                                                       39
sensitivity, and contraindications - to generate a candidate topical formulation recommendation
from the approved formulary. The LLM selects the treatment optimized for the patient's specific
severity profile and clinical context within the predefined protocol boundaries.

Pre-Deployment Performance:
Benchmarking was conducted on a held-out test set of 10,348 acne cases, strictly separated
from training and validation data throughout model development. Uncertainty quantification was
obtained via bootstrapping with 2,000 resampling iterations, yielding 95% confidence intervals.

    •​ R² = 0.947 (95% CI: 0.940-0.953) - the model explains approximately 94.7% of the
         variance in expert-assigned severity scores, exceeding the medical utility threshold of R²
         ≥ 0.90 for automated acne scoring and representing state-of-the-art performance in this
         field

    •​ Mean Absolute Error (MAE) = 0.111 (95% CI: 0.107-0.115) on the normalized IGA scale
         (0.0-4.0)

    •​ Pearson r = 0.977 (p<0.05; 95% CI: 0.974-0.980) - strong linear concordance between
         model predictions and expert labels

    •​ Spearman ρ = 0.785 (p<0.05) - robust monotonic agreement across the severity
         distribution

    •​ Bland-Altman mean bias = -0.072 - a slight systematic underestimation of severity
         below 1.8%, with 95% limits of agreement of [-0.529, +0.385], considered clinically
         acceptable given inherent inter-rater variability in human dermatologist scoring

Real-World Clinical Concordance:
Beyond pre-deployment benchmarking, real-world performance data from Stage 0 provides
additional validation. Across the last 1000 treatments in the Nolla Skin App, current licensed
clinicians agreed with the AI-generated treatment recommendation in ~96% of cases. The
remaining 4% involved minor single-step strength adjustments within the same medication class
(e.g., tretinoin 0.03% to 0.06%), with no cases requiring a materially different treatment
approach.
