# Anthropic Opens Claude Mythos 5.1 to More Vetted Security Teams

Posted Today

## Anthropic Widens Cyber Model Access

A regional hospital’s security team or a small municipal utility can now apply for reduced-safeguard access to Anthropic’s most capable models. On October 6, the company rebuilt its Cyber Verification Program into three access tiers, folding in Project Glasswing and easing safety blocks for vetted defenders on Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Mythos 5.1.

Claude Mythos – artistic impression. Image credit: Alius Noreika / AI

### Key Takeaways

- Anthropic merged Project Glasswing and its original Cyber Verification Program into one offering with Defense, Red Team and Specialized access tiers.

- Glasswing partners verified at least 129,000 vulnerabilities between April and July 2026, and Anthropic’s own open-source scans found 5,500 more; over 33,000 rate critical or high.

- In Anthropic’s tests, the Red Team tier ran without blocks and completed 34 of 50 attack scenarios, roughly matching the unrestricted model.

## Three Tiers, Three Rulebooks

For six months, the company ran two separate tracks. Project Glasswing gave organizations securing critical software access to Claude Mythos. The original Cyber Verification Program gave vetted teams reduced safeguards on Claude Opus and Claude Sonnet. Both now live in one structure, and every tier includes Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Mythos 5.1 and future models.

Defense Access covers security operations, incident response, malware reverse engineering and vulnerability validation. It is open to security teams at companies, nonprofits, universities and government bodies that defend their own systems. Critical infrastructure operators of any size, smaller security firms, open-source maintainers and individual researchers with a record of reported vulnerabilities also qualify. That puts the tier within reach of groups that cannot afford enterprise-grade security suites. Anthropic aims to answer these applications within a few days.

Red Team Access adds authorized penetration testing and red-teaming, but only against systems a team has permission to test. In-house red teams, government red teams and testing firms qualify; individual researchers do not. Real-time blocks still stop actions such as deploying ransomware, damaging physical systems or probing high-risk safety systems. Reviews take a few weeks, and applicants get Defense Access while they wait.

Specialized Access carries the fewest blocks. It serves a short list of verified organizations cleared to test systems whose failure could hurt people or disrupt markets, such as flight operating systems, power grids, telecom networks, interbank transfer infrastructure and government administrative networks. Anthropic vets each one together with the US government. Glasswing members move straight into this tier without reapproval for current models.

## What the Tests Showed

Anthropic ran Claude Opus 5.5 through CyScenarioBench, an evaluation of whether a model can plan and execute multi-stage cyber operations. Each tier faced ten challenges, five attempts each.

Without program access, every task stopped at the first prompt. In the Defense tier, 46 of 50 trials hit a block at some point and four succeeded. The Red Team tier saw no blocks at all. The model completed 34 of 50 tasks there, effectively the same as its 67.6% success rate with no safeguards.

## Glasswing’s Count So Far

Glasswing partners verified at least 129,000 software vulnerabilities between April and July 2026. Anthropic’s own open-source scanning added 5,500 between April and October. More than 33,000 of them carry critical or high severity ratings.

Anthropic calls these numbers a floor. They come from 33 partner reports, and fewer than half the partners shared patch counts, often because fixes were still underway. The company expects the true impact to be at least five times higher. Several partners told Anthropic that Mythos models sped up their bug hunting by months or even years.

## Conditions for Entry

Enrolled organizations must allow data retention so Anthropic can watch for misuse. Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, due later this fall, will let eligible organizations keep that data in cloud infrastructure they control. The program runs on the Claude Platform, Google Cloud’s Vertex AI and Microsoft Foundry, and on Amazon Bedrock only for customers eligible for those safeguards. Existing members keep their settings and get automatic review for the newest models.

The expansion follows months of unease. Anthropic’s April debut of Claude Mythos Preview raised fears that AI could break software faster than anyone could secure it, and an academic analysis of the model’s exploit skills found it far better than earlier systems at turning bugs into working attacks. Anthropic chose to keep Mythos Preview out of general release. Tiered vetting is its answer to the same tension: hand defenders offensive-grade tools, but check who they are first.

Google has reached a similar place, sending its Gemini 4 Argon model to vetted defenders through a closed program before any wider release. Faster discovery shifts the bottleneck to patching, which still depends on human teams, budgets and cyber risk management that grows more complex each year. Attackers face no such queue, as campaigns like North Korean operatives hijacking a developer’s code show.
