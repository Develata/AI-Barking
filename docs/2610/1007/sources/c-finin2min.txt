# Anthropic Opens Powerful AI Models to Vetted Cyber Teams After 129,000 Vulnerabilities Are Found

## Finin2min 2-minute summary

Anthropic is widening controlled access to its strongest AI models for qualified cybersecurity researchers. The redesigned Cyber Verification Program combines earlier security initiatives and creates three access tiers, including a tightly restricted tier for testing safety-critical systems such as power grids, flight systems and interbank payment infrastructure. The move follows security work in which partner teams identified at least 129,000 verified vulnerabilities between April and July, while Anthropic says its own open-source scanning found another 5,500 between April and October. For finance users, the important point is not that unrestricted cyber capability is being released; it is that frontier-model access is being expanded under a vetting and use-case framework intended to support defensive testing while managing misuse risk.

**Last verified:** 2026-10-07 01:14 IST

## Key verified facts

- Anthropic said its redesigned Cyber Verification Program will give vetted security professionals access to powerful models with fewer standard restrictions for approved defensive work.

- Project Glasswing partners identified at least 129,000 verified vulnerabilities from April through July, according to Reuters reporting on Anthropic’s figures.

- Anthropic said its own open-source scanning identified about 5,500 additional vulnerabilities from April through October.

- More than 33,000 of the vulnerabilities were rated critical or high severity.

- The program has three access tiers: Defense, Red Team and Specialized.

- The Specialized tier is intended for a small group testing safety-critical systems such as power grids, aviation systems and interbank transfer infrastructure.

- Eligible participants can access Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Mythos 5.1 and future models under the program.

## What actually changed

The change is mainly about **who may use the most capable models, for what purpose, and under what controls**. Ordinary product guardrails can block some prompts that resemble exploitation or intrusion even when a legitimate security researcher is trying to reproduce a vulnerability. The verification program creates a channel in which qualified professionals can be vetted, placed into an appropriate tier and given broader capability for defensive work. That is different from removing safety restrictions for everyone.

The three-tier design also signals that Anthropic is trying to match model access to risk. A security team scanning a public open-source package does not need the same privileges as a researcher testing a power-system control environment. That separation matters because the cost of an error, misuse or leaked technique is much higher in critical infrastructure.

## Why the vulnerability numbers matter

The 129,000 figure is large, but it should not be read as 129,000 newly discovered zero-day attacks on banks or governments. A vulnerability program can include flaws across many software projects and severity levels, and the number describes verified findings produced through the initiative. The more relevant signal is that AI-assisted security work is moving from demonstration projects toward industrial-scale discovery and triage.

For companies, that can shorten the time between a flaw being introduced and a defender finding it. It can also increase the volume of issues that security teams must validate, prioritize and patch. The bottleneck may therefore shift from “can we find the bug?” to “can we remediate the right bugs quickly enough?”

## Simple example for a CFO or technology head

Imagine a bank uses hundreds of open-source libraries across mobile banking, treasury systems and internal dashboards. A traditional security team may review only the highest-risk components because human testing is expensive. An AI-assisted program can inspect a much wider set of code and flag suspicious patterns. If 1,000 findings are produced, the bank still needs human validation, severity ranking, ownership and a patch timetable.

The financial value comes from reducing the probability and expected loss of a serious incident, not from the raw number of AI-generated findings. A program that produces many low-quality alerts can increase cost. A program that reliably identifies exploitable high-severity flaws early can reduce outage, fraud, legal and remediation exposure.

## Finance, audit and governance implications

Boards and audit committees should treat frontier-model cyber access as a controlled capability rather than a normal software licence. Key questions include who is authorized, what systems may be tested, how prompts and outputs are logged, whether sensitive source code can leave the company environment, and how discoveries are escalated.

Banks, insurers, payment firms and listed companies also need to connect technical findings to operational-risk and disclosure processes. A severe vulnerability may create incident-response obligations even before exploitation is confirmed. Vendor-risk teams should ask whether external researchers or AI providers can see confidential system information and what contractual restrictions apply.

## What not to misunderstand

This announcement does **not** mean Anthropic has opened unrestricted offensive cyber capability to the public. Access is described as vetted and tiered. It also does not mean every one of the 129,000 findings was a critical vulnerability, nor that every finding affected a major enterprise. Finally, finding a vulnerability is not the same as fixing it: remediation still depends on software owners, maintainers and affected organizations.

## What to watch next

Watch how Anthropic verifies researchers, which organizations receive Specialized access, how results are disclosed to software maintainers, and whether independent evidence shows that AI-assisted discovery reduces time-to-patch rather than merely increasing alert volume. For regulated financial institutions, the most important next step is whether supervisory guidance begins to address the use of frontier AI in penetration testing, red teaming and critical-system assurance.

## Finin2min bottom line

AI is becoming a practical cybersecurity tool, but powerful defensive capability also creates governance risk. The sensible finance view is to focus on controlled access, measurable remediation outcomes and accountability—not on the headline vulnerability count alone.
