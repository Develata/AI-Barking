Recent trends include multimodal models that can handle text, images, and video simultaneously, AI agents that can perform multi-step tasks autonomously, and major progress in AI for scientific research.

How is AI regulated in Europe and the United States?

Europe took the lead with the EU AI Act, which classifies AI systems by risk level and sets strict rules for high-risk applications like biometric surveillance and hiring tools. In the US, the approach has been more fragmented: a mix of executive orders, sector-specific guidelines, and state-level legislation. Both regions are actively debating how to balance innovation with safety and accountability. We cover these policy developments regularly in our feed, because regulation shapes everything from what AI companies can build to how you'll interact with AI in daily life.
