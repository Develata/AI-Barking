页面标题：Nolla Derm AI Prescription: Now Piloting in Utah

Q: What is the Nolla Derm pilot?
A: The pilot uses AI to select approved acne treatments and issue initial prescriptions and renewals for eligible adults in Utah, with physician oversight throughout the program. It runs under a 12-month agreement with Utah’s Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy.

Q: Who can participate?
A: Utah residents ages 18 and older with mild-to-moderate acne. People who are pregnant, trying to become pregnant, or breastfeeding, or who have certain conditions (such as immunosuppression, severe kidney or liver disease, or a past reaction to one of the pilot’s medications) aren’t eligible for AI prescribing and are routed to a physician. Severe acne is directed to in-person dermatology care.

Q: How does it work?
A: Download the Nolla Derm app, verify your identity with a photo ID, complete the consent process and a short health questionnaire, and take a five-angle face scan. The AI assesses your acne severity from the scan and selects an appropriate treatment plan. The whole process takes about 10–15 minutes.

Q: What can the AI prescribe?
A: Only topical acne medications from a restricted list: tretinoin, adapalene, clindamycin with benzoyl peroxide, benzoyl peroxide, and azelaic acid, some combined with niacinamide or topical spironolactone. Each plan includes a morning and an evening treatment. The pilot does not include oral or systemic medications, hormonal therapies, or isotretinoin.

Q: Will a physician review my prescription?
A: For the first 100 patients, two physicians independently approve every prescription before it is issued, which can add up to 24 hours. Up to 500 patients, the AI issues prescriptions directly and a physician reviews every case afterward, at least weekly. After that, physicians review a sample of at least 10% of prescriptions every month, plus every case involving an escalation or side effect. Moving to each new stage requires meeting safety targets and written approval from the state.

Q: What if I need more care?
A: If your scan shows more significant acne, a physician reviews your case before any prescription is issued, free of charge and usually within 48 hours. Severe or nodulocystic acne is directed to in-person dermatology care. Side effects or a lack of improvement are escalated to a physician.

Q: Can I speak with a physician?
A: Yes. You can message a licensed physician through the app at any time at no additional cost, and you can ask for a full case review whenever you like. Clinical support comes from a physician.

Q: How much does it cost?
A: The discounted subscription is $4.99 per month (normally $9.99) and includes daily AI skin scans, educational content, telehealth services, and prescription transmission. Medication costs are separate: compounded morning and evening treatments cost $49.99 with home delivery. Generic versions filled at a local pharmacy are often $10–40 at retail or covered by insurance.

Q: How do I get my medication?
A: Choose home delivery of a compounded formulation, which usually ships within 3 days and arrives 3–7 days later, or same- or next-day pickup of a generic version at a local pharmacy. The treatment recommendation is the same either way, and the AI doesn’t influence which you choose.

Q: How is my health information protected?
A: Nolla Health uses industry-standard security practices and handles patient information in accordance with HIPAA and applicable healthcare privacy laws. Your data is never sold, and it’s only shared as needed to deliver your care (for example with your pharmacy), with the state in de-identified form for oversight, or with your consent.
