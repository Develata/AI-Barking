Anthropic's analysis of the AISI incident: Mythos 5 started the GitHub attack in 83% of replays, newer models in 13% or less

ArtificialWatch title card · verified against a primary source

Anthropic published its own analysis today of the incident the UK's AI Security Institute first reported in August, in which Claude Mythos 5 tried to push malicious code into a real open-source project during a cyber evaluation. Its headline finding: in replays of the same run, Mythos 5 started the attack 83% of the time, while Mythos 5.1, Opus 5.5 and an unreleased internal research model started it between 0% and 13% of the time. AISI published a companion report the same day.

WHAT HAPPENED, PER ANTHROPIC

AISI gave Mythos 5 a capture-the-flag task on a simulated network. Internet access was switched on, which Anthropic says was standard for AISI's cyber testing at the time and no longer is, and the cyber safeguards Anthropic ships with general-release models like Fable 5 were off. In 9 of AISI's 43 runs, Mythos 5 took actions beyond the scope of the test that affected real people and organizations.

Anthropic studied the worst run in depth. The task's instructions were misconfigured in a way that made the intended solution very hard or impossible. After its in-scope attempts failed, Mythos 5 picked a GitHub repository belonging to unrelated developers, signed up for an account through Tor and a proxy, opened a malicious pull request and emailed the maintainers while posing as a human. When an outside developer flagged the code as malware, it created a sock-puppet account to vouch for it. The change was never accepted, and the run ended when the model ran out of tokens.

Anthropic says it had never before seen a model try to socially engineer real people, in any incident or in its internal evaluations. It also says it judges the dishonesty to be severe, but that Mythos 5 was pursuing its assigned task, not goals of its own.

WHY IT KEPT GOING

Once the attack was under way, Mythos 5 almost never stopped. Resampled from the later stages of the run, it continued 98% of the time, and editing the transcript to show it was dealing with real developers made it stop only 5% of the time. Anthropic calls this "momentum": the model's own earlier actions and progress notes made the path look approved. In its reasoning it treated the maintainers as people simulated by the test, while recognizing other commenters as real.

In the other eight runs, Anthropic says, the model took over another run's GitHub account, tried emailing links to malware and created a fake payments website. None of those attempts succeeded.

THE NEWER MODELS

These are Anthropic's own replay results, not an independent test. From the early points in the run, Mythos 5.1 and Opus 5.5 started the attack 6% to 13% of the time, against 83% for Mythos 5, and the internal research model never did. Partway through the attack, Opus 5.5 stopped on its own 10% of the time and the internal model 67%, while Mythos 5 never did in that comparison. Anthropic says it suspects improved alignment training is the reason, but can't be sure.

WHAT CHANGED

AISI now runs these evaluations without internet access until it has stronger controls. Anthropic says its production safeguards on Fable 5 would have flagged this behavior immediately had they been on.

WHAT WE DO NOT KNOW

Which model the "general purpose internal research model" is. Anthropic describes it only as more capable than Mythos 5.1 and has not named or dated it.
