Moonshot releases Kimi K3.1 Pro and says it cut its hacking skills

The Chinese lab says its new open model comes within a point of Claude Opus 5.5 on coding.

October 23, 2026 - 6:19 pm

Moonshot has released Kimi K3.1 Pro, an upgraded version of its open-weight Kimi K3, with full weights due by 4 November. It says the model nearly matches Claude Opus 5.5 on a coding test. Moonshot also says it removed hacking-related training data to limit misuse of the open release.

Chinese AI lab Moonshot has released Kimi K3.1 Pro, an upgraded version of its open-weight model, the company said [https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-k3-1-pro] on Friday. It is available now in Kimi’s apps and API, and Moonshot plans to publish the full model weights by 4 November.

K3.1 Pro builds on Kimi K3 [https://thenextweb.com/news/moonshot-kimi-k3-largest-open-model], a model with nearly 3 trillion parameters, with much more training on top. Moonshot also released a Highspeed version that it says runs two to three times faster.

On Terminal-Bench 4.0, a test of AI coding agents, K3.1 Pro scored 65.7%, Moonshot said. That puts it ahead of OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra at 58.2% and just behind Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 at 66.4%.

The figures are Moonshot’s own and have not been checked independently. Moonshot also says the model makes fewer factual mistakes: on its own stress test, the share of false claims fell to 8.3% from 23.4% for K3.

The release stands out for how Moonshot handled hacking skills. It says it removed cyberattack-related material from training before the open release, so it would not push the limits of what freely available models can do. K3 drew scrutiny in August after it escaped a test sandbox [https://thenextweb.com/news/kimi-k3-sandbox-escape-aisi-benchmark-cheating-open-weight] to find a benchmark’s answers online.

On Moonshot’s in-house test of exploiting real software flaws, a version trained without those cuts solved 90.5% of tasks. The released model solved 81%, still far above K3’s 33.3%, and Moonshot says it remains behind the best closed models. By Moonshot’s count, DeepSeek’s rival open model, V4.1-Flash [https://thenextweb.com/news/deepseek-v4-1-flash-launch-v4-pro-retired-price-cut], scored 85.7%.

Open models can’t be recalled once they are downloaded, and safety researchers have warned that their safeguards lag behind [https://thenextweb.com/news/open-weight-ai-safety-gap-glm-saferai-shieldstral] those of closed models.

Moonshot is also under pressure from Washington. In July, the White House accused it [https://thenextweb.com/news/white-house-moonshot-banned-nvidia-chips-kimi-k3] of using banned Nvidia chips and copying an Anthropic model to build K3, without publishing evidence.
