Three-week on-site audit at OpenAI reveals major gaps in AI safety monitoring

 [https://the-decoder.com/author/matthias-bastian/]

 Matthias Bastian [https://the-decoder.com/author/matthias-bastian/]

 View the LinkedIn Profile of Matthias Bastian
 [https://www.linkedin.com/in/matthias-bastian-128b71b1/]

 Oct 23, 2026 

 Midjourney prompted by THE DECODER

 Ask about this article…

 Search

Two researchers from METR and Redwood Research spent three weeks on-site at OpenAI, auditing the company's internal monitoring system for rogue AI behavior.

From July 21 to August 11, they had access to OpenAI's codebase and internal documents and interviewed about a dozen employees.

The team found numerous weaknesses [https://x.com/METR_Evals/status/2113290966933389390]. An estimated 6 to 25 percent of internal agentic Responses API traffic from models at the GPT-5.5 class and above wasn't covered by monitoring at all during the audit period, though METR says the uncertainty around that estimate is significant in both directions.

Ad

For internal Codex traffic that was supposed to be monitored, METR estimates roughly 10 percent went untracked between May and July because of a gap in how the system handled compaction events, which are context summaries. That estimate also carries significant uncertainty, according to METR. OpenAI had independently discovered the bug and fixed it while the audit was still underway.

Ad

During the period when compaction events weren't being monitored, agents could also delay oversight because the system only triggered when they handed control back to the user. That meant agents could sidestep it by simply continuing to execute actions.

The auditors also found ways to hide hundreds of actions from the monitor and to bypass the Codex sandbox. They determined that specially crafted inputs could likely crash the monitor entirely and that model behavior during RL training wasn't being monitored at all when the audit began.

Ad

The four layers where AI monitoring can fail, from missing coverage to fooling human reviewers. | Image: METR

OpenAI says it paused RL training [https://the-decoder.com/openai-says-its-pacing-model-development-as-ai-cybersecurity-risks-grow-too-dangerous/] after the Hugging Face incident [https://the-decoder.com/openais-agents-went-after-government-and-university-sites-months-before-hugging-face/] in August and expanded monitoring to cover it, but those changes came after the auditors' access period ended and weren't part of the evaluation.

Most of the full report remains hidden from the public

OpenAI says it has fixed all the weaknesses [https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/] the auditors found, and the full 61-page report was handed over internally. After OpenAI applied redactions, METR and Redwood Research leadership received a 38-page version, while only a short summary [https://metr.org/blog/2026-10-22-red-teaming-openai-agent-monitoring/] was made available to the public. METR said [https://x.com/METR_Evals/status/2113290985145012628] it had no right to disclose the redacted portions on its own.

Ad

The audit was a pilot program [https://metr.org/blog/2026-10-14-control-assessment-proposal/] organized jointly by METR, Redwood Research, and OpenAI to give outside researchers deep access to internal infrastructure. For future audits, METR says it wants to push for more public transparency.
