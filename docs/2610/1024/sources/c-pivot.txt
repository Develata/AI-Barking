Auditors find a quarter of OpenAI agent traffic went unmonitored

METR and Redwood Research reviewers worked on-site at OpenAI for three weeks, but OpenAI redacted large parts of their 61-page report, leaving only a summary public.
Maya ChenOctober 23, 2026

Up to 25 percent of agent traffic from OpenAI models at the GPT-5.5 class and above was going unmonitored, according to external auditors from METR and Redwood Research who spent three weeks on-site at the company examining its internal monitoring system for AI agents. The auditors characterized the gaps they found as significant, as reported by the-decoder.com on October 23.[1] [https://the-decoder.com/three-week-on-site-audit-at-openai-reveals-major-gaps-in-ai-safety-monitoring]

Most of the underlying document is not available. OpenAI redacted large portions of the auditors' 61-page report, and only a summary has been published, which limits what outsiders can check about how the 25 percent figure was derived or how broad the review was.[1] [https://the-decoder.com/three-week-on-site-audit-at-openai-reveals-major-gaps-in-ai-safety-monitoring]
