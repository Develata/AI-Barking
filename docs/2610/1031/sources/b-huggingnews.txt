Experimental trials using artificial intelligence to treat mouse epilepsy produced inconsistent medical decisions depending on which model was used. In a series of reruns of a robotic surgery procedure, GPT-6.1 Sol and Grok 4.7 each selected different gene therapy mechanisms and brain injection coordinates than the options picked by Claude Sonnet 5 for the same task.

The tests were conducted by Gabriel Ocana-Santero, cofounder of San Francisco startup Exin Therapeutics, using a motorized surgical rig. In the initial success, Claude Sonnet 5 analyzed three research papers and 19 therapy vectors to deliver a 250 nanoliter injection to reduce seizures, though the results are not yet peer-reviewed. Other models, including Claude Opus 5 and Opus 5.5, declined the live biological procedure, while a licensed veterinarian monitored the surgery to approve each step.
