                                                INTRODUCTION
         1. Innovation in artificial intelligence (“AI”) is set to define the twenty-first century.
Although the public had virtually no access to AI until November 2022, recent reports indicate

                                            1
that about half of American adults have used AI, with roughly one in four Americans doing so
daily.1 AI-related spending has also become a major growth engine for the U.S. economy.

         2. Nevertheless, the transformative power of AI does not come without danger.

Experts and insiders in the AI field nearly universally agree that AI companies must implement

reasonable guardrails to protect the public from the foreseeable risk of harm. Even OpenAI and its
CEO, Sam Altman, have warned that getting AI wrong might mean human extinction.2 And

recent developments in AI have suggested that risk is both credible and closer than anyone

previously thought.

         3. It didn’t have to be this way.

         4. Defendants OpenAI Global, LLC, OpenAI Foundation (formerly known as

OpenAI, Inc.), OpenAI OpCo, LLC, OpenAI Group PBC, OAI Corporation, LLC, OpenAI

Global Holdco, Inc., OpenAI, LLC, and OpenAI Holdings, LLC (collectively, “OpenAI” or

“Defendants”), have played a significant role in the AI boom. Angling to soon IPO at a trillion-

dollar valuation, OpenAI developed and released ChatGPT, the first generative AI chatbot to

attain widespread public use, in 2022. Now, ChatGPT is the most popular AI chatbot in the world,
commanding nearly half the American AI marketplace.3

         5. Unfortunately, however, OpenAI has devolved from its original mission as a

public-first nonprofit that prioritized safety into a cautionary tale about what can happen when the

         6. In November 2022, OpenAI released ChatGPT to the public. At the time, other AI
companies had developed working chatbots with similar abilities, but had declined to release
them to the public out of concerns about safety, inaccurate information, and starting an arms race
that would force companies to choose between developing AI safely or competing in that race.5
OpenAI chose the opposite approach. It has continued choosing it ever since.

         7. By misleading the public into believing that OpenAI is a responsible actor and that
ChatGPT was and is a safe, teen-appropriate tool that puts safety first, OpenAI has caused
consumers in Montana and elsewhere to trust and rely on OpenAI and ChatGPT more. This
deepened trust and dependence has driven up ChatGPT’s user base and engagement, enabled it to
collect vast quantities of sensitive and personal information, increased its revenue from paid
subscribers and advertising partners, and solidified its early market advantage.

         8. But the reality is very different from the misrepresentations OpenAI makes to the
public. Designed to be overly agreeable and feign human compassion, ChatGPT has proven that it
can be incredibly dangerous to vulnerable users, like children, and the public. For example,
ChatGPT has successfully coached numerous users to commit suicide or to harm or even kill

OpenAI employees have left over safety concerns.

suffered irreparable harm—including lost loved ones, disturbing cyberattacks, and now a

uniquely dangerous features and content.

         13. Plaintiff State of Montana, ex rel. Austin Knudsen, Attorney General of Montana
(“Attorney General,” “State of Montana,” or “State”) accordingly brings this action to compel
Defendants to comply with their obligations under both the Montana Unfair Trade Practices and

Consumer Data Privacy Act (“Montana Privacy Act”), and to enjoin Defendants from continuing
to engage in unreasonably dangerous activities that experts have warned could destroy the
economy, wreak havoc across private or governmental computer systems, or—as many AI
developers have recently speculated—even end humanity. In addition, the State brings this action
to vindicate the rights of Montanans affected by Defendants’ willful, dangerous, reckless, and
unlawful conduct.

intelligence posed, making it incumbent on all AI companies to prioritize safety.
