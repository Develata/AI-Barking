    Attorney General Hilgers Announces Lawsuit against OpenAI for Deceiving
                               Nebraskans about the Safety of ChatGPT

Lincoln —Attorney General Hilgers announced at a press conference today that he
is suing OpenAI for hurting Nebraska children, deceiving parents, and violating
the Nebraska Consumer Protection Act, the Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices
Act, and the Age-Appropriate Online Design Code Act.

A bipartisan group of states have also individually sued OpenAI. Nebraska
Attorney General Mike Hilgers was joined at the press conference by Iowa
Attorney General Brenna Bird, Kansas Attorney General Kris Kobach, and West
Virginia Attorney General JB McCuskey. Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, and Montana
have also sued OpenAI in their own state courts.

“The United States must win the race for artificial intelligence. The frontier labs
have the tools to continue to lead, but in their rush for market share they cannot
deceive or misinform customers about its technology. In this case, OpenAI has
done just that. It markets its AI as safe for minors, yet its products have deep flaws
that have led to suicide, violence, and harm. This lawsuit simply holds OpenAI to
the same standards under Nebraska law as any other company,” stated Attorney
General Mike Hilgers.

OpenAI developed and released ChatGPT, the first generative chatbot to attain
widespread public use, in 2022. Now, ChatGPT is the most popular chatbot in the
world, commanding nearly half the American chatbot marketplace.

However, OpenAI has been misleading the public into believing that ChatGPT is a
safe tool that puts safety first. But the reality is different from the
misrepresentations. Designed to be overly agreeable and feign human compassion,
ChatGPT has proven that it can be dangerous to vulnerable users, like children,
including coaching users to commit suicide or to harm other people.

There is a growing body of research indicating that ChatGPT is also harming
critical thinking skills, encouraging paranoias and delusions, offering dangerous
medical advice, and promoting unhealthy behaviors and relationships.

Alarmingly, OpenAI recently admitted to many instances that its advanced models
have "gone rogue" when “agents” were unleashed onto the internet and illegally
hacked into other companies and coordinated on how to lie to and deceive their
human users.

For these reasons, Attorney General Hilgers is suing to compel OpenAI to comply
with the Nebraska Consumer Protection Act, the Uniform Deceptive Trade
Practices Act, and the Age-Appropriate Online Design Code Act to stop engaging in
dangerous activities, and to vindicate the rights of Nebraskans affected by
OpenAI’s willful, dangerous, reckless, and unlawful conduct.
