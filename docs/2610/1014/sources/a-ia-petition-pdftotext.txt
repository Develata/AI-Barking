                                                INTRODUCTION
         1. Innovation in generative artificial intelligence (“AI”) is defining the twenty-first
century. AI has the potential to positively move humanity forward, but the companies developing
AI and their leaders cannot forsake the truth and dangerously mislead or assault the public while
advancing technology. OpenAI has engaged in unfair and deceptive acts and practices toward
Iowans that threaten Iowans’ safety, security, and privacy while hiding the truth about its
ChatGPT product in a manner that interferes with consumer choice—all in violation of Iowa’s
Consumer Fraud Act.
         2. Iowans use ChatGPT and other products created by and marketed by OpenAI. And
Iowans bear the risks that accompany not only the use of those products, but also the attendant
risks of developing those products, too.
         3. Although the public had virtually no access to AI until November 2022, recent
reports indicate that about half of American adults have used AI, with roughly one in four
Americans doing so daily.1 AI-related spending has also become a major growth engine for the
U.S. economy.
         4. But the transformative power of AI does not come without danger. Experts and
insiders in the AI field nearly universally agree that AI companies must implement reasonable
guardrails to protect the public from the foreseeable risk of harm. Even OpenAI and its CEO, Sam
Altman, have warned that getting AI wrong might mean human extinction. And recent

working chatbots with similar abilities but had declined to release them to the public out of
concerns about safety, inaccurate information, and starting an arms race that would force
companies to choose between developing AI safely or developing it at all.3 OpenAI chose the
opposite approach. It has continued choosing it ever since.

                                             CAUSES OF ACTION
 I. Violations of the Iowa Consumer Fraud Act: Unfair and Deceptive Practices in the

          Marketing and Design of ChatGPT.

         228. The State of Iowa re-alleges the facts above and incorporates them herein by

reference.

         229. Subsection 2(a) of the Iowa Consumer Fraud Act (“CFA”) provides:

                   The act, use or employment by a person of an unfair practice,
                   deception, fraud, false pretense, false promise, or
                   misrepresentation, or the concealment, suppression, or omission of
                   a material fact with intent that others rely upon the concealment,
                   suppression, or omission, in connection with the lease, sale, or
                   advertisement of any merchandise or the solicitation of
                   contributions for charitable purposes, whether or not a person has
                   in fact been misled, deceived, or damaged, is an unlawful practice.
 Iowa Code § 714.16(2)(a).

         230. The CFA provides that “[m]erchandise” includes “any objects, wares, goods,

commodities, intangibles, securities, bonds, debentures, stocks, real estate or services.” Id. §

714.16(1)(e). “Sale” is defined as “any sale, offer for sale, or attempt to sell any merchandise for

cash or on credit.” Id. § 714.16(1)(g). “Person” is defined to include “any natural person or the

person’s legal representative, partnership, corporation (domestic and foreign), company, trust,

business entity or association, and any agent, employee, salesperson, partner, officer, director,

member, stockholder, associate, trustee or cestui que trust thereof.” Id. § 714.16(1)(f).
                                                          78
“Advertisement” is defined to “include[] the attempt by publication, dissemination, solicitation, or
circulation to induce directly or indirectly any person to enter into any obligation or acquire any
title or interest in any merchandise.” Id. § 714.16(1)(a). “Deception” is defined as “an act or
practice which has the tendency or capacity to mislead a substantial number of consumers as to a
material fact or facts.” Id. § 714.16(1)(c). And an “unfair practice” is defined as “an act or
practice which causes substantial, unavoidable injury to consumers that is not outweighed by any
consumer or competitive benefits which the practice produces.” Id. § 714.16(1)(i).

         231. Defendants are “person[s]” who engaged in the “lease, sale, or advertisement” of
“merchandise” within the meaning of the Act. Iowa Code § 714.16(1).

         232. Defendants have engaged and continue to engage in unfair practices, many—but
not all—of which are identified in this Petition, including the following acts:

                   a. Failing to warn of ChatGPT’s dangers;
                   b. Designing, offering, and maintaining a dangerous product that provides

                            content unsuitable for children without requiring age verification;
                   c. Designing, offering, and maintaining a dangerous product where harmful

                            information such as tips on eating disorders, self-harm, and mass murder
                            are readily available to users, including young children;
                   d. Designing, offering, and maintaining a sycophantic product that
                            excessively agrees with users, and in so doing, encourages violence, self-
                            harm, depression, delusions, and paranoias;
                   e. Designing, offering, and maintaining a dangerous online product that
                            includes features that promote compulsive, prolonged, and unhealthy use,
                            including by children;

                                                          79
                   f. Designing, offering, and maintaining a dangerous product without
                            adequate safety testing;

                   g. Designing, offering, and maintaining a product that promotes self-harm to
                            users while falsely representing its safety;

                   h. Designing, offering, and maintaining a product that functions unreliably
                            while falsely representing that reliability; and

                   i. Designing, offering, and maintaining a product that falsely represents
                            itself as having human emotions and characteristics to earn the trust and
                            further engagement of its users.

         233. In addition, Defendants have made multiple deceptive representations and
omissions, many—but not all—of which are identified in this Petition, regarding ChatGPT,
including by:

                   a. Falsely assuring the public that ChatGPT is safe to use, including by

                   a. Failing to warn Iowa consumers of ChatGPT’s dangers, including the

                            children and vulnerable users, including the risks that ChatGPT will
                            promote self-harm, foster addiction and compulsive use, contribute to
                            cognitive decline, encourage violence, and solicit sensitive personal
                            information.

         235. While actual reliance is not required to establish a violation of the Iowa Consumer
Fraud Act under Iowa Code § 714.16(2)(a), OpenAI’s misrepresentations regarding the safety and
reliability of ChatGPT were highly material and directly caused consumer injury. Iowa consumers
reviewed OpenAI’s affirmative safety representations—including its “13+” Apple App Store
rating and its targeted marketing campaigns assuring users that the platform is safe for teenagers.

warnings, Iowans would have altered their use of the product, and parents would have restricted,
monitored, or completely prevented their children’s access to the platform.

         238. Defendants have violated the CFA as described above by engaging in false,
misleading, and deceptive conduct and unfair practices in the sale and advertisement of their
services, intangibles and products in Iowa. Upon information and belief, Defendants’ at-issue
practices are ongoing.

danger of what they are designing and testing with the intent that consumers continue to rely on
their products so that they can make a profit.

harm are high, particularly if ChatGPT agents gain access to systems with sensitive, financial, or
personally identifiable information, and had consumers been accurately informed, they have not
taken the same actions.

pretense, false promise, or misrepresentation, or the concealment, suppression, or omission of a
material fact in connection with these serious hacking incidents to mislead the public.
