COMPLAINT

L INTRODUCTION

1. Millions of consumers, including those in Arkansas, use ChatGPT to answer

questions, solve problems, and make important life decisions. OpenAI encourages that reliance by

marketing ChatGPT as safe, trustworthy, and suitable for teenagers, while assuring consumers that

safety is “core to our mission.” Yet ChatGPT has repeatedly produced unsafe responses.

2. OpenAI designed ChatGPT to sustain conversations, personalize its responses, and

be overly agreeable with users. Those features make ChatGPT remarkably convincing. But without
=== [PDF page 02] ===
adequate safeguards, they also make ChatGPT dangerous because of its tendency to reinforce
harmful beliefs and intentions instead of challenging them.

3. That risk has become reality. ChatGPT has encouraged suicide and self-harm,
reinforced delusions and emotional dependence, provided dangerous medical advice, and assisted
users in planning violence. These were not unforeseeable uses of the product. They reflected
known risks that OpenAlI failed to adequately address before marketing ChatGPT as safe and
trustworthy.

4. The consequences of those safety design failures have not been limited to
ChatGPT’s interactions with consumers. Within the last year, OpenAI’s advanced models have
repeatedly escaped intended restrictions, accessed other companies’ computer systems, concealed
their activities, communicated without authorization, and generated instructions contrary to human
direction.

5. OpenAI knew that products this powerful required meaningful safeguards. Its
leaders publicly acknowledged the risks, and its employees repeatedly raised serious safety
concerns. Researchers resigned, and safety teams were dissolved. As these warnings mounted,
OpenAl did not slow down. It held its foot on the gas, releasing increasingly powerful models
without adequate safeguards or testing as growth and profits repeatedly took priority over safety.

6. Yet OpenAlI’s message to consumers did not change. It continued telling consumers
that ChatGPT was safe and trustworthy. It promoted ChatGPT for consequential uses, including
healthcare and personal finance, while assuring parents that the product limits sensitive content
and includes age-appropriate protections.

7. Those assurances are particularly significant for parents. OpenAI knows that
children and teenagers regularly use ChatGPT. Yet it collects their personal information without

IV. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

A. OpenAI, ChatGPT, and the Design Features that Earn Consumers’ Trust
and Sustain Engagement.

37.  OpenAI was founded in 2015 as a nonprofit corporation committed to developing
artificial intelligence (“AI”) for the public benefit.? From the beginning, OpenAI recognized that
Al—especially increasingly powerful Al—presented serious safety risks. In response, it promised
to prioritize the public good and safety over profits.

38. A decade later, OpenAI operates the world’s leading AI tool: ChatGPT. Released
in 2022, ChatGPT answers users’ questions and responds to users’ prompts in plain language. Its
conversational format is designed to mimic human communication.

39. But ChatGPT is not designed to behave like a search engine; it is a chatbot designed
to engage users in conversation. It responds in the first person, adapts to information the user
provides, asks follow-up questions, and encourages the exchange to continue. OpenAI’s own
Model Spec instructs ChatGPT to be engaging, ask relevant questions, and avoid unnecessarily
wrapping up conversations.?

40.  ChatGPT is widely accessible. Consumers can access it through OpenAI’s website
or through a stand-alone application available from online app stores. The website is open to
anyone, including children, without age verification. ChatGPT now has more than one billion

monthly active users worldwide and over | million users in Arkansas.
