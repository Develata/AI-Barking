Scientists call for international body to oversee advanced AI

By Leo Marchandon 

Oct 13 (Reuters) - Around 400 scientists, including AI pioneers Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio, on Tuesday called for an ‌international body to oversee containment standards and incident reporting for ‌advanced cyber-capable AI systems. 

The proposed body would set industry-wide containment standards and maintain a ​registry of significant security incidents, which the scientists said should eventually be made public. 

In an open letter, the researchers said frontier AI models should initially be air-gapped from external networks and subject to independent oversight, arguing ‌that voluntary industry commitments are ⁠not enough. 

The proposal comes a month after Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, xAI and Nvidia signed a White House ⁠accord committing to voluntary safety measures without any formal enforcement mechanism. 

The letter also follows a series of incidents that researchers say exposed weaknesses in ​how advanced ​AI systems are tested and contained, ​including cases where AI models ‌bypassed insufficiently sandboxed environments and compromised external systems. 

The signatories from 34 countries said organisations developing or testing cyber-capable models should immediately adopt high-assurance containment standards while governments work towards a more formal oversight regime. 

Policymakers are increasingly debating how advanced AI systems should be supervised. EU tech ‌chief Henna Virkkunen told Reuters on Friday that ​the bloc's AI Act is sufficient to ​address risks from rogue ​AI systems. 

On Sunday, the Trump administration lifted its block ‌on US labs giving Britain's AI Security ​Institute early access ​to models for safety testing. 

Hinton and Bengio shared the 2018 Turing Award with Meta's Yann LeCun, another leading AI researcher. LeCun ​did not sign the ‌letter and has argued that recent security incidents primarily reflect ​engineering failures rather than existential AI risks. 

(Reporting by Leo Marchandon ​in Gdansk; Editing by Matt Scuffham)
