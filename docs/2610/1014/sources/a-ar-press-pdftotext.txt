           Attorney General Griffin Sues OpenAI for
        Misleading Arkansans over Safety of ChatGPT

                          Griffin: ‘OpenAI assures us that ChatGPT is safe. The
                   consequences of that assurance are lives lost and people harmed’

LITTLE ROCK – Attorney General Griffin issued the following statement after filing a lawsuit
against tech giant OpenAI:

“We’re in the midst of a technological revolution. Whether you call it artificial intelligence or
super intelligence, the bottom line is that things are rapidly changing. But one thing that has not
changed and will not change is Arkansans’ right to receive truthful information about the
products and services they use.

“That’s why earlier today I filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, the company that owns the platform
ChatGPT. Our complaint outlines numerous violations of Arkansas law, but they all boil down to
a simple fact: OpenAI represented to Arkansans that ChatGPT was safe, but they knew ChatGPT
had significant risks and limitations. They chose to hide that knowledge from consumers.

“Our complaint alleges that consumers, including minors, have used ChatGPT to acquire
instructions for self-harm, including suicide. It also describes instances where users were able to
bypass OpenAI’s safety measures and generate responses the company said its safeguards were
designed to prevent. Those examples raise serious questions about whether consumers were
given an accurate picture of the product’s safety protections.
“OpenAI assures us that ChatGPT is safe. The consequences of that assurance are lives lost and
people harmed.”

Arkansas’s lawsuit against OpenAI includes three causes of action:
    • Violations of the Arkansas Deceptive Trade Practices Act (ADTPA), including
         knowingly making false representations to consumers; unconscionable, false, or
         deceptive practices; deception and fraud in interactions with consumers; and omissions of
         material facts with the intent for consumers to rely upon them
    • Children’s and Teens’ Online Privacy Protection Act (CTOPPA) violations,
         including knowingly collecting personal information from children and teens; knowingly
         retaining the personal information of a child or teen for longer than is reasonably
         necessary; and failing to provide notice and gain consent for the collection of data and
         information
    • Common Law claims, including negligence; strict liability – design defect; strict liability
         – failure to warn; unjust enrichment; and public nuisance

Arkansas’s lawsuit seeks relief through:
    • A declaration that OpenAI violated the ADTPA and CTOPPA and an injunction from
         further violations, including an injunction from collecting the data of minors
    • A requirement that OpenAI provide clear and conspicuous disclosures of the material
         risks of ChatGPT
    • Restitution to consumers who have been affected
    • Civil penalties
    • Injunction from further advanced AI testing and creating models capable of recursive
         self-improvement until adequate safeguards are in place

Nebraska, West Virginia, Arizona, Idaho, Montana, and Utah.

About Attorney General Tim Griffin

Tim Griffin was sworn in as the 57th Attorney General of Arkansas on January 10, 2023, having
previously served as the state’s 20th Lieutenant Governor from 2015-2023. From 2011-2015,
Griffin served as the 24th representative of Arkansas’s Second Congressional District, where he
served on the House Committee on Ways and Means, House Armed Services Committee, House
Committee on Foreign Affairs, House Committee on Ethics and House Committee on the
Judiciary while also serving as a Deputy Whip for the Majority.

Griffin is currently an officer in the Arkansas Army National Guard and holds the rank of
colonel. Griffin served as an officer in the U.S. Army Reserve Judge Advocate General’s (JAG)
Corps for more than 28 years. In 2005, Griffin was mobilized to active duty as an Army
prosecutor at Fort Campbell, Kentucky, and served with the 101st Airborne Division (Air
Assault) in Mosul, Iraq.

His previous assignments include serving as the Commander of the 2d Legal Operations
Detachment in New Orleans, Louisiana; the Commander of the 134th Legal Operations
Detachment at Fort Bragg, North Carolina; and as a Senior Legislative Advisor to the Under
Secretary of Defense for Personnel and Readiness at the Pentagon. Griffin earned a master’s
degree in strategic studies as a Distinguished Honor Graduate from the U.S. Army War College,
Carlisle Barracks, Pennsylvania.

Griffin also served as U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of Arkansas, and Special Assistant to
the President and Deputy Director of Political Affairs for President George W. Bush; Special
Assistant to Assistant Attorney General Michael Chertoff, Criminal Division, U.S. Department
of Justice; Special Assistant U.S. Attorney, U.S. Attorney’s Office, Eastern District of Arkansas;
Senior Investigative Counsel, Government Reform and Oversight Committee, U.S. House of
Representatives; and Associate Independent Counsel, Office of Independent Counsel David M.
Barrett, In re: HUD Secretary Henry Cisneros.

Griffin is a graduate of Magnolia High School, Hendrix College in Conway, and Tulane Law
School in New Orleans. He attended graduate school at Oxford University. He is admitted to
practice law in Arkansas (active) and Louisiana (inactive). Griffin lives in Little Rock with his
wife, Elizabeth, a Camden native, and their three children.
