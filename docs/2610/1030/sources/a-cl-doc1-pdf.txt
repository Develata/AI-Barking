 1          Plaintiff, Karl Kahn, individually and on behalf of all others similarly situated (“the
 2 Class” and “Class Members”), hereby complains against Defendant ANTHROPIC, PBC dba

 3 ANTHROPIC, INC. (“Defendant” or “Anthropic”) and on information and belief, unless

 4 otherwise expressly stated as based upon personal knowledge, alleges as follows:

 5                                           INTRODUCTION
 6          1.      Plaintiff brings this action for Anthropic’s misleading and deceptive marketing of
 7 its Claude Max subscription plans.

 8          2.      Defendant markets and sells subscription plans to Claude, a generative AI agentic
 9 coding product. Subscribers can prompt Claude to answer research questions, draft or edit text,

10 and write code, among other things. Consumers shopping for access to a Large Language Model

11 (LLM) like Claude typically look for the best quality services, the ability to access a high level of

12 usage or “compute,” and the best price point.

13          3.      Defendant offers commercial plans (Team and Enterprise) and individual plans
14 (Free, Pro, and Max).

15          4.      Defendant’s individual subscription plans cost $17-$20 per month at the Pro tier,
16 $100 per month for the Max 5x plan, and $200 per month for the Max 20x plan.

17          5.      In its offer of monthly subscriptions to Claude, Defendant misleads prospective
18 customers into believing that its higher priced tier for individual subscriptions, Max 20x, delivers

19 expanded usage of twenty times greater than the amount of usage delivered by Defendant’s
20 lower tier Pro package.

21          6.      In fact, Defendant’s Max 20x plan delivers far less than twenty times the usage of
22 the Pro tier, delivering just six to eight times the usage of Pro.

23          7.      Similarly, Defendant’s Max 5x plan delivers far less than five times the usage of
24 the Pro tier, delivering just three-and-a-half times the usage of Pro.

25          8.      To make matters worse, Defendant entices consumers to pay the $200 monthly
26 Max 20x subscription by falsely claiming it offers a 50% savings.
