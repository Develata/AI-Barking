OpenAI Scraps the ChatGPT Pro 200 Usage Cut and Doubles Pro Limits

OpenAI cancelled the planned 50% cut to ChatGPT Pro 200 usage on 29 October, a day before it was due, and doubled Work and Codex limits on Pro 100, Pro 500 and Business Premium. The $500 Pro 500 plan stays, Plus is unchanged, and OpenAI still publishes no exact limits.

OpenAI called off the planned 50% cut to ChatGPT Pro 200 usage on 29 October, the day before it was due, and doubled Work and Codex limits on its other Pro tiers.

 Quick Summary - TLDR:

OpenAI cancelled the Pro 200 usage cut that was due to start on 30 October and says Pro 200 Work and Codex usage "will stay the same"

It also doubled Work and Codex limits for Pro 100, Pro 500 and Business Premium, effective on 29 October

The $500 Pro 500 plan, launched at DevDay on 29 September with Ultrafast access, stays

Plus usage is unchanged, and OpenAI does not publish exact numerical limits for any tier

OpenAI has reversed its plan to halve what a $200-a-month ChatGPT Pro subscription buys. On 29 October, the day before the cut was due to start, Tibo Sottiaux, who leads Codex at OpenAI, wrote on X: "We heard your kind feedback and won't be reducing usage limits for Pro 200, so your Work and Codex usage will stay the same."

The same announcement went further. "We are doubling all usage limits for all Pro 100/200/500 tiers and Business Premium, effective today," he wrote, according to BleepingComputer's report of the post.

What had OpenAI planned for Pro 200?

At DevDay on 29 September, OpenAI said that from 30 October the included Work and Codex usage on Pro 200 would fall from 20 times the Plus allowance to 10 times. Existing subscribers kept their old cap until 29 October. OpenAI described the change as a reflection of "increasingly efficient models", and it did not plan to bring back the five-hour limit.

The same announcement introduced Pro 500, a $500-a-month plan that is the only Pro tier with Ultrafast, a faster version of OpenAI's flagship model that it says generates up to 300 tokens per second in Codex, up to eight times faster than standard. OpenAI also gave affected Pro 200 subscribers a one-time grant of usage credits worth $2,500 to ease the transition. The reaction on its community forum was blunt, with subscribers asking OpenAI to explain whether Pro 200 usage was really being cut.

What changed on 29 October?

According to OpenAI's release notes, as summarised by the update tracker Releasebot and other outlets, the changes apply to Work and Codex usage, which share an allowance.

Pro 100 and Pro 500 now include twice their previous Work and Codex allowance.

Pro 200 subscribers who were on the lower allowance have been raised to the higher level. Those already on the higher allowance keep it.

Business Premium usage is also increasing, though no multiplier has been confirmed.

Plus usage is unchanged.

Dots, OpenAI's always-on assistants, keep their extended limits "for now", and their usage stays separate from default usage.

OpenAI does not publish exact numerical limits for each tier, so none of these multipliers can be checked against a stated figure. We also have not seen OpenAI say what happens to the one-time usage credits now that the cut has been dropped.

Why does the reversal matter for teams building on flat-rate plans?

The episode shows how quickly a usage allowance can move in either direction. A plan that heavy users had built daily workflows around was due to lose half its capacity, and was restored within a day of the date. For teams that run coding agents on a subscription, the lesson is that the allowance is set by the vendor, can change at short notice, and is not published as a number. Anyone depending on it for production work should know what they would do if it changed.

Updated 30 October 2026. An earlier version of this article, published late on 29 October, said the cut would take effect on 30 October. OpenAI cancelled it earlier that day, and the article has been rewritten to reflect that.
