       1. Introduction and statement of the main result

Human      We consider the following problem. Given n random points in Rd, when do they lie on the
       boundary of an ellipsoid? For small n and a standard Gaussian setting such an event has a high

       likelihood, while for sufficiently large n it is unlikely to occur. The main regime of interest is
       therefore near the phase transition, which turns out to be n ≍ d2 [BM25, MK24, SPW13].

           We study the random setting in which

                                                          x1, . . . , xn ∼ N (0, Id)
       are independent. Since ∥xi∥22 is typically close to d, the sphere already gives an approximate fit.
       The question is whether it can be deformed into a centered ellipsoid so that all of the points lie
       exactly on its boundary.

           Saunderson, Parrilo, and Willsky [SPW13] conjectured that this problem has a sharp transition
       at n = d2/4. More precisely, for every fixed ε > 0, they predicted feasibility of such an ellipsoid

       with high probability when                                1 − ε d2,
                                                             n≤  4

       and infeasibility with high probability when

                                                n ≥ 1 + ε d2.
                                                        4

           The value 1/4 comes from the geometry of the positive semidefinite cone. One way to measure
       the size of this cone is to take a random Gaussian symmetric matrix, project it onto the cone, and
       compute the expected squared size of that projection. For d × d matrices, this quantity is d(d + 1)/4,
       making 1/4 the natural candidate for the asymptotic threshold.

           Earlier work established feasibility in increasingly large ranges of n, eventually reaching n ≤ cd2
       for a sufficiently small constant c > 0. Maillard and Kunisky [MK24] predicted the sharp transition
       at 1/4 using the non-rigorous replica method of statistical physics. Bandeira and Maillard [BM25]
       later proved the 1/4 threshold for an approximate version of the ellipsoid-fitting problem. The
       corresponding exact fitting problem remained open.

           We prove the exact threshold with fixed margin. No assertion is made for the critical case when
       n/d2 → 1/4.

       Theorem 1.1 (Strict-threshold theorem). Let n = n(d) be any sequence of positive integers. For
       each d, let x1, . . . , xn be independent N (0, Id) vectors and define

                                  Fd = {S ⪰ 0 : S⊤ = S and xiT Sxi = d for all 1 ≤ i ≤ n}.

       Then, the following statements hold.
         (i) If lim supd→∞ n/d2 < 1/4, then

       P  there  exists                      S  ≻  0  with       x  T  S  xi  =  d  for  every  i  −→ 1.
                                                                    i

       (ii) If lim infd→∞ n/d2 > 1/4, then P(Fd̸ = ∅) → 0.

                                                    Concurrent work

           While completing this work, we became aware of three independent concurrent works. Misi-
       akiewicz and Wen [MW26], posted on August 10, 2026, proved the sharp d2/4 threshold for Gaussian
       ellipsoid fitting. De la Cerda, Potechin, Tulsiani, and Xu [dlCPTX26], posted on August 12, 2026,
       established the Gaussian threshold up to a vanishing multiplicative factor. Koehler and Sohn [KS26],
       posted on August 27, 2026, proved a more general universality result for independent subgaussian
       coordinates with a common fourth moment, recovering the Gaussian 1/4 threshold as a special case.

           These works and the present paper establish the Gaussian d2/4 threshold, although their
       formulations, scope, and proof architectures differ. Misiakiewicz and Wen build directly on the
       Gaussian-equivalence framework of Bandeira–Maillard. Below the threshold, they use a head–tail
       decomposition of the dual vector, with exact treatment of the sparse head and Gaussian comparison
       for the low-influence tail. Above the threshold, they combine a low-rank/diffuse decomposition with
       conditional Gaussianization and a projected Gordon escape argument.

           Our proof follows a different route. Below the threshold, we begin with the approximate fitting
       result of Bandeira–Maillard and perturb it to an exact fit using a spectrally constrained robust-loss
       construction, a sparse/diffuse decomposition of the coefficient vector, and a dyadic multiscale
       estimate. Above the threshold, we also use Gordon’s Gaussian min–max theorem, but in a different
       role. We first establish the Gaussian margin and then transfer it to centered rank-one measurements
       through a leave-one-row Gibbs interpolation, in the spirit of rigorous cavity methods [ASS03, Tal11].
       A Grassmann-net conditioning argument and a finite large-eigenvalue decomposition then extend
       the diffuse estimate to the full positive semidefinite cone.

Human                                                 17. Conclusion

           Part I constructs an exact positive definite fit whenever the aspect ratio stays a fixed distance
       below 1/4. Part II proves that the centered kernel contains no nonzero positive semidefinite matrix
       whenever the aspect ratio stays a fixed distance above 1/4. These are precisely the two assertions of
       Theorem 1.1. The proof contains no statement about the critical sequence n/d2 → 1/4.
