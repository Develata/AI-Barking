                 Abstract. We generalize the recently demonstrated equivalence between local heights and

                    boundary propagators on the Tate curve to any curve over a p-adic local field with semistable
                    reduction. Specifically, we show that the boundary two-point function of a p-adic string theory
                    with the curve as the asymptotic boundary coincides with the N´eron local height pairing on degree
                    zero divisors. We remark on the use of AI in this research, see Statement of AI use.

                                                     1. Introduction

           A link between arithmetic geometry and physics was revealed in [HJ26] by identifying the
       N´eron local height on the Tate curve with the boundary two-point function of a certain p-adic
       string theory where the Tate curve arises as the asymptotic boundary. A connection between
       string theory and arithmetic geometry in this general direction was anticipated by Yuri Manin in
       the late 1980s [Man87]. An immediate question is whether this identification extends to arbitrary
       algebraic curves. The main purpose of this paper is to answer this question affirmatively at places
       of semistable reduction: we show that for any curve X with semistable reduction over a p-adic
       local field, the boundary two-point function of the p-adic string theory agrees with the N´eron local
       height function (up to a sign convention). Thus, string theory and arithmetic geometry are closely
       linked on a fundamental level.

           The study of p-adic strings originated in the late 1980s, when it was discovered that the genus
       zero 4-tachyon scattering amplitude satisfies a striking adelic product formula [BFOW88, Zab89].
       However, soon afterwards, research directions mostly turned to a series of groundbreaking dis-
       coveries involving very fruitful interactions between quantum field theory and string theory on
       one hand, and differential geometry and topology on the other hand. Compared to subjects like
       differential geometry, topology, and the theory of differential equations, arithmetic geometry has
       so far mostly stayed on the sidelines of theoretical physics. In the last decade, there has been a
       revival of p-adic physics, as simplified or toy models, or computational tools, to help understand
       Archimedean physics. This paper continues that trend in another direction, by further examining
       the aforementioned link between string theory and arithmetic geometry. We hope this bridge can
       serve as a starting point for more substantial interactions between these two fields.

           In [HJ26], the two-point function was computed from the boundary dual action of [HJ25].
       Equivalently, one can compute a properly normalized asymptotic boundary limit of a Neumann
       function in the bulk as in [CMZ89], which is more convenient to work with for a general curve. The
       approach taken here is to identify the boundary limit of the Neumann function with the N´eron local

Human                                                   5. Discussion

           We have shown that the local N´eron pairing on an arbitrary algebraic curve (at places of
       semistable reduction) can be computed as the boundary limit of the Neumann function of the
       corresponding p-adic worldsheet action. Muse Spark has been useful at every stage of this project.
       From the very outset, Muse Spark confirmed the key insight that the form of [CMZ89, Eq. 3.2.1]
       for the Neumann function of the p-adic string worldsheet action defined on the bulk graph of a
       genus 1 curve could be generalized by replacing the loop term with a general Green’s function on
       the core graph, and that the boundary limit of this generalized Neumann function reproduces the
       N´eron pairing when evaluated on divisors of degree zero, providing a broad generalization of the
       identification initiated in [HJ26]. As evidence of the original conjecture began to accumulate, Muse
       Spark served as a powerful tool for checking the logical soundness of our arguments and iteratively
       refining them, in some cases producing large blocks of useful material that needed only light editing,
       or no editing at all before being included in the manuscript. In a future paper, we anticipate re-
       moving the semistable assumption in the main theorem 3.23 and generalizing this result to pairings
       between divisors of arbitrary degree with disjoint support (e.g. a pair of distinct points).

           The program developed in this paper suggests a number of possible directions for further in-
       vestigation which we plan to explore in future articles. The work we have presented here, along
       with the majority of the literature on p-adic string theory, has focused exclusively on real bulk
       scalar fields. On the other hand, Archimedean string theory generically accommodates fields of
       many different types arising as various resonances of open and closed strings. Consequently, one
       might reasonably hope to enrich the story of the interplay between arithmetic geometry and p-adic
       string theory by considering the potential for bulk actions associated with complex scalar fields and
       spinning fields—such as those considered in [GJT19]—to compute further invariants of arithmetic
       curves and their associated Jacobian varieties. Preliminary investigations suggest that the Neu-
       mann function for complex scalar fields coupled to a background electromagnetic field on the bulk
       graph of a Mumford curve may be related to height functions on covering spaces of the boundary

                                          23
Human                STRING TWO-POINT FUNCTION = HEIGHT FUNCTION ON A CURVE

       curve associated with torsion characters of the U (1) gauge group—i.e. Wilson loops computed on
       the reduction graph.

           Another possible avenue for future investigation would be to extend the approach taken in
       [HSYZ21], where Green’s functions for the so-called Vladimirov derivative were computed in the
       setting of p-adic string theory. In that approach, one associates a field theory on each local com-
       pletion of a given number field K. When K = Q, the relevant field theory is the dual CFT of the
       p-adic string worldsheet defined over the Bruhat-Tits tree, as in [Zab89]. The generalization pro-
       vided here suggests a clear path forward for generalizing this approach by systematically studying
       the boundary theories defined on any algebraic curve. One would expect that the boundary limit
       of the Neumann functions here identified with the local N´eron pairing should be closely related to
       the Green’s functions for the boundary theories obtained by iteratively integrating out the bulk
       fields.

           While the study of these nonlocal boundary theories may be of substantial interest in and
       of themselves, they also presumably provide a starting-point for closing the triangle of relations
       amongst three manifestly inter-related areas of research—arithmetic geometry, p-adic string theory,
       and AdS/CFT holography. Here we have focused on connecting p-adic string theory with arithmetic
       geometry, while much of the recent literature on p-adic string theory has focused on connections
       to AdS/CFT holography. The boundary action approach to computing Green’s functions directly
       on the boundary curve would close the third leg of this triangle, directly connecting arithmetic
       geometry of arbitrary algebraic curves with two dimensional conformal field theory. In fact, Yuri
       Manin anticipated such a triangle of relations over twenty years ago in [MM02].

           Among many other insights, that paper noted that there was a close analogy between the classical
       solutions of three-dimensional gravity in the presence of a cosmological constant, which, due to the
       topological nature of three-dimensional Einstein gravity, can be characterized as quotients of global
       AdS3 by a Schottky group, and the construction of Mumford curves as the quotient of a domain
       of discontinuity by a Schottky group. The genus one case of this analogy suggests a relation
       between the Tate curve and the BTZ black hole, which was already observed in [MM02] and has
       been further developed in [ESZ23, CLH21b, CLH21a] in the context of tensor network theory and
       holographic black hole entropy. The results of this paper supply the groundwork for pushing this
       analogy further, and suggest new holographic descriptions of handlebody black holes and conical
       defects in AdS3 that could naturally correspond to p-adic string theories, and their corresponding
       boundary CFTs on the bulk graphs associated with Mumford curves and algebraic curves with
       good reduction.
