       Abstract. We resolve an open question of Boulenger–Lenzmann [BL17] for the focusing mass-

       critical biharmonic nonlinear Schro¨dinger equation

                                   i∂tu = ∆2u − |u|8/N u, (t, x) ∈ I × RN , N ≥ 2.

       We prove that every radial solution with negative energy and initial data in H2(RN ) blows up
       in finite time, both forward and backward. This removes the previously unresolved possibility
       of blow-up occurring only at infinite time. The proof uses a new exponentially localized virial
       argument, together with a radial interpolation estimate adapted to the fourth-order dispersion,
       and leads to a quartic Riccati inequality forcing finite-time blow-up. We remark on the use of AI
       in this research, see Statement of AI Use.

AI         The biharmonic nonlinear Schr¨odinger equation arises as a higher-order dispersive correction to
       the classical nonlinear Schr¨odinger equation. It was introduced by Karpman [Kar96] and by Karp-
       man and Shagalov [KS97] in the study of intense laser beams propagating through bulk media with
       Kerr nonlinearity, where a small fourth-order dispersive effect is added to the usual Schr¨odinger
       dynamics. Beyond this physical motivation, the equation has become a natural model for under-
       standing how higher-order dispersion changes the qualitative behavior of nonlinear Hamiltonian
       evolution equations, in particular the competition between dispersion and self-focusing.

       The first systematic mathematical study was carried out by Fibich, Ilan and Papanicolaou

AI     [FIP02]. They proved global existence of H2-solutions in the L2-subcritical regime and for small

       of sufficiently large solutions. On the linear side, Ben-Artzi, Koch and Saut [BAKS00] established

       by Pausader [Pau07]. Pausader also obtained global well-posedness results in the energy-critical
       setting [Pau07, Pau09b, Pau09a]; see also Miao, Xu and Zhao [MXZ11].

           The focusing problem is more delicate, since the nonlinear attraction can overcome dispersion
       and drive concentration of the solution. A major step was made by Boulenger and Lenzmann
       [BL17], who developed localized virial and Riesz-bivariance methods for biharmonic NLS and
       established a general blow-up theory in the mass-critical and mass-supercritical regimes. Their
       work was followed by several developments. Bonheure, Cast´eras, Gou and Jeanjean [BCGJ19b]
       proved strong instability of standing waves by blow-up and obtained finite-time blow-up results in
       the mass-supercritical regime. Dinh [Din21] established sharp scattering-versus-blow-up dynamics
       for radial solutions in the mass-supercritical and energy-critical settings, while Gou [Gou24] ex-
       tended a number of blow-up results to cylindrically symmetric solutions. See also related work
       in [BCGJ19a, FJMMs22, FLNZ20, Kom22, ZWC+24].

       Despite this progress, the most basic finite-time blow-up question in the homogeneous mass-

       critical case remained unresolved. Boulenger and Lenzmann [BL17, Theorem 3] proved that every

       radial negative-energy solution either blows up in finite time or exists globally and grows up as

       t → ∞. In the homogeneous case, their argument leaves open the possibility of a global solution

       satisfying

                       ∥∆u(t)∥2 ≥ Ct2                                                     (1.2)

       for all sufficiently large times, with stronger subsequential growth in dimensions N ≥ 5. They

       pointed out explicitly that, in the absence of lower-order dispersion, their method does not yield

       finite-time blow-up and conjectured that finite-time blow-up should nevertheless always occur

       [BL17, Remarks 2 and 4 following Theorem 3]. Theorem 1.1 confirms this conjecture for the

       homogeneous mass-critical biharmonic NLS in every dimension N ≥ 2.

AI       Remark 1.3 (Relation to recent second-order work and novelty): A closely related defect-to-
       Riccati mechanism was introduced very recently by Nguyen and Dinh [ND26] for the four-
       dimensional mass-critical quadratic Schr¨odinger system without mass resonance. In that second-
       order problem, an exponentially localized quadratic multiplier produces a weighted first-order ki-
       netic defect

                                                         ωR(x) = 1 − e−|x|2/R2 .

       A radial weighted interpolation absorbs the nonlinear localization error, while the localized virial
       itself is controlled by the square root of the same defect. This yields a quadratic Riccati inequality
       and rules out the global grow-up branch left by earlier virial arguments.

           The present proof is motivated by that architecture, but its fourth-order realization requires
       genuinely new ingredients.

           First, the defect must vanish quartically at the origin, not quadratically. We use

                                                         ωR(x) = 1 − e−|x|4/R4 ,

       which changes the natural remainder scale to R−4 and matches the ∆2 homogeneity.

           Second, the coercive quantity is a weighted Hessian defect rather than a weighted gradient defect.
       Controlling the mass-critical nonlinear error therefore needs a new radial estimate of second order.
       After rescaling to a fixed annulus, the differentiated term appears with power 1/N ; this dimensional
       gain is exactly what makes absorption possible for all N ≥ 2 at the endpoint.

           Third, the biharmonic commutator produces lower-order terms with derivatives of the multiplier
       up to sixth order. These terms have to be recovered from the Hessian defect by one more integration
       by parts, which is harmless in second order but essential here.

           Finally, the localized virial quantity is no longer controlled by the square root of the defect, as
       in the second-order problem. Using the pointwise bound of the exponential weight together with
       the weighted gradient estimate, we obtain only a fourth-root control of the localized virial quantity.
       So, the resulting Riccati differential inequality is quartic rather than quadratic. This still forces a
       finite-time contradiction and hence finite-time blow-up.
