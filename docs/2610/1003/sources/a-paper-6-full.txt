                 Abstract. We study finite-dimensional solvable evolution algebras, that is, evolution algebras

                    for which their derived series terminates at zero. We disprove a conjecture by Garc´ıa-Mart´ınez and
                    P´erez-Rodr´ıguez. Then, in terms of idempotent subspaces, we propose a novel characterization of
                    solvable evolution algebras as well as of evolution algebras without nonzero idempotent elements.
                    We remark on the use of AI in this research, see Statement of AI Use.

                                                     1. Introduction

           Introduced by Tian in their doctoral thesis [Tia04], and subsequently in their monograph [Tia08],
       evolution algebras provide an algebraic framework for studying non-Mendelian genetics. The study
       of these algebras is an active area of research providing tools for various disciplines, including
       biology, physics, economics and logistics. Mathematically, evolution algebras are linked to graph
       theory, group theory, stochastic processes and dynamical systems. For a thorough historical survey,
       we refer to [CFNT22].

           Formally, an evolution algebra over a field K is a (non-associative) K-algebra, along with
       a distinguished basis B, called a natural basis, such that xy = 0 for distinct x, y ∈ B. We
       focus on solvable algebras, that is, algebras for which their derived series terminates at zero. These
       algebras have already been studied for their amenable properties in [FGN22] and [LPP25]. Recently,
       Garc´ıa-Mart´ınez and P´erez-Rodr´ıguez ([GP26]) proposed the following conjecture.
       Conjecture 1.1. Let E be a complex evolution algebra. Then, E is solvable if and only if E admits
       no nonzero idempotent elements.

           In this paper, we show that this conjecture is false, and in turn, provide an appropriate charac-
       terization of solvable evolution algebras in terms of idempotent subspaces. We also study evolution
       algebras which admit no nonzero idempotents.

                                          2. Preliminaries

Human      Throughout, we will consider an algebraically closed field K, and by algebra we always mean
       (non-associative) K-algebra. Whenever we refer to a finite-dimensional evolution algebra E, we
       assume that it comes equipped with a natural basis B = {e1, . . . , en} and a structure matrix
       MB (E ).

           Let V be a subspace of the evolution algebra E. We denote V 2 = Span{xy | x, y ∈ V }.
       Recursively, we define E(1) = E and E(i+1) = (E(i))2 for i ≥ 1. Then, E is called solvable if there
       exists m ∈ N+ such that E(m) = 0. It is easy to see that for i ≥ 1, E(i+1) ⊆ E(i). Hence, if
       E is finite-dimensional, then the chain E(1) ⊇ E(2) ⊇ . . . stabilizes at some l ∈ N+. We define
       E (∞) = E (l).

           Take x ∈ E. We say that x is idempotent if x2 = x, and we say that x is absolutely nilpotent
       if x2 = 0. Given a subspace V ⊆ E, we are interested in the set

      1. E is solvable,

        In turn, the existence (or lack thereof) of nonzero idempotent elements in an evolution algebra
    also admits a simple characterization. For the next result, we will need some elementary facts from
    algebraic geometry, for which we refer to [Har92].

    Theorem 3.3. Let E be a finite-dimensional evolution algebra. Then, E admits a nonzero idem-
    potent element if and only if E admits a nontrivial idempotent subspace V such that N (V ) = 0.

    Proof. Suppose x ∈ E satisfies x2 = x. Then, V = Span{x} is an idempotent subspace and

    N (V ) = 0.

        Conversely, suppose that V ⊆ E is an idempotent subspace such that N (V ) = 0. Since V 2 = V ,

    φ : P(V ) → P(V ) given by φ([x]) = [x2].
