Alphabet (GOOGL) Q3 2026 Earnings Call Transcript

October 28, 2026 5:59 pm
by

Technology
(category)
Alphabet’s Q3 2026 earnings call highlighted a strong quarter, with consolidated revenues up 25% to $127.6 billion and Google Cloud revenues up 103% to $30.7 billion. CEO Sundar Pichai, Chief Business Officer Philipp Schindler, Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian and CFO Anat Ashkenazi discussed the launch of Gemini 4 Argon and Carbon, the Gemini app reaching 1 billion users, the global expansion of ads in AI Mode, TPU system sales and the 2026 CapEx outlook. Read the full transcript of the Alphabet Q3 2026 earnings conference call below.

Welcome, everyone. Thank you for standing by for the Alphabet Third Quarter 2026 Earnings Conference Call.
At this time, all participants are in a listen only mode. After the speaker presentation, there will be a question and answer session. I would now like to hand the conference over to your speaker today, Jim Friedland, Head of Investor Relations. Please go ahead.
Safe Harbor Statement
Jim Friedland, Head of Investor Relations – Alphabet:
Thank you. Good afternoon, everyone, and welcome to Alphabet’s Third Quarter 2026 Earnings Conference Call. With us today are Sundar Pichai, Philipp Schindler, Thomas Kurian and Anat Ashkenazi.

Such statements are based on current expectations and assumptions that are subject to a number of risks and uncertainties. Actual results could differ materially. Sundar Pichai on Q3 2026 Highlights
Sundar Pichai, CEO – Alphabet and Google:
Thanks, Jim, and hello, everyone. Q3 was a strong quarter for Alphabet. Our deep full stack approach is driving momentum across the business with many opportunities ahead.
Let me give you a quick snapshot of the quarter. Alphabet revenues grew 25% year over year. We are now at a $500 billion annual run rate. That’s remarkable growth.
Search has strong momentum. Our AI investments are driving increased usage and overall query growth, including commercial queries. In fact, our year over year query growth rate is the highest it’s been in the last five years.
Google Cloud revenues grew 103%. Our AI infrastructure and solutions are the major drivers for cloud growth, and our customers see us as the leading provider here, so there’s huge demand. We are working hard to bring more capacity online to meet this long term demand. At the same time, we are seeing strong demand for our TPU systems across a wide range of customers and we expect that to continue as we scale. I’m excited to welcome Thomas to the call and he’ll share more about Cloud’s momentum.
On our AI models, customers are using our models more than ever. Our first party model APIs are now processing nearly 32 billion tokens per minute, 40% more than last quarter and more than four times what we processed a year ago. And with Gemini 4 Argon and now Carbon, which we announced today, we are excited to be leading at the frontier.
The Gemini app is now at 1 billion users and is the fastest growing product in Google’s history. Lastly, we have surpassed 150 million YouTube Music and Premium subscribers globally, including trials.
Today, I’ll talk more about our AI progress, our global consumer platforms led by Search and YouTube, then our long term bets.
Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon and Genie 4
Starting with AI, highlighted by our Gemini 4 series of frontier models. We are seeing strong demand and usage for Argon, which we launched two weeks ago. In fact, on our developer platforms, usage in the first week ramped up three and a half times faster than Gemini 3, even with capacity constraints.
Argon is proving to do really well in areas like finance, legal, cybersecurity, and science. Developers and cloud customers are already using it for things like untangling legacy code bases, accelerating scientific research, robotics, discovering product improvements, and domain specific tasks requiring in-depth reasoning.
And at Google, just in the last few weeks, teams across Chrome, Ads and Search have used Argon to write and optimize code much faster, shipping 35% more code with 30% fewer errors.
Argon is just the start of the Gemini 4 series. Today we announced Carbon. It’s our strongest frontier model with state of the art cybersecurity and long horizon capabilities. It ranks number one on the recently updated Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 59.
Carbon outperforms Argon across almost every domain like full stack web development, multimodal understanding, complex knowledge work, and scientific reasoning. We are rolling Carbon out first to a subset of trusted cyber defenders before we expand access and it’s being tested by the U.S. Government.
Beyond our Gemini 4 series, yesterday we announced Genie 4, our most advanced world model yet that simulates safe, realistic, interactive worlds with native audio in real time. This is an important building block for training robots and agents.
This rapid progress underscores our incredible model momentum. We are at the point where AI is helping us increase our training and launch velocity. I would expect the next twelve months to see continued acceleration because of RSI. We have more models coming soon, including more efficient versions to help developers optimize for speed and cost and to deploy across our billions of users.
Search and Agentic Experiences
Let’s turn to Search, which is bringing the benefits of AI to more people than any other product in the world. Just like our shift to mobile over a decade ago, we are focused on building the best search user experience followed by monetization.
We are building one seamless experience across AI Overviews, AI Mode, and traditional search.
And people are using our AI features more searching in entirely new ways. Their queries are longer, personalized, and multimodal. Queries in AI Mode are on average three times longer than traditional searches. And one in five AI Mode searches globally use voice, images, or documents. We have now crossed over 50 billion visual searches per month.
One area I am excited about is helping people get things done, be it booking reservations, filling in forms, scheduling appointments, or making purchases. As AI becomes more personal and starts taking action for people and businesses, trust and security matter more than ever. People already put their trust in Google to get things done in their daily lives and our leadership here remains an important differentiator.
We focus first on getting these agentic experiences right with the Gemini Agent for Enterprise because the bar for security, safety, and governance is extremely high. As a next step, we are bringing these features to search and also, of course, to our billion users on the Gemini app.
People are already using the Gemini app to do more than ask questions. They’re using it to do things including complex multi step agentic queries. Our Gemini 4 Series has been a really big step forward here and has already been handling these complex tasks really well. Agents will operate as a unifying layer across our products, so you will see us extend these agentic experiences beyond search in the Gemini app to Chrome, Android, and our Workspace products as well.
YouTube and Long-Term Bets
On to YouTube, which continues to lead in the living room as the number one streaming platform in the U.S. in terms of watch time. It’s also seeing good subscriber growth, as I mentioned.
At Made on YouTube last month, we announced more than 30 products and features for creators and viewers. One great example is custom feeds where you can simply pick a topic you love and get a tailored video home feed based on that topic. Philipp will say more on YouTube.
Onto our long term bets. Waymo is on a great trajectory. We have now driven more than 300 million fully autonomous miles. Waymo now operates in 15 major U.S. cities and just recently opened up service to the public in San Antonio, San Diego and Tampa with planned expansions into Canada, Germany, Singapore and Spain.
We are also seeing strong progress across our other long term initiatives. Isomorphic is using Argon to support drug design research. We demonstrated magic state cultivation for the first time in quantum computing and launched our TPUs into space with Project Suncatcher.
So it’s been a busy quarter and there’s much more to come. I want to thank our employees for their work this quarter. And over to you, Philipp.
Philipp Schindler on Ads, AI Max and AI Mode Monetization
Philipp Schindler, SVP and Chief Business Officer – Google:
Thanks, Sundar, and hello, everyone. I’ll highlight our momentum across Search, YouTube, and partnerships.
We continue to accelerate the deployment of Gemini models across our entire ads infrastructure to boost performance in the three areas I’ve highlighted before, ads quality, advertiser tools, and AI user experiences.
First, ads quality. The integration of Gemini models into our ads infrastructure is improving how we decode user intent and drive ads relevance. For example, in Q3, we made the biggest update to product listing ads in a decade, adding new AI capabilities to help users make more confident purchase decisions. Already, we’ve seen satisfaction increase by 19% and dissatisfaction drop more than half.
We’re also seeing AI models enable better prediction of clicks and conversions, stronger overall performance and deeper personalization of ads. For instance, through several innovations and personalization modeling, we delivered 18% incremental advertiser value and an 8% increase in user sentiment on Discover Ads in 2026. Overall, AI is accelerating our ads development and drives the majority of our product launches.
Second, advertiser tools. We’re ensuring every advertiser from global brands to small businesses can seamlessly participate in our AI services. AI Max and Performance Max continue to see strong adoption. To give marketers even more control, we’re now introducing a way to test different budgets and ROI targets across multiple search campaigns in a single AI Max A/B test.
To drive better performance, we integrated our AI creative tools directly into AI Max. In Q3 alone, Gemini models helped advertisers generate over 1.5 billion campaign assets, up over 7x year over year. For example, by using AI Max to capture long form and multilingual queries, Telstra saw a 52% increase in conversions and a 36% reduction in cost per order showing up in key moments however the customers search.
Third, AI user experiences. Sundar mentioned the distinct behavioral changes we’re seeing as users ask entirely new categories of questions. We think of search holistically, a unified product spanning traditional results and AI powered search. On AI Mode, many of these queries are upper funnel queries allowing us to capture new high value commercial intent. In addition, the majority of AI Mode queries are incremental, creating more monetization opportunities over time.
Additionally, our user research shows that people are comfortable with ads on these surfaces and find them highly relevant. Click through rates are already similar between sponsored and organic product list items within AI Mode. Based on these collective results, we are now scaling high quality ads within AI Overviews in AI Mode to more users and queries. This includes taking our monetization footprint global for the first time.
Today, we’re announcing the expansion of ads in AI Mode into international markets alongside multi turn ads in AI Mode in the U.S. As always, our approach is deliberate, prioritizing the user experience and engagement first, and then scaling the formats that drive proven ROI for advertisers. That discipline is what gives us a strong, durable foundation for the future.
Agentic Commerce and Universal Commerce Protocol
As Sundar mentioned, we are in the early stages of agentic experiences across our services and excited about the commercial opportunities ahead. The Universal Commerce Protocol, UCP, is being rapidly adopted by retailers. Today, UCP enables direct checkout for hundreds of thousands of brands across Google, including Chewy, Nike, Tapestry, and many more. We’re combining this with the Agent Payments Protocol as we build for the future of commerce.
Our focus spans two fronts, unlocking agentic capabilities that act on behalf of the consumer and designing practical tools that give shoppers confidence and control. We launched Universal Cart this quarter to support that second track, mirroring how people actually shop today across Google. This allows retailers to maintain their relationships with customers while helping to capture sales and avoid abandoned carts.
YouTube Creators, Viewers and Advertisers
Moving to YouTube. We view YouTube as a flywheel of creators, viewers, and advertisers.
First, creators. Every day, more than 20 million videos are being uploaded. We are making creation easier with new generative AI tools in YouTube Studio, plus a conversational editing assistant in Shorts and YouTube Create.
Looking at viewership. At Made on YouTube, we introduced short series where creators can structure their Shorts into seasons and episodes. Sequential playback makes it seamless for viewers to discover new Shorts and keep watching.
The third part is monetization, starting with ads. Our focus remains on delivering value across the entire funnel. In Q3, living room accounted for over one third of total YouTube ads revenues. Small and medium advertisers continue to adopt Demand Gen driving direct response. On YouTube, Wayfair’s Demand Gen campaigns drove a 2.3 times higher return on ad spend compared with traditional video reach campaigns.
Our YouTube Creator Partnerships program connects brands with over 3 million creators to drive highly effective campaigns. Brands like Lexus continue to partner with creators like Colin and Samir, driving an 11% lift in brand consideration.
More than 1.3 million channels are enrolled in YouTube Shopping globally. Building on this momentum, we recently announced that Amazon is joining YouTube Shopping. US creators can now tag Amazon products directly in their videos, Shorts, and live streams driving direct conversions.
Importantly, our subscription ecosystem continues to complement our advertising business. Subscriptions continue to grow faster than ads led by YouTube Music Premium. YouTube Premium has more than a 150 million subscribers. With Premium and Premium Lite, viewers have choice. As more people opt into Premium experiences, it’s a win for viewers, creators, and our business. Subscriptions help fund the creator ecosystem and drive the platform forward.
Strategic Partnerships
Finally, strategic partnerships. Businesses are choosing Google because we bring together cloud, AI, ads, and consumer platforms to accelerate their transformation. We expanded our partnership with American Express to support the ongoing modernization of their contact centers and collaborate on agentic commerce across search and wallet. And Unity signed an expanded multi year cloud deal to use our full AI stack to power the next generation game engine.
Our commercial priorities are clear, expanding advertiser ROI, systematically advancing our AI monetization efficiency, and scaling our ecosystem partnerships to support the next area of discovery.
In closing, I want to thank Googlers everywhere for the incredible execution and our customers and partners for their continued trust. Thomas, over to you.
Thomas Kurian on Google Cloud and Gemini Enterprise
Thomas Kurian, CEO of Google Cloud – Google:
Our strong momentum is driven by our integrated AI portfolio of chips, models, data, security and agent platforms, all designed to work together. We have strong diversification across our product lines, industries and customers. Google Cloud now has 18 product lines with more than $1 billion each in annual revenue.
And our growth this quarter was driven by strong growth in Google Cloud Platform with strength in both AI and core GCP. TPU system sales were also a meaningful contributor.
Over the past twelve months, more than 2,600 customers have used at least 100 billion tokens and nearly 75 at least 10 trillion tokens on Gemini Enterprise. Nearly 90% of the Fortune 100 use Gemini Enterprise and customers who use Gemini Enterprise in Q3 2025 more than quadruple their spend with us year over year.
One of the important ways in which customers are using Gemini is for knowledge work. Across all our AI services, we have sold more than 100 million seats for knowledge work. Gemini Enterprise now includes the Gemini agent that is deeply integrated across our entire cloud portfolio. It can research and answer questions, write and run code, and automate process flows with one agent, one API, and a single prompt window. It gives companies strong security and cost controls and the widest choice of frontier models.
Customers include BNP Paribas, who is deploying Gemini Enterprise to more than 65,000 employees to streamline workflows while maintaining strict enterprise grade security and data governance. Tata Steel, who deployed more than 300 specialized AI agents in just nine months to enhance manufacturing safety, optimize supply chains and cut service times for customers by 50%. Ulta Beauty, who built the Ulta AI shopping assistant, resulting in a 3x higher conversion rate than traditional browsing on their site.
Data, Analytics and Cybersecurity
Gemini is also driving acceleration for our database, analytics, and cybersecurity products. Gemini can now help users find, analyze, and visualize information from structured and unstructured data. Gemini uses our knowledge catalog to find the right sources and definitions of information, improving accuracy, lowering token cost, and making responses more reliable. Gemini uses our lakehouse to access information from multiple systems with zero copy.
To respond to agentic analysis, BigQuery improved query speed over 40% year over year while reducing query processing cost by over 50% year over year. Customers include Deutsche Telekom, which uses BigQuery, cloud storage and knowledge catalog to build a unified data lakehouse across 40 plus legacy on premise systems. Etsy, which migrated three petabytes of data to Unified Lakehouse. By using BigQuery and Iceberg, they now perform critical data joins up to 60% faster.
The growing risk of cybersecurity threats is also driving strong demand for our AI powered security platform. Our Gemini Flash cyber models are frontier models that automate vulnerability discovery across 20 programming languages and patch 2.6 times more reliably than other models. And there is even better performance to come as we roll out Argon and Carbon.
Our security platform integrates threat intelligence, cyber response prioritization, automated scanning, code remediation and monitoring. Gemini and Google Threat Intelligence autonomously analyzes millions of external events every day with 98% accuracy. And more than 95% of Wiz customers are adopting AI powered features and growth continues. These security tools protect critical infrastructure, including financial institutions like Banorte and Jack Henry and telecommunication providers such as AT&T.
AI Infrastructure and TPU Systems
All of this is powered by our leading AI infrastructure spanning our TPU systems, Axion CPUs, the latest NVIDIA GPUs, and our agent compute engine, which is specifically designed for AI agents.
TPU systems are specialized systems built for AI, combining hardware, compute, storage, and networking with systems components and optimized software for training and inference. TPU 8t and 8i offer up to 2.7 times better training performance and 80% better inference price performance, respectively, versus our previous generation of TPUs.
To make it easy for developers to port workloads to TPUs, we recently worked closely with Meta to deliver TorchTPU, which allows users to train, serve, and deploy PyTorch models on TPUs using the tools they are already familiar with and rely on.
Nearly 100 customers use TPUs today and we are seeing strong and growing demand from many others. In addition to our work with Anthropic, both directly and via Broadcom, and public reference customers like Apple who are scaling pre training of their foundation models on the latest generation of TPUs.
We are also working with new frontier model companies, including Mirendil and Physical Intelligence, capital markets firms, including Citadel Securities and Jump Trading, universities like Caltech and public sector service providers, including Kosava and Cirrascale.
Given continued strong and exhilarating demand, we have recently entered into partnerships to deliver TPU systems with both Crux AI and Oracle with more in the works. Over to you, Anat.
Anat Ashkenazi on Q3 2026 Financial Results
Anat Ashkenazi, SVP and CFO – Alphabet:
Thank you, Thomas. My comments will focus on year over year comparisons for the third quarter unless I state otherwise. I will start with the results at the Alphabet level and will then cover our segment results. I’ll end with some commentary on our outlook for the fourth quarter and full year 2026.
We had a strong third quarter, delivering our thirteenth consecutive quarter of double digit revenue growth. Consolidated revenues were $127.6 billion, up 25% in both reported and constant currency.
Total cost of revenue was $51.4 billion, up 24%. TAC was $16.4 billion, up 10%. Other cost of revenues was $35.1 billion, up 32%, driven by hardware costs primarily related to the sales of TPU systems, increase in depreciation, content acquisition costs and technical infrastructure operations costs.
Total operating expenses were up 20% to $35.6 billion. R&D expenses increased by 36% driven by compensation primarily for investment in AI talent as well as depreciation. Sales and marketing expenses were up 22% due to investments in Gemini and compensation. And G&A expenses decreased 17% primarily due to lapping of higher legal expenses that occurred in Q3 last year, partially offset by current year legal charges.
Operating income increased 30% to $40.6 billion and operating margin was 31.8%. Other income and expenses was negative $8.2 billion due primarily to unrealized loss from our investment in SpaceX from a decline in its stock price in the third quarter. Net income and earnings per share decreased due to the decline in OI&E.
Cash Flow, CapEx and Balance Sheet
We generated strong operating cash flow of $42.3 billion in the third quarter and $179.6 billion for the trailing twelve months. Operating cash was negatively impacted by the buildup of inventory for TPU system sales, as well as a $5.2 billion cash payment for a 2018 EC fine following the conclusion of the appeal process.
CapEx was $51.4 billion in the third quarter, with the vast majority of the spend in technical infrastructure to support our investments in AI. Approximately 65% of our investments in technical infrastructure in Q3 was related to servers and networking equipment, which generally have useful lives of six years. The remaining 35% was for data centers, which have useful lives of seven to forty years.
We had negative free cash flow of $9.1 billion in the third quarter driven by our investments in CapEx. Free cash flow in the third quarter was also affected by the aforementioned inventory build to support TPU system sales and payment of the EC fine. Free cash flow was $19.7 billion for the trailing twelve months.
We ended the quarter with $265.1 billion in cash and marketable securities, which includes $90.4 billion of marketable equity securities. Long term debt was $125.1 billion.
We have also entered into multi year agreements to secure capacity for technical infrastructure and inventory as well as leases and energy. At the end of the third quarter, we had future purchase commitments of $924 billion and pre commencement leases and other obligations of $152 billion. These dollar amounts reflect the maximum contractual obligations. However, a number of these agreements have variable pricing and volume flexibility, which could result in lower spend.
And as mentioned in our press release, our Board of Directors declared a quarterly cash dividend on our common stock of $0.22 per share, which is payable in December.
Google Services Segment Results
Turning to segment results, Google services revenues increased 11% to $96.3 billion reflecting strong growth in search and subscriptions. Total advertising revenues were up 11% with growth across all major verticals. As we stated in our outlook last quarter, FX was a slight headwind on year over year growth rates and had a more significant impact on sequential basis.
Google Search and other advertising revenues increased by 12% to $63.3 billion with retail driving the largest contribution followed by finance. YouTube advertising revenues increased 9% to $11.2 billion with brand driving the largest contribution and notable strength in living room. Network advertising revenues of $7.5 billion were up 1%.
Subscription platforms and devices revenues increased 11% this quarter to $14.3 billion driven by strong growth in YouTube subscriptions, particularly in YouTube Music and Premium and strong demand for AI plans in Google One.
Google services operating income increased 17% to $39.2 billion and operating margin was 40.7%.
Google Cloud Segment Results
The Google Cloud segment delivered record growth in the third quarter. Cloud revenues were up 103% to $30.7 billion driven by strong growth in GCP with strength in AI, including both AI infrastructure and AI solutions and core GCP. TPU system sales were also a meaningful contributor. GCP accounted for the vast majority of the revenue generated in Q3. The cloud revenue growth rate, excluding the impact of TPU systems, was again incredibly strong.
Cloud operating income was $11.1 billion, more than tripling year over year. Operating margin increased from 23.7% in the third quarter last year to 36.2%. The third party bridging capacity, which we discussed on our prior call, came online only at the very end of Q3 and therefore had limited negative impact to our margin in the quarter.
Google Cloud’s backlog was $561 billion in the third quarter, with the majority of the backlog related to typical GCP contracts. We expect to recognize just over 55% of the total backlog as revenues over the next twenty four months.
In Other Bets, revenues were $372 million and the operating loss was $2 billion as we continue to expand Waymo’s business and invest in key Other Bets. In Alphabet level activities, the operating loss was $7.7 billion driven by shared AI R&D expenses.
Outlook for Q4 and Full Year 2026
I’ll now turn to our outlook for the remainder of 2026. First, in terms of revenues, at current spot rates, we would expect a slightly larger FX headwinds to our consolidated revenues in Q4 relative to the prior quarter. This impact will be seen primarily in search and YouTube ads.
In Google services, we will continue to lap the search performance acceleration that began in the third quarter last year, which will pressure revenue growth in Q4.
In Google Cloud, we expect TPU system sales to continue to scale in Q4, followed by meaningful revenue ramp in 2027. With respect to cloud margins, given the supply constrained environment, we started utilizing third party compute capacity at the end of Q3 and will ramp usage in Q4. We expect margin headwinds in the near term as we utilize this capacity.
Moving to investments, our full year 2026 CapEx guidance range of $195 billion to $205 billion remain unchanged. We continue to expect our CapEx to increase significantly in 2027 and we’ll provide further details on our Q4 call.
In terms of expenses, the significant increase in our investment in technical infrastructure will continue to weigh on the P&L in the form of higher depreciation expenses and related data centers operation costs such as energy.
Before we move to Q&A, let me say a few words on how we think about return on invested capital and free cash flow. We’re in a unique position to be able to monetize our AI investments across many products that reach billions of users and enterprises. Our AI investments in search are driving our highest query growth in half a decade. Google Cloud has generated tens of billions of dollars in AI revenues year to date, and Google One is our fastest growing subscription products driven by demand for AI plans.
Throughout this investment cycle, we expect to generate an attractive ROIC that remains meaningfully above our cost of capital and anticipate generating strong cash flow from operations. Our sizable investments in technical infrastructure and inventory build of TPU systems as we scale that business will put pressure on free cash flows.
Q3 was a strong quarter highlighted by our innovation and disciplined execution across all areas of the business, including the launch of Gemini 4. I want to take this opportunity to thank our employees for their contributions to our performance. We will now open the call for Q&A. Thank you.
Question and Answer Session

As a reminder to ask a question, you will need to press 1 on your telephone. And our first question comes from Doug Anmuth with JPMorgan. Line is now open.

And then Philipp, just on query growth, fastest in five years, but obviously some deceleration in search growth. Can you just help us understand the disconnect there? Thanks, Doug. We definitely started seeing a step change, I would say, with 3.5 Flash internally as our models got better at long horizon tasks, agentic coding, etcetera. But I’m definitely seeing a huge point of inflection once we have started deploying Argon internally.
It is one of the most meaningful internal productivity levers we have seen on software engineering, directly tracking high quality code that’s getting checked in, the rate at which it’s getting checked in, reducing the number of rollbacks. But more importantly, people are tackling massive problems, as well as we are beginning to see innovation on the product and the consumer experience side from deploying those models. But all this happened only about four weeks ago.
We are now deploying Carbon internally, and Carbon is a meaningful step up from Argon. So, as we roll it out and make it all widely available to Googlers with more compute, I expect to that’s what I was referring to, and I’m even more excited since I said that in the podcast, and what I would view as a golden year ahead in terms of us being able to be more productive, and so excited for the innovation ahead. Philipp?
Philipp Schindler, SVP and Chief Business Officer – Google:
Yeah, Doug, on the second part of your question, look, in Q3, Search grew 12% year over year to 63 billion and when you look at the change in growth rate from Q2 to Q3, there were a few drivers here.
So first, there was FX. It was the single largest driver of Search deceleration relative to Q2.
Second, there were vertical specific dynamics. In retail, some of our APAC advertisers were impacted by the EU eliminating its de minimis exemption. In media and entertainment, spend normalized after an unseasonably strong Q2 driven by the FIFA World Cup, which had more matches and therefore more advertising budgets allocated in June than actually in July.
It’s also to the next part of your question. It’s worth noting that the strong growth in AI Mode queries had an impact because of the lower monetization we were seeing in AI Mode. To be clear, we feel very good about our trajectory here. Our ad experiments have shown promising results, and we’re now scaling ads within both AI Overviews and AI Mode to more users and more queries, including, as I mentioned, international markets and multi turn ads in the U.S.
OPERATOR:
Thank you. Our next question comes from the line of Brian Nowak with Morgan Stanley. Your line is now open.
Agentic Products and Search Comparisons
Brian Nowak, Analyst – Morgan Stanley:
Thanks for taking my questions. I have two. Maybe let me start with agentic. You rolled out Spark to some of the users throughout the year. I know Sundar, you’ve talked a lot about shipping more agentic products to the broader base of users.
Can you just help us understand a little bit some of the key hurdles you have to overcome technologically to ship out agentic products to the full base of users? Is it model efficiency? Is it capacity? Is it the technologies that are ready? What do we have to do technologically to do that?
And then the second one, just on to Doug’s question on Search. Anat, can you help us better understand the Search performance comparison factor that you talked about impacting Search? How do we think about that as we kind of go into 4Q, 1Q, just kind of looking at the compares? Thanks.
Sundar Pichai, CEO – Alphabet and Google:
Thanks, Brian. First of all, on Agents, what an exciting and dynamic area. I think when I look at our new models and we see the capabilities of the kind of complex tasks they can take in an automated way and execute with very, very high quality, it’s clear that the agentic capabilities we’ve all envisioned are going to be real, and so I couldn’t be more excited to bring the next generation agentic experiences to our users.
To your question, I would say a few things. I think the Gemini 4 model series has been important. We are working on distilled versions of our Carbon models, so that we can deploy it across a billion users scale efficiently, both from a compute, from a latency standpoint, and from a quality standpoint. So, that’s been an important area of investment.
Definitely making sure you need more than a capable model at scale, you need a robust harness, you need a highly secure run time and this is where GCP comes in, being able to give each and every one of our users a secure runtime with 24/7 availability in the cloud, the right ecosystem of skills and connectors. So, of that is the foundation.
We have been hard at work and we decided to roll it out to enterprise first, which we announced with our Gemini agent for enterprise, essentially because they have the highest bar for security governance controls. And it’s been well received and we are in the process of expanding that.
Later this year, we will bring that same experience to consumers, obviously starting with the 1 billion users in our Gemini app. We already see people in the app moving beyond chat to what our task and agent like experiences. And so, the trust users have in our products, the fact that all their context is right there, I think sets us up well, but we are really focused as a company on delivering that unified experience across our products and you should expect to see a lot of progress later this year. Thanks.
Anat Ashkenazi, SVP and CFO – Alphabet:
