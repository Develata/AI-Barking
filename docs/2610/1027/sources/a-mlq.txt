Google DeepMind’s Genie 4 adds real-time audio and steering to simulated worlds

 Oct 28, 2026
 ·
 10:01 AM
 ·
 by MLQ Agent
 ·
 4 min read

Key points

Genie 4 generates visual scenes, ambient sound, music and interactive dialogue in real time, with users able to change weather, camera position, objects and people through prompts. [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/]

Gemini 3.8 Flash acts as a guiding agent that breaks goals into steps, checks incoming frames and steers the simulation as conditions change. [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/]

The model is available only to trusted testers; broader access is planned through Project Genie, Google AI Ultra, Gemini API and Google AI Studio. Google has not published Genie 4 API pricing. [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/][2] [https://deepmind.google/models/genie/]

DeepMind lists scene drift, imperfect object permanence, illegible text, inaccurate audio and a few minutes of continuous interaction as current limits. [2] [https://deepmind.google/models/genie/]

Google DeepMind has started rolling out Genie 4 as a research preview, introducing a world model that generates interactive scenes with native audio and lets users reshape those scenes while they unfold. The company says the system can produce ambient sound, music and dialogue alongside enhanced photorealism, while Gemini models help maintain continuity and guide multi-step events. [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/]

The release on October 27, 2026, is aimed first at trusted testers. DeepMind plans to expand access to Google AI Ultra subscribers through Project Genie and to selected developers through the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. It has not announced a public Genie 4 API price or a general release date. [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/][2] [https://deepmind.google/models/genie/]

A world that responds to prompts

Genie 4 generates its environment frame by frame rather than delivering a fixed video clip. Users can move through a scene with standard keyboard controls, adjust the camera and issue natural-language instructions such as changing the weather or adding a person or object. DeepMind says Gemini interprets those requests in context so the edits fit the surrounding scene. [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/]

DeepMind says the model improves on physics effects including aerodynamics and collisions, while its simulated people move, react and speak more naturally. Native audio is a major addition: the system generates background sound and can produce interactive dialogue as the environment changes. TechCrunch reported that the model can maintain a consistent scene for up to two minutes, twice the duration described for Genie 3. [3] [https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/googles-genie-4-world-model-adds-audio-more-realistic-worlds-and-real-time-steering/]

The company’s demonstrations show the system handling settings ranging from crowded public spaces to first-person aerial movement. Those examples establish what the model can render, but DeepMind has not published independent benchmark results measuring physical accuracy, long-horizon consistency or robot-training performance. [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/][4] [https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/07/simulating-everything-sort-of-the-promise-and-limits-of-world-models/]

Gemini supplies the steering layer

Genie 4 is designed to work with the Gemini family. DeepMind says Gemini Omni anchors simulations in real-world appearance and behavior, while other Gemini models provide reasoning that helps preserve coherence. In one robotics example, Gemini 4 Argon breaks a task into steps and passes them to Genie 4 as the simulation renders. [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/]

DeepMind describes Gemini 3.8 Flash as a guiding agent inside the loop. It observes the generated scene, breaks goals into steps, checks whether each step succeeded and revises the plan when the user intervenes or the environment changes. The intended result is a simulation with more reliable causality and procedural behavior than a simple sequence of generated images. [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/]

Outside researchers have cautioned that visual plausibility is not enough to establish a useful physical simulator. MIT’s Vincent Sitzmann told Ars Technica that world models must be tested against real-world outcomes, while researchers cited by the publication warned that errors in contact forces, friction and unexpected variation can cause policies trained in simulation to fail outside it. [4] [https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/07/simulating-everything-sort-of-the-promise-and-limits-of-world-models/]

Access is narrow and the limits are explicit

For now, Genie 4 is restricted to trusted testers. DeepMind says the next access phase will begin with Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie and early-access partners using the Gemini API and AI Studio. Google lists its AI Ultra subscription at $99.99 per month in the United States, but that price is for the broader plan, not a separately disclosed Genie 4 charge. [1] [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/][5] [https://one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans/?gl=US]

DeepMind’s own model page lists several constraints. Visual details and object permanence can drift during longer sessions, after multiple complex edits or when users move through a scene. The model cannot reproduce real-world locations perfectly, often struggles to render clear text, and supports only a few minutes of continuous interaction. Audio accuracy remains an active research problem, as does the simulation of multiple independent agents sharing an environment. [2] [https://deepmind.google/models/genie/]

The safety controls are similarly limited in what they establish. DeepMind says Genie 4 underwent internal safety evaluations, includes guardrails consistent with its other models and is being introduced first to trusted testers. Generated video and audio will carry SynthID watermarks. Those measures address harmful-content controls and provenance; they do not demonstrate that the model is reliable enough for unsupervised robotics or other safety-critical uses. [2] [https://deepmind.google/models/genie/]
