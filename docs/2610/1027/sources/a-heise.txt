Google Genie 4 makes interactive 3D environments more realistic

 Google’s Genie 4 generates interactive AI worlds with sound and speech. Gemini improves the simulation, but the technology remains experimental.

 [/news/Google-Genie-4-KI-Simulator-fuer-interaktive-3D-Umgebungen-wird-realistischer-11502733.html]

 Advertisement

Google DeepMind has unveiled a new generation of its AI world model, Genie 4. It generates interactive 3D environments from text or image prompts that users can explore with a keyboard. Unlike traditional game engines, Genie does not render scenes from a prebuilt 3D world; instead, it continuously generates them anew with AI. Simulations are currently limited to a few minutes.

 Continue after ad

Audio generation is new: Genie 4 creates matching ambient sounds, music and speech alongside the images. Text prompts also let users interact more flexibly with the environments: They can, for example, change the weather and add objects while a simulation is running. Google says the visuals look more photorealistic, human movements more natural, and physical events such as collisions more plausible than in the previous version.

Genie 4 is initially available to selected testers. In the coming weeks, access through Project Genie is expected to follow for Google AI Ultra subscribers and selected developers through the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. Google has not announced a date for general release.

Genie 4 is still research

According to Google, the interaction between Genie 4 and Google’s Gemini models plays a key role [https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/]. The video model Gemini Omni [/news/Google-erweitert-Video-KI-Gemini-Omni-Flash-auf-Version-1-1-11433066.html?from-en=1] is meant to help simulated worlds look as realistic as possible and behave plausibly. Gemini 3.8 Flash [/news/Gemini-3-8-Flash-und-Flash-Cyber-Googles-neue-KI-Modelle-im-Ueberblick-11439421.html?from-en=1], in turn, observes the ongoing simulation, plans the steps needed to complete a task and checks whether they were carried out successfully.

Short simulation times and a lack of consistency continue to impose strict limits on Genie. According to Bloomberg [https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-27/google-upgrades-ai-world-model-that-rattled-video-game-stocks], simulations currently last up to two minutes, and, as Google researcher Hang Qi acknowledges, the environments do not remain consistent throughout.

Google has been researching Genie for years. The third generation of the world model [/news/Genie-3-Googles-Weltmodell-baut-interaktive-Umgebungen-10510919.html?from-en=1] enabled real-time interactions at 24 frames per second and 720p in 2025. In early 2026, Google first made the technology available to Google AI Ultra subscribers through the web app Project Genie [/news/Project-Genie-Google-oeffnet-experimentelles-KI-Weltmodell-fuer-Anwender-11158504.html?from-en=1], and since mid-2026 it has been available outside the U.S. [/news/Googles-3D-Weltgenerator-erzeugt-jetzt-echte-Orte-aus-Street-View-11300366.html?from-en=1] In hands-on testing, sessions were limited to around 60 seconds. There was no sound, and interaction options were severely limited.

 Continue after ad

World models search for applications

Which applications will actually take off with Genie 4 remains an open question, even for Google. According to DeepMind product manager Diego Rivas, the technology is still in the research phase. Google says early testers are already using Genie 4 to prepare movie scenes and develop interactive game prototypes. The company has also demonstrated how historical photographs can be turned into explorable scenes, and sees further potential uses in training robots and AI agents.

While traditional language models primarily learn patterns in text, world models are meant to grasp spatial and physical relationships and predict the consequences of actions. This could help AI systems plan complex tasks better and test actions virtually in advance.

Former Meta chief scientist Yann LeCun is researching a related approach with AMI Labs [/news/Weltmodell-statt-LLM-Start-up-von-Yann-LeCun-erhaelt-890-Millionen-Euro-11206213.html?from-en=1]. AI pioneer Fei-Fei Li is also developing world models with World Labs.
