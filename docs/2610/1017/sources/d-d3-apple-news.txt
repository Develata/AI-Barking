Strengthening App Review Guidelines against nonconsensual intimate imagery and bait-and-switch apps
 [/news/?id=vvzx6cul]

October 16, 2026

The App Review Guidelines are a core part of the safe and trusted experience that the App Store strives to offer users around the world — and the foundation of that trust is clear standards that are consistently enforced. 

As outlined in our App Review Guidelines, pornographic material has long been prohibited on the App Store — this includes apps that generate nudity or sexually explicit imagery, such as “nudification” apps that create sexually explicit images of real individuals without their consent. Apps that host user-generated content are required to filter objectionable material, give users a way to report offensive content, and let users block abusive accounts. Apps containing hidden or undocumented features are also prohibited on the App Store, including apps that misrepresent their purpose during review and only reveal harmful capabilities — such as “nudification” — after approval, a tactic commonly known as bait-and-switch.

We want great apps and the innovative developers behind them to succeed on the App Store, and as new risks and novel user experiences emerge, we evolve our App Review Guidelines to maintain the trust of users and developers alike. Today we’re publishing updates that strengthen and extend the existing App Store protections, continuing to build on our long-held standards for privacy, quality, and safety.

For complete details, see the App Review Guidelines [https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/].

Changes

1.2: Content moderation requirements now explicitly cover nonconsensual intimate imagery — including imagery generated using AI tools — as content that apps must proactively prevent and remove from their platforms. Developers who fail to implement adequate safeguards — or don’t remedy violations after notice — risk violating the Developer Code of Conduct and being removed from the App Store.

2.5.19: This rule clarifies our longstanding prohibition on apps misrepresenting what they do during the App Review process and then changing after approval — for example, adding features or functionality that weren’t included in the version of the app that was submitted to App Review. Apps found engaging in this conduct are subject to removal from the App Store and removal from the Apple Developer Program.

Section 5: The App Store has always prohibited apps that facilitate human trafficking or the exploitation of children (including child sexual abuse material). When we find instances of apps containing such content, we remove them from the App Store. We’ve updated Section 5 of the App Review guidelines to make clear that apps with such content will be removed from the App Store and blocked from alternative distribution.

We’re committed to keeping bad actors off the App Store, with established processes in place to review and act on apps that violate our guidelines. Last year, we prevented over 150K apps with safety issues from reaching customers and terminated over 193K developer accounts for engaging in fraud and abuse. These violations trace back to malicious actors — in some cases coordinated fraud operations — working to conceal harmful content that only becomes active once an app is approved, and as their tactics evolve, we continue to evolve our own tools and resources to prevent and catch this kind of behavior even faster. We do not hesitate to remove apps, and terminate the developers behind them, when they violate these guidelines.

Updates to Full Disk Access in macOS
 [/news/?id=p6zjojqw]

October 2, 2026

We give developers powerful APIs to build incredible capabilities into their apps for Apple products, backed by a set of controls designed to protect users’ private data. Full Disk Access largely sidesteps these controls in order to allow backup apps to function properly on the Mac. Some developers are using Full Disk Access in ways that could put users at risk, exposing everything on their systems—including files, mail, messages, and even browsing history—without users’ full knowledge and understanding. For communication apps, this can also compromise the privacy of the people users are communicating with.

Going forward, we will introduce additional controls to ensure that users who genuinely wish to grant an app this extraordinary level of access can only do so with very explicit user action. Addressing this is critical. As AI agents become increasingly capable and autonomous, the risks associated with this level of access will grow substantially. We are committed to ensuring users clearly understand these risks before granting such access, so they can make informed decisions about their own data and privacy.

Upcoming expiration of Developer ID Certification Authority (Sub-CA)
 [/news/?id=w4atic4c]

October 1, 2026

The original Developer ID Certification Authority (Sub-CA) expires on February 1, 2027. Certificates issued by this authority will stop working on that date.

What to do:

Check if you’re affected. In Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles [https://developer.apple.com/account/resources], look for certificates expiring on or before February 1, 2027. See Replacing Developer ID certificates issued from the previous Sub-CA [https://developer.apple.com/help/account/certificates/replace-developer-id-certificates] for help identifying your certificate’s authority.

Create a new certificate. Generate a replacement from the current authority, Developer ID Certification Authority (G2). Note: This certificate authority is valid until 2031, but the certificates issued by the certificate authority expire annually and must be renewed each year.

If you’re using Xcode 11.4 or earlier, update before creating your new certificate.

When prompted for a Developer ID Certificate Intermediary, select G2 Sub-CA. Choosing another option may issue a certificate that also expires in 2027.

Re-sign based on what you distribute.

Installer packages (.pkg): Starting February 1, 2027, .pkg files signed with an affected certificate will no longer install. Re-sign all packages with your new certificate before this date.

Mac apps: Previously signed and notarized Mac software (with a secure timestamp) will keep working—no action needed. For future updates, sign with your new certificate and include a secure timestamp for notarization.

Get your subscriptions ready for iOS 27
 [/news/?id=likeohx4]

September 16, 2026

Discover how you can expand the reach of your subscriptions with new features in iOS 27. You can start preparing today by making sure your apps use StoreKit 2 to handle your Apple In-App Purchases. 

Request access to Bundles and Suites

Offer multiple subscriptions as a single Apple In-App Purchase in your apps with new Bundles and Suites capabilities. With Bundles, people can access multiple subscriptions in one purchase — across a single app, multiple apps from the same developer, or even spanning multiple developers’ apps. With Suites, people get access to a single subscription that seamlessly works across a set of apps from a single developer.

The App Store provides the purchase experience, which displays all apps included in your Bundle or Suite, so it’s easy for people to download and access their subscription content right away.

To get started, review our Bundles and Suites page [https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/bundles-and-suites/], which includes eligible configurations, technical requirements, and how to submit a request form. Bundles and Suites will be available later this year in iOS 27, iPadOS 27, macOS 27, tvOS 27, or later.

Offer Bundles and Suites [http://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/bundles-and-suites/] 

Bundles and Suites video [https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/210/?time=530]

Multiseat purchasing enabled in App Store Connect

Multiseat purchases make it easy for organizations and groups to buy your subscriptions in bulk through Volume Purchasing and Group Purchases. This presents you with an opportunity to expand your subscription beyond single users, as small businesses and specific work groups or departments can access your subscription without having to go through additional administrative workflows.

With Volume Purchasing, organizations that use Apple Business and Apple School Manager can buy your subscriptions through a single Apple In-App Purchase and assign seats using a device management provider. 

With Group Purchases, a subscriber can buy multiple seats in a single Apple In-App Purchase and invite others to join. Apple handles any seat assignment and the invitation flow, to let groups — like small businesses, individual departments, or teams — easily access your subscriptions.

This opens your subscription to users who don't fit the traditional single-subscriber model: a work team splitting the cost of a productivity app, a small business outfitting a handful of employees, a department within a larger company, or a group of collaborators sharing access to a creative tool. Any of these groups can become paying subscribers today, without going through IT or procurement.

Starting today, multiseat purchases are enabled by default for subscriptions in App Store Connect. You have full control over availability and can disable multiseat purchases entirely, or selectively manage availability across the App Store, Apple Business, and Apple School Manager.

Volume Purchasing launches on October 22, 2026, and Group Purchases will launch this winter.

Changes for apps in the European Union
 [/news/?id=gmws0jgp]

August 18, 2026

Apple is making changes to its business terms for apps in the European Union, following close collaboration with the European Commission. The changes reduce complexity by moving every developer that distributes apps in the EU to a single set of business terms.

Under the new business terms, Apple will charge a commission on the sale of digital goods and services. The Core Technology Fee, a per-install fee for developers who achieve extraordinary scale, will be replaced by the Core Technology Commission, a simple 5% commission on digital transactions in apps distributed outside the App Store. Members of the Apple Developer Program can review and agree to the updated terms today in the Apple Developer Program License Agreement. Some key updates, which go into effect October 1, 2026, include:

Adjusting commission rates across the App Store, alternative payments, and alternatively distributed apps.

Allowing apps to offer alternative payment options alongside Apple In-App Purchase.

Adding child safety protections for alternative payments on the App Store.

Expanding the qualifications for developers to operate alternative app marketplaces and distribute their apps over the web.

Upcoming changes to the App Store Receipt Signing Intermediate Certificate
 [/news/?id=b6tejt6f]

October 31, 2024

As part of ongoing efforts to improve security and privacy on Apple platforms, the App Store receipt signing intermediate certificate is being updated to use the SHA-256 cryptographic algorithm. This certificate is used to sign App Store receipts, which are the proof of purchase for apps and In-App Purchases.

This update is being completed in multiple phases [https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=smofnyhj] and some existing apps on the App Store may be impacted by the next update, depending on how they verify receipts.

Starting January 24, 2025, if your app performs on-device receipt validation and doesn't support a SHA-256 algorithm, your app will fail to validate the receipt. If your app prevents customers from accessing the app or premium content when receipt validation fails, your customers may lose access to their content.

If your app performs on-device receipt validation, update your app to support certificates that use the SHA-256 algorithm; alternatively, use the AppTransaction [https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/apptransaction] and Transaction [https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction] APIs to verify App Store transactions.

For more details, view TN3138: Handling App Store receipt signing certificate change [https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3138-handling-app-store-receipt-signing-certificate-changes].
