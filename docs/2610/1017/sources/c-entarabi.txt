Meta Removes Controversial Terms Over Website Scraping and AI Training

 Published on: 18 October، 2026

Meta

Meta has removed controversial language from its commercial terms following concerns that it could allow website scraping and the use of publisher content for AI training. The company says the revision does not change its existing web crawling practices.

Meta has revised its commercial terms of use, removing language that raised legal concerns about the company’s ability to collect content from websites, including news publishers, and use it to train artificial intelligence models.

The decision follows criticism from legal experts and publishers who warned that the original wording could grant Meta broad permission to use online content without negotiating separate licensing agreements with website owners.

What Did Meta Remove From Its Commercial Terms?

The controversy began when Meta updated its commercial terms to include a provision allowing the company to use content from domains, web pages, and URLs associated with business accounts.

The provision stated that Meta could use this content to provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of its products and services.

The explicit reference to training raised concerns that Meta could interpret the agreement as permission to collect website content for developing its AI models.

According to Nieman Journalism Lab, legal experts described the provision as unusually broad, warning that it could allow Meta to scrape content from publishers operating business accounts on its platforms.

Meta Clarifies Its Website Crawling Policies

In response to the concerns, Meta stated that the updated terms did not represent any change to how the company crawls websites or collects publicly available information.

The company explained that the provisions were intended to apply specifically to its advertising and business products rather than introduce new permissions for AI training.

As reported by The Verge, Meta subsequently removed the controversial language from its commercial terms to clarify their intended scope.

In its statement, Meta acknowledged the concerns and explained that the consolidated crawling terms applied only to its advertising and business services.

The company emphasized that the clarification does not change how its web crawlers operate.

Does the Change Resolve Publishers’ Concerns?

Although Meta has removed the explicit reference to AI training, the revision does not necessarily settle the broader debate surrounding website scraping and the use of publisher content.

Questions remain about how technology companies access online information, the permissions required to collect it, and the level of control publishers have over the use of their content.

These concerns have become increasingly relevant as AI companies rely on large volumes of digital information to develop and improve their systems.

For news publishers and other content creators, the issue extends beyond AI training to include intellectual property protection and the commercial use of their work.

AI Training and Copyright Remain Under Legal Scrutiny

Meta’s policy revision comes amid continuing legal disputes over the use of copyrighted material in artificial intelligence development.

While Meta previously secured a favorable ruling in a high-profile copyright lawsuit related to AI training, other legal challenges involving the company and the broader AI industry remain ongoing.

The disputes highlight growing tensions between technology companies seeking access to large datasets and publishers demanding greater control over their intellectual property.

Meta’s decision to remove the disputed language addresses concerns about the wording of its commercial terms. However, it does not establish broader restrictions on the company’s existing web crawling practices.

As artificial intelligence becomes increasingly integrated into advertising, business tools, and digital services, transparency around data collection and content usage is likely to remain a central issue for both technology companies and publishers.
